# Finding a useful instrument

The catalogue is most useful when approached with a specific obstruction in a proof. A source paper's headline may be far from the problem at hand, while one intermediate lemma has exactly the right input and output.

This guide selects starting points from the October 16 expansion. It does not rank historical novelty or certify the source manuscripts. Proposed combinations below are research prompts, not completed applications.

## Follow a connection

[Exact constraints through approximation](../catalog/patterns/preserve-exact-constraints-through-approximation.md) connects convolution, homology, hereditary corners and integral lattices. The common question is which equations must remain exact while the construction changes.

[Information available to adaptive choices](../catalog/patterns/control-what-an-adaptive-choice-can-observe.md) connects posterior bounds, local coordinate lists and stationary sampling. The common question is whether a later choice observes more than the estimate permits.

[Operator domains and order](../catalog/patterns/match-operator-estimates-to-their-domain.md) connects smoothing, spectral comparison, clipping and stable evolution. The common question is which scalar manipulations survive in the relevant operator topology.

For a possible combined project, consider a constrained moment problem with a numerically successful candidate. The convolution mechanism could preserve linear cancellation while rationalizing, and a separate negative-square certificate could provide a strict upper margin. Before combining them, one must prove that the moment kernels, admissible supports and limiting procedures agree. Neither the catalogue nor the finite checks establish that compatibility.

## Read the evidence in order

1. Read the card's mathematical statement and limitations. Decide whether your input satisfies every hypothesis.
2. Follow the pinned source label and read the surrounding proof. The source manifest records the exact file hash and line.
3. Read the antecedent comparison. An explicit classical source is useful attribution, not a reason to discard the method. A targeted search is not exhaustive novelty certification.
4. Inspect and run the batch check. Determine whether it tests an exact identity, a finite example, a numerical approximation or a negative control. Passing examples do not prove the infinite-dimensional or asymptotic step.
5. Prove the transfer to your problem. Record a new use case only after its conclusion and its remaining dependencies are clear.
