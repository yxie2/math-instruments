# Forms of mathematical instruments

An instrument can have more than one form. These labels help browsing; they do not establish novelty, correctness or reuse.

| Form | What the record describes |
|---|---|
| `lemma` | A supporting theorem, proposition or lemma with explicit hypotheses |
| `inequality` | An estimate, bound or comparison with its parameter dependence |
| `identity` | An equality, recurrence or cancellation formula |
| `construction` | A procedure for producing an object, gadget, embedding, decomposition or cover |
| `object-or-structure` | An auxiliary object, invariant, hierarchy, operator or algebraic structure |
| `criterion` | A sufficient, necessary or equivalent condition, with its scope stated |
| `reduction` | A passage between problems, models, scales or formulations |
| `algorithm` | An effective procedure, including its model and resource bounds |
| `proof-mechanism` | A local argument, normalization, bootstrap or other proof device |
| `proof-architecture` | An organization of several devices into a larger argument |
| `computational-certificate` | A finite certificate or computation used in a mathematical argument |

The initial form labels are editorial indexing of the existing descriptions. The mathematical description takes precedence over a short label. Combined devices presented as one numbered item in the direction paper remain one initial entry; 115 entries is not a count of 115 separate new theorems.

## Novelty

The direction paper uses **new**, **new combination**, **incremental** and **unclear**, including mixed assessments. Keep the comparison and its scope, not only the label. A family-level assessment must not be copied into each instrument as if it were an independently established verdict.

## Scope

The collection includes candidate new instruments, new combinations and settings for established tools, and comparisons needed to understand their contribution. Demonstrated reuse is a later question, not an admission requirement.
