# Independent contact-length control and form-level removal of high modes

## Source manuscripts and claim

- [Continuum Coulomb hardness with binary nuclear charges](../papers/P-000311.md): selected passages extracted.
- [QMA-hardness of continuum Coulomb energy with unit nuclear charges](../papers/P-000312.md): selected passages extracted.

The sources claim QMA-hardness of continuum electronic Coulomb energy with binary-encoded positive nuclear charges and with unit nuclear charges.

## Extracted instruments

- [MI-000542: Solve contact-length variations by separating row, column and height coordinates](../instruments/MI-000542.md)
- [MI-000543: Eliminate high modes using coercivity in a form norm](../instruments/MI-000543.md)

## Dependencies and antecedents

Pathwise coordinate accumulation, separated gadget heights, rational rounding and norm Taylor bounds; variational forms, Hardy estimates, one-particle gaps and square completion.

Classical rigidity-matrix linearization and bounded elimination of a gapped complementary space.

## Review and formalization scope

Read the full contact classification and right-inverse construction, and the complete unit-charge complement-elimination proof. The single-well oracle, hopping calibration, nuclear replacement estimates and QMA reductions remain unverified.

The shared Comparator defines spinful antisymmetric H1 forms and their spectral infima, binary and unit nuclear inputs, rational distinct positions, unary electron count, threshold gaps at least one, and deterministic polynomial-time many-one QMA hardness. Both hardness target proofs contain sorry. Basic codec and promise-disjointness proofs are present; no orbital truncation or binding premise is part of the target. The scope document and every linked Comparator were read in full. No solution or Lean project was built.

Contact control is linearized, with an explicit quadratic remainder for actual lengths. Energy comparison is conditional on a coercive complement and a coupling bound in its form norm, and does not assume a ground eigenvector exists.
