# Cameron-Martin comparison transfers one local test kernel across domains

[Family 233](../families/openai-math-233.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000098.md) · [Batch B006](../../batches/B006/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Fix regular buffers $K\Subset B\Subset P$ and a sequence of bounded tests of the cables in K. In a zero-boundary reference GFF on P, select a joint subsequence and use the reverse-buffer lemma to write the conditional test mean as $k(\Phi|_B)$, with $0\le k\le1$.

The same selected k governs mixed expectations in admissible domains D containing the closure of P, provided their approximations contain the identical subdivided reference cable domain, with unchanged edges and resistances. Prescribed harmonic means must converge both in the full-field extension topology and smoothly on the buffers in the source’s finite-difference sense. Along the selected subsequence,

$$\mathbb E[T_n v(\widehat\Phi_n)]\longrightarrow\mathbb E[k(\Phi|_B)v(\Phi)]$$

for bounded continuous full-field v. No evaluation of the discontinuous k on a discrete field is required.

The transfer uses Gaussian shifts. With $\|u\|_\nabla^2=(2\pi)^{-1}\int|\nabla u|^2$, their likelihoods satisfy

$$L_u=\exp\{X_u-\|u\|_\nabla^2/2\},\quad\mathbb EL_u^r=e^{r(r-1)\|u\|_\nabla^2/2},\quad\|L_u-L_v\|_1\le\min(2,\|u-v\|_\nabla).$$

The cable likelihood coordinate converges in L2 to the full-field pairing with $-\Delta u/(2\pi)$. This permits multiplication by arbitrary bounded tests. Domain Markov decomposition supplies a harmonic shift; cutoff, smooth finite nets and interior estimates transfer the reference limit. The reverse-buffer argument then restores conditioning on the full field.

One subsequence works for countably many prescribed tests. A fixed random parameter may select the domain and mean if the conditional parent law is the corresponding GFF and the approximation hypotheses hold almost surely. The result identifies the conditional kernel of the auxiliary joint limit and equality in law of its field marginal with the parent, not equality of their realizations. The same cutoff method yields total-variation convergence of GFF restrictions on a fixed interior buffer under regular inner exhaustion.

## Role in the source argument

Allows a local test calibrated in one deterministic reference domain to be used after a separate random exploration, with a common kernel and controlled conditioning.

## Antecedents and comparison

Cameron-Martin likelihoods and Pinsker are classical. [Aru, Lupu and Sepulveda](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.09204v1) provide the metric-graph approximation context. The application addresses the current-cluster extension discussed by [Alcalde Lopez, Heeney and Lis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05886v1). The extracted package is not a new Gaussian change-of-measure theorem.

## Checks and limitations

Checked the three-covariance L2 identification, likelihood moment formula, L1 constant, cutoff argument and the fixed-parameter integration step. Finite Gaussian quadrature tests likelihood normalization and the shift-distance bound.

The common local graph, buffer separation, convergence of harmonic means and conditional GFF premise must all be retained. The subsequence is fixed before realizing a random domain. This does not establish the later forcing or no-crossing claims.

## Possible applications

Comparing local crossing or sign tests across deterministic and explored domains without asserting continuity of those events as functions of a distribution-valued field.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
