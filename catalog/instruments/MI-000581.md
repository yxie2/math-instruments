# Use a maximal truncation to bound the strictly heavy set

[Family 175](../families/openai-math-175.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000350.md) · [Batch B060](../../batches/B060/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let lambda be a probability distribution on finite X, let A be a subset, m>=0 and 0<d<1. Call u in [0,1] admissible when {x:lambda_x>u} is contained in A and

sum_x min(lambda_x,u)(1_A(x)-(1-d)) >= m u.

If any u is admissible, a largest epsilon exists. Indeed, the containment condition is the closed inequality u>=max_(x outside A) lambda_x, taking the maximum as zero when the complement is empty; the remaining inequality is continuous. Put R={x:lambda_x>epsilon}. Then R is contained in A and |R|<=m/d. If epsilon=1, R is empty. Otherwise the right derivative of the left side minus m u at epsilon is d|R|-m: all unsaturated coordinates lie in A, and coordinates exactly at epsilon contribute zero to that right derivative. A positive derivative would permit a larger admissible value. Hence it is nonpositive. The strict inequality in the definition of R handles ties and the epsilon=0 endpoint.

## Role in the source argument

Controls the heavy tail of a witness distribution before counting fragments.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Bednorz, Martynek and Meller](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.14636), Lemma 3.6 and its proof on pages 9-10, use a largest admissible truncation and its right slope to find a deficient heavy prefix. Those pages were read. The source variant adds containment in A and a linear budget m u; the cutoff principle is not new.

## Checks and limitations

Exact rational piecewise-linear maximization tests random feasible instances, ties and both endpoint values. Every strictly heavy set satisfies its containment and cardinality bounds.

This is a local truncation fact; the multiscale selector and global threshold theorem remain outside the check.

## Possible applications

Converting a weighted truncation condition into a cardinality bound without a generic-position assumption.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
