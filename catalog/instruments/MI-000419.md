# Lift a graded special-fiber isomorphism using target flatness

[Family 044](../families/openai-math-044.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000228.md) · [Batch B039](../../batches/B039/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let S be a positively graded polynomial ring over a field, S_+ its positive ideal, and B -> H a surjective graded S-algebra map. Assume H is S-flat, the induced B/S_+B -> H/S_+H is an isomorphism, and the graded kernel J is bounded below. Then B -> H is an isomorphism.

Flatness kills Tor_1^S(H,S/S_+), so tensoring the kernel sequence gives an injection J/S_+J -> B/S_+B. The special-fiber isomorphism forces J=S_+J. If J were nonzero, a nonzero homogeneous element of least degree could not be a sum of positive-degree multiples of elements of J. Thus J=0. Finite generation of J is unnecessary under this bounded-below grading hypothesis.

In the source, freeness of the equivariant cohomology target is obtained from even cohomology and a Leray-Hirsch argument; the extraction uses that as an input.

## Role in the source argument

Promotes a nonequivariant identification to an equivariant one after proving surjectivity and target freeness.

## Antecedents and comparison

Standard Tor exactness and graded Nakayama. The value is the explicit minimal interface, including boundedness below in place of a finite-generation assumption on the kernel.

## Checks and limitations

Thirty polynomial unimodular maps give positive models. S -> S/(t) shows why target flatness matters; S[t^-1]=t S[t^-1] shows why boundedness below matters.

The geometric source inputs of surjectivity, special-fiber identification and free target are not established by these tests.

## Possible applications

Lifting algebra/cohomology identifications through graded parameter deformations.
