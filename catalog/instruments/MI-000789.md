# Subtract the far-field slope before averaging logarithmic growth

[Family 350](../families/openai-math-350.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000521.md) · [Batch B085](../../batches/B085/README.md)

## Mathematical content

For V=h+integral log|x-z| dnu(z) as in the preceding decomposition, subdivide the unit square into A^2 squares, A>10R, with rho=1/A and centers y. Set c_y=grad h(y)+integral_(|z-y|>2Rrho) (y-z)/|y-z|^2 dnu(z). Then the difference of suprema of V(x)-c_y dot x on the outer disk D_(Rrho)(y) and inner disk D_rho(y) is at most
C[rho^2+integral min(1,rho^2/|z-y|^2) dnu(z)].

Lower-bound the inner supremum by its disk average. Harmonic Taylor remainder and the far-source logarithmic Hessian give the quadratic error after subtracting the slope. For near sources, rescaling cancels log rho; the unit-disk average of log|xi-t| equals (|t|^2-1)/2 for |t|<=1 and log|t| otherwise. It remains bounded below even when the source is an atom at the center.

Dyadic annuli give average_y min(1,rho^2/|z-y|^2)<=C rho^2(1+|log rho|), uniformly in z, and average of the truncated inverse-distance kernel is bounded. Integrating yields bounded average |c_y| and the stated small average residual. Without the affine subtraction, a nonzero linear profile retains first-order growth.

## Role in the source argument

Reducing mean local growth faster than the subdivision scale while keeping the cumulative change of slope controlled.

## Antecedents and comparison

Classical logarithmic kernel estimates and affine Taylor subtraction are assembled for the source normalized growth problem. Exact priority of this particular averaged interface is not established.

## Checks and limitations

Quadrature checks the atom-safe disk-average identity. Finite grids check the dyadic kernel scaling, and explicit far sources compare corrected and uncorrected local growth. The finite grids test the kernel mechanism, not the source A>10000 regime or nodal theorem.

The measure must be nonnegative for the integrated upper bounds. The transfer from these profiles to eigenfunctions and the later persistence/packing argument are separate claims.
