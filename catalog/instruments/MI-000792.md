# Convert hard-sphere gain to planes with the exact normalization

[Family 363](../families/openai-math-363.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000523.md) · [Batch B086](../../batches/B086/README.md)

## Mathematical content

With kernel |(p-xi) dot omega| and ordinary surface measure on S^2, one outgoing velocity from inputs p,xi has uniform measure on their diameter sphere, of total mass 2pi|p-xi|. Reflection pushes the two hemispheres to the outgoing direction with density |p-xi|/2. On the sphere f(v)=(v-p) dot (xi-v)=0, |grad f|=|p-xi|, so the outgoing measure is 2 delta(f(v)) dv.

Consequently the bilinear gain is P(c,h)(v)=2 integral c(dp) integral delta((v-p) dot (xi-v))h(xi) dxi. For fixed p!=v the inner measure is plane area divided by |v-p|. Positivity and Tonelli allow the first input to be a measure when the result is finite almost everywhere.

For n!=0 and a bounded density zeta on [-1,1] with integral one, integral_(n perpendicular) F(k)dA/|n|=integral F(n cross L)|n|zeta(L dot n/|n|)dL. The transverse area Jacobian is |n|^2. If |n| is bounded away from zero and |k| is bounded, the lifted parameter support and density are bounded. Conditional iteration requires no derivatives of normals depending on earlier parameters.

## Role in the source argument

Making collision parameters suitable for later rank and compactness arguments while avoiding a hidden normalization change.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Gamba, Panferov and Villani, arXiv:math/0701081](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0701081), Appendix C, Lemma 16 and proof on PDF pages 23-24, were read for the established hyperplane representation. Their angular normalization differs; the coefficient here is derived from the selected source convention. The redundant parameter is an elementary area change.

## Checks and limitations

Sphere quadrature checks collision mass and first/second moments. Symbolic coarea geometry and Gaussian plane integration verify the factor two and the redundant-coordinate Jacobian.

The representation does not prove the source gain estimates or nonuniqueness. A normal approaching zero defeats the uniform lifted support bound.
