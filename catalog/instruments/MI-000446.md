# Recover a semistable Frobenius polynomial from sufficiently precise finite torsion

[Family 030](../families/openai-math-030.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000242.md) · [Batch B042](../../batches/B042/README.md)

## Mathematical content

For a semistable abelian variety of dimension g over a local field with residue cardinality Q, the full auxiliary Tate-module Frobenius polynomial is integral and independent of the Frobenius lift. Its graded eigenvalues have magnitudes 1 on a rank-r lattice, sqrt(Q) on a 2(g-r)-dimensional good-reduction part, and Q on a rank-r toric part. Thus its coefficient of T^(2g-j) is bounded by binomial(2g,j)Q^j, giving a finite set of possibilities. The distinct magnitudes determine r; when r=0, residue-characteristic valuations of the roots give the Newton polygon.

Choose auxiliary odd d different from the residue characteristic. A modulus d^j larger than twice every coefficient bound distinguishes these integral polynomials. In a characteristic-zero abelian scheme, finite-etale torsion is locally constant as a Galois module, using local sections of the level torsor over a finite Galois extension and shrinking compatibly with its finite actions.

After a fixed extension rationalizes d-torsion, nearby fibers are semistable: inertia eigenvalues are roots of unity congruent to 1 with valuation at least one, hence equal 1 for odd d. Shrink again to fix d^j-torsion. The full Frobenius polynomials, toric ranks and, in the good case, Newton polygons then agree. The geometric semistable filtration and criterion are inputs from standard theory.

## Role in the source argument

Prescribes a common local reduction type throughout a small parameter neighborhood before automorphy lifting.

## Antecedents and comparison

Classical semistable filtration and finite-etale local constancy, combined with elementary bounded-integer reconstruction. No independent priority claim.

## Checks and limitations

Three hundred sixty bounded integral polynomial models verify coefficient bounds and uniqueness after reduction at the selected precision.

The tests do not construct abelian schemes, Galois actions or the local neighborhoods. They certify only the finite-polynomial interface.

## Possible applications

Replacing a locally varying arithmetic invariant by a finite torsion condition once its possible integral values are bounded.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
