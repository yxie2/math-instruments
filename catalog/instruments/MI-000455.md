# Build a stable graph by summing neutral and expanding modes backward

[Family 371](../families/openai-math-371.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000246.md) · [Batch B043](../../batches/B043/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Consider a split iteration w_(n+1)=A_n w_n+B_n u_n+F_n(v_n), u_(n+1)=D u_n+G_n(v_n), with ||A_n||<=1/8, uniformly bounded B_n, and ||D^(-1)||<=1. D may have eigenvalue 1. In the space with norm sup 2^n(||w_n||+K|u_n|), prescribe w_0 and impose

$$u_n=-\sum_{j\ge n}D^{n-j-1}G_j(v_j).$$

Choose K large enough to absorb the bounded u-to-w coupling, then make the nonlinear Lipschitz constants and linear errors small. Weighted summability of the forcing makes this a contraction. The neutral mode is controlled by the future forcing sum, rather than by strict contraction of D inverse.

For the source moving cutoff L_s=L exp(s/2), forcing is O(L_s^(-2-a)); its slab decay must satisfy 2 exp(-(2+a)M/2)<1. Uniform finite-coordinate strong continuity, a common contraction constant, and uniformly summable tails imply continuity of the resulting initial graph. Operator-norm continuity of the evolution operators is not needed for this conclusion.

## Role in the source argument

Selects modulation parameters so stable trajectories persist through all later slabs.

## Antecedents and comparison

Classical Lyapunov-Perron construction. The useful extracted detail is handling a neutral block together with only strong parameter continuity.

## Checks and limitations

Three ninety-step finite-horizon fixed-point models include a neutral eigenvalue, check both recurrences and verify weighted decay.

The terminal-zero truncation is a finite model. It does not certify infinite-horizon existence, uniform PDE estimates or blowup.

## Possible applications

Stable graph constructions with neutral directions and parameter-dependent evolution operators.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
