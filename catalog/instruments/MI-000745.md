# Replace a shorter clause uniformly and telescope the forcing mass

[Family 235](../families/openai-math-235.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000499.md) · [Batch B080](../../batches/B080/README.md)

## Mathematical content

For a satisfiable formula H on v variables with b forced coordinates, an independent proper r-clause kills it with exact probability q_r=(b)_r/[2^r(v)_r]. For k>=3, n>=k+1, v=n-1, epsilon=n^(-(k-1)/k) and g=ceil(2k n^(1/k)), replacing one random (k-1)-clause by g independent k-clauses decreases unsatisfiability probability by at most epsilon, uniformly in H.

If x=q_(k-1)>epsilon, necessarily b>=k and q_k>=x^(k/(k-1))/k, so g q_k>=2x. At least one clause kills H with probability >=1-exp(-2x)>=x, since x<=1/4. If x<=epsilon the bound is trivial. An unsatisfiable background also satisfies it.

Erase one chosen variable from a random k-SAT prefix of length j. Conditional on the positions of its d incident clauses, d~Bin(j,k/n), the untouched clauses form an independent proper k-SAT formula on n-1 variables, and the shortened clauses are independent uniform (k-1)-clauses. Forcing implies the untouched formula survives but the shortened augmentation fails. Successive replacements cost at most d epsilon and compare survival probabilities P_v(j-d)-P_v(j-d+dg). For fixed d their sum over j<=M is at most dg by telescoping. Use (kM/n)^d/d! only on this nonnegative difference, and average the error with its original binomial law. Then
sum_(j=0)^M E[b(F_nj)/n] <= (kM/n) exp(kM/n) g + kM(M+1) epsilon/(2n).
For M a fixed multiple of n this is O_k(n^(1/k)).

## Role in the source argument

Removing local short constraints while retaining a quantitative error that can be summed over the process.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Carenini, The Scaling Window of Random k-SAT, arXiv:2609.26222v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26222v1), Lemma 6.1 and its full proof on PDF page 14, were read. They already give the finite-alphabet power comparison through frozen-coordinate counts. The source specializes it with an explicit block length and telescoping budget; no novelty of the power comparison is claimed.

## Checks and limitations

Exact solution-set enumeration checks the killing formula. A range of n,k,b checks the block replacement bound, including its active x>epsilon regime. Monotone survival sequences check the capped telescoping inequality.

The replacement error and integrated forcing bound alone do not establish a limiting threshold.

## Possible applications

Removing local short constraints while retaining a quantitative error that can be summed over the process.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
