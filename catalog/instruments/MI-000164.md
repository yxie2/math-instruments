# A dyadic compact domain preserves fixed-length dual moments

[Family 326](../families/openai-math-326.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000104.md) · [Batch B008](../../batches/B008/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Fix m>=1 and Delta>=1. If X is Banach and c_m(X*)<=Delta, every nonzero finite-dimensional E subset X factors as

$$E\xrightarrow{\iota}G\xrightarrow{J}X,\qquad J\iota=\operatorname{inclusion},\quad\|\iota\|\le1,\quad\|J\|\le2,\quad c_m(G^*)\le12\Delta,$$

where J is injective and compact. The source constructs nested finite-dimensional E_l starting at E. Every tuple z in ell2^m(E_l) has an exact sign-moment lift H_l valued in E_(l+1), with L2 norm at most 3 Delta ||z||. Choose finitely many near-minimal lifts on a net, then correct the remaining tuple error by its explicit Rademacher sum; this explains why one finite enlargement works for every tuple.

Set $\mathcal H=(\bigoplus_{l\ge0}E_l)_{\ell_2}$ and $j(z)=\sum_{l\ge0}2^{-l}z_l$. Its tail norm is bounded by $(\sum_{l>L}4^{-l})^{1/2}$, so j is compact. Let G=H/ker(j), with quotient Q and JQ=j. The first coordinate defines iota. Near-minimal quotient representatives show J(B_G) lies in j(2B_H), hence J is compact.

The weighted shift $R(z_0,z_1,\ldots)=(0,2z_0,2z_1,\ldots)$ satisfies jR=j and QR=Q. Apply the finite-level lift to each coordinate of representatives of a tuple in G, shift the lifts one level, and multiply by two. Their L2 norm is at most 12 Delta times the tuple norm, and QR=Q makes all moments exact. Duality yields c_m(G*)<=12 Delta.

If X has ordinary AP, apply it to the compact set closure(J(B_G)). Finite-rank S_j on X then satisfy ||S_j J-J||->0, even if ||S_j|| is unbounded. This transfers any suitable uniform finite-rank operator estimate to J. In contrast, the finite-rank truncations of j need not kill ker(j), so they need not descend to G; compactness alone does not provide the finite-rank approximation of J.

## Role in the source argument

Allows the source to use AP rather than BAP while keeping the domain-dual moment estimate required by its uniform Walsh bound.

## Antecedents and comparison

Compact factorization and approximation on compact sets are established Banach-space methods; [Pisier](https://webusers.imj-prg.fr/~gilles.pisier/ihp-pisier.pdf) provides the type/cotype framework and [Gupta, Misra and Ray](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13270v2) the related tensor context. The selected construction retains fixed-length dual control through a dyadic quotient and shift. Priority is not certified.

## Checks and limitations

Read the finite-net correction, dyadic tail estimate, quotient compactness and shifted moment identity. Finite Hilbert models test exact shift compensation and moment reconstruction. A kernel example shows explicitly that a truncation need not descend to the quotient.

The space G varies with m and E. Its compact embedding is not asserted to have closed range or a bounded inverse on all of its image. The transfer to J requires AP of the target and an independently established uniform finite-rank estimate.

## Possible applications

Passing rank-independent estimates to compactly factored finite-dimensional inputs without imposing a uniform bound on AP approximants.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
