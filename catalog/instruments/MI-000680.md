# Separate current geometric tests while preserving earlier grid keys

[Family 084](../families/openai-math-084.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000449.md) · [Batch B072](../../batches/B072/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Fix q in (0,1), c=4/(1-q), and N_b=2^ceil(log2(c q^-b)). These dyadic grid sizes are nested even when q is not dyadic; c q^-b<=N_b<2c q^-b. An ordered tree assigns integer windows to edges in preorder with g unused indices between windows. A height-h window has length r_h>=g+sigma_(h-1), so its window together with its child subtree spans at most 2r_h.

A center x is stable if (x,x+2q^a] misses the grid of the preceding window whenever a is a new window's first index. The bad centers for one transition lie in intervals [gamma-2q^a,gamma), so their density is at most 4c q^(g+1). For K edges, the union has density at most 4Kc q^(g+1). At a stable center, every shift t q^n from the current window, t in [1,2], preserves every earlier grid key by nesting.

Choose the starting index so 2q^n0<=1/4. Within a window ending at b, the center and shifted points have circular separation at least (1-q)q^b, while the current cell width is at most (1-q)q^b/4. They therefore have distinct current and finer keys. The half-open endpoint convention matters: a center may start at a boundary, but reaching the next boundary changes its key.

## Antecedents and comparison

Nested grids, boundary-strip estimates and preorder blocks are established devices. The source combines dyadic grid nesting with a nondyadic geometric sequence and an explicit subtree-span budget; exact priority is not established.

## Checks and limitations

Rational q values test exact grid nesting, stable keys, boundary endpoints and within-window separation. Small trees verify preorder span bounds and gap arithmetic.

These checks do not prove independence of the source random routing or the similarity conjecture.
