# Approximate a finite odd Gaussian formula from its output toward its leaves

[Family 281](../families/openai-math-281.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000313.md) · [Batch B055](../../batches/B055/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Take a fixed finite descendant formula with Gaussian seeds, odd transmitted columns, and jointly odd smooth local maps whose derivatives have polynomial growth. Let m*=clip(z,[-1,1]). Assume the source aggregation isometry controls the L2 change in an aggregate by the L2 change in its child column. For every eta>0 the output can be approximated by sin(2theta) within eta in L2, with theta an odd bounded formula using bounded odd seeds and transmitted columns and finite trigonometric local maps.

First truncate Gaussian seeds smoothly and oddly; finite moments and polynomial derivative bounds control a fixed finite formula. Shrink the target and encode it as theta_kappa=(1/2)arcsin((1-kappa)m*). This is bounded, odd and Lipschitz, and sin(2theta_kappa)=(1-kappa)m*.

Replace an outer map by a fixed bounded smooth Lipschitz approximation before approximating its inputs. Its now-fixed Lipschitz constant determines how accurate the next inner replacements must be. Repeat toward the leaves; a uniform Lipschitz bound over all approximation choices is unnecessary. Under a symmetric input law, odd symmetrization F(x)->(F(x)-F(-x))/2 does not increase Lp error against an odd target.

Finally cut off inside a box, extend smoothly and periodically to a larger box, and approximate uniformly on the whole period by a finite Fourier sum. The global bound controls the probability outside the chosen box. Odd symmetrization leaves sine terms. The final error is at most 2||theta-theta_kappa||_2+kappa. This is qualitative approximation of a fixed finite expression, with no claimed efficient synthesis rate.

## Role in the source argument

Puts the source classical Gaussian optimization formula into bounded odd controls used by its later quantum compilation.

## Antecedents and comparison

The ingredients are classical cutoff/mollification, symmetry projection and Fourier approximation. The complete source proof was read. Its useful bookkeeping choice is to fix each outer Lipschitz map before choosing the precision of its inputs; no new universal approximation theorem or quantitative complexity bound is claimed.

## Checks and limitations

Deterministic Gaussian quadrature checks angle encoding, odd symmetrization and outer-map error propagation. Smooth periodic Fourier approximants check global boundedness and final sine-output error in a finite model.

These models do not verify the infinite rooted aggregation construction, near-optimal classical energy or QAOA synthesis.

## Possible applications

Converting finite probabilistic formulas into bounded symmetric controls while keeping composition errors under explicit control.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
