# Separate free rank and torsion in a graded finite-cut count

[Family 345](../families/openai-math-345.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000257.md) · [Batch B047](../../batches/B047/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let H be a nonnegatively graded k[U]-module with deg U=2, and suppose H/UH is finite-dimensional. Lifts of a finite homogeneous basis generate H: subtract a linear combination modulo U and divide the remainder by U, decreasing the degree until the process stops.

A polynomial with nonzero constant coefficient acts injectively, since the lowest nonzero homogeneous part of an element survives. Every torsion element is consequently killed by a power of U. Finite generation and Noetherianity give a common such power, so the torsion submodule T is finite-dimensional. Set t=dim(T/UT)=dim ker(U|H) and rho=rank_(k[U])(H/T). The torsion-free quotient is graded free, UH intersect T=UT, and dim(H/UH)=rho+t.

If a finite-dimensional graded space B fits the Gysin short exact pieces with contributions H/UH and a shifted ker U, then dim B=rho+2t. For sufficiently large D, dim H^D+dim H^(D-1)=rho, because torsion has disappeared and each free generator contributes in exactly one of these two degrees.

The source identifies this eventual rank with the reflection count at the half cut using additional weight-two Gysin and localization inputs. That geometric identification is not a consequence of the module calculation alone. A grading bounded below is essential for the generation argument: k[U,U^(-1)] has H/UH=0 but is nonzero and not finitely generated over k[U].

## Role in the source argument

Makes the source doubling count exact by separating free contributions from finite torsion.

## Antecedents and comparison

Classical graded Nakayama induction and structure theory over k[U]. The selected proof is self-contained algebra once the source Gysin sequence is supplied; no new algebra theorem is claimed.

## Checks and limitations

Exact nilpotent Jordan-block models check the torsion kernel/cokernel equality and eventual two-degree free-rank count, including free generators of both parities. The Laurent module is an analytic control for the missing lower degree bound.

No finite example establishes the equivariant cohomology model, localization or the closed-geodesic conclusion.

## Possible applications

Extracting stable free rank from high degrees while measuring finite-cut defects through torsion generators.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
