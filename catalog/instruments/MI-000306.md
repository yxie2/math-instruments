# A saturated initial flag defines a signed chain residue

[Family 310](../families/openai-math-310.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000172.md) · [Batch B025](../../batches/B025/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let X be a subposet of nonidentity elementary abelian p-subgroups and let sigma=[A_1<...<A_r=A] lie in X, with rank(A_i)=i. On augmented chains, retain only those containing sigma and delete this initial flag, leaving the strict continuation above A. Send sigma itself to the empty chain of degree -1. The resulting map R_sigma lowers degree by r and satisfies

$$R_\sigma\partial=(-1)^r\partial R_\sigma.$$

Saturation is what makes the flag initial: no nonidentity subgroup can precede rank one, and no rank can occur between consecutive ranks. Deleting a vertex in the flag destroys the residue. Deleting a continuation vertex has boundary sign (-1)^r times its sign in the continuation. A face of a chain missing sigma still misses sigma.

The augmented boundary of one vertex is the empty chain, whose boundary is zero, so the identity includes the lowest degrees. It sends cycles to cycles and boundaries to boundaries. In particular, if a cycle has a residue representing a nonzero homology class of X_(>A), the original cycle is not a boundary.

If the chosen flag skips a rank, a containing chain can have an extra vertex below or between its entries. Those additional boundary faces invalidate this simple residue rule.

## Role in the source argument

Detects a propagated cycle through its coefficient along a prescribed full flag.

## Antecedents and comparison

The signed boundary calculation is a standard simplicial residue argument. The source makes the complete rank flag and degree-minus-one conventions explicit; no novelty certification was attempted.

## Checks and limitations

3,528 exact chain identities are checked for 49 saturated flags in the 15 nonzero subspaces of F_2^3. A flag starting at a plane gives an explicit unsaturated failure.

The checks do not establish that a suitable nonzero residue exists in the source propagation argument or verify its group reductions.

## Possible applications

Proving that a chain is not a boundary by extracting a smaller homological witness.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
