# Indexed predecessor moments control exceptional sets through residue descent

[Family 025](../families/openai-math-025.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000142.md) · [Batch B017](../../batches/B017/README.md)

## Mathematical content

At one level let Y=rho X, 0<rho<1, and let B be the next-level bad set, with |B|=delta Y. Except for epsilon X exceptional predecessors, each bad u in (Y,X] has at least rho|I|/2 indices i whose reduction lands in B. The inherited bad set below Y is contained in B. If D_i(h) bounds the number of predecessors landing at indexed target (h,i), then

$$|H|\le\varepsilon X+|B|+\frac{2}{\rho|I|}\sum_i\sum_{h\in B}D_i(h).$$

If sum_(1<=h<=Y) D_i(h)^r<=Y M^r uniformly in i, r>1, Holder gives |H|/X<=epsilon+rho delta+2M delta^(1-1/r). Repeated list values keep different indices on both sides of the count. The factor Y=rho X cancels the apparent loss 1/rho.

In the Egyptian-fraction application, t_i divides one common M0, z=ceil(C_j t_i/u), h=uz-C_j t_i, so u divides C_j t_i+h and D_i(h) can be bounded by the number d_X(C_j t_i+h) of divisors at most X. The identity

$$\frac{u}{M_0C_j}=\frac1{(M_0/t_i)z}+\frac1{zC_j/C_{j+1}}\frac h{M_0C_{j+1}}$$

transfers an expansion from the next level whenever C_j/C_(j+1) is a positive integer. The shifted truncated-divisor moment is a separate analytic input.

For delta_j<=epsilon+A delta_(j+1)^alpha, 0<alpha<1, A>=1 and delta_d=0, subadditivity gives

$$\delta_0\le\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}A^{(1-\alpha^i)/(1-\alpha)}\varepsilon^{\alpha^i}.$$

With alpha=1-1/r, d<=K log S, fixed r>=8K, epsilon=exp(-c m), m>=S/(2 log S), and A=exp(2S^(1/4)), the bound is at most d exp(2r S^(1/4)-c m S^(-1/4)), tending to zero. The moment order is fixed before S grows.

## Role in the source argument

Prevents many reduction levels from accumulating an uncontrollable exceptional numerator set.

## Antecedents and comparison

Double counting, Holder and power-recurrence unrolling are classical. The source credits [Erdos's 1952 divisor-sum method](https://www.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1952-08.pdf) for the separately proved moment input. The indexed reduction, shift uniformity requirements and cancellation of rho are the extracted interface.

## Checks and limitations

Read the predecessor count and complete recurrence argument. Finite residue maps, including repeated divisor entries, check the indexed degree bounds and Holder step. Positive rational identities check changes of denominator scale; log-domain recurrence computations check the unrolled estimate.

The existence of many good reductions and the uniform divisor moment are hypotheses here. A moment estimate for each fixed shift with shift-dependent constants does not supply the needed uniform bound.

## Possible applications

Propagating constructive coverage through many reduction levels when a few targets may have many predecessors.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
