# Extract one Taylor residue class with a root-of-unity filter

[Family 359](../families/openai-math-359.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000382.md) · [Batch B063](../../batches/B063/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let g be holomorphic near the closed ball of radius S and bounded there by K, with homogeneous Taylor pieces g_r. For 0<=h<N and R|v|<S,

(1/N)sum_(q=0)^(N-1) exp(-2pi i hq/N)g(R exp(2pi i q/N)v)=sum_(j>=0)g_(h+jN)(Rv).

Cauchy's estimate on the line through v gives |g_r(Rv)|<=K theta^r, theta=R|v|/S. If theta<=exp(-0.99c/N) and c>=1, the tail after j=k-1 has magnitude at most K exp(-0.99kc)/(1-exp(-0.99c)). The denominator is uniformly positive, so the bound is C K exp(-0.99kc) with absolute C independent of N,h,R,S. Taking k=1 isolates the leading term to error C K exp(-0.99c). Absolute convergence justifies swapping the Taylor series and finite Fourier sum. The h<N restriction avoids missing lower nonnegative degrees in the same residue class.

## Role in the source argument

Separates a low-degree holomorphic jet from higher degrees in a disk-testing construction.

## Antecedents and comparison

This is classical finite Fourier orthogonality and the Cauchy coefficient estimate. The source uses the uniform-in-N tail in a much larger phase-grid and geometric construction; that application is not a new Fourier-filter theorem.

## Checks and limitations

Random polynomials check residue-class extraction. Analytic geometric-series models check the infinite-series identity and the explicit uniform tail bound. An h>=N control detects the missing lower residue term.

The filter checks do not establish the phase-grid separation, testing criterion or negatively curved Liouville construction.

## Possible applications

Recovering homogeneous holomorphic coefficients from finitely many phase samples with an explicit aliasing tail.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
