# Separated product comparisons yield aggregate moments despite nearby dependencies

[Family 171](../families/openai-math-171.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000131.md) · [Batch B015](../../batches/B015/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let U be finite and let 0<=Z_v<=L on an event A. Each role v has a near set N(v) containing v, with |N(v)|<=f|U|. Fix an integer n>=1, K>=1 and deterministic d_v>=0. For every sequence v_1,...,v_r, r<=n, with each later role outside all earlier near sets, assume

$$\mathbb E[\mathbf1_A\prod_{j=1}^r Z_{v_j}]\le K^n\prod_{j=1}^r d_{v_j}.$$

Then

$$\mathbb E\left[\mathbf1_A\left(|U|^{-1}\sum_v Z_v\right)^n\right]\le K^n\left(|U|^{-1}\sum_v d_v+nfL\right)^n.$$

Expand the power using n independent uniform indices. Remove each position whose index lies in the near set of any earlier index, including earlier removed positions. Each removed factor costs L; the retained sequence satisfies the product hypothesis. For a fixed removal pattern, drop separation restrictions on the retained indices and sum indices in reverse order. A retained position contributes the average of d_v. A removed position has at most nf|U| available indices and contributes at most nfL. Summing the patterns gives the binomial bound. No symmetry of the near relation is required for this counting argument.

Markov's inequality then controls large averages on A. If nfL is small and the mean comparison is bounded, a sufficiently large fixed threshold gives exponentially small tails in n. The estimate does not bound the probability of A failing.

## Role in the source argument

Converts separated-role estimates into simultaneous column-load bounds without asserting independence of all roles.

## Antecedents and comparison

The proof is a standard high-moment expansion with a proximity count. The extracted value is the explicit nfL correction, directed near-set convention and retained success event; historical novelty is not asserted.

## Checks and limitations

Read the removal rule and reverse summation. Exact block-dependent finite models check the moment inequality for several orders, including an arbitrary success event. A common-random-bit control shows why the product hypothesis cannot be dropped.

Bounds on individual expectations alone do not imply the separated-product hypothesis. The success indicator must stay inside that hypothesis. The near sets and comparison means are fixed for the experiment being tested.

## Possible applications

Bounding congestion or weighted loads when only well-separated choices admit joint estimates.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
