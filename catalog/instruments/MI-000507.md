# Bound radial orbit descriptions by a chain of label vectors

[Family 151](../families/openai-math-151.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000284.md) · [Batch B051](../../batches/B051/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let Sigma be the sphere C union {infinity}. Suppose a continuous map T sends all of Sigma into D_A={|z|<=A}, and on this disk T(re^(i theta))=R(r)e^(i theta), where R:[0,A]->[0,A] is continuous, nondecreasing and R(0)=0. Then every fixed finite product T^q has zero topological entropy.

Partition the radius interval into H short ordered intervals and the angle circle into K short arcs, fine enough to give epsilon-small polar cells. If b is the nondecreasing radius label, the length-N itinerary (b(r),b(Rr),...,b(R^(N-1)r)) is coordinatewise nondecreasing as a function of the initial radius r. Its distinct vectors form a chain in {1,...,H}^N. Their coordinate sums strictly increase at every change and range from N to NH, so there are at most 1+N(H-1) itineraries.

The angle stays fixed, including after a radius reaches zero by using its original angle. Thus at most K[1+N(H-1)] descriptions are needed in the disk. A fixed finite cover of Sigma^q accounts for time zero, while all later iterates lie in D_A^q. Therefore s_(Sigma^q)(n,epsilon)<=C{K[1+(n-1)(H-1)]}^q. This grows polynomially in n. The map outside the disk may be nonradial or nonmonotone; only its first-image containment is used.

## Role in the source argument

Shows that the register dynamics on the source attracting sets add no entropy.

## Antecedents and comparison

This is the elementary ordered-itinerary proof of zero entropy for monotone interval dynamics, combined with a fixed angular label and a finite transient. The complete source proof was read; no historical priority is asserted for this formulation.

## Checks and limitations

All nondecreasing self-maps on small ordered finite sets are enumerated, and their radius-label itinerary counts are compared with 1+N(H-1). A nonmonotone eight-state map exceeds the bound and demonstrates the need for order.

Finite ordered sets test the counting argument but do not establish continuity of any source register map or validate its global construction.

## Possible applications

Bounding orbit complexity of saturated radial registers and products with a finite initial transient.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
