# Posterior domination retains the mass of a required event

[Family 171](../families/openai-math-171.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000131.md) · [Batch B015](../../batches/B015/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Condition on earlier information. Let pi be a prior on candidates z, and let F_z(t) be the joint likelihood density of data t and a required event, relative to one common data measure. Thus each F_z has total mass at most one. Put m(t)=integral F_z(t) d pi(z), and let Q be any reference probability density fixed by the earlier information.

For epsilon>0, the joint probability of the required event and {m(t)<epsilon Q(t)} or {m(t)=0} is at most epsilon. If also F_z(t)<=exp(s)Q(t), then outside this exception the posterior is dominated as a measure by exp(s)/epsilon times pi:

$$\frac{F_z(t)}{m(t)}\,d\pi(z)\le e^s\varepsilon^{-1}\,d\pi(z).$$

The small-denominator bound follows by integrating m on {m<epsilon Q}; zero-density data have zero joint mass. For any nonnegative test h, integrating the posterior against the unnormalized required-event data measure gives exactly

$$\int m(t)\int h(z,t)\frac{F_z(t)}{m(t)}d\pi(z)dt=\int\!\int h(z,t)F_z(t)dt\,d\pi(z).$$

Set the integrand to zero where m=0. Recompute the required event and every candidate-dependent selection as z varies. The exceptional mass is not a bound conditional on the required event: that bound would be divided by its probability.

## Role in the source argument

Controls posterior concentration while preserving the mass that must later cancel in a joint expectation.

## Antecedents and comparison

This is Bayes' formula and a likelihood-ratio truncation. The planted-posterior viewpoint also appears in [Mossel, Niles-Weed, Sun and Zadik, Sections 2.1-2.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.11347v2); that paper is a comparison, not an attribution of this exact event interface.

## Checks and limitations

Read the likelihood and cancellation proof. Exact finite priors, subprobability likelihoods, zero-density cells and reference laws check the exception and domination inequalities; a rare-event example shows the difference from conditional failure probability.

A posterior formed with a frozen event from another candidate need not satisfy the identity. The reference Q must be a probability law and must be fixed before the candidate-dependent data selection.

## Possible applications

Keeping rare-event tests valid through selection and posterior resampling.
