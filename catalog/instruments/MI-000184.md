# Nearest-mismatch brackets make periodic occurrence lower bounds sound

[Family 128](../families/openai-math-128.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000113.md) · [Batch B010](../../batches/B010/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Remove empty, duplicate and contained input strings, and let V contain all substrings of the remaining nonempty instance, including the empty word. Fix a periodic text A aligned with a nonempty word s. Let b_{A,s}(r) count interior placements of s in r whose entire interior matches A in that alignment, but whose two endpoint letters mismatch. Define B_A(s;m)=sum_r b_{A,s}(r)m(r). Only r at least two letters longer than s contribute.

In any real word T, the nearest mismatches on both sides of an occurrence determine a unique bracket. Call it blocked when that bracket belongs to V. Then B_A(s;N_T) is exactly the number of blocked occurrences of s, hence is at most N_T(s). Multiplicity matters: one bracket may contain several placements.

Compute m(s) in descending length as the least nonnegative integer satisfying m(s)>=sum_c m(cs), m(s)>=sum_c m(sc), and m(s)>=1 for required words. Also impose, for every period-p text A, v=A[0:|v|) in V and nonempty prefix w with |v|=|w|+kp, k>=1,

$$m(v)>B_A(v;m)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad m(w)\ge B_A(w;m)+k+1.$$

The rules are finite: enumerate v, p<|v| and k, retaining periods whose repeated prefix agrees with v, including nonleast periods. Each right-hand side uses only longer words.

For every common superstring T, m(s)<=N_T(s). In the induction, an unblocked actual occurrence of v gives k+1 unblocked period-spaced occurrences of w. If every actual v is blocked, the trigger instead forces some bracket r to have N_T(r)>m(r). That single extra bracket contributes at least k+1 placements of w. Omitting this second case would leave a gap: a trigger for m does not imply an unblocked actual v.

Thus W=sum_c m(c)<=OPT. On the hierarchical graph with up edge pref(s)->s and down edge s->suf(s), set u(s)=m(s)-sum_c m(cs) and d(s)=m(s)-sum_c m(sc). Both are nonnegative, all vertices balance, and total up and down multiplicities are W. Required words are used. For the input a^h, the period rule yields m(a^j)=h-j+1 and W=h, whereas extension rules alone allow W=1.

## Role in the source argument

Supplies a combinatorial lower bound paying for periodic repetitions before the later graph-connection argument.

## Antecedents and comparison

The hierarchical graph is established in the [Collapsing Superstring Conjecture framework](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.08669v3). The selected additional mechanism is the bracket multiplicity and its two-case forcing proof; the search does not establish priority of that mechanism.

## Checks and limitations

Finite binary-string instances check the recursion against actual occurrence counts, bracket multiplicities, graph balance and the constant-word example. The checks include nonleast periods and words with no mismatch on one side.

W is a lower bound, not by itself a superstring length or approximation guarantee. The resulting balanced graph need not be connected; the claimed factor two requires a separate connection budget.

## Possible applications

Lower bounds for string assembly, periodicity-sensitive relaxations and audits of multiplicity constraints in overlap graphs.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
