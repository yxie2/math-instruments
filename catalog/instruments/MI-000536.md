# Discard Fourier phases through a positive lattice Gaussian ratio

[Family 239](../families/openai-math-239.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000305.md) · [Batch B054](../../batches/B054/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let Lambda be a full-rank lattice in a Euclidean subspace E, let S be positive self-adjoint on E, and sample Y in Lambda* with mass proportional to exp(-pi|S^(-1)Y|^2). For independent Bernoulli-p vectors xi,xi' on a coordinate set J put X=xi-xi' and phi(t)=1-p+p exp(2pi i t). Poisson summation gives

E_Y product_(i in J)|phi(uY_i+s_i)|^2 = E_X[exp(2pi i sum_i s_i X_i) f(uX)],

where f(t)=sum_(lambda in Lambda)exp(-pi|S(lambda-projection_E t)|^2)/sum_(lambda in Lambda)exp(-pi|S lambda|^2). The ratio is nonnegative and at most one. Taking absolute values therefore bounds the product average uniformly over all phases by E_X f(uX).

For d=dim E and D(t)=dist(S projection_E t,S Lambda), reserving half the Gaussian exponent and using the centered translate bound yields f(t)<=2^(d/2)exp(-pi D(t)^2/2). Suppose that, after conditioning on xi' and the other coordinates of xi, subset sums in m remaining coordinates have pairwise quotient distance greater than 2R. At most one pattern lies within R of the required lattice translate. Its probability is at most b^m, b=max(p,1-p). Consequently the Fourier product average is at most b^m+2^(d/2)exp(-pi R^2/2).

The source chooses m proportional to tau N and R proportional to sqrt(N), using a preceding separation lemma. That separation and its probabilistic hypotheses are additional inputs, not consequences of Poisson summation.

## Role in the source argument

Turns the source separated quotient sums into Fourier decay uniform over arbitrary row phases.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Regev-Stephens-Davidowitz, A Reverse Minkowski Theorem](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1611.05979), equation (5) and Claim 2.2 with its complete proof on PDF pages 9-10, give Poisson summation and the Gaussian translate maximum, with attribution to earlier lattice-Gaussian work. Those passages were read. The remaining Bernoulli conditioning step is elementary; no priority claim for the source random-matrix interface is made.

## Checks and limitations

Truncated one-dimensional dual and primal Gaussian sums are compared with explicit tail margins. Finite Bernoulli pattern enumeration checks phase removal, the Gaussian distance estimate and separated-pattern small-ball bounds.

The checks do not prove the source high-dimensional subset separation, simultaneous lattice event or sharp matrix singularity rate.

## Possible applications

Obtaining Fourier small-ball bounds that remain valid under arbitrary deterministic phases once quotient subset sums are separated.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
