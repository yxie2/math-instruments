# Use an adapted filtration to preserve locally nilpotent derivations and their invariants

[Family 047](../families/openai-math-047.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000176.md) · [Batch B026](../../batches/B026/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let a domain A have an integer-indexed degree filtration and finite generators h_i. Assume every element has a polynomial representative in those generators whose maximum monomial weight equals its actual degree. For a nonzero locally nilpotent derivation D, set j=max_(Dh_i!=0)(deg Dh_i-deg h_i).

The adapted-representative hypothesis and Leibniz imply deg Dh<=deg h+j for every h. Consequently

$$D_0([h]_d)=[Dh]_{d+j},\qquad D_0^m([h]_d)=[D^mh]_{d+mj}$$

define a homogeneous nonzero locally nilpotent derivation on gr A. A generator attaining the maximum proves nonzeroness. If an intermediate leading class vanishes, the degree drop persists under later iterates. Thus the conclusion holds for negative j as well as positive j, and initial forms of D-invariants remain D_0-invariant.

For any LND delta on a characteristic-zero domain, its order nu(f)=max{n:delta^n f!=0} satisfies nu(fg)=nu(f)+nu(g). At the top Leibniz order exactly one term remains, with a nonzero binomial coefficient. Therefore the kernel is factorially closed. Also delta(h)!=0 implies delta(h) is not divisible by h, since differentiation lowers order by one while multiplication cannot lower it.

Adaptedness cannot be omitted: in k[x,y] with ordinary total degree and generators x,x^2+y, D=partial_y has generator shift -2, but D(y)=1 drops degree only by one. The cancellation needed to represent y is exactly the missing hypothesis.

## Role in the source argument

Transfers additive actions to the source degeneration, then uses divisibility of invariant elements to force invariant factors.

## Antecedents and comparison

The source explicitly invokes the established filtered-derivation method of Kaliman-Makar-Limanov and the classical LND order argument. The extraction retains their essential representative and characteristic assumptions rather than treating generator estimates as sufficient by themselves.

## Checks and limitations

1,152 graded-iterate identities and 192 LND order-product identities pass in weighted polynomial models, including negative weights and shifts. The nonadapted-generator example fails the proposed bound, and characteristic two defeats factorial closure for partial_x.

The source associated-graded presentation is an input not verified here. These models do not establish its rigidity or non-polynomiality theorem.

## Possible applications

Replacing nonlinear additive actions by homogeneous ones while keeping enough invariants to derive a contradiction.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
