# Subgroup counts of Z_p^d stabilize to a nonzero p-adic unit

[Family 314](../families/openai-math-314.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000173.md) · [Batch B025](../../batches/B025/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let a_j(d) count open subgroups of index p^j in Z_p^d, with d>=1. Project a subgroup onto the first coordinate. If the image is p^a Z_p and the kernel in the remaining coordinates has index p^b, there are p^b choices of lift of the first generator modulo that kernel, and a+b=j. Hence

$$a_j(d)=\sum_{b=0}^j p^b a_b(d-1),\qquad\sum_{j\ge0}a_j(d)t^j=\prod_{u=0}^{d-1}(1-p^ut)^{-1}.$$

Write prod_(u=1)^(d-1)(1-p^u t)^(-1)=sum_m b_m t^m. Every b_m is divisible by p^m, and a_j(d)=sum_(m=0)^j b_m. Therefore

$$a_j(d)\longrightarrow c_d=\prod_{u=1}^{d-1}(1-p^u)^{-1}\quad\text{in }\mathbb Z_p,$$

with the stronger explicit congruence a_j(d)=c_d modulo p^(j+1). The limit is a unit because every denominator factor is a p-adic unit. For d=1 the count and empty product are identically one.

These counts generally grow in the ordinary real absolute value. Their convergence is p-adic, and arises because successive contributions have increasing p-divisibility. The source uses the nonzero limit to detect a remaining kernel in a profinite group argument.

## Role in the source argument

Provides a nonvanishing p-adic counting limit for the source kernel-detection step.

## Antecedents and comparison

The count and recursion are explicitly in [Tamanoi, Lemma 5-5](https://msp.org/agt/2001/1-1/agt-v1-n1-p06-p.pdf). The extracted p-adic limit follows directly by separating the u=0 factor. Neither the count nor this elementary consequence is presented as a newly discovered theorem.
