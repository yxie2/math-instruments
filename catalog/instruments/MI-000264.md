# The first nonzero digit of a step transfers cyclic avoidance to product colorings

[Family 160](../families/openai-math-160.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000151.md) · [Batch B020](../../batches/B020/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let N,k>=2, and let c:Z/NZ->{0,1} have no monochromatic k-term progression with nonzero common difference. Repeated residues are allowed in this cyclic condition. For m>=1 color 0,...,N^m-1 by the m-tuple of colors of its base-N digits. This uses at most 2^m colors and has no monochromatic integer k-term progression.

For an integer progression a+j d with d>0, let i be maximal with N^i dividing d, and write d=N^i e. Its length inside the interval ensures i<m. The i-th digit of a+j d is

$$\left\lfloor\frac{a+j d}{N^i}\right\rfloor\bmod N
=\left(\left\lfloor\frac{a}{N^i}\right\rfloor+j e\right)\bmod N.$$

Since e is not divisible by N, these digits form a cyclic progression with nonzero step. Their c-colors cannot all agree. Thus W_(2^m)(k)>N^m, and W_r(k)>N^floor(log_2 r) for r>=2 by allowing unused colors. No primality or unique factorization into prime powers is needed for the base N.

## Role in the source argument

Converts the source cyclic two-color construction into a bound uniform over larger numbers of colors.

## Antecedents and comparison

Digit and product colorings are established. [Fox-Hunter, Section 2 and Lemma 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.02541v1) explicitly use digit avoidance and finite-group product colorings. The source records the carry calculation needed for this interval transfer; it is not a new product-coloring principle.

## Checks and limitations

Read the complete transfer proof. Exhaustively enumerate binary cyclic colorings for N=2,...,8 and k=3,4,5, then check every integer progression in their two-digit lifts, including composite bases.

The cyclic hypothesis is stronger than avoidance only in 0,...,N-1 with no wraparound. This lemma does not supply such a cyclic coloring or the source lower bound for its size.

## Possible applications

Transferring modular progression-free constructions to longer intervals while controlling the number of colors.
