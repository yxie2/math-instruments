# Amplify classwise central mass using rounded midpoint weights

[Family 232](../families/openai-math-232.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000498.md) · [Batch B080](../../batches/B080/README.md)

## Mathematical content

For nonnegative finitely supported f,g,u,v on Z^m, if f(s)g(t)<=u(floor((s+t)/2))v(ceil((s+t)/2)) for all pairs, then (sum f)(sum g)<=(sum u)(sum v). In one dimension the monotone-coupling entropy inequality and the variational bound sum nu log(a/nu)<=log sum a give this directly on positive supports, without a positivity regularization. Coordinate induction gives the general case without factorization.

For integer 1-Lipschitz arrays on a finite connected graph with nonempty fixed integer boundary, both rounded midpoints remain admissible. On each edge their differences c,d satisfy c+d=a+b, |c-d|<=1, so |c|+|d|=|a+b|<=|a|+|b|. Hence weights W=x^(sum edge differences), 0<x<=1, obey W(mid_-)W(mid_+)>=W(s)W(t). A signed linear statistic with coefficient absolute sum at most A incurs rounding error at most A/2.

Partition the support into at most J classes, requiring midpoint admissibility and the weight inequality only for pairs in the same class. Suppose each class has unconditional mass at least c in |Y|<=M, with M>=A/2. For intervals I_j=[(2^j-4)M,(2^(j+1)+4)M], choose a class carrying at least p_j/J of the mass p_j=mu(Y in I_j), pair it with that class's central mass, and apply midpoint comparison. Both outputs lie in I_(j-1), yielding p_j<=(J/c)p_(j-1)^2. The I_j overlap at most twice; a small initial p_j exists among finitely many indices. Iteration, the same argument for -Y and tail integration give exponential tails and all finite moments at scale M. Outputs need not stay in their input class.

## Role in the source argument

Turning a separate coarse central estimate into uniform higher moments while allowing auxiliary classes to change under midpoint rounding.

## Checks and limitations

Finite graph height arrays check rounding admissibility, edge energy and signed-statistic errors. Quantile couplings check the one-dimensional entropy input, including zero masses. Midpoint sums and interval recursions are evaluated on finite weighted examples.

No annular estimate, central mass bound for the actual surface model, or Gaussian field identification is supplied. A central bound only for the union of classes is insufficient.
