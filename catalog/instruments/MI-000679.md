# Pay a cyclic trace with the energy gained by four parity-compatible children

[Family 082](../families/openai-math-082.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000448.md) · [Batch B072](../../batches/B072/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let real edge matrices W_v:H_(v+1)->H_v define row stars R_v=[W_v,W_(v-1)*], and set e=sum_v tr((R_v R_v*)^(3/2)). For diagonal Haar involutions D_v, choose the four signs with s0 s1 s2=1 and C_v=(I+s_v D_v)^(1/2). The child edges are C_v W_v C_(v+1), reflecting doubled atom weights.

Conditional on s_v, each neighboring sign is uniform. Their correlation causes no extra term because the child Gram contains a sum of the two incident contributions, not their product. Its conditional average is C_v R_v R_v* C_v. Convexity and equality of nonzero Gram eigenvalues reduce its average energy to [Phi(T_v+U_v)+Phi(T_v-U_v)]/2, where T_v=R_v*R_v and U_v=R_v*D_v R_v.

Since -T_v<=U_v<=T_v, the supported Hessian comparison gives a midpoint gap at least b_(T_v)(U_v)/(2sqrt(2)). Thus delta=(1/4)sum_children e_child-e>=0. The real mixed trace inequality |tr(D0 W0 D1 W1 D2 W2)|<=(5/3)sum b_(T_v)(U_v) yields |tau|<=(10sqrt(2)/3)delta. Its proof embeds all edges in one symmetric zero-diagonal block matrix M; tr((DM)^3)=6tau, and a supported eigenbasis estimate bounds this cubic trace by the common quadratic cost.

## Role in the source argument

Turns a signed local triangular integral into a positive increment that can telescope.

## Antecedents and comparison

Convex Gram energies and martingale telescoping are classical. The source-specific interface is the four parity-compatible children and the mixed cyclic-trace payment. Exact priority of this finite-dimensional combination is not established.

## Checks and limitations

Random real matrices, including rank-deficient cases and unequal even vertex dimensions, test conditional Gram averages, the mixed trace bound, midpoint costs and the final local energy increment.

The tests do not establish the global tree summation or the measurable-input limit.

## Possible applications

Using pairwise independence when full independence is unavailable in a constrained subdivision.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
