# Principal-unit first-order maps and prime-to-residue torsion reduction

[Family 179](../families/openai-math-179.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000153.md) · [Batch B020](../../batches/B020/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let O be a local domain with maximal ideal n and residue field k. For a nonzero t in n, the principal units 1+tO form a multiplicative group, and

$$\lambda_t:1+tO\longrightarrow(k,+),\qquad 1+tL\longmapsto L\bmod\mathfrak n$$

is a group homomorphism. The domain and t!=0 make L unique. Multiplication replaces L,M by L+M+tLM, whose residue is their sum. Inversion replaces L by -L/(1+tL), whose residue is -L. Negative exponents therefore give the same signed-sum rule as positive exponents.

If k has characteristic p and a finite-order element z of O has residue 1, its order is a power of p. Indeed, if its order is p^a m with p not dividing m, then z^(p^a) has order m. If m>1, cancel z^(p^a)-1 in the domain from the geometric-series identity; reducing the resulting sum gives m=0 in k, a contradiction. In particular prime-to-p torsion with residue 1 equals 1.

These two facts turn an exact multiplicative product relation among principal units into an additive relation among its first-order coefficients. The torsion argument cannot discard a p-power root of unity.

## Role in the source argument

Converts global character-product information into local linear equations in a common residue field.

## Checks and limitations

Read both proofs. Finite integer quotients at p=2,3,5,7 check product and inverse coefficients and the p-power behavior of principal units.

Finite quotients have zero divisors and serve only as models of well-defined reductions from the integer domain. They do not replace the domain assumption or verify the source global character-product theorem.

## Possible applications

Linearizing multiplicative constraints at a ramified prime while preserving the necessary torsion-order restriction.
