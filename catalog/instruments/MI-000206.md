# Clique-ended path systems turn a forbidden cycle length into an amount interval

[Family 189](../families/openai-math-189.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000124.md) · [Batch B013](../../batches/B013/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let Q be a clique of size 3<=t<=k. A path system consists of paths with distinct endpoints in Q and nonempty, pairwise disjoint interiors outside Q; their distinct endpoint pairs form a linear forest on Q. Let q be the number of internal vertices in total and v the number of incident clique vertices. Every integer length ell with q+v<=ell<=q+t is realized by a simple cycle when the system is nonempty: expand each forest chain, choose ell-q-v unused clique vertices as singletons, and concatenate through clique edges. A single expanded chain closes using its endpoint clique edge.

Hence in a graph without a (k+1)-cycle no such system has k+1-t<=q<=k+1-v. Choose maximum amount L<k+1-t, then the fewest paths e at that amount. A competing system with amount A, e' paths and v' incident vertices cannot satisfy

$$L+\mathbf1_{\{e'\ge e\}}\le A\le k+1-v'.$$

Above the threshold it closes a forbidden cycle; below it it improves the chosen amount or the tie-break.

Outside-path exclusions also give independent-set packing. For X containing distinct x,y define B0(x)=N(x) minus X and B_(r+1)(x)=N_(G-X)[B_r(x)]. If no simple outside x-y path has between one and r+s+2 internal vertices, B_r(x) and B_s(y) are disjoint and anticomplete. Intersections or connecting edges would concatenate to such a path. A singleton {x} may be assigned radius minus one, with the same bound when the other option is a ball; two singletons require xy to be a nonedge separately.

## Role in the source argument

Converts improved path systems into forbidden lengths and converts forbidden short paths into separated independent-set contributions.

## Antecedents and comparison

Clique completion and path concatenation are elementary. [Keevash, Long and Skokan](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.06376v1) provide earlier cycle-complete Ramsey context, not an attribution of this exact budget. No claim that the closure principle itself is historically new is made.

## Checks and limitations

Read the endpoint, singleton and length cases. Constructed weighted forests check the explicit cycles for every permitted length. Exhaustive small graphs check the outside-ball separation implication.

Shared interior vertices, repeated endpoint edges or a represented cycle can invalidate the construction. The amount convention and singleton exception must be preserved.

## Possible applications

Reducing exact-cycle problems to small path budgets and packing mutually anticomplete regions from excluded path lengths.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
