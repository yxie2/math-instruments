# A binary identity couples two first-order character sums

[Family 179](../families/openai-math-179.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000153.md) · [Batch B020](../../batches/B020/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let P be a finite odd-order group in the source's abelian character setting, J=sum_(x in P) x, and H0,H1,H2,H3 integral group-ring elements whose coefficients are all signs. Put

c=(H0+H1+H2+H3)/2, d=(H0-H1+H2-H3)/2,
g=(H0-H2)/2+i(H1-H3)/2, and b=(H1+H3)/2.

Then d-c=-2b and g-c=(i-1)b-(H2+iH3). Coefficientwise sign parity gives H2+iH3=(1+i)J+2U for an integral Gaussian group-ring element U.

Work in a local domain O containing the evaluated coefficients and i, at a prime over 2; assume each evaluated c_S is a unit. For a finite signed family of characters chi_S, with signs epsilon_S in {1,-1}, only chi_empty is trivial. Set r_S=d_S/c_S and w_S=g_S/c_S. The identities give r_S in 1+2O and w_S in 1+(1+i)O. Their first-order coefficients satisfy

$$\overline{(r_S-1)/2}+\overline{(w_S-1)/(1+i)}=\overline{J_S/c_S}.$$

Here i reduces to 1 and 1-i reduces to 0. Now retain the crucial additional input: both R=product_S r_S^epsilon_S and W=product_S w_S^epsilon_S are odd-order roots of unity. They have residue 1, so R=W=1. Applying the two additive first-order maps and summing the displayed identity yields sum_S epsilon_S overline(J_S/c_S)=0.

Every nontrivial character kills J, whereas J_empty=|P| is odd and hence a unit. The sum has one nonzero term, a contradiction. In the source, the signed characters are indexed by subsets of prime divisors of u, and the hard alternating-product proposition supplies the odd-order input. That proposition is not proved by the binary identity.

The order-four row (1,1,1,-1) gives c=d=1 and g=i. Its ratio W=i has residue 1 at (1+i) but even order, showing why the retained hypothesis cannot be omitted.
