# Localize SU(2) word-map degree by cyclic conjugation

[Family 249](../families/openai-math-249.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000192.md) · [Batch B030](../../batches/B030/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let f:SU(2)^m -> SU(2)^m be a coefficient-free word map with nonsingular integer exponent-sum matrix B. Let O be open, invariant under simultaneous conjugation, contain the identity and have boundary disjoint from f^{-1}(1). If b is the number of roots of the maximal-torus restriction in O, then

$$\deg(f,O,1)=b\,\operatorname{sign}(\det B)\ne0.$$

The torus map has |det B| roots, each with tangent sign sign(det B), and b>=1. For an odd prime p>b, conjugation by exp(2 pi i/p) acts freely away from the torus. At a fixed root its tangent representation is R^m plus C^m; equivariance makes the normal derivative complex linear. A small invariant normal perturbation N+sI makes it invertible without changing the torus map. Its real determinant is positive, so the full local sign remains sign(det B).

Protect these fixed-root neighborhoods. Ordinary parameterized bump perturbations on the remaining free-action charts, propagated to their p translates, make every remaining zero regular while preserving the isolating boundary. Nonfixed zeros occur in p-point orbits of equal sign. Therefore the selected degree is congruent to b sign(det B) modulo p. Repeating for arbitrarily large odd primes gives the stated integer equality. The no-variable case is the identity of a point, of degree one.

## Role in the source argument

Keeps a solution inside a specified component region of word equations as coefficients are subsequently varied without a boundary crossing.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Frøyshov 2013, Proposition 2.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1304.0936) states the Gerstenhaber-Rothaus global degree formula. That statement alone does not locate a solution in an arbitrary invariant isolating region. No exhaustive priority search for this localization was completed.

## Checks and limitations

Forty-five exact torus-fiber counts and sixty exact complex-to-real determinant identities check algebraic ingredients.

The tests do not compute local degree or certify the equivariant perturbation argument, and do not establish the source Eilenberg-Ganea conclusion.

Localized continuation for equivariant word systems with nonsingular exponent sums.
