# Recover the ideal lattice and trace finiteness ideals from the extended trace cone

[Family 301](../families/openai-math-301.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000210.md) · [Batch B034](../../batches/B034/README.md)

## Mathematical content

For each closed two-sided ideal I of a C*-algebra, define tau_I(a)=0 for positive a in I and infinity otherwise. These are exactly the additive idempotents of the extended lower-semicontinuous trace cone. Moreover

$$I\subseteq J\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\tau_I+\tau_J=\tau_I.$$

Thus an additive cone bijection determines an ideal-lattice order isomorphism. For a trace tau let Fin(tau) be the closed ideal generated by finite-trace positives and Ker(tau) its zero ideal. In the canonical trace-cone topology,

$$\lim_{r\downarrow0}r\tau=\tau_{\operatorname{Fin}(\tau)},\qquad\lim_{r\uparrow\infty}r\tau=\tau_{\operatorname{Ker}(\tau)}.$$

Consequently a continuous positively homogeneous additive cone equivalence preserves both ideals. The first limit must be interpreted in the cut-based cone topology; it need not be the naive pointwise limit on uncut positive elements. For separable algebras the ideal-lattice isomorphism also identifies primitive ideal spaces with their hull-kernel topology.

## Antecedents and comparison

This construction is already explicit in [Elliott, Robert and Santiago](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0805.3122), the paragraph on ideal traces in Section 3.1 and equation (3.4) in Section 3.2. It is established machinery, not a new source discovery.

## Checks and limitations

An exhaustive 1,364 pairs of ideals in finite commutative direct sums verify the additive-idempotent inclusion rule.

Finite models do not test non-pointwise cone convergence or the source global classification theorem.

## Possible applications

Recovering ideal and support data from a trace invariant without adding a separate ideal-lattice invariant.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
