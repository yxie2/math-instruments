# A sumset bound converts hidden grid entropy into fair-pair capacity at finer scales

[Family 148](../families/openai-math-148.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000129.md) · [Batch B014](../../batches/B014/README.md)

## Mathematical content

For a finite law nu on F subset R, define c_ell as the largest sum of nonnegative weights w_e over unordered pairs with 2^(-ell)<=|x-y|<2^(1-ell), subject to (1/2)sum_(e contains x) w_e<=nu{x}. Thus 0<=c_ell<=1; for a two-point law the sole nonzero capacity is twice its smaller mass. With H^0_rho(nu)=H(floor(X/rho)), the source proves for every integer k>=1

$$k(H(\nu)-H^0_\rho(\nu))\le\log_2|kF|+60000k^4\sum_{2^{-\ell}\le2\rho}c_\ell.$$

The constant does not depend on the least positive spacing in F. Reveal the sum Y of k independent samples, at entropy cost at most log2|kF|, and refine their known rho-grid cells by random nested cuts with reference scales rho_h=rho 4^(-h). Parents have length between rho_h/2 and 2rho_h, at most eight children, and new cut densities at most 20/rho_h.

The key one-level estimate is linear rather than a binary-entropy loss. Conditional on the parent cells let m_i be the means and a>0 the distance from all m_i to the new cuts. Put p_i=Pr(|X_i-m_i|>=a/(4k)) and t=sum p_i. Two or more exceptional coordinates have probability q<=min(t,t^2). The flag B={|Y-sum m_i|>=a/2} has probability at most t and is already known from Y. Outside B and the multiple-exception event all child labels equal their mean labels. Therefore

$$H(\text{child labels}\mid Y,\text{parent cells})\le h_2(q)+3k(q+\Pr B)\le9k\sum_i p_i.$$

Averaging the cuts bounds each p_i by a constant times E|X_i-m_i|/rho_h. Independent pairs within the parent cells form a symmetric coupling with marginal nu; their band masses are feasible pair weights. Summing the geometric scale factors yields the displayed constant. The proof charges no separate entropy for B because the sum was revealed first.

## Role in the source argument

Finds useful small-distance pair mass from exact entropy that remains invisible at a coarse scale, with a sumset controlling the cost of conditioning.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Varju, Proposition 21](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1602.00261v4) gives an earlier scale-local fair-pair decomposition. The selected estimate instead totals capacities below a scale using a sumset term and randomized refinements. No exhaustive novelty claim is made.

## Checks and limitations

Read the complete nested-partition and charging proof. Linear programs check capacities and finite-law inequalities; exact finite product distributions check the observed-sum child-label bound, including rare exceptional events.

The samples are independent conditional on their separate parent cells. Dropping the conditioning on Y can restore a p log(1/p) loss. Numerical capacity tests do not certify the general nested-partition argument.

## Possible applications

Extracting useful two-point submeasures from very finely clustered finite laws without a separation assumption.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
