# Delete order-index obstructions from a structured prime reservoir

[Family 029](../families/openai-math-029.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000398.md) · [Batch B065](../../batches/B065/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Fix finitely many positive prime bases. Assume a reservoir of at least c x/(log x)² primes x<p<2x with p=4rQ+1, Q>x^0.9 prime, and every prime factor ell of r between exp((log x)^0.1) and exp((log x)^0.3). Choose the prescribed progression by CRT so each odd base is a quadratic nonresidue; p=5 modulo 8 handles base 2. Quadratic reciprocity then makes every order index odd. A nontrivial index has a prime factor Q or one of the controlled ell.

For the Q obstruction and a fixed base q, put j=(p-1)/Q<2x^0.1. Such p divides the product of q^j-1 over this range. Comparing logarithms bounds their number by O_q(x^0.2/log x).

For each controlled ell, assume the uniform exceptional bound C_q x/[ell(ell-1)log x]+x^(1-eta), for a fixed eta>0. Summing gives O_q[x exp(-(log x)^0.1)/log x+exp((log x)^0.3)x^(1-eta)], which is o(x/(log x)²). The first term uses the telescoping majorant sum_(n>=L)1/[n(n-1)]; the second counts at most the upper endpoint many primes. A finite union over bases removes only o of the reservoir, leaving a positive lower bound of the same order. These are asymptotic comparisons; the very small source eta does not imply a practical numerical threshold. The reservoir and exceptional bounds remain assumptions.

## Role in the source argument

Separates a simultaneous primitive-root conclusion into explicit analytic inputs and an elementary deletion argument.

## Antecedents and comparison

The Kummer/index reduction is classical, as reviewed in [Moree, section 4](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0412262). The structured reservoir is a source-specific conditional input. This card does not claim the unconditional resolution of Artin's conjecture.

## Checks and limitations

Finite CRT and quadratic-character checks verify the progression mechanism. Exact products test the large-factor divisibility count; logarithmic ratios check the direction of the asymptotic estimates without underflow.

No numerical test establishes either assumed analytic counting estimate or a usable threshold for the asymptotic lower bound.

## Possible applications

Identifying precisely which prime-counting estimates would suffice for simultaneous generation by finitely many bases.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
