# Control integrated intensity distortion by the square-root likelihood

[Family 227](../families/openai-math-227.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000495.md) · [Batch B079](../../batches/B079/README.md)

## Mathematical content

For a nonexplosive marked process with reference predictable intensities r, let Q be absolutely continuous with respect to P up to a finite stopping time tau. Write Z=dQ/dP and rb for its changed intensities in the same filtration. Set J=integral sum r(sqrt(b)-1)^2 dt. Then
Q{J>A, log Z<=B} <= exp((B-A)/2).

With initial density z_0, the likelihood identity gives sqrt(Z) exp(J/2)=sqrt(z_0) times the stochastic likelihood exponential with intensity ratios sqrt(b). Conditional expectation of that nonnegative exponential is at most one, while E_P sqrt(z_0)<=1. Localize integrated reference, changed and square-root rates, then use Fatou to obtain E_P[sqrt(Z) exp(J/2)]<=1. On the event in question, sqrt(Z) exp(-J/2)<=exp((B-A)/2); multiplying and integrating proves the tail bound. Zero ratios are permitted, with the expression set to zero where Z=0.

This generic inequality does not by itself supply polynomial bounds on log Z for tested bridges. Those source-specific inputs are separate, and bounds averaged over a boundary law are not uniform bounds for every fixed endpoint pair.

## Role in the source argument

Converting likelihood bounds into pathwise control of accumulated rate distortion.

## Checks and limitations

Exact Poisson likelihood calculations and truncated sums with controlled tails check the square-root identity and event bound, including zero changed rates and an initial density change.

Constant-rate Poisson tests do not verify a general predictable intensity construction or the SK bridge estimates.
