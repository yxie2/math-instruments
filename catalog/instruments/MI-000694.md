# Preserve a nonzero matrix evaluation through a finite coefficient ring

[Family 116](../families/openai-math-116.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000463.md) · [Batch B074](../../batches/B074/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let V_i be N by N matrices over k[t]_(t), the rational functions regular at zero. Reduce their entries modulo t^E and replace each residue by its E by E multiplication matrix on k[t]/(t^E). This produces matrices T_i of size NE over k.

For any extension field F/k and noncommutative polynomial f over F, suppose a polynomial D with D(0)!=0 makes D f(V) polynomial entrywise with every nonzero entry of degree less than E. If f(V)!=0, some such numerator survives modulo t^E. Reduction is a unital algebra map, so f of the reduced matrices is nonzero. Multiplication on F[t]/(t^E) is faithful, since a nonzero element acts nontrivially on 1. Its block extension is also faithful and commutes with evaluation and scalar extension. Hence f(T)!=0 over F.

For the source strictly triangular operators, each nonzero product follows a strictly increasing chain of indices. Its denominator uses each factor t^r-1 at most once, so D=product_(r=1 to N-1)(t^r-1) clears every polynomial evaluation. The numerator degree is at most binomial(N,2)+n(N-1), giving E one larger. Relative primality of the denominator factors is unnecessary, including in small characteristic. No shift automorphism on the truncated ring is needed.

## Role in the source argument

Descends rational-function matrix evaluations to matrices over the original coefficient field.

## Antecedents and comparison

Truncation of a local ring and its faithful regular representation are classical. The source-specific input is one denominator and degree bound valid for all relevant word evaluations; the descent lemma itself is elementary algebra.

## Checks and limitations

Exact small-prime computations compare block-matrix evaluation with evaluation in the truncated ring. Boundary examples show why the strict degree bound and regular denominator matter.

The tests do not establish that the source rational-function tuple hits every bounded-size formula.

## Possible applications

Replacing field extensions or transcendental coefficients by finite matrices while retaining a known nonzero witness.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
