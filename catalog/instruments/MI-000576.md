# Factor a sparse masked operator through edges with their labels retained

[Family 251](../families/openai-math-251.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000345.md) · [Batch B059](../../batches/B059/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let s_1,...,s_n be a list in a countable group, allowing repeated elements, and let v_i be unit vectors in C^k. Suppose every synthesis matrix V_J of at most p of these indexed vectors has norm at most b_0. For |c(x,i)|<=1 define

(T_c xi)(x)=sum_i c(x,i) v_i v_i^* xi(x s_i).

Assume each row has at most p active labels, and each incoming column y has at most p labels i with c(y s_i^(-1),i) nonzero. Then ||T_c||<=b_0^2.

Keep the edge index (x,i) in an auxiliary l2 space. Analysis sends xi to v_i^*xi(x s_i), multiplication applies c(x,i), and synthesis sums v_i times each edge coordinate at its source x. Thus T_c=S M_c F. Row sparsity bounds ||S|| by b_0. Regrouping the analysis norm by arrival y bounds ||F|| by b_0, since for each fixed label i there is exactly one possible source y s_i^(-1). The diagonal mask has norm at most one.

If two labels carry the same group element, they still give different edge coordinates. Their contributions to a common block are summed only during synthesis. Collapsing these labels prematurely would discard the multiplicity information used in both bounds.

## Role in the source argument

Controls operator norm using local vector-family bounds despite coincidences among group shifts.

## Antecedents and comparison

This is an explicit analysis/multiplier/synthesis factorization, a classical Hilbert-space technique. The selected source makes the repeated-label bookkeeping and the two separate sparsity conditions precise. No earliest-priority claim is made for the interface.

## Checks and limitations

30 finite cyclic-group masks with repeated labels compare the assembled operator with the edge factorization and verify the three norm bounds. Maximum factorization error is below 4e-16.

The test does not construct masks from nonamenability or prove a lower distance from the translation commutant.

## Possible applications

Bounding block operators from locally sparse incidence patterns without assuming distinct labels or shifts.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
