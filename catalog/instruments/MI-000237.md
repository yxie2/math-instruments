# Complementary fractional comparisons support a bounded quantum tournament

[Family 284](../families/openai-math-284.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000138.md) · [Batch B016](../../batches/B016/README.md)

## Mathematical content

For m>=2 let a_ij+a_ji=1 for i!=j and a_ii=0. A reversible coherent comparison of an unordered pair produces these win probabilities using at most c queries and retains all workspace. Put T=ceil(log2(1000m)), epsilon=1/(1000T). There is a quantum algorithm using O(c sqrt(m) T^2 log T) queries on every branch that returns at most T candidates. If one candidate i* has a_(i*,j)>=1-epsilon against every other j, its inclusion probability is at least 1-2m/2^T-T epsilon.

The ideal process starts with w_i=1, samples p with probability w_p/W, W=sum w_i, and sets w_i'=w_i a_ip. Complementarity gives

$$\mathbb E[W'\mid w]=(W^2-\sum_iw_i^2)/(2W)\le W/2.$$

Until i* is chosen its weight is at least (1-epsilon)^T>1/2, so the ideal miss probability is at most 2m/2^T. A uniform superposition of i followed by fresh comparisons against the previous pivots has all-win weight w_i, total success W/m, and conditional index law exactly w_i/W.

Bounded amplification with success threshold gamma=1/(2m) and failure budget epsilon preserves the full conditional successful state. One implementation chooses zero Grover iterations half the time and a uniform length in {0,...,M-1} half the time, M=ceil(10/sqrt(gamma)); each trial succeeds with probability at least 9/40 when p>=gamma. A fixed number of repetitions gives failure at most epsilon. Thus each stage can be coupled to the ideal pivot, failing with probability at most epsilon before i* is selected. The query cap holds even when no dominant candidate exists. No transitivity, order or bias between the remaining candidates is required.

## Role in the source argument

Searches candidate certificates using possibly ambiguous local comparisons while keeping a fixed worst-case query budget.

## Checks and limitations

Read the drift, successful-state preservation and history-conditioned coupling proofs. Finite weighted tournaments check drift and miss probabilities; coherent comparison states check the conditional index law, and matrix simulations check successful-state preservation under varying amplification lengths.

Fresh workspaces are needed for the product of comparison probabilities. The routine returns a short list; identifying the dominant candidate from that list requires the application-specific verifier.

## Possible applications

Quantum search among candidates when comparison subroutines may be unpromised away from the correct candidate.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
