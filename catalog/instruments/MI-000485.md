# Periodize a buffered covering window without losing boundary coverage

[Family 092](../families/openai-math-092.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000264.md) · [Batch B048](../../batches/B048/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let a bounded convex body K in R^n have volume V and lie in Q_r=[-r,r]^n. Let X be a locally finite set of centers with K+X=R^n. The finite set S=X intersect (a+Q_(L+r)) covers a+Q_L, including its boundary. Repeating S by 2L Z^n covers all space. Distinct residue classes modulo that lattice give a periodic density at most V |S|/(2L)^n.

Consequently, if theta_per is the infimum over finite unions of lattice cosets, every center set and every cube obey V #(X intersect (a+Q_R))/(2R)^n>=theta_per(1-r/R)^n. This bounds both upper and lower cube densities below by theta_per.

For balls, integrate the buffered-window inequality over a in z+B_(R-beta), beta=sqrt(n)(L+r). Each center is counted on a set of a of volume at most (2(L+r))^n. Tonelli yields the lower bound theta_per [L/(L+r)]^n(1-beta/R)^n for the normalized center count in z+B_R. First send R to infinity at fixed L, then L to infinity.

Conversely, a bounded fundamental cell shows that every periodic center set has all these intensities equal to the number of distinct cosets divided by lattice covolume. Thus the infima over coverings agree for periodic, cube upper/lower and ball upper/lower density conventions. These are equalities of infima; the individual statistics of one irregular covering need not coincide. Density counts overlapping translates separately, not just the volume of their union.

## Role in the source argument

Transfers the companion lower bound against all finite-coset coverings to unrestricted locally finite coverings.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Prosanov, On a Relation Between Packing and Covering Densities of Convex Bodies](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00454-019-00121-x), Theorem 2.1, states the classical periodic reduction and attributes it to Rogers. The statement was retrieved and read; the source supplies the explicit finite-window and density-convention argument.

## Checks and limitations

Exact rational one-dimensional interval models verify coverage of a repeated buffered window, including seams. An unbuffered window can leave a gap when a needed center lies just outside it.

The check does not establish the high-dimensional random covering lower bound. It illustrates the buffer mechanism; the general proof is the selected geometric argument.

## Possible applications

Reducing universal covering-density lower bounds to finite periodic patterns with explicit boundary control.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
