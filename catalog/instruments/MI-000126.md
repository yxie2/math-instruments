# Removing affine obstructions from double-divergence tests without a global lower bound

[Family 091](../families/openai-math-091.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000086.md) · [Batch B003](../../batches/B003/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let $\mu$ have a smooth, strictly positive, even probability density on $\mathbb R^n$. Let $\tau$ be smooth, even and symmetric, with $0<\tau(x)\le\Lambda I$. Put $Af=x\cdot\nabla f-\tau:D^2f$. Assume both

$$\mathbb E[hAf]=\mathbb E[\nabla h\cdot\tau\nabla f],\qquad
\mathbb E[(Af)(A-1)f]=\mathbb E\|\tau^{1/2}D^2f\,\tau^{1/2}\|_F^2$$

for compact smooth tests. Then the $L^2(\mu)$ closure of $\{A(A-1)f:f\in C_c^\infty,\ f\text{ even}\}$ is the entire centered even subspace.

The proof first shows that every $L^2$ distributional solution of $A(A-1)g=0$ is affine. Local ellipticity gives smoothness. Cutoff estimates establish $Ag\in L^2$ before splitting $g=(g-Ag)+Ag$ into eigenvalue-zero and eigenvalue-one parts. The first is constant; graph-norm cutoff approximation and the Hessian identity force the second to be linear. Parity and centering then remove both obstructions.

## Role in the source argument

Extends a dual variance inequality from compactly supported double divergences to arbitrary centered even L2 tests. This justifies substituting the unbounded polynomial slab observable into the final dual inequality.

## Antecedents and comparison

The operator belongs to the moment-measure diffusion of [Klartag](https://arxiv.org/abs/1309.2767v1); the source credits the transport-Hessian identity to [Kolesnikov–Livshyts–Rotem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.12099v3). Orthogonal-complement density and cutoff methods are standard. The useful formulation here requires only local strict positivity and a global upper matrix bound, with the necessary operator identities stated explicitly.

## Checks and limitations

Read the cutoff estimates, the L2 splitting and the graph-norm approximation in full. The bundled calculation checks the Gaussian Hermite model, where A has eigenvalues k and A(A-1) has eigenvalues k(k-1).

The Gaussian check illustrates the affine kernel; it does not establish density for a general matrix field. Neither parity nor the Hessian identity can be silently omitted. No operator-domain assertion is assumed merely from the formal expression for A.

## Possible applications

A reusable density argument when a weighted second-order operator has an affine kernel, its coefficients may degenerate at infinity, and a compact-test Hessian identity is available.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
