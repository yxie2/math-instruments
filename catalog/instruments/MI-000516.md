# Transfer projectivity through internal norms without a monoidal evaluation

[Family 208](../families/openai-math-208.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000289.md) · [Batch B052](../../batches/B052/README.md)

## Mathematical content

For a finite p-group H over a field k of characteristic p, a finite-dimensional kH-module M is projective exactly when the norm nu_H:M_H->M^H induced by sum_h h is an isomorphism. The regular module has one-dimensional coinvariants and invariants, and its norm identifies their generators. This proves one direction for all finite groups.

For the p-group converse, kH is local and self-injective and its socle is the line generated by N_H=sum_h h. Every nonzero left ideal meets that socle. If N_H m is nonzero, the map kH->M sending a to am is injective and splits. Remove free summands until the remainder M' has N_H M'=0. A nonzero remainder has nonzero coinvariants by Nakayama, so its zero norm cannot be an isomorphism. Thus an isomorphic norm forces M to be free.

Now let C be a finite tensor category with exact tensor products and exact faithful k-linear evaluation omega=Hom(P,-) for a projective generator P. Exact evaluation preserves finite invariants, coinvariants and norms and reflects isomorphisms. If independent p-groups H_i act on X_i and every omega(X_i) is projective, their internal norms are isomorphisms. Independent actions give (X tensor Y)_(H times K)=X_H tensor Y_K and the analogous invariant identity, under which the product norm is the tensor product of the norms. Evaluation therefore proves omega(tensor_i X_i) projective for the product group. Restriction to any subgroup preserves projectivity. No identification omega(X tensor Y)=omega(X) tensor omega(Y) is required.

## Role in the source argument

Allows the source minimal-nonprojective-rank argument to use tensor products despite the evaluation functor not being monoidal.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Chebolu-Christensen-Minac, Ghosts in modular representation theory](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0609699), Proposition 2.1 and Proposition 4.6 in the retrieved version, use norm vanishing on projective-free modules and prove the socle-annihilator argument; those passages were read. The full selected source norm criterion and internal transfer proof were checked directly.

## Checks and limitations

Exact cyclic p-group Jordan modules test the norm criterion and mixtures of free and nonfree summands. Independent group actions test tensor factorization of norm operators. The abstract internal-category step is supported by its proof, not simulated by these finite matrices.

This does not prove restricted tilting, Frobenius exactness or the existence of a fiber functor for every category.

## Possible applications

Transporting a tensor-product property through an exact faithful linear functor when the property is detected by a canonical morphism.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
