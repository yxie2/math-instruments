# Convert a very-general violation into a universal equal-multiplicity profile

[Family 039](../families/openai-math-039.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000419.md) · [Batch B068](../../batches/B068/README.md)

## Mathematical content

For ordered distinct r-tuples of plane points, existence of a nonzero degree-d form with prescribed nonnegative integral multiplicities is a Zariski-closed condition over Q: it is the rank-deficiency locus of the Taylor evaluation matrix. There are countably many profiles. Remove every such locus that is proper; the complement is nonempty, for example by taking affine coordinates algebraically independent over Q.

If a profile occurs at a tuple outside this exceptional union, its locus must be the entire configuration space. It is therefore universal. Cyclically permute its r multiplicities, choose a nonzero form for each permuted profile at any fixed tuple, and multiply the forms. The product has degree r d and multiplicity at least m=sum_i m_i at each point. Consequently a violation d<=sum_i m_i/sqrt(r), with positive sum, yields a universal equal-multiplicity profile of degree D=r d and D/m<=sqrt(r). Taking powers preserves universality. This reduces a proposed very-general counterexample; it does not rule one out.

## Role in the source argument

Allows later arguments to test a hypothetical violation at specially chosen configurations.

## Antecedents and comparison

Determinantal closedness, countable very-general loci and symmetrization by products are classical. No independent new Nagata theorem follows from the extracted reduction.

## Checks and limitations

Exact plane jet matrices distinguish collinear from noncollinear configurations; cyclic profile sums and actual polynomial products check degree and multiplicity bookkeeping.

Finite examples cannot verify the nonempty complement of all countably many loci or eliminate universal profiles.

## Possible applications

Moving a hypothetical generic interpolation failure to a controlled special configuration.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
