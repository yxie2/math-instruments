# Separate partition capacity constraints using minimum spanning trees

[Family 174](../families/openai-math-174.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000348.md) · [Batch B060](../../batches/B060/README.md)

## Mathematical content

For a connected finite loopless graph H, nonnegative capacities z_e and q>=0, the inequalities z(cross(P))>=q(|P|-1) for all vertex partitions P are equivalent to

sum z_e ell_e >= q MST(ell) for every ell in [0,1]^E.

For necessity, let P_a be the components of edges of length less than a. Kruskal gives MST(ell)=integral_0^1 (|P_a|-1) da. Every edge crossing P_a has length at least a, so integrating its partition inequality proves the displayed bound. Conversely set ell=1 on edges crossing a fixed partition and zero elsewhere. Every spanning tree has length at least |P|-1, yielding the partition inequality. This direction does not assume that the parts induce connected graphs.

With rational input, maximize qv-z dot ell subject to 0<=ell<=1, 0<=v<=n and v<=ell(T) for every spanning tree T. An MST oracle separates the last constraints. A positive optimum supplies a violated inequality in z; a nonpositive optimum certifies all partition inequalities. Polynomial separation follows from rational optimization-separation equivalence. This local oracle does not itself construct a thin tree or analyze the full algorithm's binary multiplicities.

## Role in the source argument

Replaces exponentially many vertex-partition tests by a standard combinatorial optimization oracle.

## Antecedents and comparison

The ingredients are classical Kruskal integration and spanning-tree polyhedral separation. The full selected source proof was read; the cited general optimization-separation theorem is a dependency, not re-proved or independently audited here.

## Checks and limitations

Thirty LP instances agree with exhaustive partition deficits. One hundred twenty rational length assignments satisfy the exact Kruskal integral identity.

Small LPs validate the interface and signs, not the asymptotic bit complexity of ellipsoid optimization or the global thin-tree algorithm.

## Possible applications

Testing whether nonnegative capacities support a prescribed fractional tree-packing level.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
