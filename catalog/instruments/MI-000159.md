# Selected coordinate pairs give a uniform weighted Hilbert coefficient

[Family 020](../families/openai-math-020.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000101.md) · [Batch B007](../../batches/B007/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let k>=2 be an integer, and let Y be an irreducible h-dimensional subvariety of $a_i^kb_i=n-\theta_i$ with distinct theta_i, 2<=h<=d, a nonempty open where every a_i is nonzero, and n nonconstant. Choose h-1 of the a_i algebraically independent over C(n), then one further distinct index. The selected a_i and n satisfy one irreducible equation $F_1(a,n)=\sum_I a^If_I(n)$, with no a_i dividing F1. Let the pair image retain these h pairs (a_i,b_i). Give a_i weight U>0 and b_i weight V>0, fixed rational numbers.

Put $\nu_{i,I}=\operatorname{ord}_{n-\theta_i}f_I$, $e_i=\min_I(I_i+k\nu_{i,I})$, and

$$D=\max_I\{U|I|+(kU+V)\deg f_I\}-U\sum_i e_i.$$

Then D>0 and the source obtains

$$H_{\rm pair}(T)=\frac{(kU+V)^{h-1}D}{U^hV^h}\frac{T^h}{h!}+O(T^{h-1}+1).$$

To see the algebraic mechanism, factor $f_I(n)=\prod_i(n-\theta_i)^{\nu_{i,I}}g_I(n)$. Extract the coordinate factors to get the polynomial

$$C_1=\sum_I a^{I+k\nu_I-e}b^{\nu_I}g_I(a_1^kb_1+\theta_1).$$

Together with the h-1 equations equating $a_i^kb_i+\theta_i$, it describes the pair image. At an affine boundary a_i=0, no other a_j can vanish, and the surviving distinct Laurent monomials show that C1 cuts the boundary dimension down. After weighted homogenization, the all-nonzero-a part at infinity is cut by a nonzero leading form; the other part lies in coordinate h-planes. These checks exclude top-dimensional boundary components and justify the complete-intersection Hilbert series.

With integer weights A=L U, B=L V, Q=kA+B, E=L D, that series is

$$\frac{(1-z^Q)^{h-1}(1-z^E)}{(1-z)(1-z^A)^h(1-z^B)^h}.$$

The pole at 1 has order h+1; every other root-of-unity pole has order at most h, so no periodic term changes the leading coefficient. Fixed weights and bounded degree make the error uniform even when coefficients and blockwise linear coordinates move. Pullback gives H_Y>=H_pair. If the function-field extension has degree at least two, a coordinate xi outside the image field gives $H_Y(T)\ge H_{\rm pair}(T)+H_{\rm pair}(T-\omega)$, doubling its leading lower bound.

## Role in the source argument

Supplies weighted polynomial-space growth uniformly for the moving subvarieties encountered by the affine counting argument.

## Antecedents and comparison

Weighted homogenization and Hilbert-series asymptotics are classical tools in determinant methods; see the context in [Salberger](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0508326v3) and [Xiao](https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.05587v2). The explicit extracted weight D and the paired-coordinate boundary checks are the selected source-specific calculation.

## Checks and limitations

Read the affine-boundary, weighted-infinity and function-field arguments. Exact rational examples verify the extracted equation on the nonzero-coordinate open. Formal power-series computations check leading coefficients, including nontrivial roots of unity in the weights; these are not a general symbolic proof of the geometric assertions.

Nonconstant n, distinct theta_i, irreducibility and the nonempty nonzero-a open must be retained. A Hilbert series for a guessed list of equations is insufficient without the boundary and saturation argument. Uniformity requires fixed weights and a degree bound.

Determinant estimates on families of paired multiplicative equations whose coefficients or lattice coordinates vary.
