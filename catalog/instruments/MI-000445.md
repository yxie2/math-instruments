# Certify saturation of a rational span from independent prime reductions

[Family 008](../families/openai-math-008.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000241.md) · [Batch B042](../../batches/B042/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let K be finite free over Z_(2), and let w_1,...,w_s have linearly independent images in K/2K. Then they are rationally independent and

$$(\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb Q}\{w_i\})\cap K
=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb Z_{(2)}}\{w_i\}.$$

For rational independence, scale a nonzero relation so all coefficients lie in Z_(2) and at least one is a unit; reduction gives a contradiction. For saturation, if v=sum c_i w_i lies in K but some coefficient has a power of two in its denominator, multiply by the smallest 2^e clearing all such denominators. At least one resulting coefficient is a unit, while the sum lies in 2K, again contradicting independence.

The same argument works over Z_(p). Equivalently, the column matrix has a maximal minor that is a unit in the local ring, providing a left inverse there. This does not say every modular solution of an unrelated equation lifts.

## Role in the source argument

Ensures old Hall-word values cannot acquire a new leading class merely through rational coefficients with hidden powers-of-two denominators.

## Antecedents and comparison

Standard local-ring linear algebra and saturation. The source applies it to Hall words, whose independent reductions must be proved separately.

## Checks and limitations

One hundred exact odd-minor examples recover localized coefficients and reject half-integral ones. The vector 2 in Z_(2) is a negative control when reduction independence is dropped.

The Hall-word independence and the final dimension bound are not established by these lattice models.

## Possible applications

Transferring modular independence to rational independence while controlling integral intersections.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
