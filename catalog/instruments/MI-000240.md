# Three equal factors are the sole obstruction to a middle-third grouping

[Family 023](../families/openai-math-023.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000140.md) · [Batch B017](../../batches/B017/README.md)

## Mathematical content

A positive partition x_1+...+x_r=1 with every x_i<2/3 has a subset sum strictly between 1/3 and 2/3 unless the partition is exactly (1/3,1/3,1/3).

If any part is already in the open interval, it is the required subset. Otherwise all parts are at most 1/3. If some a<1/3, start with it and add parts until first exceeding 1/3. The sum is at most 2/3. Equality would mean that the previous sum and the last part were both 1/3; then a together with that last part lies strictly inside the interval. If no smaller part exists, the partition consists of three thirds.

If factor counts are bounded, parts have a fixed positive lower bound and a fixed upper bound below 2/3, compactness makes the interior gap uniform outside any fixed neighborhood of the exceptional vector. Grouping the selected prime factors and their complement gives a shorter product with logarithmic exponent in [1/3+delta,1/2]. Fixed dyadic support ratios can be absorbed by decreasing delta for large scale.

In the exceptional three-factor case, write AB approximately X and let B be the largest prime scale. If H=B^2/A is at most (log X)^C, then B is at most a constant times X^(1/3)(log X)^(C/3). Since all three factors are at most B, each is also at least X^(1/3) times a fixed negative power of log X. Only O((1+log log X)^3) triples of dyadic scales then occur. Prime-density bounds remain a separate analytic input.

## Role in the source argument

Identifies the only small balanced-product transition that needs a special sparse-prime estimate.

## Antecedents and comparison

The subset-sum proof and compactness argument are elementary and are not asserted to be historically new. Targeted searches for the exact grouping formulation did not establish a pre-release attribution; that absence is not evidence of novelty.

## Checks and limitations

Read the proof and its dyadic consequence. Exhaustive rational partitions check the exceptional case and all admissible subsets; boundary controls show why the strict upper bound on individual parts is needed.

Without a lower bound on parts or a bounded factor count, this compactness argument supplies no uniform gap. The grouping lemma alone proves no cancellation in bilinear sums.
