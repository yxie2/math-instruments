# Recover a Fourier certificate normalization from a first-shell slope

[Family 090](../families/openai-math-090.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000452.md) · [Batch B072](../../batches/B072/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Assume a radial Schwartz Fourier pair F(x)=G1(b|x|^2), Fhat(x)=G2(b|x|^2) on a covolume-one triangular lattice whose dual is a rotation. With shell coordinate s_a, Poisson summation gives sum_a G1(t s_a)=t^-1 sum_a G2(s_a/t). Schwartz decay permits two derivatives termwise on compact positive t intervals.

Suppose all nonzero shell values vanish, all G2 shell slopes vanish, and G1' is zero except on the first shell, where G1'(1)=-Q1 exp(-pi h). There are six first-shell vectors. At t=1, Poisson gives G1(0)=G2(0). Its derivative gives -6Q1 exp(-pi h)=-G2(0), hence a positive common normalization without a numerical origin estimate.

The second derivative gives sum_n r(n)n^2[G1''(n)-G2''(n)]=2G2(0), because d^2/dt^2[t^-1 G2(s/t)] at one equals 2G2(s)+4sG2'(s)+s^2G2''(s). If the supplied global signs make later G1 nodes maxima and G2 nodes minima, their curvature contributions are nonpositive. With G_i=exp(-pi h s)H_i, this conditionally yields H1''(1)-H2''(1)>=2(1-pi h)Q1. The normalization identity itself needs no global signs.

## Role in the source argument

Fixes a remaining scale factor exactly and supplies an additional consistency test for interpolated curvatures.

## Antecedents and comparison

Poisson summation and differentiation under Schwartz bounds are classical. The source chooses exact shell jets so the first derivative isolates the origin value; exact priority of this normalization interface is not established.

## Checks and limitations

Symbolic differentiation checks both dilation derivatives and the H-to-G conversion. Exact shell enumeration checks multiplicity six; Gaussian Fourier pairs test the undropped Poisson identity and its first two derivatives numerically.

These tests do not verify existence of the claimed zero-jet Fourier pair, its signs or its sharp packing consequence.

## Possible applications

Replacing an unstable numerical normalization by an exact compatibility identity.
