# Deletion and Gaussian translation bracket the loss of a winning score

[Family 096](../families/openai-math-096.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000091.md) · [Batch B004](../../batches/B004/README.md)

## Mathematical content

In the source’s optimal partition with the fewest active cells, let $z_1,\ldots,z_m$ be the nonzero Gaussian first moments, $C=\sum_i|z_i|^2$, $X_i=\langle z_i,g\rangle$, $r_i=|z_i|/\sqrt C$, $P_i=\Pr(X_i=\max_jX_j)$ and $\alpha=m/(m-1)$. Under its putative-counterexample reduction, m>=5, the centroids sum to zero, have negative pairwise inner products, and span dimension m-1. The expected loss from deleting score i satisfies

$$C(1-\sqrt{1-\alpha r_i^2})
\le\mathbb E(X_i-\max_{j\ne i}X_j)_+
\le\alpha\phi(q)\bigl(q\Phi(q)+\phi(q)\bigr),\quad q=\Phi^{-1}(P_i).$$

The lower bound deletes z_i, recenters the remaining list and uses the extremal Gaussian-maximum inequality; its remaining squared norm is $C-\alpha|z_i|^2$. For the upper bound, choose h with $\langle h,z_i-z_j\rangle=1$ for every j!=i. The winning cone K_i has gap tail $p_i(u)=\gamma(K_i+uh)$ and derivative $p_i'(0)=-\langle h,z_i\rangle=-1/\alpha$. Ehrhard concavity makes $\Phi^{-1}(p_i(u))$ lie below its tangent at zero. Integrating the resulting Gaussian tail gives the upper bound.

## Role in the source argument

Converts deletion optimality into a lower bound on a cell’s probability relative to its normalized centroid radius. This supplements rearrangement estimates in the finite-dimensional elimination.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Khot–Naor](https://arxiv.org/abs/0807.4626) already provide the Gaussian maximum/partition framework. Ehrhard’s Gaussian Minkowski inequality is an established input; the source derives the particular translating vector and deletion bracket. The separate [three-dimensional propeller theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/1112.2993) is also an input, not newly extracted as an all-dimensional proof.

## Checks and limitations

Checked the recentered norm, the basis needed to solve for h, the sign and normalization of the translated-measure derivative, and the integrated tangent formula. The bundled calculation tests the exact recentering identity and Gaussian-tail integral.

Optimality and the self-consistency between winning cones and their actual first moments are necessary. Arbitrary Gaussian score vectors need not obey this bracket. Checking the bracket does not reproduce the source’s later numerical certificates or prove the global propeller bound.

## Possible applications

Turning removal or pruning operations in Gaussian optimization into quantitative mass constraints by combining a variational lower bound with a translated-set upper bound.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
