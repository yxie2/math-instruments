# Rationalize a convolution input while retaining exact cancellations and moments

[Family 172](../families/openai-math-172.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000249.md) · [Batch B044](../../batches/B044/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let nu:Z^k -> Q have finite support, zero pushforward to each Z^k/Lambda_i, zero mass and first moment, and second moment C. Assume U C U^T=I. For every eta>0 the source constructs an integer n, finitely supported rational mu, and positive rational zeta with the same zero pushforwards, second moment sum mu(lambda)(lambda/n)(lambda/n)^T=zeta C, and sum mu(lambda)_-||U lambda/n||^2<eta zeta.

Use the smoothing lemma to choose b, put d_n(lambda)=n^(2-k)b(lambda/n), and mu_n=nu*d_n. Pushforward commutes with convolution. Exact cancellation of the zeroth and first moments gives second moment (sum d_n)C before scaling, independently of approximation errors. Taylor expansion yields n^k mu_n(lambda)=D_C b(lambda/n)+O(1/n) uniformly on a fixed rescaled support, so weighted negative sums converge to the strict continuum bound.

Fix a sufficiently large n. On its finite support, perturb d_n to rational d. The strict positivity and negative-mass bounds survive by continuity. Setting mu=nu*d retains all cancellation and moment identities exactly. Directly rounding the entries of mu would not have this guarantee.

## Role in the source argument

Turns analytic smoothing into finite rational multiplicities used to form coordinate-permuted configurations.

## Antecedents and comparison

Classical exact convolution-moment identities plus rational density in a finite-dimensional parameter space.

## Checks and limitations

One hundred rational lattice convolutions verify total and first moments, second-moment scaling and every tested quotient class cancellation.

The finite algebra checks do not establish the asymptotic negative-part estimate or the initial tensor-to-stencil construction.

## Possible applications

Converting approximate analytic constructions into rational data under exact linear constraints.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
