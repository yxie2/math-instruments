# Turn bounded Hartogs coordinates into a forbidden holomorphic real-part bound

[Family 359](../families/openai-math-359.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000381.md) · [Batch B063](../../batches/B063/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let B be the unit ball in C² and M_phi={(z,w):z in B, exp(phi(z))|w|²<1}. Assume no holomorphic H on B and constant C_H satisfy Re H(z)<=C_H+4log(1/(1-|z|))+phi(z). Then no bounded holomorphic F:M_phi->C³ has a nowhere-zero complex Jacobian.

If all components are bounded by B0>=1, restrict F to the zero section. Cauchy estimates in base coordinate disks of radius delta=1-|z| give |partial_(z_j)F_l(z,0)|<=B0/delta. The fiber radius is exp(-phi(z)/2), so |partial_w F_l(z,0)|<=B0 exp(phi(z)/2). The six determinant terms imply |d(z)|<=6B0³ delta^(-2)exp(phi(z)/2), where d is the Jacobian on the zero section. If d never vanishes, simple connectedness supplies a holomorphic logarithm ell. Then H=2ell has Re H=log|d|²<=2log6+6logB0+4log(1/delta)+phi, a contradiction.

The base estimate uses only the zero section and needs no neighboring uniform fiber radius. The conclusion rules out bounded coordinates and hence a biholomorphism to a bounded domain. It does not assert that a bounded map's determinant is identically zero or that every bounded holomorphic function is constant: the two base coordinates are bounded nonconstant functions.

## Role in the source argument

Separates an analytic no-minorant construction from the geometric metric-transfer problem.

## Antecedents and comparison

The proof combines classical Cauchy estimates, the determinant expansion and a holomorphic logarithm. Targeted searching did not establish an external source for this exact Hartogs interface. The no-minorant potential and negative metric construction are separate source claims.

## Checks and limitations

Random derivative matrices with the prescribed column bounds check the determinant and logarithmic exponents. Explicit bounded polynomial maps on a constant-radius product domain check the Cauchy estimates and illustrate that such a domain does not satisfy the no-minorant hypothesis.

These calculations do not construct the obstructing potential or prove completeness or curvature pinching.

## Possible applications

Converting the absence of a holomorphic minorant into a global obstruction to bounded local coordinates.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
