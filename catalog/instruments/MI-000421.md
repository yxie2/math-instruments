# Detect polynomial tensors by finitely many scalar evaluations without discarding coefficients

[Family 053](../families/openai-math-053.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000229.md) · [Batch B039](../../batches/B039/README.md)

## Mathematical content

After the source pure-slot projection, the odd permutation sign of the standard symplectic representation cancels the explicit sign twist. The resulting color tensors inject into B tensor Sym^k(W_ord tensor U). Symplectic duality interprets these as homogeneous degree-k polynomials on Hom_Q(U,W), with coefficients in B.

There are finitely many rational scalar points whose evaluation map on the finite-dimensional scalar polynomial space has a rational left inverse. Tensor that left inverse with any coefficient vector space B. Evaluations are jointly injective even when B is infinite dimensional or has nilpotents. No map B -> Q is applied.

The same argument works modulo classes supported in a specified codimension: use the finitely many evaluation points and take the union of their supports, which preserves the codimension lower bound. Exactness of finite-group coinvariants over Q justifies passage through the symmetrization step.

## Role in the source argument

Turns a high-arity tensor identity into finitely many coefficient-valued tests without invoking a faithful cohomological realization.

## Antecedents and comparison

Classical polarization and finite interpolation; the useful source interface is preservation of the coefficient algebra and of the support quotient.

## Checks and limitations

Seven exact rational left-inverse matrices, for two or three scalar variables, recover four independent exterior-algebra coefficient coordinates including nilpotents.

These are polynomial linear-algebra tests, not computations of the source Chow groups or their support filtration.

## Possible applications

Finite detection of tensor relations with nonreduced coefficients or support conditions.
