# Turn an invariant product kernel into a finite-dimensional spectral obstruction

[Family 150](../families/openai-math-150.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000479.md) · [Batch B076](../../batches/B076/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let a measure-preserving flow on a standard probability space induce a strongly continuous unitary group U_t on complex L2. Assume every flow eigenfunction is constant. Frequency zero first gives ergodicity. If the product flow had a nonzero mean-zero invariant L2 kernel K, both its one-variable marginals would vanish.

The complex-linear operator Th(x)=integral K(x,y)h(y) dmu(y) is nonzero and Hilbert-Schmidt, annihilates constants and maps into L2_0. Product invariance gives U_t T=T U_t. Therefore T* T is a nonzero positive compact operator on L2_0 commuting with the group. A positive eigenvalue supplies a finite-dimensional invariant eigenspace. Commuting unitaries there have a common eigenvector; strong continuity makes its scalar character exp(i lambda t). This contradicts the hypothesis.

The compactness step is indispensable: merely producing an arbitrary bounded commuting operator would not produce a finite-dimensional invariant space.

## Role in the source argument

Converts exclusion of individual eigenfunctions into ergodicity of the product flow.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Peterson, Lecture notes on ergodic theory](https://math.vanderbilt.edu/~peters10/teaching/Spring2011/ErgodicTheoryNotes.pdf), the proof of Proposition 1.5.6 on page 18 and Proposition 1.7.5 on pages 23-24, were read. They give the established Hilbert-Schmidt/finite-dimensional obstruction. This is a classical spectral mechanism, not a new weak-mixing criterion. This retrieved edition has different numbering from the source bibliography.

## Checks and limitations

Finite cyclic actions check zero marginals, operator commutation, the nonzero positive spectrum and invariant eigenspaces for product kernels.

The tests model the obstruction, not an example of a nontrivial finite weakly mixing flow. They do not certify any billiard flow.

## Possible applications

Auditing the spectral conclusion after a problem-specific eigenfunction exclusion.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
