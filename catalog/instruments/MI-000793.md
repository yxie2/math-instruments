# Pull back convergence in measure through a stable almost-everywhere submersion

[Family 363](../families/openai-math-363.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000523.md) · [Batch B086](../../batches/B086/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let Phi_n->Phi in C^1_loc on an open subset of R^k, k>=d, and rank DPhi=d almost everywhere. Suppose a_n are arbitrary measurable densities supported on a fixed compact K, |a_n|<=A. If F_n are supported on a fixed compact B in R^d, |F_n|<=M and F_n->0 in measure, then integral |F_n(Phi_n(q))a_n(q)|dq->0.

Boundedness on B gives ||F_n||_1->0. Remove an open neighborhood of the critical set in K of arbitrarily small measure. At every remaining point choose a nonzero d-by-d minor and append the unused coordinates to Phi. On a sufficiently small convex ball, C^1 convergence makes the augmented Phi_n injective with a uniform positive Jacobian bound. Finitely many such charts cover the remaining compact set, and change of variables bounds each integral by a constant times ||F_n||_1. The deleted set costs at most AM times its measure.

No continuity or convergence of a_n is used; moving measurable restrictions can be included in those densities.

## Role in the source argument

Passing smallness through nonlinear parameter maps without demanding convergence of every moving cutoff.

## Antecedents and comparison

This is a classical regular-chart/coarea compactness mechanism. Its usefulness here is robustness under varying collision maps and arbitrary bounded masks.

## Checks and limitations

Exact shrinking-interval examples under perturbed full-rank maps verify decay with oscillating masks. Constant maps demonstrate failure when the rank assumption is removed; unbounded concentrated densities demonstrate why the common bound matters.

C^0 convergence alone supplies no uniform Jacobian control. The source full-rank collision-map assertion is a separate theorem and is not checked by these examples.

## Possible applications

Passing smallness through nonlinear parameter maps without demanding convergence of every moving cutoff.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
