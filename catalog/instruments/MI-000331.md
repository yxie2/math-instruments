# Close a Hessian equation on the gradient and scalar trace ambiguity

[Family 372](../families/openai-math-372.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000184.md) · [Batch B028](../../batches/B028/README.md)

## Mathematical content

On a connected open U in R^n, n>=2, suppose smooth real or complex functions satisfy eta_ij=sum_a B_ij^a eta_a+delta_ij q. Put v=grad(eta). For each s choose one i different from s, without summing over i. Commuting eta_iis and eta_sii gives

$$\partial_s q=\partial_i\sum_a B_{si}^a v_a-\partial_s\sum_a B_{ii}^a v_a.$$

Substitute the original equation for every derivative of v. This produces a closed homogeneous first-order system

$$\partial_s v_a=\sum_b B_{as}^b v_b+\delta_{as}q,\qquad \partial_s q=\sum_b C_s^b v_b+d_s q,$$

where C_s^b=partial_i B_si^b-partial_s B_ii^b+sum_a(B_si^a B_ai^b-B_ii^a B_as^b) and d_s=B_si^i-B_ii^s.

If eta vanishes on a nonempty open subset, then v=q=0 there. Along every piecewise smooth path the system becomes a homogeneous linear ODE, so zero initial data propagate. Connected open subsets of Euclidean space are path connected; hence eta=q=0 throughout U. The ability to choose i different from s is essential. In dimension one, eta''=q with arbitrary smooth eta provides no such uniqueness.

## Role in the source argument

Removes a scalar Hessian ambiguity after equality of restricted transport blocks has been established.

## Antecedents and comparison

This is a finite-type prolongation followed by ordinary differential equation uniqueness. The useful feature is the explicit closed system and the absence of a separate analytic unique-continuation theorem.

## Checks and limitations

Two hundred forty exact coefficient closures and a symbolic variable-coefficient solution verify the displayed formulas.

The check does not establish that the elasticity comparison satisfies this Hessian relation; that is an additional source argument outside this audit.

## Possible applications

Uniqueness for overdetermined scalar equations whose Hessian is controlled by first derivatives and one scalar trace term.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
