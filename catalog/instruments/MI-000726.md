# Glue conditional order couplings without changing the quenched reference marginal

[Family 218](../families/openai-math-218.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000490.md) · [Batch B078](../../batches/B078/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let lambda be a deterministic law, mu_omega an environment-dependent law, and U,W their vectors in {0,1}^m. Let p and q_omega be laws of a bit e and a vector v, with p(e=1)>=c>0. Suppose law_lambda(U) is stochastically below p(v|e=1), and q_omega(v|e=1) is below law_mu_omega(W) whenever that conditioning exists. There is a measurable coupling pi_omega of lambda and mu_omega such that
E pi_omega(U not<=W) <= (6/c) E TV(p,q_omega).

Write d_omega=TV(p,q_omega). On d_omega<=c/2, q_omega(e=1)>=c/2 and conditional TV is at most 4 d_omega/c. Glue a lower order coupling, a maximal middle coupling, and an upper order coupling, then extend from vectors to full samples by their conditional laws. On the remaining environments use any coupling with the same marginals. Markov's inequality bounds their probability by 2 E d_omega/c. A deterministic choice among feasible finite coupling matrices gives measurability; null fibers can be assigned arbitrarily.

The first marginal is lambda separately for every omega. Replacing this requirement by equality after averaging over omega would not give a quenched comparison.

## Role in the source argument

Transfers finite conditional estimates to full samples while preserving the comparison law in each environment.

## Antecedents and comparison

The proof assembles standard order and maximal couplings with a good/bad environment split. No new general coupling theorem is claimed.

## Checks and limitations

Finite linear programs construct optimal order couplings for several environment laws, check both marginals in each environment and the stated expected failure bound, including environments where the conditioning event has zero probability.

Finite tests do not establish the source Ising stochastic comparisons or any interface convergence.

## Possible applications

Keeping annealed and quenched conclusions distinct in random-medium comparison arguments.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
