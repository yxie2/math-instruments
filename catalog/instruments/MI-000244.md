# A greedy prefix protects an exact Egyptian-fraction denominator

[Family 025](../families/openai-math-025.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000142.md) · [Batch B017](../../batches/B017/README.md)

## Mathematical content

For integers m>=4 and T>=2m^2, start with unreduced (R,q)=(m-1,m). While R>0 and q<T, put a=ceil(q/R), choose n=a unless a=m, in which case choose n=m+1, and update (R,q)=(nR-q,nq). This yields

$$1=1/m+\sum_{i=1}^j1/n_i+R/q,\qquad q=m\prod_i n_i,$$

with distinct increasing n_i>=2, none equal to m, 0<=R<2m, q<T^2, and j<3+log2(log2 T). If R>0 then q>=T and R/q<2m/T<=1/m and R/q<1/n_i for every prefix term. Therefore every positive unit fraction in any expansion of the remainder has a denominator different from the marker and all prefix denominators.

At ordinary steps R strictly decreases and the remainder is less than its square. The skipped-marker step can occur only once, increases R by less than a factor two and still leaves a remainder below the new reciprocal. After the first step the remainder is below 1/2, giving doubly exponential decay apart from that one exception. Not reducing q preserves the product structure. Continuing without the cutoff terminates because the integer R decreases except at that single step, proving existence for every m; for m=2,3 use {2,3,6}. A short tail still requires the separate uniform short-expansion theorem.

The established padding lemma increases the length of a distinct expansion of 1 by exactly one while retaining any chosen denominator m. Split the largest unmarked denominator b as 1/(b+1)+1/(b(b+1)). If the marked maximum blocks that split of the second largest s, a factorization s=ab gives 1/(s+a)+1/(b(s+a)); the exceptional prime case is excluded by reducing the total-sum identity modulo s. This padding need not be an injection on whole expansions.

## Role in the source argument

Keeps an exact marked denominator through tail completion and subsequent increases of the prescribed length.

## Antecedents and comparison

The padding statement is already [van Doorn-Tang, Lemma 2.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.22083v2), not a release novelty. Greedy subtraction is classical. The selected candidate interface is the explicit skipped-marker prefix with an unreduced denominator and a strict remainder gap.

## Checks and limitations

Read both proofs. Exact arithmetic tests the skipped step, prefix bounds and protected tail, and enumerated distinct expansions test padding for every possible marked denominator, including a marked maximum.

Preserving a multiple of m would not suffice here. The prefix alone supplies no O(log log m) bound for an arbitrary tail. Padding preserves a marker, not necessarily all previous denominators.

## Possible applications

Adding exact denominator constraints without allowing a later cleanup or padding step to erase the prescribed term.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
