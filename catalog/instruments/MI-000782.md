# Balance an atomless measure using an inward-pointing parameter cylinder

[Family 337](../families/openai-math-337.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000518.md) · [Batch B085](../../batches/B085/README.md)

## Mathematical content

On a complete simply connected manifold with Sec<=-b^2, b=0 or 1, let mu be an atomless probability measure supported compactly inside B_D(o). For r=d(p,x), theta=exp_p^(-1)(x)/r and z=a sn_b(r/2)/cs_b(r/2), define Phi_(p,a)=(2z theta/(1+z^2),(1-z^2)/(1+z^2)). At p use the north pole. There are p in B_D(o) and finite a>0 with integral Phi_(p,a) dmu=0.

Transport the vector integral to T_o M and write p=exp_o xi. On |xi|=D, convexity of distance to o makes its horizontal component strictly inward. At a sufficiently small lower scale its last component is positive. Atomlessness gives uniform small-ball mass on the compact set of centers: otherwise shrinking balls with a fixed positive mass and converging centers create an atom. Choose delta with all such masses below 1/4, and a large finite upper scale with a t_b(delta)>=sqrt(3). The last component is then at most -1/8.

Brouwer applied to the projection of zeta+F(zeta) onto the cylinder gives a fixed point. The projection inequality and each inward face condition exclude the boundary, including corners. An interior fixed point has F=0.

## Role in the source argument

Selecting test functions with zero weighted mean while keeping the map center and scale finite.

## Antecedents and comparison

Classical spherical centering, made explicit through a two-parameter cylinder. The source relates it to Li-Yau conformal centering; that original paper was not independently read in this batch.

## Checks and limitations

Continuous circular probability measures check the scale and inward face signs by high-order quadrature. An atom of mass greater than one half demonstrates why universal balancing cannot ignore atomlessness.

Numerical quadrature does not prove Brouwer or the curved-space comparison. This lemma alone gives no isoperimetric inequality.

Selecting test functions with zero weighted mean while keeping the map center and scale finite.
