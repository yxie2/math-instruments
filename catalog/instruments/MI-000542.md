# Solve contact-length variations by separating row, column and height coordinates

[Family 275](../families/openai-math-275.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000311.md) · [Batch B055](../../batches/B055/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Place original square-lattice vertices at spacing two. On each oriented edge use local coordinates (along-axis,height), with physical height sign sigma=1 on horizontal edges and -1 on vertical edges. A negative gadget has A=(1,0), B=(1,1); a positive gadget has A=(1/2,h), B=(3/2,h), h=sqrt(3)/2. Its unit contacts are LA,AR,AB in the negative case and LA,AB,BR in the positive case, plus BA contacts between consecutive positive gadgets. Every other pair is at least sqrt(2) apart.

For arbitrary desired first-order contact changes d_ij, the source constructs displacements with u_ij.(s_j-s_i)=d_ij and max|s_i|<=6m max|d_ij|, where m is the total site count. Set all original heights to zero. On each run of negative edges accumulate a_R=a_L+d_LA+d_AR, a_A=a_L+d_LA, a_B=a_A and z_B=sigma d_AB. On each path of positive elevated contacts accumulate their along-axis differences. Then set z_A=[d_LA-(a_A-a_L)/2]/(sigma h) and z_B=[d_BR-(a_R-a_B)/2]/(sigma h).

Rows set original x coordinates, columns set original y coordinates, and no ancilla belongs to both procedures. Thus lattice cycles create no scalar consistency condition. Every assignment is linear over Q(sqrt(3)) with polynomially many arithmetic operations. Rational rounding with per-site error at most tau/4 and input errors at most tau/4 gives contact errors below tau.

At base scale D and displacement bound S<=D/4, an actual contact length is D+d_ij+O(S^2/D), while every noncontact stays at least sqrt(2)D-2S apart. The construction solves the linearized constraints; it does not solve an exact nonlinear prescribed-length problem.

## Role in the source argument

Allows independent hopping calibration in the source Coulomb reduction without an ill-conditioned general linear solve.

## Antecedents and comparison

The first-order formula is the classical rigidity-matrix derivative of Euclidean edge length. The source explicit row/column/height elimination and its complete contact classification were read. A targeted search did not identify an earlier matching gadget right inverse, but that does not establish novelty of the construction or the full reduction.

## Checks and limitations

Finite signed square-lattice layouts enumerate every pair distance and every contact, solve random contact assignments by the displayed construction, and check displacement bounds and nonlinear length remainders, including square cycles and extra positive-gadget contacts.

The checks do not establish the source hopping asymptotics, continuum encoding size or QMA-hardness.

## Possible applications

Designing geometric interaction gadgets whose first-order edge lengths can be adjusted independently with explicit conditioning bounds.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
