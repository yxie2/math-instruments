# Track geometric component counts through the constant field

[Family 034](../families/openai-math-034.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000412.md) · [Batch B067](../../batches/B067/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Work over a characteristic-zero field k. For an integral k-variety V, let k_V be the relative algebraic closure of k in k(V). Then [k_V:k] equals the number of geometric irreducible components, which form one Galois orbit, and is birationally invariant. If V is proper and normal, H^0(V,O_V)=k_V: properness makes global functions algebraic over k, while an algebraic element of the function field is integral over every local ring and hence regular by normality.

If V dominates a geometrically integral Z, the regular extension k(Z)/k is linearly disjoint from k_V/k. The compositum k_V k(Z), of degree [k_V:k] over k(Z), lies in the generic fiber's constant field. Therefore the arithmetic component degree cannot decrease on passage to the generic fiber. If X is normal projective with global functions k and X->Z is a contraction to a normal base, both X and Z are geometrically integral, and the normal projective generic fiber has global functions k(Z), by localization of f_*O_X=O_Z. This preserves arithmetic information that would be lost by immediately replacing k by its algebraic closure.

## Role in the source argument

Transfers a bound on a generic-fiber constant extension back to the arithmetic number of geometric components.

## Antecedents and comparison

These are classical facts about regular extensions, linear disjointness, proper global functions and normality. The source packages them for arithmetic Stein-degree induction. No new constant-field theorem is claimed.

## Checks and limitations

Exact irreducible constant-field polynomials remain irreducible over Q(t) in model extensions; tensor base changes and conjugate component factorizations check degree preservation. A non-geometrically-integral base supplies a failure of the disjointness step.

Finite field-extension models do not prove the source uniform Stein-degree bound or boundedness inputs.

## Possible applications

Keeping Galois component counts visible during geometric induction.
