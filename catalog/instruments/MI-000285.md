# A periodic graph without an infinite path has a finite-cell component bound

[Family 028](../families/openai-math-028.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000161.md) · [Batch B022](../../batches/B022/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let a locally finite graph on a subset A of Z^2 have edges determined by a fixed bounded set of steps, and be invariant under translations by Q Z^2. Assume it has no infinite injective path. Every component is finite: an infinite component would give a finitely branching tree of finite simple paths of arbitrarily large length, and hence an infinite branch.

If two distinct points x,y in one component satisfy y-x in Q Z^2, translation by y-x takes that component to another containing y, hence to itself. A nonempty finite subset of Z^2 cannot be invariant under a nonzero translation. Every component therefore injects into (Z/QZ)^2 and has at most Q^2 vertices.

In the source, A avoids finitely many chosen Gaussian prime factors above rational primes p=1 mod 4, and Q is their product. Every Gaussian prime outside this avoiding set is an associate of a selected factor; put those finitely many exceptions in E. If K_D counts the lattice vectors of length at most D, including zero, then each exceptional vertex has at most K_D-1 neighbors. A component meeting E attaches to at most (K_D-1)|E| old components. The Gaussian-prime component bound is consequently

$$\max\{Q^2,\ |E|+(K_D-1)|E|Q^2\}.$$

The arithmetic task is to produce a finite avoiding set with no infinite bounded-step path. That task is retained as a separate premise. Without periodicity, a disjoint union of finite paths of increasing lengths has no infinite path but has no uniform component-size bound.

## Role in the source argument

Converts the finite sieve obstruction into a uniform statement for all starting Gaussian primes, including the removed exceptions.

## Checks and limitations

Read the full proof. Exhaustive period-two and period-three nearest-neighbor lattice patterns are classified by exact lifted-cycle detection; finite components and components formed by adjoining exceptional vertices satisfy the bounds.

These are generic periodic graph examples, not Gaussian-prime obstruction certificates. The main finite sieve theorem remains unaudited. Local finiteness and actual lattice translation invariance are required.

## Possible applications

Upgrading pointwise finiteness to a uniform bound in periodic discrete models, with controlled finite defects.
