# Trap trajectories on a compact maximum plateau of a finite affine minimum

[Family 149](../families/openai-math-149.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000477.md) · [Batch B075](../../batches/B075/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let F=min_lambda L_lambda be a finite minimum of affine functions, globally bounded above by M, and let K={F=M} be nonempty and compact inside the interior of a control region B. Along a differentiable trajectory x'=f(x), its right derivative is min_(lambda active at x) r_lambda dot f(x). Inactive labels have a positive gap at that point, so for a short right interval they cannot affect the minimum; taking the minimum of the active first-order expansions proves the formula.

Assume every active derivative is nonnegative on B. Then F(x(t)) is nondecreasing on each segment staying in B. If, in the relevant invariant class outside K, every active derivative is at least delta>0, F increases there by at least delta times elapsed time. The right-derivative-to-monotonicity step follows by adding a small positive linear function and using its compact-interval maximum.

A trajectory starting in K remains there: monotonicity and F<=M pin its value to M, while K being strictly inside B excludes a first exit. A smooth vector field then has a global continuation because the trajectory stays in a compact subset of its domain. Any global trajectory in the class that eventually stays in B must enter K within the finite budget (M-F(x(t0)))/delta. Without the uniform positive delta, monotonicity alone need not give finite entry.

## Role in the source argument

Turns local active-face inequalities into a global compact trapping and eventual-entry statement.

## Antecedents and comparison

The derivative formula, first-exit argument and compact continuation are classical. The source-specific challenge is constructing the plateau and proving the uniform active-slope hypothesis; those inputs are kept separate here.

## Checks and limitations

Explicit contracting flows test active ties, monotonicity, invariance and entry-time budgets for a compact polyhedral plateau. An asymptotic-only example tests the need for a uniform positive slope bound.

The checks do not establish the source affine-label construction or permanence theorem.

## Possible applications

Organizing a nonsmooth Lyapunov proof around a plateau rather than a single attracting point.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
