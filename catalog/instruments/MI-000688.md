# Cover the plane by squares centered on a strongly sheared lattice

[Family 100](../families/openai-math-100.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000457.md) · [Batch B073](../../batches/B073/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Closed unit squares centered at (i+alpha j,beta i+j), i,j integers, cover the plane when 0<=alpha beta<1. For a target (u1,u2), set R(t)=floor(t+1/2) and F(i)=R(u1-alpha R(u2-beta i)). This integer map is nondecreasing when the shears have the same sign and constant if their product is zero. Since F(i)=alpha beta i+O(1), there are integers i-<i+ with F(i-)>=i- and F(i+)<=i+. Iteration from i- is nondecreasing and remains at most i+, so it reaches a fixed point. Take j=R(u2-beta i); both coordinate errors are at most 1/2.

For a real trace-zero planar matrix A, an orthonormal basis makes both diagonal entries zero: the quadratic form values on perpendicular unit vectors sum to zero, and continuity supplies a zero. Write the off-diagonal entries b,c, so bc=-det A. If -L^-2<det A<=0, then alpha=sb and beta=sc satisfy the covering condition for |s|<=L. This gives square coverings for the affine flow I+sA in one common orientation. The inverse bound ||(I+sA)^-1||<=(1+L||A||)/(1+L^2 det A) controls the relevant centers. Nilpotent A allows every finite time interval, even with large shear.

## Role in the source argument

Supplies a local linear covering with explicit center control for later nonlinear approximation.

## Antecedents and comparison

The fixed-point mechanism and trace-zero basis choice are elementary established tools. The source also cites lattice tilings by cubes; that primary comparison was not independently checked here. No priority claim is made for the covering statement.

## Checks and limitations

Exact rational shears and target points test integer iteration, coverage, negative shears, zero products and unbounded individual shears; examples outside the product hypothesis show holes.

Finite models do not establish the nonlinear cylinder approximation.

## Possible applications

Covering distorted grids without imposing a small norm on the linear map.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
