# Transfer two-sided cohomology vanishing through a trivial extension

[Family 199](../families/openai-math-199.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000316.md) · [Batch B056](../../batches/B056/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let R be finite-dimensional over k, D=Hom_k(-,k), and P a totally acyclic complex of finite projective left R-modules with differentials P_j to P_(j-1). Put Z=coker(P_1 to P_0), and assume Z is nonprojective and H^a Hom_R(P,Z)=0 for a>0 and a<=-2. Then A=R semidirect DR and M=A tensor_R Z give a finite symmetric algebra and a finite nonprojective module with Ext_A^a(M,M)=0 for every a>0.

The essential tensor step is specific to P. Finite-projective evaluation gives I=DR tensor_R P isomorphic to D Hom_R(P,R). Total acyclicity makes I exact, with injective terms. Thus the restriction of A tensor_R P is P direct-sum I, which is exact even though A as a right R-module need not be flat. Induction preserves the finite-projective terms by preserving splittings.

Set N=DR tensor_R Z. Right exactness and exactness of I give an injective coresolution 0 to N to I_(-1) to I_(-2) to ... . Applying Hom_R(Z,-) identifies its degree-a term with D Hom_R(P_(-a-1),Z). Consequently Ext_R^a(Z,N)=D H^(-a-1) Hom_R(P,Z) for a>0. In particular a=1 uses degree -2; degree -1 need not vanish. Adjunction then splits Ext_A^a(M,M) into Ext_R^a(Z,Z) and Ext_R^a(Z,N).

If M were projective, tensoring a split presentation with the quotient A to R would make Z a summand of a finite free R-module. Tensor functors preserve split identity composites without requiring flatness. This contradicts the assumed nonprojectivity.

## Antecedents and comparison

The ingredients are standard finite-projective evaluation, exact vector-space duality and induction-restriction adjunction. The complete source proof was read. The background [Cibils-Marcos-Redondo-Solotar](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0102194) concerns split-algebra Hochschild cohomology; it is not cited as proving this module-transfer proposition. A targeted search did not establish earliest priority for this conditional formulation.

## Checks and limitations

Finite upper-triangular algebra modules test the nondegenerate evaluation pairing. Explicit complexes check the negative degree reversal. A square-zero algebra gives a nonflat dual-module tensor counterexample while split contractible complexes remain exact.

The program does not construct a nonprojective Z satisfying the infinite vanishing hypothesis. That hypothesis is the substantive input to this conditional result.

## Possible applications

Transporting homological constructions through an extension by proving exactness on the needed resolution instead of assuming flatness globally.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
