# Protect entropy against an arbitrarily biased subsequence selector

[Family 170](../families/openai-math-170.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000338.md) · [Batch B058](../../batches/B058/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Suppose a length-N stream law is pointwise at most C_0 times the iid uniform law on an alphabet of size A. A selector, with arbitrary dependent auxiliary randomness, outputs a deterministic-length ell subsequence in increasing order, or the reversed sequence with a fixed bijection applied to its symbols. For any prescribed tuple f, the event that f is output requires its occurrence in one of at most 2 binom(N,ell) position/orientation choices. Therefore

Pr(F=f)<=2 C_0 binom(N,ell) A^(-ell),

and H(F)>=ell log A-log(2 C_0 binom(N,ell)). This remains true even if the selector is chosen adversarially from the entire stream. Conditioning on an event of probability at least c, including an event depending on auxiliary randomness, increases stream domination by at most 1/c.

In the source, A=Q_d Q_(d-1), Q_j=1+q+...+q^j, sigma=log q, N=floor(q^d sigma), and ell is comparable to k=floor(q sigma^(1+eta)). Since log A=(2d-1)sigma+O(1/q) and log(eN/ell)=(d-1)sigma-eta log sigma+O(1), the bound becomes H(F)>=d sigma ell+eta ell log sigma-O(k). The positive logarithmic surplus is what a separate compression argument would have to contradict.

A related direction-sensitive event transfer states Q(E)<=(KL(P||Q)+P(E))/(1-e^(-1)). It follows by inserting f=-1_E into KL(P||Q)>=E_P f-log E_Q exp(f). This is a bound on the reference event mass Q(E) in terms of KL(P||Q), not a license to interchange the two measures.

## Role in the source argument

Keeps a quantitative entropy floor valid after adaptive selection and bounded-probability restrictions.

## Antecedents and comparison

The proof is the classical maximum-atom bound for Shannon entropy plus a union bound over possible selected positions. The rare-event estimate is a direct exponential variational bound for relative entropy. The source contribution at this extraction level is its explicit parameter balance and orientation/conditioning interface; no new general entropy principle is asserted.

## Checks and limitations

Twelve exhaustive small-stream models use the lexicographically largest or smallest subsequence in either orientation, under uniform and nonuniform dominated laws. Atom caps, entropy and auxiliary-dependent conditioning pass. A further 3,200 finite event comparisons check the stated KL direction.

The verification does not implement the source projective flags, geometric support learning or recursive compression, and therefore does not certify a Ramsey lower bound.

## Possible applications

Maintaining entropy lower bounds through data-dependent choices where treating the selected objects as independent would be invalid.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
