# Turn negative moment squares into one-variable upper certificates

[Family 005](../families/openai-math-005.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000240.md) · [Batch B042](../../batches/B042/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Suppose a regularized interaction has negative moment terms -kappa sum A_k^2/k-(1/2) sum(B_k-2C_k)^2/k. For freely chosen trial sequences p,v, the inequality -a^2<=b^2-2ab gives the upper bound

$$\kappa\sum_k\frac{p_k^2}{k}+\frac12\sum_k\frac{v_k^2}{k}
-2\kappa\sum_k\frac{p_kA_k}{k}-\sum_k\frac{v_kB_k}{k}+2\sum_k\frac{v_kC_k}{k}.$$

If A,B,C are empirical moments, the linear terms separate into one-variable averages. Add the external fields and bound each average by its one-variable supremum. Every admissible trial sequence gives a valid upper certificate; it need not be an optimizer.

In the source, p,v decay geometrically. Removing empirical diagonals adds O_epsilon(1/n) and endpoint-log corrections. The retained endpoint coefficients stay positive for n>=24, forcing uniform decay to minus infinity at singular endpoints. This confines maximizers to common compact sets. Trial-series damping errors are bounded by epsilon times their l1 norms. Take n -> infinity first, then epsilon -> 0; reversing these limits would not remove O_epsilon(1/n). A nonnegative multiplier for a separately proved moment constraint can also be added, with its finite-size cost retained.

## Role in the source argument

Reduces the source many-particle determinant majorant to explicit trial functions and one-dimensional supremum bounds.
