# Transfer a bounded-ball L2 modulus through repeated tracial ultrapowers

[Family 295](../families/openai-math-295.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000208.md) · [Batch B034](../../batches/B034/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let (N,rho) be a finite von Neumann algebra with faithful normal tracial state, and R:N->N a bounded linear map. Assume every operator-norm-bounded sequence x_j with ||x_j||_2->0 also has ||R(x_j)||_2->0. This is equivalent to a uniform modulus on each operator-norm ball: for every r and epsilon, some delta satisfies ||x||<=r and ||x||_2<delta implies ||Rx||_2<epsilon. Failure would produce a contradicting sequence with ||x_j||_2<1/j.

The coordinate map therefore preserves the L2-null ideal and induces R^omega on the tracial ultrapower, with norm at most ||R||. To transfer continuity on the radius-r quotient ball, use a representative bounded by r+1, choose the original modulus at radius r+1 and output tolerance epsilon/2, and require quotient L2 norm less than delta/2. An ultrafilter-large set then satisfies the original delta bound.

The argument repeats for a second ultrapower and uses no separability. It does not require a global L2-Lipschitz constant, but it does require the bounded-ball modulus.

## Role in the source argument

Makes the same linear map well-defined and continuous after both ultrapower passages in the source rigidity argument.

## Antecedents and comparison

This is standard uniform continuity and ideal-preserving quotient descent. The radius and tolerance slack makes the repeated use explicit.

## Checks and limitations

Two hundred forty finite matrix-sequence examples verify uniform L2 bounds for two-sided multiplication maps.

No finite test simulates a free ultrafilter or proves the general descent statement; the general selected proof was read.

## Possible applications

Transferring norm-bounded linear maps through tracial asymptotic constructions without assuming separability.
