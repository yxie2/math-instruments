# Balance restriction slopes with a bounded monotone average

[Family 195](../families/openai-math-195.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000287.md) · [Batch B052](../../batches/B052/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let X be a smooth integral projective complex surface that is an effective Cartier divisor in an integral Noetherian scheme W, with conormal J|X=O_X(H) ample. Let E be coherent torsion-free and P=E|X torsion-free of rank r>0. Normalize slope by mu(B)=c1(B).H/(rank(B)H^2), so tensoring by O_X(H) raises slope by one.

When the largest and smallest Harder-Narasimhan slopes differ by more than one, take the last semistable quotient G of P, put B=ker(P->G), and replace E by ker(E->i_*G). The Cartier Tor sequence gives 0->G(H)->E'|X->B->0. The new restriction is torsion-free, and E' agrees with E off X. Its maximum slope cannot exceed the old maximum, while its average rises by rank(G)/r>=1/r. Since average never exceeds maximum, this process terminates with slope width at most one. The bound uses the initial maximum minus initial average, not a claim that every slope moves monotonically.

The value of width control is a mixture identity. For positive rank weights w_i summing to one, normalized first characters m_i and variances v_i=2q_i-m_i^2, the extension variance satisfies v=sum w_i v_i+sum w_i(m_i-mbar)^2. If the m_i differ from the balanced slopes by one common shift, their variance is at most 1/4. Thus a common upper bound v_i<=C gives v<=C+1/4, without requiring P itself to be semistable.

## Role in the source argument

Provides the narrow slope interval needed to compare the source upper and lower Chern-character bounds.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Chen-Sun, Algebraic tangent cones of reflexive sheaves](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1808.02172), Theorem 1.4(I) and the exact modification sequence in the proof of Lemma 3.2, give optimal extensions by Hecke transforms; these passages were read. Their analytic reflexive-sheaf setting differs from the selected Noetherian Cartier-divisor lemma. The retrieved version calls the sequence antecedent Corollary 2.6.

## Checks and limitations

Exact weighted slope models verify monotonicity of the maximum, the increase in mean, finite termination and the variance-mixture identity. These models check the numerical termination mechanism, not the existence of sheaf modifications.

The finite models do not verify the cone extension, Bogomolov hypotheses or the claimed counterexample.

## Possible applications

Converting a bounded spread of semistable factors into a quantitative bound for a possibly unstable extension.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
