# Use a convex flux defect instead of substituting a mean pointwise

[Family 354](../families/openai-math-354.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000190.md) · [Batch B029](../../batches/B029/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let 0<alpha<=1 vary along a slice curve of perimeter p, with arclength mean m=hv/p. Fix lambda=49/50 and g(X)=(1-lambda X^2)/sqrt(1-X^2). Its minimum is 7/25 at D=sqrt(48)/7. Define G(X)=g(min(X,D)) for all X>=0. This is nonincreasing, takes values in [7/25,1], and is a minorant of g on [0,1).

For J in [0,1] set f_J(X)=1/X-lambda X-J sqrt(1-X^2)/X. Then

$$X^3 f_J''(X)=2-J\frac{2-3X^2}{(1-X^2)^{3/2}}\ge0.$$

Take J=G(m). The definition gives f_J(m)>=0, including m=1. Jensen with normalized arclength yields

$$\int\frac{d\ell}{\alpha}-\lambda hv\ge G(hv/p)\int\frac{\sqrt{1-\alpha^2}}{\alpha}\,d\ell.$$

A perimeter lower bound p_0<=p can now be substituted safely: hv/p_0>=hv/p and G is nonincreasing, so it only weakens the lower bound. G remains defined even if the substituted argument exceeds one. Under the source nested-slice coarea hypotheses, the inequality integrates in slice area to A>=lambda h V+int_0^s gamma(v)dv whenever gamma(v)<=G(hv/p).

Directly substituting the mean into a pointwise ratio is unjustified; the convex defect is the reason the averaged inequality holds.

## Role in the source argument

Combines flux information and perimeter lower bounds in the source cylinder-slab transition estimate.

## Antecedents and comparison

This is an explicit application of Jensen with a carefully chosen monotone minorant. Its numerical parameters belong to the source estimate; the averaging principle is classical.

## Checks and limitations

Four hundred weighted slice models pass, and the relevant scalar derivative is symbolic. A two-value control shows that pointwise substitution of the mean can fail even though the integrated inequality holds.

The source geometric flux, nesting and coarea hypotheses are not verified by the scalar tests.

## Possible applications

Converting moment constraints into integral estimates when pointwise substitution and monotone comparison would otherwise be invalid.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
