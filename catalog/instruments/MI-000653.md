# Force hyperkahler rotation by a vanishing restricted class

[Family 041](../families/openai-math-041.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000422.md) · [Batch B068](../../batches/B068/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let Y be hyperkahler of real dimension 4n with complex structures I,J,K, and T a compact connected oriented embedded I-special-Lagrangian submanifold. If the restriction of [omega_K] to T vanishes, then T is J-complex and holomorphic Lagrangian for omega_K+i omega_I.

The I-holomorphic volume form is (omega_J+i omega_K)^n/n!. Special Lagrangian calibration makes its period's absolute value equal to Vol(T). Since omega_K restricts to an exact form, Stokes removes every term involving it, leaving Vol(T)=|integral_T omega_J^n/n!|. Wirtinger's inequality bounds this absolute period by the integral of its pointwise absolute density, which is at most Vol(T). All inequalities are equalities. The continuous nonnegative deficit vanishes everywhere, so all tangent planes are J-invariant. The I-Lagrangian condition then also gives omega_K|T=0 by the quaternion identities.

If T is diffeomorphic to a real 2n-torus, it is a complex torus. Its Kahler Albanese map induces an integral H^1 isomorphism, hence all cohomology isomorphisms by the torus exterior ring and has degree one. Every Kahler class is pulled back from the Albanese; a positive-dimensional fiber would have zero Kahler volume. Thus the map is finite birational to a normal torus and is an isomorphism.

## Role in the source argument

Converts a topological vanishing condition on an existing special Lagrangian into a holomorphic Lagrangian torus.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Harvey and Lawson, Calibrated geometries](https://archive.ymsc.tsinghua.edu.cn/pacm_download/117/6313-11511_2006_Article_BF02392726.pdf), Chapter II section 6 (printed pages 70-71) gives the complex-plane equality characterization, and Chapter III Theorem 1.10 (page 89) gives special-Lagrangian calibration. These statements and proofs were read. The selected source combines them with Stokes and Albanese arguments.

## Checks and limitations

Exact quaternionic plane models check the calibration inequality, equality for J-complex planes and special-Lagrangian examples that fail rotation when the omega_K condition is removed.

The finite models do not prove existence of a compact input submanifold, the cohomological hypothesis, the Albanese theorem or SYZ.

## Possible applications

Turning cohomological information about a calibrated torus into a change of complex structure.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
