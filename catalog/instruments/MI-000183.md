# Scalar packing encodings retain a bounded defect budget through conservation

[Family 118](../families/openai-math-118.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000112.md) · [Batch B010](../../batches/B010/README.md)

## Mathematical content

The source uses five item roles X,A,U,D,F and thirteen subclasses. Ten homogeneous integer count equations characterize four intended five-item subclass patterns. Each item's coefficients delta_j have absolute value at most four. Set p(i)=sum_{j=0}^9 100^j delta_j(i), with |p(i)|<P0. For a five-item tuple, p-sum zero is equivalent to all ten equations: each aggregate digit has absolute value at most twenty, so reduction modulo 100 and division recover every zero digit.

For labels v=1,...,n put Q_v=3^v and q(i)=Q_v on X,D, q(i)=-2Q_v on A, and zero otherwise. With |w(i)|<=6 define

$$a_i=\tfrac15+\gamma p(i)+\mu q(i)+\nu w(i),\quad
\gamma=(1000P_0)^{-1},\quad\mu=\gamma/(1000Q_n),\quad\nu=\mu/1000.$$

All sizes exceed 1/6 and are below 7/30. Thus bins have at most five items. For a feasible five-item bin the integral primary sum is nonpositive; if it vanishes, the secondary sum is nonpositive; if both vanish, feasibility is exactly sum w(i)<=0. Smaller scales cannot reverse a nonzero integral score. Negative primary sums can be feasible and must not be silently excluded.

Suppose there are 5B items, total primary and secondary scores zero, and a packing uses at most B+c bins. At most 5c bins are nonfull, containing at most 25c items. Their total primary score can compensate for at most 25cP0 full bins with negative primary score. Hence at most K0=25c(1+5P0) items lie outside zero-primary table tuples.

In a table tuple, Q_X+Q_D<=2Q_A forces v_X,v_D<=v_A; equality of the score forces all three labels equal. Equal per-label inventories for X,A,D then give, for every prefix S={1,...,h}, E_r(S)=O_A(S)-O_r(S), r=X,D. Here E counts table tuples with the r-label in S but anchor outside S, and O counts items outside table tuples. If at most K items are outside, E_r(S)<=K. For each fixed anchor label, at most 2K tuples have a smaller X or D label; adding at most K outside anchors gives a 3K bad-anchor bound. This is per label, not a claim of only 3K defects globally.

## Role in the source argument

Allows a small additive bin surplus to create only a controlled number of unusable resources, despite feasible bins with unintended patterns.

## Antecedents and comparison

Large-base encoding is classical; [Jansen-Pirotton-Tutas, The Support of Bin Packing Is Exponential](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.ESA.2025.48) uses such encodings in bin packing. The selected package makes the conservation and prefix-defect estimates explicit. It is not a claim that scalar feasibility forces every bin to be an intended tuple.

## Checks and limitations

Enumerated all thirteen-subclass five-item multisets, checked the four zero-score patterns, and tested rational scale separation and label inequalities. Finite monotone label matchings check the prefix conservation identity and per-label defect bound.

The defect argument requires the balanced inventory and zero total primary score. Good tuples and a small defect budget alone do not prove the LP gap or NP-hardness; those require the geometric and soundness constructions.

## Possible applications

Robust numerical encodings of combinatorial constraints when near-optimal packings may contain a bounded number of malformed bins.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
