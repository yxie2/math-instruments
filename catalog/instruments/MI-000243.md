# A common comparison forces uniform phase limits without phase continuity

[Family 024](../families/openai-math-024.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000141.md) · [Batch B017](../../batches/B017/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let theta(x) take values in a phase set S, with every s attained along some sequence x_n -> infinity. Suppose A_H:S -> R and a single comparison C(x), independent of H, satisfy

$$\limsup_{x\to\infty}|C(x)-A_H(\theta(x))|\le\zeta_H,\qquad\zeta_H\to0.$$

Then |A_H(s)-A_K(s)|<=zeta_H+zeta_K for every s, by comparing both approximants with C along the same exact-phase sequence. Hence A_H converges uniformly to A, with sup_s|A_H(s)-A(s)|<=zeta_H, and C(x)=A(theta(x))+o(1). Eventual lower and upper bounds on C pass to A at every phase. No continuity of any A_H or A is needed.

In the source, the comparison is first made to a normalized arithmetic count M_f(x;H)/G_m, with error eta_H, and the finite-coefficient approximation adds epsilon_H; thus zeta_H=eta_H+epsilon_H. The phase is the fractional part of (log B-log log B)/lambda, B=log log x, lambda>0. This expression is continuous, strictly increasing for B>e and unbounded. Solving it equal to n+s gives exact phase s at arbitrarily large x.

The common quantity C must not depend on H. Dense phase visits alone also do not replace exact visits for discontinuous coefficients: values at a never-visited phase may be changed arbitrarily without altering the comparison. The source proves regular variation by a separate fixed-scaling count, not by assuming its limit coefficient is continuous.

## Role in the source argument

Establishes a well-defined arithmetic coefficient without defining that coefficient in terms of the unknown asymptotic count.

## Antecedents and comparison

The proof is the standard uniform Cauchy criterion and a triangle inequality on exact subsequences. The source-specific feature is the discontinuous arithmetic phase parameter and the use of one comparison independent of the truncation depth.

## Checks and limitations

Read the complete argument. Explicit discontinuous phase functions check the common-comparison inequalities and uniform error bound. Controls with an H-dependent comparison or an unvisited exceptional phase fail the required implication.

The finite arithmetic approximation and its vanishing errors are separate analytic inputs. Finite examples illustrate the logic, not the asserted totient asymptotic.

## Possible applications

Constructing phase-dependent asymptotic coefficients when continuity is unavailable but each phase can be realized exactly.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
