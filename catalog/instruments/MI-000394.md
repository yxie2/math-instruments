# Turn near-integral samples into exact repeated-root divisibility

[Family 164](../families/openai-math-164.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000216.md) · [Batch B036](../../batches/B036/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Fix e>=1, q>=e, A>0 and H>=2q+2. Let t_z=t_0+mz lie in [S,2S), with m w-smooth and every t_z coprime to W=product_{p<=w}p. Suppose w,S tend to infinity, Z dominates every fixed power of S, deg(t^e theta)<=q, and ||t_z^e theta(z)||_{R/Z}<=A(S/Z)^e for z=0,...,H-1.

Interpolate the nearest integers at q+1 well-spaced sample nodes by R. Its coefficients have a common fixed denominator D_H, and interpolation bounds give |R-t^e theta|<=C_q A(S/Z)^e throughout the fixed interval. At a=-t_0/m, each derivative R^(j)(a), j<e, is tiny. If nonzero it has absolute value at least 1/(D_H m^q). The scale separation forces all these derivatives to vanish exactly, so R=t^e M with rational deg M<=q-e.

All denominator primes of M divide D_H m and are eventually at most w. Also R(z) is integer: it lies on a fixed rational mesh and is too close to an integer to differ. Since t_z is coprime to all those denominator primes, M(z) is integer. Division by t_z^e gives |theta(z)-M(z)|<=C_q A Z^{-e}. The threshold can depend on H although C_q does not.

## Role in the source argument

Upgrades approximate phase integrality to exact integer-valued polynomial structure on rough progressions.
