# Recover the square of a pair interaction from its low-particle restrictions

[Family 269](../families/openai-math-269.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000506.md) · [Batch B082](../../batches/B082/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let V be a subspace of the two-particle exterior space, with orthonormal basis v_p. If B(v) is the adjoint of left wedge multiplication, define L_k(|v><w|)=B(v)^*B(w) and H=L_2(P_V)=sum_p B(v_p)^*B(v_p). These lifts preserve positivity and agree with their coefficient matrices on k particles.

Define W_3(v tensor u)=v wedge u and W_4(v tensor w)=v wedge w. Then on the entire fermionic Fock space,
H^2=H+L_3(W_3(W_3^*W_3-I)W_3^*)+L_4(W_4W_4^*).
Normal ordering the two middle pair operators leaves only two-, three- and four-body coefficients. On two particles H is a projection, determining the first coefficient. On three particles H=W_3W_3^*, determining the second as H_3^2-H_3. With no contractions the four-body term is sum_(p,r) B(v_p wedge v_r)^*B(v_p wedge v_r). Both the sum and V tensor V use ordered pairs, so there is no additional factor two.

The calculation is algebraic and independent of particle number; rotational invariance is useful in the source application but unnecessary for this identity. The three-body coefficient need not be positive, whereas the four-body lift is positive.

## Role in the source argument

Reducing a many-particle operator square to small coefficient matrices without losing normalization factors.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Ortiz, Nussinov, Dukelsky and Seidel, arXiv:1306.3268](https://arxiv.org/abs/1306.3268), PDF pages 4-5, were read for the established pair-annihilator pseudopotential representation and rank-one pair sectors. The normal-ordering mechanism is classical Wick calculus; no new general fermionic identity is claimed.

## Checks and limitations

Direct creation and annihilation matrices for arbitrary complex pair subspaces check the full Fock identity, its low-sector restrictions and the ordered-pair factor.

This identity does not prove the source three- and four-body coefficient inequalities or a uniform Laughlin gap.

## Possible applications

Reducing a many-particle operator square to small coefficient matrices without losing normalization factors.
