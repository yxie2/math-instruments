# Derivative weights turn harmonic sieve paths into a probability process

[Family 021](../families/openai-math-021.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000102.md) · [Batch B007](../../batches/B007/README.md)

## Mathematical content

For the standard lower and upper linear-sieve functions f,F, the source uses positive weights $\phi_e(s)=s^2f'(s)$ and $\phi_o(s)=-s^2F'(s)$, with its explicitly specified small starting extensions. They satisfy

$$\phi_i(s)=\int_{m_i(s)}^\infty\frac{t+1}{t^2}\phi_{i'}(t)\,dt,\quad m_e(s)=s-1,\quad m_o(s)=\max(2,s-1),$$

where i' is the opposite parity. These identities follow by differentiating the sieve delay equations and integrating backwards from the vanishing tail. They normalize the transition

$$K_i(s,dt)=\frac{(t+1)\phi_{i'}(t)}{t^2\phi_i(s)}\mathbf1_{t\ge m_i(s)}\,dt.$$

At gap r, cutoff b and ratio s=r/b, set $x=r/(t+1)$, $r'=rt/(t+1)$ and s'=t. The support gives x<=b, with s'>=2 on odd-to-even transitions. The original harmonic measure dx/x becomes dt/(t+1). Dividing it by K gives $(r'/r)^2\phi_i(s)/\phi_{i'}(s')$. Hence for every finite prefix,

$$dW_{\rm harmonic}=d\mathbb P\left(\frac{r_{\rm end}}{r_{\rm in}}\right)^2\frac{\phi_{i_{\rm in}}(s_{\rm in})}{\phi_{i_{\rm end}}(s_{\rm end})}.$$

All intermediate weights cancel. If the starting ratio and endpoint ratio/gap lie in fixed compact ranges with positive weights and r_in is of order B, the endpoint factor is of order B^-2. This is a weight conversion, not a guarantee that the desired endpoint is reached. Subsequent cutoffs are the previously selected exponents, so all exponents exceed ell precisely when the last one does: $s_{\rm end}<r_{\rm end}/\ell$.

The ordinary state ranges are even s>=2 and odd s>=1. The source permits the initial even extension down to 1.98 and odd down to 0.95; only the first odd state of its specified starts can fall below 1.

## Role in the source argument

Converts occupation probabilities for a continuous alternating process into the small positive harmonic contribution needed near the linear-sieve boundary.

## Antecedents and comparison

The linear-sieve functions are established; [Iwaniec](https://www.impan.pl/en/publishing-house/journals-and-series/acta-arithmetica/all/19/1/97719/on-the-error-term-in-the-linear-sieve) is a source antecedent. Normalization by a positive function and telescoping path likelihoods are classical. The selected package is the derivative normalization and its explicit squared-gap factor; it is not a new change-of-measure principle.

## Checks and limitations

Checked the Jacobian, support conditions and finite-prefix telescoping. Numerical checks use the explicit low-range derivative formulas and arbitrary finite positive endpoint weights to verify the algebraic identity; they do not certify tail asymptotics, regeneration or occupation limits.

The future-integral identities and positivity are required to call K a probability kernel. A finite product identity does not imply optional-stopping or an infinite-path identity without integrability. The source needs separate renewal and arithmetic estimates to obtain its final bound.

## Possible applications

Analyzing weighted decreasing-prime trees through probabilistic occupation estimates while retaining an exact conversion back to the original sieve weights.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
