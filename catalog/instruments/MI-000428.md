# Enforce equality of two matching-edge choices by signed cancellation

[Family 108](../families/openai-math-108.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000233.md) · [Batch B040](../../batches/B040/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Replace two outer edges of weights w and 1, with distinct endpoints, by three internal rows and columns carrying H=[[-1,-1,1],[-1,-1,1],[1,1,1]]. Attach each outer row to the corresponding first or second internal column and each internal row to its outer column. All boundary weights are 1 except the first incoming weight -2w.

For used incoming set I and outgoing set O, unequal cardinalities admit no completion. Otherwise the completion sum is (-2w)^(1 in I) times the permanent of H with rows O and columns I deleted. The only nonzero states are I=O=empty, of weight -2, and I=O={1,2}, of weight -2w. All four balanced singleton states cancel.

Thus the two original edge choices agree, up to a common factor -2. When many gadgets share outer endpoints, first fix the complete external matching choices. Internal completion sums then factor because their internal vertices are disjoint. Shared outer endpoints are handled by the external matching constraint.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Dell et al., Section 3 and Figure 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1206.1775) explicitly use a signed equality gadget with nonzero both/none states and cancellation of the others. Its displayed weights differ from this source normalization. Those selected passages were checked; equality gadgets are not new in principle.

## Checks and limitations

Sixty-four exact boundary-state computations cover four weights including zero and negative values.

Signed cancellation is essential. This is not an unweighted matching gadget or a lower-bound proof.

## Possible applications

Composing weighted matching gadgets while retaining exact normalization and interface behavior.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
