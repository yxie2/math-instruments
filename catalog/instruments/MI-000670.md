# Transport a differential commutator through formal spectral calculus

[Family 065](../families/openai-math-065.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000439.md) · [Batch B071](../../batches/B071/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let T=T0+K lie in a completed matrix differential-operator algebra, with T0 an order-zero holomorphic matrix and K filtration-positive. Each coefficient has finite differential order, without requiring a common order bound. On a fixed local contour avoiding the spectrum of T0, set R0=(xi-T0)^(-1) and R_T=sum_(j>=0)(R0 K)^j R0. The filtration makes each coefficient a finite sum. Define f(T) by integrating f(xi)R_T(xi)/(2 pi i).

The resolvent identity gives unital multiplicativity, composition and orthogonal idempotents for separated spectral groups. If [T,lambda]=zT, then
[f(T),lambda]=z (xi f'(xi))(T).
Indeed [R_T,lambda]=z(xi R_T^2-R_T)=-z partial_xi(xi R_T); integrate by parts. Any continuous algebra element commuting with T also commutes with f(T).

For a xi-independent invertible gauge M in a common allowed Laurent algebra, R_(MTM^-1)=M R_T M^-1 and f(MTM^-1)=M f(T) M^-1, provided both calculi and their products are defined there. Laurent lower bounds are coefficientwise. These statements do not assert analytic convergence of a z-series.

## Role in the source argument

Preserves the quantum differential commutator while splitting spectral groups and changing frames.

## Antecedents and comparison

The inverse-commutator and contour identities are classical functional calculus. The source packages them in a completed differential-operator algebra with coefficientwise order and Laurent bounds. No new general functional-calculus theorem is asserted.

## Checks and limitations

Exact truncated translation operators, polynomial functions of them, lambda-dependent matrix gauges and spectral projectors test commutators, covariance, idempotence and commutation.

The finite models do not verify a geometric quantum connection or the full projective-bundle construction.

## Possible applications

Building spectral decompositions without losing a differential relation under frame changes.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
