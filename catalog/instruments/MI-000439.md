# Lift a kernel cycle through a square-zero perturbation using a commutator

[Family 320](../families/openai-math-320.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000238.md) · [Batch B041](../../batches/B041/README.md)

## Mathematical content

In the source truncated differential forms, D=dF wedge (-) anticommutes with the exterior differential d. Thus d induces a differential delta on H(Omega,D). Multiplication by b commutes with D and induces B. The commutator delta B-B delta is induced by db wedge (-).

Let x be homogeneous in ker B intersect ker delta and let a be a D-cycle representing x. Both terms of the induced commutator kill x, so db wedge a is a D-boundary. Choose a prime of the same exterior degree with D a prime=-db wedge a. Then

$$(D+t\,db\wedge(-))(a+t a')=0\quad\text{mod }t^2.$$

The expansion has zero constant term, its linear term is the chosen boundary equation, and higher terms vanish because t^2=0. Lifts on independent tensor factors combine over separate square-zero parameters with the graded tensor convention, retaining mixed parameter products.

## Role in the source argument

Produces parameter-dependent cycle representatives used to contract source law identities without losing kernel information.

## Antecedents and comparison

Standard first-order obstruction theory: a perturbation of a cycle exists exactly when its first obstruction is a boundary. The commutator supplies that boundary condition for the selected kernel cycles.

## Checks and limitations

Sixty exact finite chain models check the first-order boundary equation and cycle expansion.

The tests do not implement the source full graph-form construction. A first-order lift does not imply unobstructed lifts in powers of one parameter.

## Possible applications

Cycle representatives compatible with infinitesimal parameter deformations and tensor contractions.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
