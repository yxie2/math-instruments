# Finite derivations retain a system-wide normalization counterexample in a finite specification

[Family 245](../families/openai-math-245.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000158.md) · [Batch B021](../../batches/B021/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Suppose every legal expression of a pure type system P is weakly normalizing for full beta reduction, but some legal expression E is not strongly normalizing. Then a finite subspecification P0 has the same two properties.

A non-strongly-normalizing E cannot be a literal sort. Correctness of types therefore supplies a finite typing derivation for E even if E originally occurred as an expected type. At each conversion rule choose a finite raw conversion chain witnessing its side condition. Collect all sorts in the derivation and these chains, and all axiom pairs and product-rule triples used. This gives finite S0,A0,R0 supporting the same derivation.

A forward beta step introduces no new sort symbol: substitution only copies symbols already in the redex. This includes reductions inside domain annotations and dependent products. Hence the infinite raw reduction from E stays in syntax over S0. Conversely every P0 judgment is a P judgment. A legal P0 term has a finite normalizing reduction by the system-wide hypothesis, and the same symbol-preservation argument keeps it in P0. Normality is purely syntactic and is unchanged by the restriction.

The conversion witnesses must be included when choosing S0: a reverse beta step can insert an annotation containing a sort not present in its contractum. Also, this argument does not assert that weak normalization of one term implies strong normalization of that term.

## Antecedents and comparison

This is the standard finite-support principle for finitary derivations, together with elementary beta-substitution syntax. The source makes conversion chains and reduction inside annotations explicit. No independent novelty claim is made.

## Checks and limitations

Read the full proof. A de Bruijn syntax model checks sort support for contextual beta steps, including annotations, and demonstrates an erasing weakly normalizing raw term with a looping argument. A reverse-expansion example shows why conversion-chain sorts must be retained.

The program checks raw syntax only, not the entire typing metatheory or existence of a legal normalization counterexample. System-wide weak normalization remains an essential assumption.
