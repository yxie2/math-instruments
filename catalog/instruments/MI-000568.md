# Gain a logarithm from uniform independent-set occupancy

[Family 170](../families/openai-math-170.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000337.md) · [Batch B058](../../batches/B058/README.md)

## Mathematical content

If a triangle-free graph H has n vertices and maximum degree at most a real D>=1, then alpha(H)>=n log(D+1)/(8(D+1)). The graph may be edgeless.

Choose an independent set I uniformly from all independent sets, including the empty set. Fix x and condition on I outside its closed neighborhood. Let Z_x be the number of neighbors not blocked by that outside set. Triangle-freeness makes these Z_x vertices mutually nonadjacent. Exactly 1+2^(Z_x) completions remain: include x, or choose any subset of its available neighbors. Thus the conditional probability of x is 1/(1+2^(Z_x))>=2^(-Z_x)/2, while the conditional expected number of selected neighbors is Z_x 2^(Z_x-1)/(1+2^(Z_x))>=Z_x/4.

Writing mu=E|I|/n and choosing x uniformly gives E Z_x<=4D mu by double counting selected neighbors. Jensen then yields mu>=2^(-4D mu)/2. If mu<log(D+1)/(8(D+1)), this fixed-point inequality forces mu>1/(2 sqrt(D+1)), contradicting log u<=sqrt u for u>=1. Finally alpha(H)>=E|I|. The logarithmic improvement comes from counting all neighborhood completions, not from a greedy maximal independent set.

## Role in the source argument

Supplies the classical logarithmic independence gain in the source upper-bound induction.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Alon, Independence Numbers of Locally Sparse Graphs and a Ramsey Type Problem](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~nalon/PDFS/indrams1.pdf), proof of Proposition 2.1 on PDF pages 3-4, conditions a uniform independent set outside a closed neighborhood and counts available completions. Those proof pages were read. The source specializes this classical method to independent neighborhoods and chooses convenient constants.

## Checks and limitations

64 triangle-free graphs are checked by exhaustive independent-set enumeration. Their 2,655 outside-neighborhood conditionings satisfy the exact completion probabilities and expected-neighbor formula; the fixed-point and independence bounds also pass.

The tests do not establish the Ramsey lower-bound constructions or sharp logarithmic exponents.

## Possible applications

Obtaining a logarithmic improvement over greedy independence bounds when neighborhoods have controlled structure.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
