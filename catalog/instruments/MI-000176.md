# A midpoint coupling controls first-moment derivatives by cell-mass derivatives

[Family 374](../families/openai-math-374.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000110.md) · [Batch B009](../../batches/B009/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let rho be uniform on a compact convex body K contained in the radius-R ball. Fix distinct slopes y_i and intercepts c_i such that every cell $P_i=\{x\in K:x\cdot y_i+c_i=\max_j(x\cdot y_j+c_j)\}$ has positive mass M_i. Write B_i=integral_(P_i) x d rho. For any intercept direction q, holding the slopes fixed, the source proves

$$\sum_i\frac{|DB_i[q,0]|^2}{M_i}\le162R^2\sum_i\frac{|DM_i[q,0]|^2}{M_i}.$$

The comparison couples uniform points X_i,Z_i in the cells at c-sq,c+sq. A classical triangular-quantile construction gives a midpoint law with Lebesgue density at most the reciprocal geometric mean of those cell volumes. Its midpoint belongs to the unperturbed cell: the opposite intercept errors cancel in every defining affine inequality. With perturbed masses alpha_i,beta_i and gamma_i=sqrt(alpha_i beta_i), gamma_i times that law is dominated by rho restricted to P_i.

Test this domination by R^2-|x|^2, using its midpoint excess |X_i-Z_i|^2/4. Since the two perturbed cell families each partition K, summing their endpoint integrals cancels the background. If $A=\sum_i\gamma_i\mathbb E|X_i-Z_i|^2$ and $B=\sum_i(\alpha_i-\beta_i)^2/\gamma_i$, the remaining estimates give $A/4\le R^2B/8+(R/2)\sqrt{AB}$, hence A<=5R^2B.

The moment difference is bounded by $R|\alpha_i-\beta_i|+\alpha_i\sqrt{\mathbb E|X_i-Z_i|^2}$. For each fixed configuration, choose s sufficiently small that alpha_i,beta_i,gamma_i lie between M_i/2 and 2M_i. Squaring and summing gives the constant 162; division by (2s)^2 and passage to the derivative gives the displayed estimate. The permitted s can depend on the smallest mass. The derivative constant does not.

## Role in the source argument

Supplies a uniform estimate that survives small masses and small faces, avoiding a quantitative lower bound on the cell-Laplacian spectral gap.

## Antecedents and comparison

The midpoint density estimate follows from established [McCann displacement concavity](https://www.math.toronto.edu/mccann/papers/advances.pdf); the source also gives a Knothe proof. The selected addition is its application to opposite intercept perturbations and partition cancellation to control first moments by masses. The constant is sufficient, not claimed optimal.

## Checks and limitations

Read the triangular substitution, midpoint inclusion, cancellation and derivative limit. Exact interval-cell examples and two-dimensional polygon clipping check normalized masses, moments and their finite-difference derivatives. Box couplings check the midpoint density bound. These are finite examples, not general geometric proof certification.

The source is uniform and the cells convex. Positive masses are required at the differentiated configuration; no common finite perturbation radius is asserted as masses vanish. Moving-site derivatives alone are not covered by the displayed inequality.

## Possible applications

Uniform sensitivity estimates for partitions defined by competing affine functions when direct spectral-gap estimates deteriorate.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
