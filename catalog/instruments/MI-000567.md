# Predict bounded center tests from a short strict history prefix

[Family 167](../families/openai-math-167.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000336.md) · [Batch B058](../../batches/B058/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let omega(p,q) be a finite point-center law, with marginals mu,kappa. Draw two points p,r independently conditional on q, and let pi be their joint law. Put D=KL(pi || mu tensor mu), in natural units. An independent random test T specifies a code c_T:P->[M], features f_T(c) of norm at most B, and center coefficients g_T(q) of norm at most B'.

Take K iid tests, choose J uniformly in {1,...,K}, and retain only H(p)=(c_(T_1)(p),...,c_(T_(J-1))(p)). For a fresh test T independent of that prefix, predict f_T(c_T(p)) by its mean m_(T,H(p)) under mu conditioned on that point own history. Then the average over prefixes and fresh tests of

|E_omega <f_T(c_T(p))-m_(T,H(p)), g_T(q)>|

is at most 4BB'(D/K)^(1/4). Some fixed prefix attains this bound averaged over the subsequent fresh test, and it has at most M^(K-1) history values.

For fixed tests, the KL chain rule for the pair of transcripts spends at most D across K steps. The reference next-code distribution uses the product of the two separate posterior marginals, even when their histories differ. Pinsker and averaging bound the mean conditional total variation by sqrt(D/(2K)). Each centered feature residual U has norm at most 2B. Its product expectation vanishes under the reference law, while under the wedge law E_pi<U(p),U(r)>=0 equals E_kappa ||E_(p|q) U(p)||^2. Cauchy-Schwarz against g and then across steps gives the fourth-root bound. The strict prefix makes replacing the next iid test by a fresh test valid.

## Role in the source argument

Replaces dependence on a center by bounded-complexity point histories while preserving a subsequent random test.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Ahlswede, An Elementary Proof of the Strong Converse Theorem for the Multiple-Access Channel](https://www.math.uni-bielefeld.de/ahlswede/homepage/public/44.pdf), Section 4, Lemma 3, pages 9-11 of this copy, uses short conditioning to reduce coordinate dependence; the lemma and proof were read. The source adds an own-posterior residual and a fresh-test discrepancy guarantee. This comparison identifies a methodological antecedent, not an equivalence or a proven novelty claim.

## Checks and limitations

Nine finite wedge laws, including three independent controls, enumerate 243 complete test sequences and 729 prefix steps. Conditional KL spending, Pinsker, the wedge-square identity and the final average discrepancy pass, with chain-rule error below 5e-16.

Tests use finite code maps and features and do not prove the source geometric incidence bounds, integrated coordinate tests or unit-distance power saving.

## Possible applications

Compressing finite probability models for later bounded tests while keeping the test independent of the chosen history.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
