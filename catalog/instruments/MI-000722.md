# Condition transverse spin coordinates to inherit a lower-dimensional bound

[Family 215](../families/openai-math-215.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000487.md) · [Batch B077](../../batches/B077/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Write a spin sigma_x in S^(n-1), n>3, as (rho_x s_x,v_x), where v_x is in R^(n-3), rho_x=sqrt(1-|v_x|^2) and s_x is in S2. Under the independent uniform sphere reference, represent sigma by (U,V)/sqrt(|U|^2+|V|^2), with independent standard Gaussian blocks U in R3 and V in R^(n-3). The direction U/|U| is uniform and independent of (|U|,V), hence independent of v. At all sites the conditional reference directions remain independent uniform S2 variables.

Conditioning on all v splits the interaction into the constant sum b_xy v_x dot v_y plus sum b_xy rho_x rho_y s_x dot s_y. Thus the conditional directions have exactly the free O(3) Gibbs law with couplings b_xy rho_x rho_y. If 0<=b_xy<=beta these remain in [0,beta]. A first-component bound 0<=<s_x^1 s_y^1><=A0 exp(-m0 distance), uniform over all such arrays, transfers after multiplication by rho_x rho_y<=1 and averaging. Coordinate symmetry then yields the O(n) dot-product bound with prefactor n A0.

Zero rho has probability zero under the finite Gibbs law; alternatively an arbitrary independent direction there decouples. Uniformity in the entire coupling array, not just constant couplings, is essential for the transfer.

## Role in the source argument

Reduces higher-dimensional spin estimates to a three-component estimate with quenched inhomogeneous couplings.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Aru, Garban and Sepulveda, arXiv:2212.06767](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.06767), Proposition 2.4 and the preceding conditional-density calculation on page 9 of the retrieved PDF, were read. They give the established O(N)-to-O(N-1) projection. The source uses its block-coordinate form and a uniform O(3) input; no new projection principle is claimed.

## Checks and limitations

Finite conditional angular grids compare normalized full and reduced Gibbs weights for multiple dimensions and inhomogeneous couplings. Gaussian samples illustrate independence of direction and transverse data.

Discrete angular grids test the density factorization, not the continuous sphere law or the required O(3) exponential bound.

## Possible applications

Transferring estimates that are uniform over bounded ferromagnetic conductances to larger spin dimensions.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
