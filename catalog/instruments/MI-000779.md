# Round one coefficient vector inside all its integer constraints

[Family 335](../families/openai-math-335.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000516.md) · [Batch B084](../../batches/B084/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let v in R^N encode all finite local coefficients. Form the matrix A of all bounded integer rows b in the prescribed range with b dot v=0, including incidence and duplicate-slot equations. Choose pivot data deterministically and clear a nonzero pivot minor to obtain integer vectors b_1,...,b_k spanning ker_R A. Their entries have a bound K from the bounded rows and Hadamard's determinant estimate.

Write v=sum t_j b_j. Independently round each t_j to adjacent multiples of 1/D with its mean unchanged, using ONE vector of random choices for all cells. The resulting v' lies in ker A and (1/D)Z^N, has expectation v, and satisfies ||v'-v||_infinity<=kK/D. Choose D large enough for the desired local error. All incidence and equality relations hold exactly for every outcome.

Only finitely many bounded row sets occur. Lexicographic pivot choices make the construction Borel on each such stratum. The source's finite-type equivariant encoding then transfers the same coefficient rounding to every translated cell. Independent rounding of cell faces would lose the shared constraints.

## Role in the source argument

Obtaining exactly compatible rational chains while retaining an unbiased average and a uniform local error.

## Antecedents and comparison

This is rational-subspace rounding by an integer basis, with source-specific finite-profile and equivariance bookkeeping. No new general rounding principle is claimed.

## Checks and limitations

Exact rational enumeration of all rounding outcomes checks expectation, the integer grid, kernel membership and the deterministic error bound. Independently rounding equal coordinates supplies a constraint-breaking control.

Finite profile bounds, equivariance of the encoding and geometric mass estimates are separate hypotheses. Rounding in the kernel does not establish them.
