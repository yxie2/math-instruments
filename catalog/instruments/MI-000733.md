# Recover loop positivity and planar dual odds directly from Euler identities

[Family 223](../families/openai-math-223.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000493.md) · [Batch B078](../../batches/B078/README.md)

## Mathematical content

For a planar ribbon neighborhood of the open subgraph, the boundary-loop count satisfies ell=2k+|A|-|V|, up to the fixed boundary-cap convention. At d=sqrt(q) and edge odds v=d, d^ell is a fixed factor times q^k d^|A|. All weights are positive for every q>0, including q<1. A bounded number of local re-pairings changes loop count by a bounded integer, giving bounded weight ratios. On a torus the formula includes -2g, with genus g in {0,1}, so the extra factor remains bounded for fixed q.

For a connected plane graph and the dual complement A*=E minus A, Euler gives k*(A*)=|A|-|V|+k(A)+1. Choosing v*_e=q/v_e yields
w*(A*)/w(A)=q^(|E|-|V|+1)/product_e v_e,
independent of A. Hence duality transforms odds by q/v and fixes sqrt(q), corresponding to p=sqrt(q)/(1+sqrt(q)). This exact self-dual identity is not a proof that the model is critical. Boundary caps must be noncrossing and their incidence conventions must be retained.

## Role in the source argument

Establishes positive local comparison weights and exact duality without importing an unavailable monotonicity principle.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Grimmett, arXiv:math/0205237](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0205237), Section 6.1 on PDF pages 27-28, was read through the Euler-count derivation of planar duality. These are classical finite identities. No critical-point or scaling conclusion from that older survey is used.

## Checks and limitations

Independent component counting and ribbon-boundary permutations verify the loop identity on small embedded graphs. Exact complementary dual configurations verify a constant weight ratio for heterogeneous odds and q below and above one.

The checks concern finite planar graphs. They do not establish torus topology in general, positive association, criticality or SLE convergence.

## Possible applications

Separating finite algebraic positivity from probabilistic monotonicity and conformal-limit claims.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
