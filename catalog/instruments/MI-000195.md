# Finite observation scores amplify a variance-to-degree advantage into Boolean correlation

[Family 192](../families/openai-math-192.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000118.md) · [Batch B011](../../batches/B011/README.md)

## Mathematical content

For a finite observation F of N independent signs, set H_N=sum X_i, T_F=E[H_N|F] and v(F)=E T_F^2. A positive cell degree bound D means every output indicator has real multilinear degree at most D. Every function of F has that bound, even when different labels yield the same numerical score. Functions of independent observation blocks have degree at most the sum of their cell bounds.

For M independent copies, Z_M=(sum T_i)/sqrt(Mv) has cell bound MD and conditional score exactly sqrt(Mv)Z_M. The central limit theorem gives Z_M converging to a standard normal, with fourth moment E T_F^4/(Mv^2)+3(M-1)/M. The bound is uniform over M for this fixed F, not over all later amplification stages.

Suppose a fixed rule W on r inputs has output indicators decomposing, on every finite product alphabet, into terms depending on at most d<r inputs. Suppose also it retains variance at least d+eta, eta>0, for independent standardized laws sufficiently close to Gaussian in the stated weak-convergence and uniform-fourth-moment sense. Apply W to r independent M-copy averages. Its new cell bound is dMD and its retained variance is at least Mv(d+eta), increasing v/D by the fixed factor 1+eta/d. Each stage chooses its own sufficiently large finite M; start from one revealed sign and iterate finitely many times.

The transfer condition follows when a fixed predictor-error function has at most quadratic growth and Gaussian-null discontinuities: clipping gives weak-convergence transfer and fourth moments give uniform integrability. Finally set f=sign(sum T_i), assigning positive sign at zero. Then

$$\sum_j\widehat f(\{j\})=\mathbb E|\sum_iT_i|=\sqrt{Mv(F)}\,\mathbb E|Z_M|,\qquad\deg f\le MD.$$

Since E|Z_M| tends to sqrt(2/pi)>1/2, any v(F)>4C^2D eventually yields signed singleton sum greater than C sqrt(deg f). Positive correlation ensures the readout is nonconstant.

## Role in the source argument

Turns a fixed, possibly tiny local information gain into arbitrarily large violations of a proposed square-root degree bound.

## Antecedents and comparison

The conditional score and probability limits are standard; see [Durrett’s fifth-edition author text](https://sites.math.duke.edu/~rtd/PTE/PTE5_011119.pdf). The source combines them with cell-degree bookkeeping and a specific reporting advantage. Related [parity decision-tree bounds](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1506.01055v1) use a different cost measure.

## Checks and limitations

Read the degree expansion, score collision, fourth-moment and stage-order arguments. Exact Fourier calculations for small observation partitions check cell degrees and Boolean readout identities; finite convolutions check averaging scores and the fourth-moment formula.

A reporting rule with a uniform positive gain is a necessary input; arbitrary observations do not amplify. The proof gives no practical uniform copy-count bound. Weak convergence alone is insufficient without the moment control for predictor errors.

## Possible applications

Amplifying local gains in algebraically restricted information extraction and transferring finite-valued statistics into Boolean Fourier lower bounds.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
