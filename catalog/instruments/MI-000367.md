# Transfer labeled masses through cell capacities without exceeding available energy

[Family 079](../families/openai-math-079.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000202.md) · [Batch B032](../../batches/B032/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let old cell capacities x_i and receiving energies y_j satisfy x_i<=A sum_{j in N(i)} y_j+epsilon_i, with A>=1 and epsilon_i>=0. Suppose each receiving cell j lies in at most D neighborhoods, D>=1. Let labeled assignments obey sum_lambda m_lambda,i<=x_i.

Write Y_i=sum_{j in N(i)}y_j. Proportionally trim each cell's assignments until their sum is at most A Y_i; the deleted mass is at most epsilon_i. If Y_i>0, transfer each retained assignment to j in N(i) with amount

$$\frac{m_{\lambda i}y_j}{ADY_i}.$$

For each receiving cell, the total from one incident old cell is at most y_j/D, so the total over all labels and incidences is at most y_j. Summing over j gives exactly m_lambda,i/(AD). Aggregating contributions from different old cells to the same label incurs no further loss. If Y_i=0, all assignments in that cell are removed within epsilon_i.

## Role in the source argument

Transfers terminal fractional packet assignments after predecessor and overlap estimates have been supplied.

## Antecedents and comparison

This is elementary proportional allocation. It exposes the exact AD factor, zero-capacity convention and additive error budget.

## Checks and limitations

One hundred twenty exact rational allocation models verify every receiving capacity, the total deletion budget and each aggregated label mass, including zero-capacity cases.

The source predecessor energy and overlap bounds are hypotheses; no wave packet kernel is tested by this allocation calculation.

## Possible applications

Transporting several labeled resources under bounded overlap and additive approximation errors.
