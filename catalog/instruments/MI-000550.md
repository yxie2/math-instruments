# Bound coordinate inversion by integral exterior volume

[Family 203](../families/openai-math-203.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000319.md) · [Batch B056](../../batches/B056/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let D be an n by l integral matrix of rank l, and V its real column space. Cauchy-Binet gives det(D^T D)=sum_I det(D_I)^2, the squared norm of the integral exterior vector d_1 wedge ... wedge d_l. If n,l and this Gram determinant are bounded, only finitely many such exterior vectors occur. Each nonzero decomposable exterior vector determines its l-plane, so only finitely many subspaces V occur. Every injective restriction of a coordinate projection to one of these spaces has a bounded inverse.

The same volume argument yields an explicit elementary consequence: for any coordinate projection pi injective on V, ||(pi|V)^(-1)||<=sqrt(det(D^T D)). Indeed det((pi D)^T pi D) is a positive integer. The product of the singular values of pi|V is its square root divided by sqrt(det(D^T D)), hence at least the reciprocal of that denominator. Every singular value is at most one, so the smallest is at least the product. This quantitative consequence is included as an immediate deduction, not as a separate novelty claim.

The source applies this to a decomposition matrix, whose Gram matrix is the Cartan matrix. Bounded dimensions and Cartan determinant provide the needed exterior bound. This does not bound all entries of a chosen integral basis: the matrices with columns (1,0,1) and (N,1,N+1) have Gram determinant 3 for every integer N, while their entries grow. Their column plane is constant.

## Role in the source argument

Controls recovery of character-space coordinates from suitable rows without assuming that a chosen basis itself has bounded coefficients.

## Antecedents and comparison

This is a direct application of the classical Cauchy-Binet identity and integral exterior coordinates. The complete source proof was read. A targeted search did not establish a new priority claim for its block-theoretic application; the catalogue records the reusable elementary reduction.

## Checks and limitations

Exact determinants and minors are compared for integer matrices, and every injective coordinate projection in the finite models is checked against the volume bound. Arbitrarily large unimodular shears and nonintegral near-vertical lines distinguish the necessary hypotheses.

The checks do not prove bounded Cartan determinants or the source global finiteness theorem. Integrality is essential to the displayed uniform numerical argument.

## Possible applications

Replacing a potentially unbounded integral basis by bounded geometric data when only stable coordinate recovery is needed.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
