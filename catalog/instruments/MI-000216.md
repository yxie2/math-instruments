# Triple independence fixes positive squares and preserves an operator rectangle under polar normalization

[Family 145](../families/openai-math-145.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000128.md) · [Batch B014](../../batches/B014/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let F(x):H2->H1, G(y):H4->H3, K(c):H3->H1 and N(d):H4->H2 be strongly measurable contraction families between separable Hilbert spaces. Each triple of (x,y,c,d) has product law. Assume almost surely

$$FN=KG,\qquad F^*K=NG^*.$$

Assume the essential spans of the ranges of each family and its adjoint fill their specified target and source spaces. Then all eight positive squares F*F, FF*, and the corresponding squares of G,K,N are independent of their parameters almost surely and are injective. Their polar parts are measurable unitaries and obey U_F U_N=U_K U_G.

The rectangle equations imply KK*F=FNN*, K*KG=GN*N, F*FN=NG*G and FF*K=KGG*. In each equation the three parameters are independent. Comparing two independent choices c,c' with the same x,d gives (K(c)K(c)*-K(c')K(c')*)F(x)=0. The essential range span of F makes the difference zero. The other equations and adjoints handle the remaining squares. Countable dense vector tests give a common conull operator equality. A vector in the kernel of a fixed source square is orthogonal to every essential adjoint range, hence is zero; target squares work likewise.

Put A=|F| and B=|G|. From A^2N=NB^2, polynomial approximation gives AN=NB. The first rectangle becomes (U_F N-K U_G)B=0. The injective positive B has dense range, so cancellation is valid without an inverse bound. Uniqueness of the two polar decompositions of this common operator gives the unitary rectangle. Strong measurability follows by F(F*F+epsilon I)^(-1/2) as epsilon decreases to zero.

## Role in the source argument

Removes varying positive factors from operator-valued rectangle data while retaining the algebraic identity.

## Antecedents and comparison

Fubini, positive functional calculus and polar uniqueness are classical. The source-specific input is the pair of rectangle equations with triple independence and essential-span reduction; this extraction makes those assumptions explicit rather than asserting a new general spectral theorem.

## Checks and limitations

Read the four square identities and dense-range cancellation. Finite matrix models with three-wise independent parameters, noncommuting coordinate gauges and ill-conditioned positive parts check both rectangle identities and the polar conclusion. Controls show why the independence and span assumptions matter.

Pairwise independence alone is not supplied by the proof. Without full spans the polar parts may be partial isometries. In infinite dimensions injectivity does not supply a bounded inverse.

Passing from positive operator data to unitary cocycles under probabilistic rectangle constraints.
