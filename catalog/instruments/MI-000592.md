# Bound the number of witnesses in an arbitrary intersection by rank descent

[Family 254](../families/openai-math-254.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000361.md) · [Batch B061](../../batches/B061/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Suppose a collection of subobjects contains a top object of integer rank r, is closed under pairwise intersection, and every strict intersection step lowers a nonnegative integer rank. Every arbitrary intersection is then the intersection of at most r selected members. Start at the top. Whenever the current intersection Q is not contained in some family member P, replace Q by Q intersect P. Each such step strictly decreases rank, so after at most r steps no obstruction remains. The selected intersection is contained in every member and equals the full intersection. The empty-family convention is the top object.

Intersecting all members containing a specified subset gives its unique smallest closed object. Meets are intersections and joins are closure of unions.

For the source Artin application, the input is stronger than simply announcing a parabolic intersection: Q=gA_Yg^(-1) intersects P in g c^(-1)A_R c g^(-1), with R contained in Y and c in A_Y. If R=Y, the intersection is Q itself. A strict step therefore reduces the displayed size |Y|, giving r=|S|. This extraction is conditional on that substantive intersection description and does not prove it.

## Role in the source argument

Derives arbitrary intersections and closure once pairwise parabolic structure is available.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Moeller, Paris and Varghese, On parabolic subgroups of Artin groups](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.13044), Proposition 1.5/2.6 and Corollary 1.6/Proposition 2.9, already use strict rank decrease to obtain arbitrary intersections and parabolic closure under pairwise closure. Pages 3 and 7-8 were read, including the descent argument. The rank-sized bound follows from that same finite-rank mechanism.

## Checks and limitations

One hundred eighty coordinate-subspace models verify strict rank decrease, equality with the full intersection, and the witness bound.

Coordinate models do not prove the pairwise Artin intersection input. The claim is explicitly a conditional reduction.

## Possible applications

Replacing infinitely many intersection constraints by boundedly many witnesses in finite-rank closure systems.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
