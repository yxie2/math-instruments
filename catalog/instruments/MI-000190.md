# An equal-marginal one-sided displacement bound controls mean squared motion

[Family 127](../families/openai-math-127.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000116.md) · [Batch B011](../../batches/B011/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let U,V have the same real distribution, finite mean m and finite variance sigma^2. If V-U<=a almost surely for a>=0, then E(U-V)^2<=8a sigma. No independence or exchangeability assumption is used, and downward jumps can exceed a.

Use the increasing primitive g(t)=(t-m)|t-m|/2. For u<v,

$$\frac{(v-u)^2}{4}\le g(v)-g(u)\le\frac{v-u}{2}(|u-m|+|v-m|).$$

The first estimate follows by splitting at m; the second integrates the chord bound for the convex function |t-m|. Since g(U),g(V) are integrable and have equal expectations, E|g(V)-g(U)|=2E[(g(V)-g(U))_+]. The positive part occurs exactly when V>U, where the one-sided displacement bound applies. Thus E|g(V)-g(U)|<=2a sigma. The pointwise lower increment bound finishes the argument.

In particular a=0 forces U=V almost surely. The finite-variance hypothesis is used to integrate the quadratic primitive; this proof is not an infinite-variance statement.

## Role in the source argument

Bounds the energy of a reflected grade-index coupling with binomial marginals and a one-sided support restriction.

## Antecedents and comparison

The equal-law primitive cancellation is explicitly present in [Rollin, Section 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0611050v2). The source selects this quadratic primitive for the stated one-sided estimate. Searches did not establish that its constant or formulation is historically optimal or new.

## Checks and limitations

Read both increment inequalities. Exact finite stationary cycles check equality of primitive expectations, asymmetric jumps and the squared estimate; nonmatching marginal examples show the equal-law condition is essential.

The constant eight is a proved bound in the source, not a sharpness claim. A finite one-sided increment without equal marginals gives no such stationary estimate.

## Possible applications

Quantitative stationary-descent bounds and energy estimates for nonreversible couplings with controlled increments in one direction.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
