# Certify scalar and matrix polynomial signs by normalized Bernstein coefficients

[Family 348](../families/openai-math-348.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000520.md) · [Batch B085](../../batches/B085/README.md)

## Mathematical content

On an interval write p(r+(s-r)z)=sum a_j z^j with degree at most N. Its normalized Bernstein coefficient is h_i=sum_(j<=i) a_j binom(i,j)/binom(N,j). Since the basis binom(N,i)z^i(1-z)^(N-i) is nonnegative and sums to one, min h_i is a certified lower bound.

On a triangle, substitute its affine coordinates to obtain P(z,w)=sum P_de z^d w^e. The coefficient at (i,j), i+j<=N, is sum_(d<=i,e<=j) P_de binom(i,d)binom(j,e)/(binom(N,d+e)binom(d+e,d)). Expanding z^d w^e(z+w+1-z-w)^(N-d-e) proves the formula. The corresponding multinomial basis is a nonnegative partition of unity.

Apply conversion entrywise to a symmetric polynomial matrix. If every coefficient matrix is positive semidefinite, the matrix is positive semidefinite throughout the triangle. Exact rational arithmetic can retain boundary zeros; testing only vertices or a floating-point grid cannot replace the coefficient certificate.

## Role in the source argument

Reducing a continuum of scalar or matrix inequalities to auditable finite exact data.

## Antecedents and comparison

This is established Bernstein-basis positivity, also explicitly attributed in the source to Boudaoud-Caruso-Roy and Roy. The conversion proof was read; no independent review of the source-specific large certificate tables or supplied verification package is claimed.

## Checks and limitations

Exact rational reconstructions check interval and triangular formulas, degree elevation and matrix-valued convex combinations. A polynomial positive at vertices but negative inside rejects vertex-only testing; a nonnegative polynomial with a negative Bernstein coefficient shows the condition is not necessary.

A negative coefficient does not disprove positivity. Certifying the general conversion does not validate the source-specific polynomials or the geometric inequalities from which they were derived.
