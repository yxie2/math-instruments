# Invert the polar operator in a self-dual lattice Gaussian integral

[Family 015](../families/openai-math-015.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000406.md) · [Batch B066](../../batches/B066/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let B be a nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form over Q_p, L a full self-dual lattice, and Haar measure self-dual with vol(L)=1. For an invertible B-self-adjoint P, put q_P(x)=B(Px,x)/2. Then

integral_L psi(q_P(x)) dx = omega(P)|det P|_p^(-1/2) integral_L psi(-q_(P^-1)(x)) dx,

where |omega(P)|=1 and omega(T* P T)=omega(P) for every invertible T. Completing the square gives q_P(x)+B(x,y)=q_P(x+P^-1 y)-q_(P^-1)(y). Integrate over p^(-N)L and pair with compactly supported Fourier test functions: the resulting Gaussian constant stabilizes. Differencing its squared absolute value yields vol(p^(-N)L) vol(P^-1 p^N L)=|det P|_p^(-1), once q_P is integral on the latter lattice. Pair the Fourier identity with the self-dual indicator of L. A linear change of variables cancels its determinant against the normalization, proving phase invariance.

For a rank-two module over a local etale cubic algebra and multiplication P=u rho(alpha), det rho(alpha)=Norm(alpha)^2, giving a Norm(alpha)^(-1) factor and inverse parameter u^(-1)alpha^(-1). The polar operator is P, including at p=2; one must not discard the factor 1/2 in q_P.

## Role in the source argument

Trades a large-denominator quadratic phase for an inverse-parameter integral with explicit determinant decay.

## Antecedents and comparison

This is Weil's classical quadratic Fourier formula. The source cites Weil, Chapter I section 14, and gives the normalization and stabilization argument in full, which was read. The historical Weil text itself was not retrieved in this batch.

## Checks and limitations

Finite residue sums model scalar and diagonal p-adic integrals at p=2,3,5, checking stabilized Gaussian constants, unit phases, inverse-parameter identities and scalar-square phase invariance.

Finite diagonal models do not establish the source denominator-shell estimates, local densities or global quartic equidistribution.

## Possible applications

Extracting determinant decay from local oscillatory integrals while keeping the dyadic normalization correct.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
