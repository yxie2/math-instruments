# Gain short-time spatial smallness by lowering the Holder exponent

[Family 352](../families/openai-math-352.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000291.md) · [Batch B053](../../batches/B053/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Use the fourth-order parabolic metric d_p((x,t),(y,s))=|x-y|+|t-s|^(1/4). Suppose 0<alpha<alpha'<1 and a function g has zero initial trace, elapsed scaled time at most eta<=1, and [g]_(alpha')<=M. Then ||g||_infinity<=M eta^(alpha'/4). Splitting distances at the crossover between 2||g||_infinity and M d_p^(alpha') gives

[g]_alpha <= (2||g||_infinity)^(1-alpha/alpha') M^(alpha/alpha') <= C M eta^((alpha'-alpha)/4).

Apply this to every spatial derivative through order four of a zero-data solution whose full higher-exponent parabolic norm is bounded uniformly. The weaker norm retaining these spatial derivatives, including their temporal Holder quotients, is small by eta^((alpha'-alpha)/4). The first time derivative is excluded: at the initial face the equation can give u_t=f.

The source supplies the exact circle solution u_N(z,t)=N^(-4-a)(1-exp(-N^4 t)) cos(Nz), f_N=N^(-a)cos(Nz), T_N=N^(-4). At t=T_N the fourth spatial derivative has spatial alpha seminorm of order N^(alpha-a). Thus equal exponents a=alpha give no uniform smallness, whereas a=alpha' gives the stated scale. This is a genuine exponent-gap requirement, not merely a choice of notation.

## Role in the source argument

Makes the source exterior correction small in its spatial norm while retaining the temporal Holder control needed for gluing.

## Antecedents and comparison

The two-bound crossover proof is the standard interpolation inequality between a supremum norm and a stronger Holder seminorm. The complete source proof and its explicit fourth-order Fourier-mode control were read. No new interpolation theorem is claimed.

## Checks and limitations

Finite parabolic grids check the crossover inequality and zero-trace bound. Exact Fourier-mode formulas check the equation, the exponent-gap scaling and the non-small initial time derivative.

The tests assume the higher-regularity bound; they do not establish a uniform Schauder theorem or the source Calabi-flow solution.

## Possible applications

Short-time perturbation arguments that need fourth spatial derivatives small while allowing the forcing to be nonzero at the initial time.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
