# Kill completed generators using a uniform weight gap

[Family 069](../families/openai-math-069.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000254.md) · [Batch B046](../../batches/B046/README.md)

## Mathematical content

The selected step concerns a filtered system of finite C[[h]]-modules with compatible finite-length weight structures, strict transition maps and homogeneous pro generators. Multiplication by h lowers weight by two. Suppose all target finite-length quotients have weights at most the same C, independent of the stage and length.

For a homogeneous source generator v of weight w, choose a with C-2a<w. If a later transition kills v modulo h^a, its images in every longer quotient lie in the h^a tail, whose weights are at most C-2a. Strictness and homogeneity force those images to vanish. Shorter quotients vanish too, so the completed image is zero. Finite generation and filteredness let one later transition kill all source generators.

In the source, logarithm exact sequences, stabilized images and mixed Hodge splitting supply these structures; a separate finite-rank comparison is then used to transfer the zero transition to non-torsion parameters. Those additional inputs are not consequences of the elementary weight-gap argument.

The uniform bound matters. For the system C[[h]] with every transition multiplication by h, each colimit modulo h^n is zero, but the module colimit is C((h)), not zero. Giving the generator at stage i weight 2i makes transitions weight preserving while the upper weight bound grows with i.

## Role in the source argument

Bridges classwise finite-logarithm vanishing and a finite transition that kills an entire completed module.

## Checks and limitations

234 finite weight models check the gap; thirty controls show how an increasing target bound permits nonzero h-power maps.

The checks do not prove the mixed Hodge, perfectness, stabilized-image or non-torsion rank inputs.

## Possible applications

Detecting when finite-order vanishing can be upgraded to completed-module vanishing without exchanging limits improperly.
