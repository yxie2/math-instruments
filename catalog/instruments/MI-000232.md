# Dyadic rounding plus a rare canonical guess calibrates success exactly

[Family 279](../families/openai-math-279.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000136.md) · [Batch B016](../../batches/B016/README.md)

## Mathematical content

An ordinary W-bit transition has good-output probability s>0, a dyadic rational, and one specified good encoding y0. Mix it with probability 1-delta with a uniform W-bit guess of probability delta=2^(-t), t>=1. For dyadic 0<z<=A=(1-delta)s, set

$$E=W+t+2,\quad C=2^{-E}\lfloor2^Ez/A\rfloor,\quad R=z-CA.$$

Retain ordinary good outputs with probability C. Retain a guessed output only when it equals y0, then with probability R 2^W/delta. Both retention probabilities are dyadic in [0,1], the latter at most 1/4, and the total retained-good probability is exactly z. The division z/A need not be dyadic: only its integer floor enters the implemented coin. Polynomial bit lengths of the inputs give polynomial arithmetic cost. Retention means adding a flag, not postselecting.

A second calibration starts from a known dyadic success p>1/2 with a unique verifiable J-bit successful output. Use the ordinary preparation half the time and a uniform J-bit guess half the time. Put c=2^(-(J+2)) floor(2^(J+2)/(2p)) and e=1/4-pc/2. Retain ordinary success with c and the unique correct guess with 2^(J+1)e. Total success is exactly 1/4. For the resulting state psi and success projection Pi,

$$ (2|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|-I)(I-2\Pi)|\psi\rangle=2\Pi|\psi\rangle.$$

One ordinary amplification step therefore makes success certain. Implementing the first reflection requires the entire preparation and its inverse, including all records and garbage.

## Role in the source argument

Converts computable dyadic masses to an exact quarter without adding input-dependent rotation gates.

## Antecedents and comparison

The reflection step is standard [Brassard-Hoyer-Mosca-Tapp amplitude amplification, Section 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0005055v1). The selected interface is filling a downward-rounded probability with a canonical uniform-guess branch; no claim of new amplification theory is made.

## Checks and limitations

Read both calibration proofs. Exact rational arithmetic checks rounding, dyadic denominators, coin ranges and total masses, including non-dyadic ratios. Complex finite states check the quarter-success reflection identity.

A good canonical output must be recognizable when its filter is evaluated. Calibration does not supply a verifier or an exact formula for the input success probability.

## Possible applications

Exact success calibration in finite-gate algorithms whose useful transition probabilities can be reconstructed.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
