# Reduce a matching cardinality threshold using a sparse permutation network

[Family 120](../families/openai-math-120.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000212.md) · [Batch B035](../../batches/B035/README.md)

## Mathematical content

For a graph G on n vertices and 0<=k<=floor(n/2), put b=n-2k. Take a rearrangeable directed acyclic permutation network with s inputs and outputs, where s is the least power of two at least n. Its O(s log(s+2)) positions route every permutation by vertex-disjoint paths.

Split each position v into v_in,v_out with an idle edge; replace an arc u->v by u_out v_in. Attach each original vertex to its corresponding network input. Select b outputs and attach one mandatory sink to each. Retain original graph edges. In a perfect matching, a split pair is either idle or both endpoints are matched externally. Counting external inputs and outputs shows exactly b original vertices enter the network, leaving exactly k original edges in the decoded matching.

Conversely, route the b unmatched vertices of any size-k matching to the selected outputs, extending the assignment to a full permutation. Keep those paths and use idle edges elsewhere. The construction has size and construction time O(m+n log(n+2)); decoding selects original edge identifiers in linear constructed size. The network routing proof colors even cycles in the bipartite multigraph of input pairs versus output pairs and recurses on the two colors.

## Role in the source argument

Reduces exact cardinality search to perfect matching without adding quadratically many edges.

## Antecedents and comparison

Rearrangeable permutation networks are classical. The extracted bookkeeping is the sparse split-position gadget and direct original-edge decoder.

## Checks and limitations

All 213 cardinality thresholds for simple graphs through four vertices are checked by independent exhaustive matching search, including isolates and k=0.

Finite correctness checks are not a certificate for the source almost-linear matching solver or its randomized work bounds.

## Possible applications

Sparse reductions that need both a yes/no equivalence and an explicit matching witness.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
