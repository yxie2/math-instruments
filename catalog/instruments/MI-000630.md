# Bound a curve-map degree using five fibers visible modulo a prime

[Family 009](../families/openai-math-009.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000399.md) · [Batch B066](../../batches/B066/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let f:C->P¹ be separable of degree n over an algebraically closed field, with genus g<=G. In each of m distinct fibers, suppose at most N_i points have ramification index not divisible by a fixed prime ell. A fiber has at most N_i+n/ell points: every remaining point consumes at least ell units of degree. Its contribution to the different is at least n minus its number of points. Riemann-Hurwitz therefore gives

[m(1-1/ell)-2] n <= 2G-2+sum_i N_i.

For m=5 the coefficient is positive even at ell=2, yielding a uniform degree bound. A further rational fiber difference has zeros and poles in two fibers, so at most 2n orders visible modulo ell. In positive characteristic p different from ell, first remove the maximal Frobenius power from the function; multiplying orders by p^r preserves whether they vanish modulo ell. The source supplies a common bounded-genus residual curve and transfers point-order supports to it through separate reconstruction arguments; those are conditional inputs to this extracted inequality.

## Role in the source argument

Turns finitely many controlled anchor supports into a uniform support bound for a pencil.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Stacks Project, Lemma 53.12.4, Tag 0C1F](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0C1F) gives Riemann-Hurwitz with the different and d_x>=e_x-1. Its statement and proof were read. The five-fiber estimate is elementary bookkeeping from that theorem.

## Checks and limitations

Integer partitions modeling ramification fibers check the point-count and different inequalities; exact rational arithmetic checks the positive five-fiber coefficient and the failure of positivity for four fibers at ell=2.

Arbitrary partitions need not arise from a cover. The checks do not construct residual curves or certify field reconstruction.

## Possible applications

Recovering a degree bound from sparse ramification data modulo a prime.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
