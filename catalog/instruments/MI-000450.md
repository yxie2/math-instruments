# Convert commuting Taylor envelopes into a norm error bound

[Family 282](../families/openai-math-282.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000244.md) · [Batch B043](../../batches/B043/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let g on R^d be bounded and smooth with bounded Hessian and C=one half sup ||g''||. Suppose h>=0, Y_1,...,Y_d and h_g strongly commute, and their joint spectral variables obey, for every z in a fixed countable dense subset,

|h_g-g(z)-grad g(z) dot (Y-z)| <= C|Y-z|^2+C h.

Intersect the countably many full-measure sets and let z tend to the joint value Y on each fiber. Then |h_g-g(Y)|<=C h, so ||(h_g-g(Y))psi||<=C||h psi|| for psi in Dom h. The argument also supplies the required domain inclusion. In the source the two sides come from separate estimates for g and -g.

This is a scalar inequality on common spectral fibers. For general noncommuting operators, a two-sided quadratic-form inequality does not by itself imply the displayed norm bound.

## Role in the source argument

Controls localized observables by their center variables and a positive spread operator.

## Checks and limitations

120 diagonal models check sine/cosine Taylor bounds and the norm estimate.

These examples assume the common spectral envelope; they do not derive it from the QFT axioms.

Replacing localization profiles by functions of commuting center observables with explicit domain control.
