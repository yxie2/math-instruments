# Insert an elliptic graph germ into a continued spherical phase

[Family 077](../families/openai-math-077.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000388.md) · [Batch B064](../../batches/B064/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Put q_s(xi)=-sqrt(1-|xi|²), Q_s=-1+|xi|²/2. A continued spherical phase has form Q_s+zeta(q_s-Q_s), where 0<=zeta<=1, zeta=1 on a small inner ball and vanishes outside twice that ball. Choose zeta_0 with an annular plateau 1/2 near xi1. The resulting q has Hessian I+O(rho²), hence is uniformly convex for small fixed rho.

For a target germ h0 with H=h0''(0)>0 and B=q''(xi1), choose A=H^(-1/2)B^(1/2), so A transpose H A=B. An ambient affine change matches the value, gradient and Hessian of h0(A(xi-xi1)) with q; call the resulting graph psi. With a cutoff vartheta supported in a radius-2 ball and equal to one in the unit ball, set R_epsilon=vartheta((xi-xi1)/epsilon)(psi-q). Two-jet matching gives ||D^j R_epsilon||<=C epsilon^(3-j) for j=0,1,2. Thus q+R_epsilon stays uniformly convex and equals the target graph near xi1.

On this annulus D=q_s-Q_s is strictly positive. The modified cutoff zeta=zeta_0+R_epsilon/D is smooth, and for epsilon small stays in [1/4,3/4] on its support. Hence the modified phase still belongs to the stated spherical-continuation class. All choices depend on the fixed graph, not on packet scales or later exponents.

## Role in the source argument

Checks an exact phase-class hypothesis before transferring a packet result to arbitrary positive curvature.

## Antecedents and comparison

The ingredients are classical positive-matrix normalization, matching jets and a Taylor cutoff. The source adds the useful observation that the spherical remainder is nonzero in an annulus, allowing the perturbation to be absorbed into the cutoff itself. Exact external priority for this interface was not established.

## Checks and limitations

Positive matrices check two-jet normalization. Explicit annular cubic perturbations check Hessian positivity, cutoff range and the epsilon scaling of differentiated remainders.

Local finite checks do not certify any packet theorem, uniformity over varying graph families or global restriction estimates.

## Possible applications

Transferring a local analytic theorem whose phase class is narrower than merely uniformly convex graphs.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
