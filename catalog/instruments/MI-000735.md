# Fix a microscopic amplitude by one normalized macroscopic coefficient

[Family 224](../families/openai-math-224.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000494.md) · [Batch B079](../../batches/B079/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Suppose bounded nonnegative amplitudes c_e from two models multiply the same limiting fixed-scale test J_chi(h). A first response is normalized exactly to one. Uniform integrability permits a nonnegative cutoff chi losing at most z+o(1), while local replacement and fixed-scale convergence imply, for every subsequential limit c_* in either model,
|1-c_* J_chi(h)|<=z+o_h(1), with 0<=J_chi(h)<=C_chi.

Taking z<1/4 and small h excludes c_*=0. If all amplitudes are bounded by C, the same inequality gives J_chi(h)>=(1-z-o_h(1))/C. For two subsequential limits,
|c_+-c_-|<=2C(z+o_h(1))/(1-z-o_h(1)).
First send h to zero, then z to zero. Every limit agrees, including limits from different models, so both amplitude sequences converge to a common positive value.

For a fixed higher coefficient, repeat local replacement at separated insertion locations, giving c_e^k times a common square-kernel integral. Fixed-scale limits, ordered replacement errors and uniform integrability then identify the whole coefficient. These are hypotheses, not consequences of the scalar calibration argument. The method compares a common response rather than equating the models' numerical mesh sizes.

## Role in the source argument

Removing an unknown relative scale through a shared measurable response.
