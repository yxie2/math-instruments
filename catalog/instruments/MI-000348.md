# Retain matrix rank modulo a prime using row norms and determinant divisibility

[Family 252](../families/openai-math-252.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000193.md) · [Batch B030](../../batches/B030/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let M be an integer matrix with every row of Euclidean norm at most sqrt(r), where r>=1. For a prime q>r, put d=rank_R(M). A nonzero d-by-d minor B has |det B|<=r^{d/2}. If its rank modulo q is e, row elimination modulo q lifts to integer row operations making d-e rows divisible by q. Hence q^{d-e} divides det B, and

$$\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_q}M\ge d\left(1-\frac{\log r}{2\log q}\right)\ge d/2.$$

For an r-by-r block matrix Y of D-dimensional regular permutation matrices, assume all nondegenerate rectangle words are nonidentity. A regular permutation has trace zero unless it is the identity, giving tr(YY^T)=r^2D and tr((YY^T)^2)=(2r^3-r^2)D. Cauchy-Schwarz on the nonzero eigenvalues yields rank_R(Y)>=r^2D/(2r-1)>=rD/2. Each row has squared norm r, so rank_Fq(Y)>=rD/4. No assumption q does not divide D is used.

## Role in the source argument

Supplies a lower rank bound uniform over finite quotient sizes, including characteristic dividing that size.

## Antecedents and comparison

The proof combines standard minor divisibility, Hadamard inequality and the trace form of stable rank. Its quotient-uniform characteristic choice is the useful application detail.

## Checks and limitations

Seventy-four integer-matrix models include six actual rank drops. Six regular-block models verify both trace identities and use q=D, explicitly allowing characteristic to divide the group order.

The rectangle-word nonidentity condition is an input. The tests do not establish it in the source infinite group.

## Possible applications

Rank estimates in modular representations without semisimplicity assumptions.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
