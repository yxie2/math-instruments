# A density angle gives orthogonal orbitals with a common formal potential

[Family 278](../families/openai-math-278.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000135.md) · [Batch B016](../../batches/B016/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let n>0 be continuous, even under reflection in a plane, with integral 3, local H^2 regularity and sqrt(n) in H^1. Suppose an odd angle theta has integral n sin^2(theta)=1, finite integral n|grad theta|^2, and sufficient local regularity for the following equations. Assume cos(theta)>0 and

$$n^{-1}\operatorname{div}(n\nabla\theta)=-d\sin(2\theta),\qquad d>0.$$

Set g=sqrt(n) cos(theta) and h=sqrt(n) sin(theta). Then g is positive and even, h is odd, ||g||^2=2, ||h||^2=1, and <g,h>=0. Their squares sum to n. Under the source's local H^2 orbital regularity,

$$V=\frac{\Delta g}{2g}=\frac{\Delta\sqrt n}{2\sqrt n}-\frac{|\nabla\theta|^2}{2}+d\sin^2\theta$$

is in L^2 locally and satisfies (-Delta/2+V)g=0 and (-Delta/2+V)h=dh in distributions. The lower bound for continuous g on each compact set justifies division by g. The gradient identity

$$|\nabla g|^2+|\nabla h|^2=|\nabla\sqrt n|^2+n|\nabla\theta|^2$$

provides global H^1 control. The source obtains the angle from a constrained minimization and proves the required local regularity; this card extracts the subsequent calculation conditional on those inputs. It does not infer that V belongs to L^(3/2)+L^infinity or defines a global closed Schrödinger form.

## Role in the source argument

Supplies the trial determinant and a common formal potential used by the later ensemble certificate.

## Antecedents and comparison

This is the Sobolev version of polar-coordinate differentiation, not a newly discovered chain rule. Its use in a constrained density construction is the selected source interface. The related positive-orbital identity is classical; see [Frank-Seiringer, Section 2.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0803.0503v2).

## Checks and limitations

Read the orbital proof. Local derivative data satisfying the angle equation check both orbital equations and the gradient identity; paired finite samples check the parity and normalization bookkeeping.

Existence and regularity of an appropriate angle are separate analytic tasks. Numerical derivative identities do not prove them, nor the Coulomb density estimates.

## Possible applications

Constructing orbital certificates while keeping density normalization and formal potential assumptions explicit.
