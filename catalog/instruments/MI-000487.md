# Build an absorption bijection from an exactly lifted correction

[Family 324](../families/openai-math-324.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000266.md) · [Batch B048](../../batches/B048/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let D=E direct-sum_infinity U for real Banach spaces, Q:Z->U bounded linear, g:D->U a bi-Lipschitz bijection, and K:D->Z Lipschitz. Assume the exact identity QK(t,u)=g(t,u)-u. Then F:Z direct-sum_infinity E->Z, F(b,t)=b+K(t,Qb), is a bi-Lipschitz bijection.

For d in Z, set (t_d,u_d)=g^(-1)(Qd) and G(d)=(d-K(t_d,u_d),t_d). The identities QF(b,t)=g(t,Qb) and Q(d-K(t_d,u_d))=u_d prove GF=id and FG=id. In particular, no surjectivity or right-inverse assumption on Q is used: the formula itself produces the required b with Qb=u_d.

If q=||Q||, L_K=Lip K and c_g>0 is a lower Lipschitz constant of g, then Lip F<=1+L_K max{1,q}, and Lip G<=max{1+L_K q/c_g,q/c_g}. The exact identity is essential; an approximate lift needs its own stability argument.

This criterion says what an infinite-dimensional absorption construction must supply. It does not, by itself, produce g or K, nor does it exclude a linear copy of E in Z.

## Role in the source argument

Turns the companion source coordinate construction into an absorption map with a concrete inverse.

## Antecedents and comparison

Triangular correction maps have classical antecedents, including [Godefroy-Kalton Theorem 2.12](https://kaltonmemorial.missouri.edu/assets/docs/sm2003c.pdf). The selected source proves its stated lift identity and inverse implication directly. Its geometric construction and no-linear-c0 assertion are separate claims.

## Checks and limitations

Finite-support sequence shifts test the two inverse identities in the c0 model where a coordinate is inserted. A separate nonsurjective Q example tests that no quotient section is needed. An inexact correction breaks the displayed inverse.

The sequence-shift example already contains a linear c0 copy and therefore does not test the source counterexample. It checks only the algebraic criterion.

## Possible applications

Nonlinear absorption or stabilization problems where a correction can be lifted exactly through a bounded linear observation map.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
