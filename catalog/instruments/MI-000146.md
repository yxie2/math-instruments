# Compatible conditional copies turn innovation uniqueness into measurable recovery

[Family 236](../families/openai-math-236.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000095.md) · [Batch B005](../../batches/B005/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let X be the source’s countable continuous observation array and W its causal innovations, which form independent Brownian coordinates relative to the observation history. Disintegrate the joint path law as $K(w,dx)\,\mathsf w(dw)$ and, conditional on the entire W path, sample two independent copies $X^1,X^2$ from K.

The necessary compatibility identity is

$$\mathbb E[F(X_{\le t})\mid W]=\mathbb E[F(X_{\le t})\mid W_{\le t}]$$

for bounded past functionals F. Future W increments are independent of the original observation past, and W up to t is a function of that past. The identity implies that W remains Brownian in the joint past filtration of the two copies. Equations involving stochastic integrals are transported using causal elementary approximations, the Itô isometry and maximal $L^2$ convergence; equal pair laws alone are not a substitute for this step.

If an applicable comparison proves $X^1=X^2$ almost surely, then for each coordinate v and rational q,

$$\mathbb E[(X_v^1(q)-X_v^2(q))^2\mid W]=2\operatorname{Var}(X_v(q)\mid W)=0.$$

The countably many evaluations determine continuous paths, so K is almost surely a point mass. Integrating rational evaluations against K and taking dyadic piecewise-linear limits gives a Borel recovery map $X=F(W)$, with a fixed default on exceptional inputs. In the Ising observation law $X_v(t)=t\sigma_v+B_v(t)$, integer-time slopes then recover every spin almost surely.

## Role in the source argument

Provides a rigorous route from equality of two specially coupled observations to an IID factor, including the filtration condition needed to compare their stochastic equations.

## Antecedents and comparison

This is an application of the established compatible-coupling and strong-solution framework of [Kurtz](https://arxiv.org/html/1305.6747v2), especially the conditional point-mass principle and joint compatibility lemmas. [Nam, Sly and Zhang](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.09484v2) already use Brownian observation equations for Ising IID factors. The release’s detailed path recovery is not presented here as a new general Yamada-Watanabe theorem.

## Checks and limitations

Checked the future-increment independence argument, transport by elementary stochastic integrals, and rational-coordinate recovery. A finite conditional-kernel calculation tests the variance identity and includes a non-Dirac negative control.

Equality of the copies must be proved; it is not supplied by disintegration. The innovations must be Brownian relative to the full observation past. The source’s pointwise equivariance upgrade is a separate local symmetrization argument, not an uncountable intersection of almost-sure events.

## Possible applications

Testing whether an equivariant observation model can be decoded from independent innovations once a suitable conditional-copy uniqueness estimate is available.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
