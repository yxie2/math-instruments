# Stabilize a finite family of harmonic jets by a smooth right inverse

[Family 365](../families/openai-math-365.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000525.md) · [Batch B086](../../batches/B086/README.md)

## Mathematical content

For L=a^(ij)partial_i partial_j+B^i partial_i with positive definite scalar principal tensor and matrix drift, admissible value-free two-jets (p,H) satisfy a^(ij)H_ij+B^i p_i=0. Suppose a fixed finite family of harmonic columns has scaled limits consisting of affine coordinate columns and all trace-free quadratic columns, with uniform smooth convergence on a compact parameter set.

At the limit, the quadratic columns produce arbitrary trace-free Hessians and the affine columns correct their gradients at any point. Trivialize the moving constraint space by gradient and trace-free Hessian; positivity of a determines the remaining trace. In these coordinates the column matrix T has full row rank at the limit, uniformly on the compact set. Small perturbations preserve a positive lower bound for its singular values. The right inverse T^*(TT^*)^(-1) is smooth with uniform bounds of every fixed order.

Rescaling columns of vanishing order one or two is legitimate only after verifying the corresponding jets vanish on both sides; agreement on an open set accumulating at the contact point supplies those common jets by continuity.

## Role in the source argument

Reducing comparison of differential operators to a finite, uniformly controlled family of solutions.

## Antecedents and comparison

This is classical stable spanning and the full-row-rank pseudoinverse. The source-specific input is the finite family of paired harmonic columns; it is not supplied by this algebraic lemma.

## Checks and limitations

Explicit affine and trace-free quadratic polynomials in dimensions two through five check spanning at many points, perturbation stability and the right-inverse identity, including two complex fiber components.

A pointwise rank assertion without compact uniform control need not yield uniform bounds. The tests do not construct boundary-controlled harmonic functions.

Reducing comparison of differential operators to a finite, uniformly controlled family of solutions.
