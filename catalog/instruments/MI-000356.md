# Build a complete Borel set at the finite-class mass threshold

[Family 259](../families/openai-math-259.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000197.md) · [Batch B031](../../batches/B031/README.md)

## Mathematical content

For a Borel subrelation T of a countable probability-measure-preserving relation, define kappa(T)=int 1/|[z]_T| d lambda(z), using zero for infinite classes. For every epsilon>0 there is a Borel set M meeting every T-class with lambda(M)<=kappa(T)+epsilon.

On classes of finite size k, choose the least point in a fixed Borel ordering. The k ordered sheets have equal measure by measure preservation of partial isomorphisms, so the selected sheet has mass lambda(Z_k)/k. Their union contributes exactly kappa(T).

On the infinite-class part, take finite Borel partitions increasing to a point-separating family. Let k_n(z) count atoms meeting the class, so k_n(z) tends to infinity. Mark atoms independently with probability p, let A be their union, and add the entire complement of its T-saturation. This meets every class for every marking. Its expected measure is p lambda(Z_inf)+int_{Z_inf}(1-p)^{k_n(z)}d lambda(z). Choose p small and then n large; dominated convergence makes the expectation smaller than epsilon. Some deterministic marking has that small mass.

## Role in the source argument

Provides the small complete sets needed to compress graphings relative to a supplied subrelation.

## Antecedents and comparison

This is a classical complete-section principle. The explicit construction keeps the exceptional unhit classes and accounts exactly for finite classes.

## Checks and limitations

Seven finite models enumerate every atom marking and verify the expected complete-set mass and reciprocal-class-size identity.

The finite tests illustrate the identity; they do not replace the infinite-class dominated-convergence step or audit the subsequent compression theorem.

## Possible applications

Cost bounds and measurable graph constructions requiring a small set that meets all classes.
