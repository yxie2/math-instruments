# Spectral whitening fills zero-density sets without an inverse lower bound

[Family 144](../families/openai-math-144.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000127.md) · [Batch B014](../../batches/B014/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let U be unitary, let G have spectral measure p(z) dz on the circle, and suppose v has spectral measure dz for the same U. Put E={p=0}. The vector

$$f'=\bigl(\mathbf1_{E^c}/\sqrt p\bigr)(U)G+\mathbf1_E(U)v$$

has spectral density one and satisfies

$$\|f'-G\|^2=\int(\sqrt p-1)^2\,dz=\|G\|^2+1-2\int\sqrt p\,dz.$$

The first multiplier can be unbounded, but it is defined on G because its squared integral against p dz is |E^c|. The two terms have disjoint spectral supports. On E, G vanishes and the second term supplies exactly Lebesgue density. No positive lower bound for p is needed. In a real Koopman setting, real vectors and symmetric densities give real corrected vectors through real even multipliers.

The source constructs an initial real flat vector for the twist S(theta,u,a)=(theta+phi(u),u,a). On an interval [u0,u1] where phi'>0 choose an integer m>0 with m(phi(u1)-phi(u0))=1, and set A(u)=1_[u0,u1] sqrt(m phi'(u)/2). Then A(u)(e(m theta)+e(-m theta)) is real and mean zero. The two orthogonal theta modes each push half of Lebesgue measure around the spectral circle.

For vectors F,G with spectral densities for the same U, the total variation estimate is ||p_F-p_G||_1 <= (||F||+||G||)||F-G||. Flat density gives an orthonormal integer orbit, but does not by itself give its completeness.

## Role in the source argument

Corrects an approximate spectral packet while retaining an exact distance estimate and handling vanishing density.

## Antecedents and comparison

The mechanism is classical measurable functional calculus; [Taylor, Spectral Theory, Section 1](https://mtaylor.web.unc.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/16915/2018/04/chap8.pdf) supplies a multiplication-operator formulation. The explicit zero-set patch and its use in this construction are the extracted interface.

## Checks and limitations

Read the flat-vector change of variables and whitening identity. Finite spectral-fiber models include zero densities, very small positive densities and multiplicity two; they check the identity and demonstrate that flatness need not imply cyclicity.

The auxiliary flat vector must belong to the same unitary representation. The correction need not be smooth, and an independent completeness argument remains necessary.

## Possible applications

Normalizing spectral vectors without dividing uniformly by a density, and separating spectral flatness from multiplicity.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
