# Robust witness menus admit balanced mixtures and simultaneous feasible profiles

[Family 171](../families/openai-math-171.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000131.md) · [Batch B015](../../batches/B015/README.md)

## Mathematical content

A finite menu of patches on two host sides is available with tolerance kappa if deleting at most kappa N labels on each side always leaves some patch with both supports retained. Each patch i carries probability laws mu_i,nu_i supported on its two sides. There is a probability mixture of the patches with coordinatewise E mu_i,E nu_i<=4/(kappa N).

To see this, assign nonnegative prices to all labels, of total P. Fewer than kappa N labels on either side have price above 2P/(kappa N). A surviving patch avoids these expensive labels, so its pair of laws has total price at most 4P/(kappa N). Convex separation from the coordinatewise upper box yields the desired mixture. This is a load bound for averages; it does not require every patch to be balanced.

For a second interface, finitely many players have finite action sets and choose independently from mixed profiles. Conditional on their actions, each output vector follows a fixed rule that does not depend on the profiles. Suppose for every choice of the other profiles player j has a mixture meeting its prescribed expected-vector upper bound b_j. Then some collection of profiles meets every bound simultaneously. Feasible response sets {q_j:E_(q_j,p_-j) X_j<=b_j} are nonempty compact convex sets with closed graph. Kakutani's theorem applied to their product gives a fixed point. The statement supplies existence, not convergence of best-response iteration.

## Role in the source argument

Balances patch loads and makes interacting local expected bounds simultaneously feasible before random sampling.

## Antecedents and comparison

Convex separation and [Kakutani's 1941 theorem](https://doi.org/10.1215/S0012-7094-41-00838-4) are established. The publisher full text was not retrievable in this batch; the specific finite response argument was checked in the release source. No new fixed-point theorem is claimed.

## Checks and limitations

Read the price threshold and closed-graph argument. Finite menus with certified deletion tolerance are solved as linear programs; a two-player system checks simultaneous feasibility and demonstrates cycling of best responses.

Availability must quantify over all permitted deletions. If output rules change discontinuously with the profiles, the fixed-point argument is not justified. Mixtures need not be deterministic patch choices.

## Possible applications

Controlling label congestion when each local construction is feasible against any choices of the others.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
