# Finite ell1 cuts retain a contractive affine reconstruction

[Family 332](../families/openai-math-332.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000106.md) · [Batch B008](../../batches/B008/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Given finitely many real ell1 vectors x_1,...,x_n, set v(k)=min_i x_i(k). For every nonempty proper B subset [n], define

$$b_B(k)=\left(\min_{i\in B}x_i(k)-\max_{i\notin B}x_i(k)\right)_+,\quad w_B=\|b_B\|_1.$$

Retain only positive weights, at most 2^n-2 of them; each b_B is a nonnegative ell1 vector. Let z_i(B)=1_(i in B) and $T(u)=v+\sum_Bu_Bb_B$. With $\|u\|_{1,w}=\sum_Bw_B|u_B|$ and $\|u\|_H^2=\sum_Bw_Bu_B^2$,

$$T(z_i)=x_i,\quad\|x_i-x_j\|_1=\|z_i-z_j\|_{1,w}=\|z_i-z_j\|_H^2,\quad\|T(u)-T(u')\|_1\le\|u-u'\|_{1,w}.$$

At each original coordinate, only the nested upper level sets have nonzero b_B, whose values are exactly the gaps between successive distinct data values. This gives reconstruction by telescoping. For each pair of data points all contributing signed gaps have the same sign, proving equality of distances. General contractivity follows from the triangle inequality. Infinite original coordinate support causes no difficulty because there are finitely many cuts and the b_B are summable. If all points coincide there are no positive cuts, and T is constant.

The construction remembers the ambient vectors, not only their distance matrix. Thus an arbitrary nonlinear modification u_i of the cut coordinates can be sent back to ell1, with both approximation and pairwise costs controlled. The image need not lie in the linear span of the original data.

## Role in the source argument

Allows nonlinear smoothing in a finite weighted cube and transfers the resulting points back to the given ambient ell1 space.

## Antecedents and comparison

Finite cut representation is classical; it also appears explicitly in [Cheng, Wang and Xiang](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.08749v2). The selected feature here is the recorded contractive reconstruction into the original ambient ell1, needed when new points may leave the data subspace.

## Checks and limitations

Read the coordinate-level gap proof. Exact integer and rational examples test reconstruction, distance equality and contractivity, including ties, negative coordinates and repeated points.

The number of potential cuts is exponential in the number of data points. A general metric with a convenient Hilbert embedding need not admit this positive cut reconstruction. Complex coordinates require a separate realification argument.

## Possible applications

Designing nonlinear modifications of finite ell1 data through weighted binary coordinates while controlling the cost in the original target space.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
