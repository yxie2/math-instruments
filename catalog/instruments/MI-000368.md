# Match endpoint jets before choosing a thin complex parameter collar

[Family 042](../families/openai-math-042.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000203.md) · [Batch B033](../../batches/B033/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let H_s(q) be a smooth family for s near a compact real interval, holomorphic in q on a common neighborhood of a compact convex set. Work in finitely many fixed graph-spray charts, and fix a desired real C^j approximation. First approximate the coordinate families by polynomials in s, matching endpoint jets through order M at adjacent chart boundaries. Fix those polynomials before choosing a thin complex collar of width delta.

On an overlap of width O(delta), the transition error and its fiber derivative are O(delta^{M+1}). A linear jump solver with norm at most K/delta turns this into corrections of size O(delta^M). The nonlinear correction map is a contraction once KA delta^M<1/2. Cauchy estimates give real derivatives through order j of size O(delta^{M-j}); choose M>j+2 and then delta small.

The result is a jointly holomorphic approximation on a neighborhood that can shrink with the approximant. Extra holomorphic parameters remain passive. This is not an assertion that the original smooth family extends holomorphically to a fixed neighborhood.

## Antecedents and comparison

The local ingredients are established approximation and Cartan splitting tools. [Forstneric 2013, Section 3.3](https://users.fmf.uni-lj.si/forstneric/papers/2013AFST.pdf) gives the additive splitting by a fixed dbar operator. The specific thin-collar exponent accounting is extracted from the source.

## Checks and limitations

Exact endpoint derivative identities and 21 complex overlap models verify the polynomial orders and the one-power loss.

These checks do not construct graph sprays or prove the manifold-valued approximation theorem. The source selected proof was read.

## Possible applications

Replacing smooth homotopies by holomorphic parameter families when the complex collar is allowed to depend on the approximation.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
