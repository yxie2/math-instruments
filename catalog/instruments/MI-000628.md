# Test Kummer splitting by two finite congruences

[Family 029](../families/openai-math-029.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000397.md) · [Batch B065](../../batches/B065/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let a be a nonzero integer, q a prime, and p a prime not dividing aq. In the splitting field K_q=Q(zeta_q,a^(1/q)), p splits completely if and only if p=1 modulo q and a^((p-1)/q)=1 modulo p.

The excluded primes contain the discriminant primes of T^q-a, so reduction is separable and Frobenius is defined. Frobenius fixes zeta_q exactly when p=1 modulo q. Under that condition its ratio on a chosen root reduces to a^((p-1)/q), and reduction is injective on the q-th roots of unity. Thus both congruences are equivalent to trivial Frobenius on all generators. Equivalently, the cyclic group F_p^* shows that q divides the index (p-1)/ord_p(a) precisely under these conditions.

## Role in the source argument

Translates a prime divisor of a multiplicative-order index into a splitting event suitable for prime counting.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Pieter Moree, Artin's primitive root conjecture: a survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0412262), section 4, page 8 of the retrieved PDF, gives this same criterion and reduction. The selected source argument is a reuse of classical background, not a new criterion.

## Checks and limitations

Exhaustive small-prime tests compare the congruences, the number of distinct roots of T^q-a modulo p and divisibility of the multiplicative-order index.

Finite-field tests do not prove the analytic splitting-prime estimates used elsewhere in the source.

## Possible applications

Turning failure of primitive-root generation into algebraic splitting conditions.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
