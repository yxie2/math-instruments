# Feedback fields combine telescoping Gram bounds with power-sum Fourier sparsity

[Family 283](../families/openai-math-283.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000137.md) · [Batch B016](../../batches/B016/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let V=[A B; C D] be unitary on k port and s internal coordinates, d=k+s, with ||D||<=r<1. For diagonal unitary Z, set X=(I-DZ)^(-1)C, F=A+BZX, E=[I;ZX], O=[F;X], R=OF*. Then VE=O and conservation of norm makes F unitary. Also R=[I;Y] with Y=(I-Z*D*)^(-1)Z*B*, and FE*=R*V.

Truncate the lower blocks at L terms to get E_L and R_L. Each tail has norm at most tau=r^L/(1-r). The maps x -> average-normalized sum_z |z> E_L(z)*x, and similarly R_L, have Gram matrices between (1-r^(2L))I and I whenever averaging includes a common root-of-unity phase of order greater than L. Indeed, writing Z=omega Z', different lengths become orthogonal, and

$$\sum_{\ell=0}^{L-1}W^\ell(I-WW^*)(W^*)^\ell=I-W^L(W^*)^L.$$

Here W=DZ' for E and W=Z'*D* for R. The pointwise relation gives an intertwining error at most 2 tau for the field maps.

For d<=2^n choose b=nL+ceil(log2(L+1)), G=(Z/2^b Z)^(L+1), v_i=(1,i,...,i^L), and Z_ii(z)=chi_(v_i)(z). A sum of at most L such vectors determines its multiset: no coordinate wraps modulo 2^b, the first records length, and Newton identities recover the monic root polynomial from the power sums. Every nonzero Fourier monomial of E_L* or R_L* contains the phase of its internal input index. A fixed frequency therefore permits only input indices in one multiset of size at most L. The zero-frequency row has only its matching port column. Thus every Fourier row has at most L nonzero entries.

## Role in the source argument

Creates nearly isometric embeddings into a field of smaller unitary problems while retaining short row support for coherent input erasure.

## Antecedents and comparison

The feedback construction is in the classical unitary-colligation framework; see [Fritzsche-Katsnelson-Kirstein, Section 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0805.4732v1). Newton identities and Fourier orthogonality are standard. The phase-code and field-encoding combination is the candidate interface extracted here.

## Checks and limitations

Read the feedback, Gram and support proofs. Finite unitary blocks check unitarity, tails, averaged Gram bounds and intertwining. Exact multiset enumeration checks absence of modular collisions and the allowed input indices at each frequency.

These tests do not establish a contraction block for every target unitary. The phase group can be enormous; efficient implementation depends on the separate oracle and slot model.

## Possible applications

Turning paths through an internal linear system into short Fourier support with quantitative norm control.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
