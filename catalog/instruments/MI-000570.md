# Absorb a countable bit matrix with finite row and column translations

[Family 240](../families/openai-math-240.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000339.md) · [Batch B059](../../batches/B059/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let a(n,k),a'(n,k) be binary matrices indexed by natural numbers, and put d=a+a' modulo two. Allocate the discrepancy by

U_n={k<=n:d(n,k)=1}, and V_k={n<k:d(n,k)=1}.

Every U_n and V_k is finite, although there is no uniform bound on their sizes. The two regions partition the matrix, so 1_(k in U_n)+1_(n in V_k)=d(n,k).

In the source, row fibers are torsors for the finite subsets of a countable base order B and column fibers are torsors for the finite subsets of omega. Their bit relation obeys e(z+u,w+v)=e(z,w)+1_(b in u)+1_(n in v). After matching two countable dense orders without endpoints by back-and-forth, enumerate one base as b_k. Translate target row representatives by the images of U_n, and target column representatives by V_k. Their bits now agree with the source representatives. Extending equivariantly across each torsor preserves every bit, since both additional indicator terms are carried along by the same coordinate map.

The fiber systems for different parameter tuples are disjoint and have no additional cross-relations. Thus these local maps combine with any parameter-set bijection to give the source isomorphism for countable bases. The finiteness is per row or column; this construction does not supply an analogous uncountable decomposition.

## Role in the source argument

Explains why the source countable-base structures lose all apparent freedom in their bit matrices.

## Antecedents and comparison

The allocation is the elementary lower/upper triangular decomposition of a countable array, combined with classical dense-order back-and-forth. No earliest-priority claim is made for this torsor implementation.

## Checks and limitations

48 rectangular windows verify the split and 9,504 arbitrary translated torsor bits. Support sizes satisfy the explicit row and column bounds.

Finite windows do not prove countable back-and-forth or the source categoricity tail. Those selected countable arguments were read analytically; uncountable base bounds remain unaudited.

## Possible applications

Removing countable arrays of local discrepancies when each row and column permits only finitely supported changes.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
