# Recover vertex potentials from a vanishing cyclic sum

[Family 133](../families/openai-math-133.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000468.md) · [Batch B074](../../batches/B074/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let r>=3, let each A_i be nonempty, and let g_i:A_i x A_(i+1)->F2, with cyclic indices. If sum_i g_i(a_i,a_(i+1))=0 for every full address in the product, there are Y_i:A_i->F2 with g_i(u,v)=Y_i(u)+Y_(i+1)(v).

Fix a base address b. Taking the rectangular difference in coordinates i,i+1 kills every term except g_i, so g_i(u,v)=c_i+alpha_i(u)+gamma_i(v), where c_i=g_i(b_i,b_(i+1)) and the deviations vanish at their base points. Varying coordinate i alone gives alpha_i=gamma_(i-1); evaluating at b gives sum c_i=0. Choose C_1 arbitrarily and solve C_i+C_(i+1)=c_i around the cycle; over F2 the final equation holds exactly because the constants sum to zero. Set Y_i=alpha_i+C_i.

Singleton factors cause no difficulty. The full-product hypothesis allows independent rectangular variations, and r>=3 ensures there is no second edge depending on the same pair of coordinates. For r=2, two equal nonseparable tables can cancel in the global sum, so the conclusion need not hold.

## Role in the source argument

Upgrades consistent image sums to actual offsets on shared graph readouts.

## Antecedents and comparison

This is an elementary mixed-difference and potential decomposition. Exact priority of its use in this graph encoding is not established; no new general cohomology theorem is claimed.

## Checks and limitations

Exhaustive binary tables on a three-cycle verify every zero-sum instance and reconstruct its potentials. Additional singleton examples and a two-cycle counterexample test the hypotheses.

The local identity does not verify the source global isomorphism criterion or EXPTIME reduction.

## Possible applications

Patching local pairwise data when a global cancellation identity holds on every independent coordinate choice.
