# Logarithmic variogram domination extends smooth limits to finite-energy signed measures

[Family 225](../families/openai-math-225.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000096.md) · [Batch B006](../../batches/B006/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Suppose a centered height field has stationary lattice increments, variogram $V(a)=4\kappa\log(1\vee|a|)+O(1)$ uniformly in lattice displacement a, convergence of separated increment covariances to those of $K_\kappa(x,y)=-2\kappa\log|x-y|$, and a Gaussian limit on smooth compactly supported neutral tests. The source extends this limit to every compactly supported finite signed measure mu of mass zero satisfying

$$\iint\log_+\frac1{|x-y|}\,|\mu|(dx)|\mu|(dy)<\infty.$$

This is ordinary absolute integrability for the total variation product; it implies that mu is nonatomic. Mixed absolute logarithmic integrals are finite for any two such measures, by dyadic-square Cauchy-Schwarz, first over cells and then over scales.

For arbitrary real mesh delta, rounded lattice points obey

$$|V(\lfloor x/\delta\rfloor-\lfloor y/\delta\rfloor)-4\kappa\log\delta^{-1}|\le C(1+|\log|x-y||).$$

The bound includes distinct points rounded to the same face. The covariance of two increments is a four-variogram combination, so its four divergent constants cancel. Represent a neutral measure by differences integrated against its equal-mass Jordan parts; the resulting four-logarithm bound is integrable. Dominated convergence gives all pairing covariances.

Mollification $\mu_\epsilon=\mu*\rho_\epsilon$ converges in logarithmic energy. Applying the covariance limit to $\mu-\mu_\epsilon$ gives $\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow0}\limsup_{\delta\downarrow0}\|\langle h_\delta,\mu-\mu_\epsilon\rangle\|_2=0$. The smooth Gaussian limit and the same approximation for the limiting field then identify the joint law for every finite family of these measures.

## Role in the source argument

Extends a distributional height limit to rough but finite-energy observables without treating point evaluations as finite-variance GFF variables.

## Antecedents and comparison

Mollification in logarithmic energy and approximation in L2 are established potential-theoretic methods. The source packages them with a lattice-rounding bound and ordinary signed integrability in the six-vertex setting. The related [DKLM theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06268v1) is an antecedent and input, not a new theorem attributed to this extraction.

## Checks and limitations

Read the dyadic mixed-energy proof, four-variogram cancellation and mollification argument. Finite positive covariance examples verify the increment and neutral-pairing identities; rounding examples include distinct continuum points that land in the same cell.

A neutral atomic measure generally fails the absolute-energy hypothesis. Smooth-test convergence alone is insufficient: the uniform variogram majorant and separated covariance limit are required. Finite covariance tests do not prove those model-specific estimates.

## Possible applications

Passing scaling limits from smooth tests to line, curve or singular measures whose absolute logarithmic energy is finite.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
