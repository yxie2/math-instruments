# Affine sign-pattern counting that retains boundary equalities

[Family 160](../families/openai-math-160.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000151.md) · [Batch B020](../../batches/B020/README.md)

## Mathematical content

For s affine hyperplanes in R^e, the number of realized joint signs in {-,0,+} is at most

$$\sum_{d=0}^e\binom{s}{d}\sum_{j=0}^{e-d}\binom{s}{j}\le(e+1)^2(s+1)^e.$$

Strict sign vectors correspond to convex regions of the complement. Adding a hyperplane splits at most as many regions as the induced arrangement on it has, so induction gives sum_(j=0)^e binom(s,j).

For a realized vector with zero signs, intersect all its zero hyperplanes. If this nonempty affine space A has codimension d, choose d equations with independent normals defining A; consistency makes the remaining zero equations consequences. There are at most binom(s,d) choices. Hyperplanes containing A force zero signs; the others give strict signs in dimension e-d. Summing the strict-region bound over choices and d proves the formula. Each term is bounded by (s+1)^e, giving the displayed coarse estimate.

Repeated hyperplanes do not create new regions, and restricting the parameter space to any subset only removes sign vectors. This is useful when half-open interval labels must be counted exactly, rather than up to a measure-zero boundary.

## Role in the source argument

Bounds literal label words in a low-dimensional parameter space even when the ambient number of coordinates is large.

## Antecedents and comparison

The strict-region recurrence and general-position formula are classical; see [Stanley, Lemma 2.1 and Proposition 2.4](https://www.cis.upenn.edu/~cis6100/sp06stanley.pdf). The extracted proof explicitly partitions by the rank of zero equations. No new arrangement theorem is claimed.

## Checks and limitations

Read the full proof and the prior general-position formula. The verification program enumerates ternary sign patterns in small arrangements, testing strict feasibility by linear programming, with repeated and dependent hyperplanes.

The program uses numerical linear programming; it is a consistency check, not an exact proof of arbitrary real feasibility. The upper bound is intentionally coarse and does not establish the source coloring construction.

## Possible applications

Counting discretized parameter families without losing equality cases at cell boundaries.
