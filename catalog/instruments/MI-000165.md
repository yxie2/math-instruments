# A finite reflecting lattice lift transfers Markov type to odd edge sums

[Family 327](../families/openai-math-327.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000105.md) · [Batch B008](../../batches/B008/README.md)

## Mathematical content

A finite marked chain has an involution e->e* reversing the source and target of each edge, and edge probabilities satisfying $\pi(e^-)q(e)=\pi(e^+)q(e^*)$. Start in pi. If a normed space V has Markov type p with constant K and $g(e^*)=-g(e)$, then

$$\mathbb E\left\|\sum_{i=1}^n g(E_i)\right\|^p\le K^p n\,\mathbb E\|g(E_1)\|^p.$$

The labels need not be differences of a function on the original states. Choose one edge from each two-element reversal orbit, r in total, and assign its two orientations lattice displacements plus/minus the corresponding basis vector in Z^r. A fixed point of reversal has zero label and displacement. Lift the chain to S times Q_R, where $Q_R=\{-R,\ldots,R\}^r$ and R>n. Reject any move leaving the box by staying at the full state.

Accepted transitions satisfy detailed balance for pi times uniform(Q_R); rejections add only holding probabilities. The potential $\Phi(s,a)=\sum_j a_jg(e_j)$ has increment g(e) on accepted moves and zero on rejections. Its one-step cost is no larger than the original label cost. If the initial lattice point lies in Q_(R-n), every n-step proposal sequence is accepted. This event is independent of the original marked trajectory and has probability $\alpha_R=((2(R-n)+1)/(2R+1))^r$. Therefore the lifted n-step potential cost is at least alpha_R times the desired edge-sum cost. Apply Markov type and let R tend to infinity.

The same bound holds if the finite span of the labels is finitely representable in a Markov-type space: transfer through embeddings with distortion tending to one. Enlarging the lattice box does not enlarge that span.

## Antecedents and comparison

The definition and smooth-space context come from [Naor, Peres, Schramm and Sheffield](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0410422). The lift is a finite-box realization of an additive path coordinate with reversible boundary rejection. No new general Markov-type definition or prior smooth-space theorem is attributed to the source.

## Checks and limitations

Read the detailed-balance and independent-interior-event argument. Exhaustive short reversible walks with non-gradient labels check the Hilbert p=2 bound; explicit box transitions check balance and the potential increments. A directed-cycle control violates the bound when reversibility is removed.

Stationarity and edgewise reversibility are required. Reversibility only of the unmarked state chain does not suffice if the marks have incompatible probabilities. The construction grows rapidly with the number of edge orbits and is used as a proof device.

## Possible applications

Controlling cocycle-like sums, currents and marked transition observables using a metric inequality for vertex maps.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
