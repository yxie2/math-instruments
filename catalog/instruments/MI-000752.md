# Keep failed observations in the filtration when rolling back an adaptive computation

[Family 261](../families/openai-math-261.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000503.md) · [Batch B081](../../batches/B081/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Start with independent fair binary digits, independent auxiliary randomness and uniformly random orders. Query indices are measurable with respect to retained past information. Conditional on that history, unqueried digits remain independent fair, unused auxiliary variables retain their product law, and the rest of each random order is uniformly ordered among its remaining entries.

The induction first checks finite cylinder events. A query of an entire remaining binary suffix is handled by increasing finite cylinders and a monotone-class argument. Installing an exact previously represented random value reveals its true suffix at its first exact query; it does not create a fresh independent copy. Choices or limits measurable from retained answers add no new information. An almost surely finite stopping time is treated by partitioning an event according to its stopping value.

Failed trials may roll back the computed numerical state, but all their answers and the decisions they caused must stay in the sigma-field. Otherwise the advertised conditional product law can fail: a coin already observed to be zero is not fair when a retry reaches it.

## Role in the source argument

Reusing a large random environment in an adaptive argument without silently resampling previously observed information.

## Antecedents and comparison

This is the classical deferred-decisions principle with explicit bookkeeping for suffix queries, random orders, stopping times and rollback. No new probability independence theorem is claimed.

## Checks and limitations

Exact finite adaptive query trees verify the conditional product law and remaining-order law at every history and stopping leaf. A forgotten-failure control exhibits the biased reused digit.

Finite tests do not validate the source infinite algorithm, its termination or its spectral consequences. Arbitrary infinite stopping is not covered.

## Possible applications

Reusing a large random environment in an adaptive argument without silently resampling previously observed information.
