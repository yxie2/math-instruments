# A reciprocal-logarithm potential pays for overlapping recursive pieces

[Family 181](../families/openai-math-181.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000121.md) · [Batch B012](../../batches/B012/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let T>=4 and epsilon=1/32. A binary recursive split of a node of order u>T has positive child orders u1,u2 satisfying u1<=3u/4, u2<u and u1+u2<=u+epsilon*u1/log_2(u)^2. The total order can increase because the two pieces share vertices. Define

$$f(x)=1+\frac1{\log_2(T/2)}-\frac1{\log_2(\max\{x,T/2\})}.$$

Then 1<=f<=2 and u1 f(u1)+u2 f(u2)<=u f(u). Indeed max(u1,T/2)<=3u/4, so

$$f(u)-f(u_1)\ge\frac{\log_2(4/3)}{\log_2(u)^2}.$$

Monotonicity bounds f(u2) by f(u); the excess mass costs at most epsilon*u1*f(u)/log_2(u)^2, which is absorbed since epsilon*f(u)<=1/16<log_2(4/3). Telescoping through the tree yields total leaf order at most n f(n)<=n(1+1/log_2(T/2)). This counts repeated vertices at each leaf separately.

In the source, a sparse vertex boundary B of U creates pieces U union B and V minus U; small |B| supplies the excess-mass hypothesis. The potential statement itself only needs the displayed size conditions and a finite terminating split tree.

## Role in the source argument

Keeps the total size of overlapping boxes close to the original graph order before the later decomposition stages.

## Antecedents and comparison

Sparse-cut recursion and small-side charging already appear in [Conlon, Fox and Sudakov, Lemma 3.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1310.0632v1). This card isolates the source potential for repeated vertex multiplicity; it is not a novelty claim for sparse-cut decomposition itself.

## Checks and limitations

Read the reciprocal-log difference and telescoping argument. Sampled valid child sizes and complete finite recursive trees check subadditivity and the total leaf bound, including nonzero overlaps.

The precise decay of the allowed overlap and the fixed shrinkage of one child matter. Bounding only the number of levels would lose the sharp multiplicity estimate.

## Possible applications

Recursive decompositions whose pieces share a small boundary, with total volume measured with multiplicity.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
