# Turn a weighted Laplacian estimate into an inverse-weight variance bound

[Family 348](../families/openai-math-348.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000520.md) · [Batch B085](../../batches/B085/README.md)

## Mathematical content

On a closed connected manifold with normalized volume, suppose K is bounded above and below by positive constants and E[(Delta phi)^2]>=E[K|grad phi|^2] for all smooth phi. Then Var(a)<=E[|grad a|^2/K] for a in H^1.

Solve Delta phi=a-Ea with zero mean. Integration by parts expresses Var(a)=-E<grad a,grad phi>. Weighted Cauchy-Schwarz bounds this by the square root of E[|grad a|^2/K] times the square root of E[K|grad phi|^2], which is at most Var(a)^(1/2). Divide when nonzero and pass by H^1 density.

In the source Ric=3g and K=4+v^2+b^2, where v=|W_+|/sqrt(6), b=|W_-|/sqrt(6) in its endomorphism normalization. Its preceding curvature contraction and the nonnegative-sectional-curvature bound supply the required Laplacian inequality. This card isolates the duality step and treats that geometric inequality as an input.

## Role in the source argument

Turning a differential coercivity estimate into a usable variance bound while preserving spatially varying gains.

## Antecedents and comparison

Poisson duality and weighted Cauchy-Schwarz are classical. The source-specific proposed weight rests on the curvature identities, which are not independently certified by the finite analogue.

## Checks and limitations

Connected weighted-graph Laplacians check the analogous operator inequality and inverse-edge-weight variance conclusion for random vectors. A weight exceeding the spectral bound gives a failing control.

The graph calculation does not certify the manifold curvature contraction, and the conclusion cannot be asserted for an arbitrary positive K without the stated operator inequality.
