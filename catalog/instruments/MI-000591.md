# Turn stable-length polarization into a character on a centralizer

[Family 254](../families/openai-math-254.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000360.md) · [Batch B061](../../batches/B061/README.md)

## Mathematical content

For commuting CAT(0) isometries, stable length ell(g)=lim d(o,g^n o)/n defines a seminorm on their exponent vectors whose square is quadratic. Apply the CAT(0) quadrilateral inequality to orbit parallelograms, divide by n^2 and pass to limits. Applying the resulting inequality again to u+v,u-v gives equality in the parallelogram law. Polarization gives <g,h>=(ell(gh)^2-ell(g)^2-ell(h)^2)/2.

For a geometric action on a proper CAT(0) space and fixed g, h-><g,h> is a homomorphism on C_G(g), even for h_1,h_2 that do not commute. If ell(g)=0, nonnegativity of the quadratic in the integer exponent of g forces the pairing to vanish. Otherwise put D=ell(g)>0 and choose x on an axis. The limit b(z)=lim_n(d(z,g^n x)-nD) exists, is 1-Lipschitz, and the quadrilateral inequality shows b(hz)-b(hw)=b(z)-b(w) for h commuting with g. Thus chi(h)=b(x)-b(hx) is additive, chi(g)=D and |chi(h)|<=ell(h). Applying this bound to g^t h for both signs of arbitrarily large integer t forces D chi(h)=<g,h>.

The geometric-action assumption supplies semisimplicity and an axis for positive stable length. The elementary commuting-family quadratic statement requires less.

## Role in the source argument

Allows a braid-length calculation to add pairings across noncommuting centralizer elements.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Rodenhausen, Centralisers of polynomially growing automorphisms of free groups](https://hdl.handle.net/20.500.11811/5725), Theorem 14.6 and proof on pages 136-137, gives the parallelogram law for infimal displacement of commuting complete-CAT(0) isometries; those pages were read. The source works with stable length and spells out the standard Busemann centralizer character. No earliest-priority claim is made for this interface.

## Checks and limitations

Eighty examples in the crystallographic group Z times (Z^2 semidirect D4) compute stable lengths from finite-order linear parts and verify centralizer additivity, including noncommuting pairs.

Euclidean models check the identities, not the general CAT(0) limit argument or the 116-generator obstruction.

## Possible applications

Extracting abelian numerical constraints from centralizers that are themselves nonabelian.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
