# Use ICC to recover a full commensuration-germ stabilizer

[Family 286](../families/openai-math-286.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000367.md) · [Batch B062](../../batches/B062/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let Gamma and Lambda be countable ICC groups. A germ of an isomorphism between finite-index subgroups identifies maps that agree on a further finite-index subgroup. The action of Gamma times Lambda is (g,h)d=Ad(h) d Ad(g)^(-1). For a representative delta:A->B, its graph fixes d. The full stabilizer C_d is itself the graph of a uniquely defined extension delta_d:A_d->B_d, with finite-index domains, and contains the original graph with finite index.

The key point is that an element centralizing a finite-index subgroup has a finite conjugacy class; ICC forces it to be identity. Thus the map from a group element to its inner germ is injective. The two coordinate projections of C_d are injective, which gives the graph description and extension. If a finite-index subgroup of C_d fixes another germ x, compare representatives on a common finite-index subgroup. The equality of inner germs forces their values to agree there, so x=d. Hence the only finite C_d-orbit in the germ space is {d}; the same holds for the presenting graph. The graph of a restricted representative need not already be the full stabilizer.

## Role in the source argument

Makes germ labels intrinsic before classifying intertwiners and composing correspondences.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Vaes, Explicit computations of all finite index bimodules for a family of II1 factors](https://arxiv.org/abs/0707.1458), Definitions 6.6-6.8 and Theorem 6.9, supplies prior commensuration, projective induction and fusion calculus. PDF pages 33-34 were read. This is an antecedent comparison, not verification that the exact abstract-germ lemma is stated there.

## Checks and limitations

Reduced free words check inner-action separation on powers of two generators in bounded windows. A finite-group control shows that inner germs need not be injective without ICC. The general finite-index argument is analytical.

No finite test certifies all finite-index subgroups of an ICC group or the arithmetic bimodule classification.

## Possible applications

Avoiding duplicate or noncanonical labels caused by choosing too small a representative subgroup.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
