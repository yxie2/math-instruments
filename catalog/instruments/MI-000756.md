# Retain thermal coherences in a nonnegative purification and compare by a common measure

[Family 267](../families/openai-math-267.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000505.md) · [Batch B081](../../batches/B081/README.md)

## Mathematical content

For a bosonic nonnegative heat kernel B_t^sym and partition function Z_beta, define Phi(X,W)=Z_beta^(-1/2)B_(beta/2)^sym(X,W). Semigroup composition and the commuting symmetry projection give integral Phi^2=1 and Gamma(X,Xbar)=integral Phi(X,W)Phi(Xbar,W)dW. The constant-orbital occupation fraction is
(1/V) integral |integral Phi(x,X',W) dx|^2 dX' dW.
Keeping W when cutting a thermal path slab preserves this off-diagonal information; replacing Phi by the square root of the position density would generally lose it. The source's slab construction integrates the two outer kernels to B_(beta-delta)^sym, and composition with the central slab recovers Z_beta and the exact thermal time marginals.

For finite measures P,Q with a common absolutely continuous measure nu of mass m, suppose integral (dnu/dP)^2 dP<=C_P and similarly C_Q. Holder with exponents 4,4,2 gives Aff(P,Q)=integral sqrt(dP dQ)>=m^2/sqrt(C_P C_Q). Zeros cause no problem when absolute continuity is respected. Affinity increases under a common Markov kernel and is superadditive under paired sums of measures.

For unit-volume spatial cells v,w the measures with densities V Phi(x,X',W)^2 and V Phi(y,X',W)^2 on x in v,y in w have affinities whose sum divided by V^2 is exactly the occupation fraction. This identity retains the same auxiliary W in both factors.

## Role in the source argument

Transferring a common favorable path submeasure into an occupation bound while retaining mixed-state coherences.

## Antecedents and comparison

Square-root purification, semigroup composition and the Holder affinity estimate are classical. The source combines them into a thermal path comparison interface. No independent priority claim is made for that assembly.

## Checks and limitations

A finite symmetric two-particle heat kernel checks normalization, exact marginals, reduced occupation and the cell affinity sum. General finite measures check the common-measure bound; equal position densities with different coherences control the information lost by diagonal replacement.

Finite heat-kernel checks do not establish the dilute-gas estimates, hard-core path geometry or condensation at positive temperature.

## Possible applications

Transferring a common favorable path submeasure into an occupation bound while retaining mixed-state coherences.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
