# Globalize a locally injective map through its homotopy class

[Family 360](../families/openai-math-360.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000293.md) · [Batch B053](../../batches/B053/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let X and N be nonempty connected compact topological n-manifolds without boundary. If a continuous locally injective map f:X->N is homotopic to a homeomorphism, then f is a homeomorphism.

Invariance of domain makes f a local homeomorphism. Its image is open and compact, hence closed, and connectedness makes it surjective. Every fiber is compact and discrete, hence finite. For a fiber {q_1,...,q_k}, choose disjoint local inverse neighborhoods U_i with images V_i. Removing the image of the compact complement from the intersection of the V_i gives an evenly covered neighborhood. Thus f is a finite covering.

The homotopy identifies f_* with the isomorphism induced by the homeomorphism, up to base-point change, so f_* is surjective. A connected covering has sheet number equal to the index of its image subgroup, hence has one sheet. Equivalently, lifting an image loop and using the surjectivity of f_* rules out two points in one fiber. No orientability assumption or signed degree calculation is needed. Local injectivity alone would allow a multiple-sheet covering.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Hatcher, Algebraic Topology, Chapter 1](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher/AT/ATch1.pdf), Propositions 1.31 and 1.32 on printed page 61, prove the lifting and sheet-index statements; both proofs were read. The selected source supplies the compact local-homeomorphism-to-covering step. This is classical topology.

## Checks and limitations

The general argument was checked analytically. Finite cyclic-cover models illustrate the sheet-index mechanism and show that local injectivity without the homotopy condition permits multiple sheets.

Finite covers do not verify the source geometric local-injectivity assertion for arbitrary potentials or its weak-MTW convexity theorem.

## Possible applications

Replacing a delicate global injectivity calculation by local injectivity and control of a map homotopy class.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
