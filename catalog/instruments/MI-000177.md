# Test a mass Laplacian in its range to avoid an inverse-norm loss

[Family 374](../families/openai-math-374.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000110.md) · [Batch B009](../../batches/B009/README.md)

## Mathematical content

The source uses the following finite-dimensional energy principle. Let m_i>0 sum to one, let G be a symmetric matrix with kernel the constants and range the zero-sum vectors, and let Bq be a vector-valued tuple satisfying

$$\sum_i |(Bq)_i|^2/m_i\le C^2\sum_i|(Gq)_i|^2/m_i.$$

Suppose r has weighted mean zero and, for every q, $\sum_i r_i(Gq)_i=-\sum_i v_i\cdot(Bq)_i$. Choose q with (Gq)_i=m_i r_i; existence follows from the range statement. No estimate of q is needed. Weighted Cauchy-Schwarz gives

$$\|r\|_{\ell_2(m)}^2\le C\|v\|_{\ell_2(m)}\|r\|_{\ell_2(m)},\qquad\|r\|_{\ell_2(m)}\le C\|v\|_{\ell_2(m)}.$$

For Laguerre cells, G is the fixed-site mass Laplacian, B is the first-moment derivative, and C=sqrt(162)R by the preceding instrument. Move sites along the pairs of an optimal finite coupling with fixed masses m_i; normalize intercepts by sum m_i c_i=0. Differentiating the mass constraints gives exactly the displayed dual identity with r_i=c_i' and v_i=y_i'. Thus intercept speed is bounded by sqrt(162)R times target speed, without a smallest-eigenvalue factor.

Optimal-coupling monotonicity ensures distinct sites at every interior time, even when endpoint sites repeat. Integrating the potential derivative x dot v_i+r_i gives suitably normalized endpoint potentials with $\|u_0-u_1\|_{L^2(\rho)}\le(1+\sqrt{162})R W_2(\mu,\nu)$. The earlier convex-gradient interpolation bound then converts a linear potential estimate to a one-third map estimate. That interpolation is credited as an established ingredient, not the new step.

## Role in the source argument

Removes a degenerating inverse-Laplacian constant from the fixed-mass transport path estimate.

## Antecedents and comparison

The [semi-discrete mass Laplacian](https://arxiv.org/html/1603.05579v2) and [convex-gradient interpolation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.05934v2) are established. The selected source mechanism tests the dual constraint against a range preimage and controls only its image. The abstract principle stated here is a direct extraction from that calculation.

## Checks and limitations

Read the range, symmetry and fixed-mass identities. Finite weighted graph Laplacians with a tunably small positive eigenvalue check the energy bound and illustrate how the chosen q can grow while the bound on r remains uniform. Convex cell examples test the actual moment input separately.

Existence of a range preimage and the uniform B-versus-G estimate are both required. A generic ill-conditioned linear system has no such bound. Potential normalization matters; arbitrary additive constants cannot obey the stated potential estimate.

## Possible applications

Constraint sensitivity problems where a derivative factors through a poorly conditioned operator but has a uniform bound in the norm of its image.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
