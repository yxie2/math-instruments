# Use affine side relations to remove a plane-wave eigenfunction

[Family 150](../families/openai-math-150.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000479.md) · [Batch B076](../../batches/B076/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Suppose the analytic part of a billiard argument has supplied F(x,v)=c(v) exp(i lambda x dot v) in the triangle interior, and its phase-removed lift is spatially constant across each regular crossing chart. A side reflection x=Rp+d then forces c(v)=c(Rv) exp(-i lambda d dot v), almost everywhere. This comparison uses open chart regions, so it does not require a boundary trace of F.

Place the origin at an angle alpha with alpha/pi irrational. The two incident sides have d=0, and composing their relations makes c invariant under rotation by 2 alpha. Each Fourier coefficient satisfies (1-exp(2ik alpha)) c_hat(k)=0; only the constant mode survives. The third side has d nonzero. If c is a nonzero constant, continuity makes exp(-i lambda d dot v)=1 for every direction. Since d dot v fills a nontrivial interval, lambda=0.

For an L2 eigenfunction first apply z -> z/(1+|z|), which is bounded, injective and commutes with unit-modulus multiplication. The bounded case then implies the original conclusion. Crucially, side reflections alone do not establish the plane-wave hypothesis.

## Role in the source argument

Separates the short geometric obstruction from the difficult analytic rigidity step.

## Antecedents and comparison

Irrational rotation removes nonzero Fourier modes by a classical argument. The source packages this with the affine phase at the third side and a trace-free crossing-chart comparison; no new Fourier theorem is claimed.

## Checks and limitations

Checks affine reflection phases, composition of two reflections, rational-angle surviving modes as a control, and the bounded equivariant transform.

Finite direction samples cannot prove irrationality or exclude all nonzero frequencies. Transverse rigidity remains a dependency.

## Possible applications

Identifying which analytic reduction is needed before a reflection symmetry can rule out eigenfunctions.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
