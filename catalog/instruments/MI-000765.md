# Upgrade sequential covariance limits to uniform trace estimates

[Family 291](../families/openai-math-291.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000509.md) · [Batch B082](../../batches/B082/README.md)

## Mathematical content

If self-adjoint X_j under states have every scalar moment converging to those of a centered finite-dimensional Gaussian quadratic q_B, their spectral laws converge weakly to that law, without any uniform operator norm bound. Bounded second moments give tightness; for an even 2r>d, the bound integral_(|x|>K)|x|^d<=C_r K^(d-2r) makes moments pass to each weak limit. The Gaussian quadratic has an exponential absolute moment and is moment determinate: equality of even moments gives an exponential moment for any competing law, and analytic characteristic functions then agree.

In the source, assume the ordered joint moment input along EVERY sequence of extreme traces whose finite covariance matrices converge. The fixed polynomial prescription gives scalar quadratic laws and difference second moments. Tracial Duhamel gives ||exp(itX)-exp(itY)||_(2,phi)<=|t| ||X-Y||_(2,phi). The covariance-normalized Gaussian estimates consequently bound each limiting invariance error and each of finitely many Fourier moments, with a strict tolerance margin.

If uniform bounds over extreme traces failed, choose a failing sequence, fix one failed test by a subsequence, and extract a convergent finite covariance matrix from the compact set of positive matrices with diagonal one. The assumed sequential input and strict margin contradict failure. No compact extreme trace boundary and no uniform convergence rate in covariance are required. For fixed N the squared invariance error is continuous affine in the trace and the Fourier absolute value is continuous convex; their closed convex sublevel sets contain every extreme trace, hence all traces by Krein-Milman.

## Role in the source argument

Obtaining one approximation that works for all traces without assuming compactness of the extreme boundary.

## Antecedents and comparison

This combines the classical determinate method of moments, the Duhamel estimate and compactness by contradiction. The source-specific contribution being extracted is the choice of a finite covariance parameter and the order of quantifiers. No new general compactness theorem is claimed.

## Checks and limitations

Growing-norm diagonal examples check bounded continuous spectral tests and higher-moment tail controls. Noncommuting matrices check tracial Duhamel, and convex mixtures check extension of finite error bounds from extreme states.

Finite tests cannot prove the sequential joint-moment input. Pointwise convergence for each fixed trace would not justify the uniform conclusion; moving traces are essential.
