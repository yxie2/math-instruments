# Turn a graded complement into a chain retraction that controls one kernel

[Family 305](../families/openai-math-305.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000511.md) · [Batch B083](../../batches/B083/README.md)

## Mathematical content

For a finite graded complex (V,D) and a degree-zero map S, set N=ker S and K=N intersect D^(-1)N. Then K is a subcomplex. Choose a graded complement N=K plus E. If De lies in N for e in E, then e lies in K and is zero; hence D is injective on E and DE intersects N trivially.

Choose any graded complement C' to K plus E plus DE. Define h(De)=e and h=0 on K plus E plus C'. Let Q=Dh+hD. Its image lies in E plus DE, it is the identity there and vanishes on K. Thus Q^2=Q and DQ=QD. The map r=1-Q is a chain idempotent with r(N)=K, r|K=1 and 1-r=Dh+hD. Its total image is not claimed to equal K: surviving cohomology outside N may remain.

For finitely many such complexes, R=tensor_b r_b is chain homotopic to the identity via H=sum_b r_<b tensor h_b tensor 1_>b, with sign (-1)^(sum of degrees before b) on homogeneous tensors. Cross-slot differential terms cancel and the remaining terms telescope to DH+HD=1-R. A plain arbitrary vector-space projection would not ensure this chain identity.

## Role in the source argument

Improving factors to lie in a differential-compatible kernel while preserving cohomology and tensor pairings.

## Antecedents and comparison

This is the standard removal of a contractible pair, tailored to the subspace ker S without requiring DS=SD. The signed tensor homotopy is classical. Priority of this exact kernel-compatible formulation is not established.

## Checks and limitations

Exact rational graded examples check r^2=r, Dr=rD, r(N)=K and the homotopy, including complements whose differential has extra components. Signed tensor products check telescoping and reject a missing-sign variant.

The construction is not a deformation retraction of all V onto K. It does not establish the source geometric hypotheses or obstruction theorem.

## Possible applications

Improving factors to lie in a differential-compatible kernel while preserving cohomology and tensor pairings.
