# Control interval endpoint defects using the sign of midpoint displacement

[Family 370](../families/openai-math-370.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000181.md) · [Batch B027](../../batches/B027/README.md)

## Mathematical content

For n>=3 and transverse separation lambda>0, let K(s,s') be a positive constant times (lambda^2+(s-s')^2)^{(2-n)/2} and D=(s-s')^2/(lambda^2+(s-s')^2). For bounded intervals I,J let bar(K)_I average the two I-endpoint values, and similarly for J. For nonnegative c_I,c_J,

$$\int(c_I+c_J)K\le\int\left[c_I\bar K_I+c_J\bar K_J+((n-2)\min(c_I,c_J)D+|c_I-c_J|)K\right].$$

All integrals are over I times J. Put h=s-s' and d_0=m_I-m_J. Two integrations by parts give the exact identity

$$\int(2K-\bar K_I-\bar K_J)=(n-2)\int\frac{h^2-d_0h}{\lambda^2+h^2}K.$$

The pushforward density of h is rho(h)=F(|h-d_0|), where F(r)=max(0,min(|I|,|J|,(|I|+|J|)/2-r)). Since F decreases, pairing h and -h proves d_0 int hK/(lambda^2+h^2)>=0. Multiply the resulting equal-coefficient estimate by min(c_I,c_J); bound each remaining endpoint defect by int K to obtain the formula.

The same nonnegative inequality permits an unbounded interval only when its own coefficient is zero. That endpoint term is defined as zero without evaluating endpoints. If at most one coefficient is positive, the |c_I-c_J| term already proves the inequality pointwise. This avoids an invalid infinity-minus-infinity manipulation, particularly in dimension three.

Supplies a geometric comparison independent of the PDE for the source pressure estimate.

## Antecedents and comparison

The proof uses classical integration by parts and symmetry of interval convolution. Its explicit midpoint term prevents the erroneous shortcut of assuming the intervals are centered together.

## Checks and limitations

175 bounded interval pairs in dimensions three through nine satisfy the identity and weighted inequality by quadrature; maximum identity error is below 6e-15.

Quadrature is not a universal proof. Unbounded cases are handled only by the written nonnegative argument and the zero-coefficient convention.

## Possible applications

Comparing linewise potential interactions when interval lengths, centers and weights differ.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
