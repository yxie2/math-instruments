# Measure calibration error by a decreasing raywise sequence

[Family 212](../families/openai-math-212.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000484.md) · [Batch B077](../../batches/B077/README.md)

## Mathematical content

For a geodesic ray p_0,p_1,... in a metric T, define a_j(x)=T(x,p_j)-T(p_0,p_j). The geodesic property and triangle inequality make this sequence decreasing and bounded below by -T(p_0,x). Let a_infinity(x) be its limit. Then B_p(x,y)=a_infinity(x)-a_infinity(y) is the limit of T(x,p_j)-T(y,p_j), is additive and antisymmetric, and satisfies |B_p(x,y)|<=T(x,y).

On the ray, B_p(p_i,p_j)=T(p_i,p_j) for i<=j. Removing a finite prefix leaves B unchanged, so coalescing rays have the same function. Moreover B_p(x,p_j)=a_infinity(x)+T(p_0,p_j), giving the exact nonnegative defect
T(x,p_j)-B_p(x,p_j)=a_j(x)-a_infinity(x) ->0.

These are deterministic consequences of a minimizing ray. They do not imply linearity of B, coalescence of arbitrary rays, or absence of a two-sided geodesic.

## Role in the source argument

Provides calibrated potentials and a quantitative error term for later path comparisons.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Ahlberg and Hoffman, arXiv:1609.02447](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.02447), Lemma 4.1 on page 10 and its proof on page 11 of the retrieved PDF, were read. They contain the monotone-limit argument and basic Busemann properties. The displayed defect is an immediate bookkeeping consequence; it is not a new existence theorem.

## Checks and limitations

Exact tree metrics check additivity, calibration and tail invariance; Euclidean rays exhibit positive defects decreasing to zero. A nongeodesic path gives a monotonicity failure control.

Finite models do not test the probabilistic geometric structure used by the source headline theorem.

## Possible applications

Tracking how close a finite route is to being calibrated by its asymptotic direction.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
