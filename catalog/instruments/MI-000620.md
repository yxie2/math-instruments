# Approximate uniformly locally integrable envelopes by Gaussians

[Family 080](../families/openai-math-080.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000389.md) · [Batch B064](../../batches/B064/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let h>=0 on R² with A=sup_z integral_(B(z,1)) h<infinity, and K_a(z)=(4pi a)^(-1)exp(-|z|²/(4a)). Then K_a*h is finite at every point for every a>0 and converges to h at every Lebesgue point as a decreases to zero. Global L1 integrability is unnecessary.

Cover dyadic annuli beyond radius rho by O((1+2^ell rho)²) unit balls. Their contribution is at most C_rho A a^(-1)exp(-rho²/(8a)) for small a. At a Lebesgue point, H_x(r)=integral_(|z|<r)|h(x-z)-h(x)|=o(r²). Radial integration by parts against the decreasing Gaussian bounds the nearby contribution by C epsilon once H_x(r)<=epsilon r². The far contribution tends to zero.

The source uses countably many such error envelopes M_n for a time-continuous representative v and smooth approximants. If their common pointwise errors satisfy M_n(x)->0 and the smooth Gaussian errors R_n(a)->0 uniformly in time, positivity gives sup_t|K_a*v_t(x)-v_t(x)|<=K_a*M_n(x)+M_n(x)+R_n(a). First let a tend to zero, then n tend to infinity. This is conditional on the existence and uniform local mass bounds of those envelopes; it does not prove the preceding maximal estimate.

## Role in the source argument

Controls Gaussian tails and avoids an uncountable intersection of time-dependent exceptional sets.

## Antecedents and comparison

The lemma is a classical heat approximate-identity argument extended from global L1 to a uniform local mass class. The source spells out the annular estimate and the order of limits for time envelopes.

## Checks and limitations

Periodic nonintegrable-in-total-mass functions and a locally integrable singular profile check Gaussian convolution limits, normalization and exponentially small distant tails.

Model convolutions do not establish the endpoint maximal estimate, the error envelopes or all-time Schrodinger convergence.

## Possible applications

Passing from positive local error envelopes to a common pointwise Gaussian limit.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
