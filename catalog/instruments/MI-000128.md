# A fixed-point reweighting turns average electrical localization into uniform row control

[Family 094](../families/openai-math-094.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000087.md) · [Batch B003](../../batches/B003/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Use the normalized-gradient space $V$ above. For each positive pair probability $\lambda$, there is a probability $\sigma\ge\lambda/2$ such that the orthogonal projection $P$ onto $V$ in $\ell_2(\sigma)$ satisfies

$$\|P\|_{\infty\to\infty}=\max_e\sum_f|P_{ef}|\le4\log(2n).$$

Both $P$ and $I-P$ remain contractions in $\ell_2(\sigma)$. To obtain the weights, let $R_e(\sigma)=\sum_f|P_{ef}(\sigma)|$ and $L=2\log n$. The established electrical localization bound implies $\sum_e\sigma_eR_e^2\le L^2$. On $\{\sigma:\sum\sigma=1,\ \sigma\ge\lambda/2\}$ define

$$u_e(\sigma)=\frac{\sigma_eR_e(\sigma)^2}{2L^2},\qquad
\Phi_e(\sigma)=u_e(\sigma)+(1-\sum_fu_f(\sigma))\lambda_e.$$

This is a continuous self-map because $\sum u\le1/2$. At a Brouwer fixed point, $u_e\le\sigma_e$ gives $R_e\le\sqrt2L$, which is the stated bound. The positive lower weights keep the projection’s Gram matrix invertible on the compact domain.

## Role in the source argument

Projects clipped or truncated random increments back onto increments arising from consistent point labels, controlling both the L2 error and the coordinate magnitude.

## Antecedents and comparison

The logarithmic bound for the entrywise absolute transfer-current matrix is the theorem of [Gurel-Gurevich, Nachmias and Sachdeva](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24130v1). This card extracts the subsequent weight selection, not that established localization theorem as a new discovery. Brouwer reweighting is an established general technique; priority for this particular projection formulation is unresolved.

## Checks and limitations

The proof of the self-map and its row estimate was checked. Finite complete-graph examples verify the conjugation to a Euclidean projection, the weighted average row identity and the fixed-point inequalities at numerical solutions.

Numerical fixed-point iteration is only a check on examples: Brouwer’s theorem does not imply convergence of the naive iteration. The bound concerns this graph-gradient projection and does not hold automatically for arbitrary subspaces.

## Possible applications

A template for turning an averaged matrix bound into simultaneous row bounds while preserving a prescribed fraction of every original test weight.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
