# Compare parity lifts by binary solvability and expose a dual witness

[Family 133](../families/openai-math-133.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000467.md) · [Batch B074](../../batches/B074/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let tau:G->T be a homomorphism to a connected finite simple type graph with positive degrees. In X_b, a vertex over u is a binary tag z on every neighbor type of tau(u), with sum z=b(tau(u)). Adjacency requires a base edge and equality of the two tags on the opposite types. Each fiber has 2^(degree-1) tags, independently of parity.

Fix phi:F->G and put a=tau phi. Its lifts are solutions in source-indexed variables z_(x,a') of vertex equations sum_(a') z_(x,a')=b(a(x)) and edge equations z_(x,a(y))+z_(y,a(x))=0. The coefficient matrix is independent of b. At b=0 its kernel is nonempty; at a twist on type 1 the solution set is either empty or a kernel coset. Summing over projections gives hom(F,X0)>=hom(F,Xstar), with strictness exactly when at least one twisted system is inconsistent. Other projections cannot cancel that deficit.

Binary duality says inconsistency is witnessed by vertex weights s and edge weights r such that sum_(y adjacent x, a(y)=a') r(xy)=s(x) for every neighbor type a', and sum_(a(x)=1)s(x)=1. For adjacent types, summing the same edge weights from either side equates their total vertex weights. Connectedness therefore forces every type mass to be one. Variables at distinct source vertices remain distinct even if phi maps them to the same base vertex.

## Role in the source argument

Turns a graph-count inequality into a local parity witness suitable for a later separator argument.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Roberson, arXiv:2206.10321v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10321v1), Theorem 3.6 and Lemma 3.8 on pages 11-12, give the corresponding lift-count comparison and binary duality. Both were read, including the theorem proof. The source keeps an additional base vertex over each template type; the mechanism is explicitly an antecedent, not a new counting principle.

## Checks and limitations

Small graphs compare direct homomorphism enumeration, projected binary-system counts and dual certificates, including repeated projected images. Type masses are checked for every enumerated dual witness.

The tests do not establish bounded-treewidth choice extraction or WL/complexity claims.

## Possible applications

Preventing cancellation when aggregating many possible projections of a combinatorial witness.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
