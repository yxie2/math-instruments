# Detect a persistent obstruction in almost-commuting clock and shift matrices

[Family 289](../families/openai-math-289.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000369.md) · [Batch B062](../../batches/B062/README.md)

## Mathematical content

For n>=3, let omega=exp(2 pi i/n), U e_j=omega^j e_j and V e_j=e_(j+1 mod n). Then UV=omega VU and ||UV-VU||=|omega-1| tends to zero. For unitaries whose multiplicative commutator W=UVU*V* has no eigenvalue -1, put kappa=(2 pi i)^(-1)Tr Log(W), with the principal logarithm. Since det W=1, kappa is integer and is constant along paths avoiding the logarithm cut. For the clock and shift it is 1; for commuting unitaries it is 0.

If commuting approximants approached these pairs in norm, connect corresponding endpoints by unitary logarithm paths. Their distances from the original endpoints are bounded by the endpoint distances. Along the paths, the norm of W-I is bounded by the original commutator norm plus twice each endpoint displacement, so it is eventually less than 2. The index cannot change, contradicting 1 versus 0. This proves an asymptotic obstruction without optimizing the numerical separation constant.

## Role in the source argument

Supplies a matrix obstruction later inserted into a near-inclusion construction.

## Antecedents and comparison

The pair is Voiculescu's classical example. [Exel and Loring, Almost commuting unitary matrices](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9939-1989-0975641-9) explicitly gives an elementary winding-number proof, as confirmed by its retrieved abstract/introduction. Full PDF retrieval failed in this audit, so its full proof was not independently checked. The complete source logarithmic-index proof was read.

## Checks and limitations

Clock-and-shift matrices, small unitary paths and commuting controls check commutator norms, branch separation and the integer index.

Finite paths illustrate the invariant. They neither optimize distance to commuting pairs nor verify the later von Neumann near-inclusion construction.

## Possible applications

Separating small algebraic defects from small norm perturbations to exact relations.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
