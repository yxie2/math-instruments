# Insert lattice witnesses while preserving old operations and fences

[Family 206](../families/openai-math-206.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000324.md) · [Batch B057](../../batches/B057/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Given a finite lattice P, a<b in P, and a bounded finite lattice Q, identify the bottom and top of Q with a,b. Introduce no comparison between an old vertex v and a fresh interior vertex w except v<=w exactly when v<=a, and w<=v exactly when b<=v. This private insertion is again a lattice and preserves all old meets and joins. For incomparable cross pairs, v meet w=v meet a and v join w=v join b. A previous cover c<d is destroyed exactly when the insertion has endpoints c,d and nonempty interior.

Suppose the proper part of [a,b] has two separate comparability components, each containing two strictly comparable vertices. Vertices in different components have meet a and join b. Every interior vertex therefore has two comparable complements in another component. The source calls such an interval fenced.

A private insertion cannot merge two old proper components: if one or both insertion endpoints are proper vertices of [a,b], all new comparisons enter their existing component; if the endpoints are a,b, the new interior has no comparison with the old proper part. Thus the two reserved comparable pairs persist through every finite sequence of private insertions. Two separate chains with two fresh interior vertices each create this property permanently.

The intended group-theoretic use is elementary: if [D,X] is a fenced subgroup interval and R is normal in X, the modular identity (DR join U) meet V=U join (DR meet V), for U<=V, rules out D<DR<X. Applying it to two comparable complements would otherwise give U=V. Hence R<=D or DR=X.

## Role in the source argument

Allows the source to add many finite tests while retaining previously created obstructions to intermediate normal-subgroup labels.

## Antecedents and comparison

This is an elementary endpoint gluing operation and a use of the classical subgroup modular identity. The full selected proofs were read. A search did not identify an earlier exact formulation of the source private-insertion convention; no claim of historical novelty for lattice gluing or fences is made.

## Checks and limitations

54 insertions into chains, a diamond and a Boolean lattice check all meets, joins and old covers. Five further construction sequences verify the fence property at 35 successive stages.

These finite lattice calculations do not prove that the source final lattice is nonrepresentable, or validate its classification-dependent reductions.

## Possible applications

Assembling finite combinatorial witnesses whose earlier order-theoretic properties must survive later insertions.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
