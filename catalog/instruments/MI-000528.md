# Extract limiting moments with an Abel transform and randomized Taylor coefficient

[Family 217](../families/openai-math-217.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000297.md) · [Batch B054](../../batches/B054/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Suppose m_l tends to m with |m_l-m|<=C l^(-a), a>0. Let J>=2 have probability l^(-2)/C_0, C_0=sum_(l>=2)l^(-2), and let P satisfy E[P|J=l]=m_l and a uniform conditional second-moment bound. In the source P is a product of k differences with independent copies conditional on J: conditioning first on copy zero identifies its expectation with a centered kth moment.

For Re z>0 define B(z)=C_0 z E[P exp((1-z)log J)]=z sum_(l>=2)m_l l^(-1-z). Subtract m inside the sum. The difference is holomorphic for Re z>-a. Comparing the constant series with the integral from 1 to infinity removes its pole, extending B across zero with B(0)=m and to a half-plane containing Re z>=0. Its Taylor series about z=1 therefore converges absolutely at z=0.

Writing w=1-z, its coefficients are E[A_h], where A_h=C_0 P[(log J)^h/h!-1_(h>=1)(log J)^(h-1)/(h-1)!]. Thus sum_h E[A_h]=m. This is a sum of expectations: for each fixed J the pointwise sum of the bracket is zero, so interchanging the infinite sum and expectation is invalid.

The bound t^h/h!<=4^h exp(t/4) yields ||A_h||_2<=C_1 4^h. Draw H with probability 2^(-h-1), take 256^h independent copies of A_h, and output W=2^(H+1) times their average. The deviations of these averages have summable L1 norms, at most C_1 4^(-h); absolute summability of their means proves E|W|<infinity and EW=m. The experiment is finite almost surely, but its expected number of replicates is sum_h 2^(-h-1)256^h=infinity. This representation is not a finite-expected-cost estimation algorithm.

## Role in the source argument

Represents each source limiting moment through a single randomized finite construction, conditional on its earlier moment convergence estimates.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Rhee-Glynn, Unbiased Estimation with Square Root Convergence for SDE Models](https://web.stanford.edu/~glynn/papers/2015/RheeG15.pdf), equation (10) and the single-term discussion on printed pages 1029-1030, provide the classical random-index estimator and explicitly distinguish its moments from expected computational cost. Those passages were read. The Abel/Taylor coefficient construction here is source-specific; no exhaustive priority claim is made.

## Checks and limitations

Exact finite-copy calculations test the centered-moment identity. A solvable Dirichlet-series model tests Taylor recovery; exact telescoping and variance formulas expose the invalid interchange and infinite expected replication cost.

Finite checks do not prove the source polynomial moment convergence, a limiting distribution or finite expected computation time.

## Possible applications

Encoding an analytically identified limit as the expectation of one integrable random variable when practical computational cost is not the objective.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
