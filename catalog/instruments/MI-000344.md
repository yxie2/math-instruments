# Turn a minimum-norm mixture of crossings into dual weights

[Family 246](../families/openai-math-246.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000191.md) · [Batch B030](../../batches/B030/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let V be a finite family of nonzero nonnegative crossing-incidence vectors, and let P minimize squared Euclidean norm over conv(V). Then P is nonzero and the projection inequality gives <P,v> >= ||P||^2 for every v in V. Consequently rho=P/||P||^2 is admissible for the modulus problem min ||rho||^2 subject to <rho,v>>=1.

Conversely, an admissible omega has <omega,P>>=1, so Cauchy-Schwarz gives ||omega||^2>=1/||P||^2. Thus the modulus is exactly 1/||P||^2.

If every opposite crossing u intersects every v, so <u,v>>=1, then <u,P>>=1. The same P is therefore admissible for the opposite family, with energy equal to the reciprocal of the first modulus. This proves an upper bound on the opposite modulus; equality requires an additional duality statement. In the source, the planar blocker lemma supplies the intersection property after localization to a rectangle.

## Role in the source argument

Converts large crossing modulus into small separating weights for the scalar construction.

## Antecedents and comparison

The minimum-overlap formula and optimizer relation are [Albin and Poggi-Corradini, Theorem 5.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1605.08462). The extracted proof is a concise convex-projection version with a crossing-intersection application.

## Checks and limitations

Thirty-six grid-family models solve the density problem and independently find a convex decomposition of its normalized optimizer. The maximum residual is below 1.4e-15.

The finite models do not verify the planar limiting or scalar-limit constructions.

## Possible applications

Discrete extremal length, crossing-energy certificates and convex mixtures of combinatorial objects.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
