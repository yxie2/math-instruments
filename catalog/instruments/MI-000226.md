# A partially acyclic complex with odd-order stabilizers computes low-degree group homology

[Family 177](../families/openai-math-177.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000133.md) · [Batch B015](../../batches/B015/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let K act without inversions on a nonempty simplicial complex Z. Suppose every nonempty simplex stabilizer is finite of odd order and reduced H_j(Z;F2)=0 for 0<=j<=h. Then

$$H_j(K;\mathbb F_2)\cong H_j(C_*(Z;\mathbb F_2)_K)\qquad(0\le j\le h).$$

The subscript denotes coinvariants. The right side is computed by simplex-orbit chains with boundary induced by deleting vertices. It need not be interpreted as the homology of an ordinary simplicial quotient unless that quotient identification is separately justified.

Each chain module is a direct sum of permutation modules F2[K/H]. Averaging over an odd-order H splits its augmentation F2[H]->F2, so F2 is projective over F2[H]; induction makes F2[K/H] projective over F2[K]. The augmented chains are exact through degree h. Retain terms and differentials through h+1, then extend by projectives mapping onto the next kernel. This gives a full projective resolution with the same data needed to compute homology in degrees <=h. Taking coinvariants proves the comparison.

Full contractibility is unnecessary; the degree range is governed by the supplied acyclicity. Odd stabilizer order is essential over F2. For example, a group of order two acting trivially on a point has orbit-chain H1=0 but group H1=F2.

## Role in the source argument

Replaces a full classifying-space model by an acyclic range and finite stabilizer information.

## Antecedents and comparison

These are classical projective-resolution and averaging arguments. [Brown's lectures, Sections 1, 3 and 5](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~kbrown/papers/cohomology_hangzhou.pdf) describe the relevant framework. The extraction makes the truncated degree range and orbit-chain convention explicit.

## Checks and limitations

Read the resolution extension. Exact F2 computations on joins of three finite sets compare orbit chains with cyclic-group homology in low degrees; odd-order averaging idempotents are checked separately. An even-order trivial action supplies a negative control for the stabilizer assumption.

Acyclicity and the stabilizer order must both be established. Orbit boundary multiplicities are retained modulo two; they are not silently replaced by one boundary per distinct orbit face.

## Possible applications

Computing low-dimensional homology of quotients when only partial acyclicity is available.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
