# Reduce perfect matching to maximum degree three with odd incidence paths

[Family 120](../families/openai-math-120.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000212.md) · [Batch B035](../../batches/B035/README.md)

## Mathematical content

For a loopless graph with no isolated vertices, replace a vertex of degree r by a path on 2r-1 vertices and attach its r original incidences to the odd positions. Every even position is matched internally to an odd one. Since there are r odd and r-1 even positions, exactly one odd position uses an external edge in any perfect matching. These external edges therefore decode to a perfect matching of the original graph.

Conversely, the odd position chosen by an original matching edge leaves even-length paths on each side, which can be matched internally. The new graph has maximum degree three and exactly sum_v(2 deg(v)-1)=4e-v vertices. Construction and both witness conversions take O(v+e). A degree-one vertex becomes a single port. An isolated vertex is detected separately and certifies that perfect matching is impossible.

## Role in the source argument

Feeds a bounded-degree perfect-matching subroutine while preserving explicit edge witnesses.

## Antecedents and comparison

The source credits this path replacement to Dahlhaus and Karpinski, [Perfect matching for regular graphs is AC0-hard for the general matching problem](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0022000092900054), 1992. The publisher metadata was retrieved; its full text was not available in this audit. No new degree-reduction claim is made.

## Checks and limitations

All 814 nonisolated simple graphs through five vertices are tested by exhaustive matching search; sizes, maximum degrees and decoded witnesses are checked.

The reduction does not prove the speed or correctness of the source matching engine.

## Possible applications

Transferring perfect-matching algorithms and witnesses to subcubic instances with only linear expansion.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
