# Clear prime-to-p denominators before taking a finite cofiber

[Family 308](../families/openai-math-308.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000513.md) · [Batch B083](../../batches/B083/README.md)

## Mathematical content

For finite CW spectra F_0,F_1, compactness of F_0 and the filtered-colimit description of localization give
[(F_0)_(p),(F_1)_(p)]=[F_0,F_1] tensor Z_(p).
Thus a map between their p-localizations has the form f/s with p not dividing s. Multiplication by s is an equivalence after p-localization. Pre- or postcomposing by it identifies the cofiber of f/s with the p-localization of cofib(f).

A stable map between finite CW spectra is represented after a finite suspension by a cellular map between finite CW models. Its finite mapping cone, desuspended, is again finite. Consequently p-localizations of finite CW spectra are closed under the finite suspensions and successive cofibers used in the source construction.

The conclusion concerns finite p-local spectra, namely localizations of finite spectra; it does not say the localized sphere is itself a finite integral CW spectrum. The denominator must be invertible at the chosen prime.

## Role in the source argument

Preserving a finite model while realizing a finite tower of maps initially given only after prime localization.

## Antecedents and comparison

This is standard compact-object localization and finite mapping-cone theory. The source gives the denominator-clearing argument explicitly; no new finiteness theorem is claimed.

## Checks and limitations

Finite chain-complex analogues check an explicit mapping-cone isomorphism after clearing an invertible denominator. Reduction modulo several primes verifies the unit condition and contrasts a denominator divisible by p.

Chain-complex tests verify the algebraic analogy only. The spectrum-level compactness theorem and the existence of the source attaching maps are not numerically tested.
