# Amplify adjacent-power overlap by hiding labels in a fixed-count schedule

[Family 122](../families/openai-math-122.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000466.md) · [Batch B074](../../batches/B074/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Suppose trace laws for w^k and w^(k+1) share a sublaw omega U, with 0<omega<1/8. Use g=2 floor(FP/(2k+1)) chunks and a common tail. A uniform schedule has g/2 or g/2+P long chunks, giving total exponents FP or FP+P. For each chunk, independently emit U with probability omega, hiding its short/long label; otherwise reveal the label and emit the normalized residual law. Joining the outputs gives the genuine trace law.

Conditional on the hidden-flag set of size M, the visible long count has hypergeometric mass p_m(v)=choose(g-M,v) choose(M,m-v)/choose(g,m). Visible label sequences are exchangeable, so their total variation is exactly that of the count. Coupling schedules with m and m+1 long labels by flipping a uniform short label yields p_(m+1)(v)-p_m(v)=t(v-1)p_m(v-1)-t(v)p_m(v), t(v)=(g-M-v)/(g-m). The sequence t(v)p_m(v) is unimodal; hence the adjacent total variation equals its maximum and is at most max p_m.

When M is between omega g/2 and 2omega g and m is central, Stirling bounds max p_m=O((omega g)^(-1/2)). Summing P adjacent changes costs O(P/sqrt(omega g)); atypical flags have exponentially small probability. With F>=CkP, C sufficiently large compared with 1/omega, and sufficiently large P, the final overlap is at least 2/3. Only the common component hides the labels; revealing those labels would destroy this smoothing mechanism.

## Role in the source argument

Turns weak overlap of neighboring powers into strong overlap after a controlled increase in length.

## Antecedents and comparison

Common-part coupling and hypergeometric smoothing are established probabilistic mechanisms. The source cites Zaharopol (1989) for a related positive-contraction smoothing argument; that primary paper was located but its propositions were not independently checked. No priority claim is made for this quantitative word-power formulation.

## Checks and limitations

Exact hypergeometric masses verify the adjacent difference identity, unimodal flux formula and total-variation bound, including endpoint supports. Small common-component models check hiding and postprocessing.

Finite checks do not verify the asymptotic Stirling constants or the source recursive lower-bound construction.

## Possible applications

Smoothing a hidden fixed-count perturbation without assuming independent labels in the schedule.
