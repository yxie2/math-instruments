# Recover the return coefficient from all jump directions and fresh seam averages

[Family 271](../families/openai-math-271.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000508.md) · [Batch B082](../../batches/B082/README.md)

## Mathematical content

For a symmetric finite-range walk with rates S J(v) whose support generates Z^3, let phi(k)=S sum_v J(v)(1-cos(k dot v)) and D=(S/2)sum_v J(v) vv^T. Then phi has only the torus zero at zero and phi(k)=k^T D k+O(|k|^4), with D positive definite. Fourier inversion and compactness away from zero yield sup_(x,y)p_t(x,y)<=C(1+t)^(-3/2). Rescaling k by t^(-1/2) and dominated convergence give
p_t(x,x)=t^(-3/2)[(4pi)^(-3/2)(det D)^(-1/2)+o(1)].

With ell slots per site, each complete ideal traversal contains one freshly averaged uniform seam permutation. Its inverse has the same average. Equal rates to every target slot preserve conditional uniformity after that seam. Thus any forward/reverse direction word of n traversals has exact diagonal probability ell^(-1)p_(n beta)(x,x). Uniformity for a bounded range of n follows from finiteness of the words. Adjacent forward and reverse seams are independent averages; they do not cancel as a fixed permutation and its inverse would.

Killing only lowers the return probability. The source's exponentially small interior killing error uses a separate localization estimate and is not included as a certified input here.

## Role in the source argument

Avoiding a wrong leading constant when coarse geometry uses only selected bonds but the physical walk uses every interaction.

## Antecedents and comparison

The local limit is the classical Fourier proof for an irreducible finite-range continuous-time lattice walk. The useful source bookkeeping is the full interaction diffusion matrix and fresh-seam convention; no new local central limit theorem is claimed.

## Checks and limitations

Anisotropic walks with exact Bessel-function return probabilities check the determinant coefficient. Finite slot kernels check every short direction word; a fixed permutation/inverse pair demonstrates the difference from fresh averages.

Spanning R^3 alone is insufficient: a proper sublattice creates additional torus zeros and changes the coefficient. No interacting ferromagnet comparison or Bloch law is established by this ideal-walk calculation.
