# Recognize separable quotients from a norm-separable evaluation image

[Family 323](../families/openai-math-323.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000245.md) · [Batch B043](../../batches/B043/README.md)

## Mathematical content

An infinite-dimensional Banach space X has a separable infinite-dimensional quotient if and only if there is a closed infinite-dimensional F subset X* for which R_F(X) is norm separable in F*, where R_F(x)(f)=f(x). F can be chosen separable.

For the reverse direction choose x_j whose evaluations are dense. Inductively choose unit f_n in F annihilating x_1,...,x_n and all earlier finite half-norming sets D_m for span(f_1,...,f_m). Prefix projections on G=closed span(f_n) have norm at most 2, and coordinate functionals f_n# have norm at most 4. Evaluation at x_j is supported on indices below j, so R_G(X) lies in Z=closed span(f_n#), a separable infinite-dimensional space.

For h in Z*, finite vectors v_m=sum_(i<=m) h(f_i#) f_i obey ||h||<=sup_m||v_m||<=2||R_G* h||. No convergence of v_m is asserted. The adjoint lower bound implies surjectivity by separation and a geometric residual-halving construction. Conversely, a quotient T:X -> Y and a separable closed infinite-dimensional U subset Y* give F=T*U; the evaluation image factors through separable Y.

## Role in the source argument

Replaces an arbitrary quotient search by norm separability of a restricted dual evaluation image.

## Checks and limitations

Fourteen finite models check coordinate normers and the residual-halving surjection mechanism.

Finite-dimensional surjectivity does not verify the infinite-dimensional equivalence or the claimed independence result.

## Possible applications

Reducing quotient existence to finding a dual subspace with a small evaluation image.
