# Pair-equality projections are dominated by the slice transposition generator

[Family 131](../families/openai-math-131.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000114.md) · [Batch B010](../../batches/B010/README.md)

## Mathematical content

On the uniform slice X_{N,q}={x in {0,1}^N:sum x_i=q}, let tau_ij swap coordinates and G=(1/2)sum_{i<j}(I-tau_ij). Let Q_ij be orthogonal projection onto z_ij=1{x_i=x_j}-P(x_i=x_j), with Q_ij=0 when z_ij is zero. For all integers N>=0 and 0<=q<=N,

$$\sum_{i<j}Q_{ij}\preceq G.$$

For N>=3 and 1<=q<=N-1 put r=N-q and p=[q(q-1)+r(r-1)]/[N(N-1)]. Then ||z_ij||^2=p(1-p)>0. The Gram map with columns z_ij/sqrt(p(1-p)) acts on pair coefficients. Split that coefficient space into constants, c_ij=u_i+u_j with sum u_i=0, and arrays with every row sum zero. Their dimensions are 1, N-1 and binom(N,2)-N (the third space is absent for N=3).

Constants map to zero because the number of equal-coordinate pairs is fixed on a slice. On the second space, the Gram eigenvalue is

$$\mu_1=\frac{(2q-N)^2}{2(N-2)p}\le N/2.$$

Its image consists of centered linear functions, where G has eigenvalue N/2. On the third space its image is a row-zero quadratic and G has eigenvalue N-1. Positivity and trace binom(N,2) give mu_2<=(N-1)/(N-3)<=N-1 for N>=4. On the orthogonal complement of the Gram image the projection sum vanishes while G is positive. This proves the comparison. For N<=2 or q=0,N all z_ij vanish; no division by a zero variance is used.

## Role in the source argument

Controls a summed quadratic error arising when disjoint row-pair updates of graph realizations interact.

## Antecedents and comparison

The source credits [Filmus, Orthogonal basis for functions over a slice of the Boolean hypercube](https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.0142), particularly the low-degree decomposition and transposition spectrum. The selected comparison is specialized to centered equality indicators; it is not a new general slice decomposition.

## Checks and limitations

Enumerated finite slices build the full projection and transposition matrices, compare spectra and check positive semidefiniteness numerically, with all boundary slices included. The balanced N=4,q=2 case attains the quadratic comparison.

This local inequality is only one ingredient in the source global mixing proof. It does not alone establish a spectral gap for every graph degree fiber. Numerical eigenvalue checks do not prove the all-N assertion.

## Possible applications

Controlling pairwise equality statistics in exchangeable fixed-cardinality models and local-to-global Markov-chain variance arguments.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
