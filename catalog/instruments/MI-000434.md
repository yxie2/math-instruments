# Identify finite-energy gradients with a closed cocycle space

[Family 315](../families/openai-math-315.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000236.md) · [Batch B041](../../batches/B041/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let a connected simply connected cellular two-complex have bounded vertex degree, bounded two-cell boundary size and bounded edge-cell incidence. In l2 of oriented cellular edges, let D be the gradients df of arbitrary real vertex functions whose gradients are square summable, and D_0 the closure of gradients of finitely supported functions.

The cellular coboundary d_1 is bounded. Every cocycle integrates along paths to a potential: simple connectivity makes the integral path independent. Hence D=ker d_1 is closed. Boundedness of d_0:l2(vertices)->l2(edges) and density of finite supports give D_0=closure(im d_0), contained in D.

Thus D/D_0 is reduced first l2-cohomology in this model. If its von Neumann dimension is positive, D_0 is a proper subspace of D. The source connects that dimension to its geometric model by a separate chain-comparison result. No claim that im d_0 itself is closed is needed.

## Role in the source argument

Produces a nontrivial finite-energy quotient from a positive first l2-Betti-number input.

## Antecedents and comparison

Classical path integration and reduced Hilbert cohomology. The statement distinguishes arbitrary finite-energy potentials from square-summable potentials.

## Checks and limitations

Nine exact filled-fan complexes identify cocycle kernels with gradients. An unfilled three-cycle gives a control for missing simple connectivity.

Finite complexes do not test infinite-range closedness or the source l2-Betti-number comparison. No Singer theorem is certified.

## Possible applications

Building analytic objects from nonzero reduced first cohomology while retaining the correct closure convention.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
