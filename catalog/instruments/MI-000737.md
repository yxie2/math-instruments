# Average posterior entropy production through a concave two-point conductance

[Family 227](../families/openai-math-227.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000495.md) · [Batch B079](../../batches/B079/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let mu be a probability on binary configurations and mu_Y its posterior under any observation channel. For a positive fixed function z, define I_mu(z) as the sum over sites and fibers of ab/(a+b) times (z_+-z_-)(log z_+-log z_-), where a,b are the joint masses of the two fiber points. Then E I_muY(z)<=I_mu(z).

The conductance c(a,b)=ab/(a+b), extended by zero on the axes, is concave: its Hessian is -2(b,-a)(b,-a)^T/(a+b)^3. The posterior masses average to the original masses, and each test factor is nonnegative and independent of Y, so Jensen applies fiber by fiber.

If every posterior mu_Y is product, entropy tensorization and Ent_pi(z)<=Cov_pi(z,log z) give E Ent_muY(z)<=E I_muY(z)<=I_mu(z). Thus for z=f^2 and N=mu(f^2), the normalized remaining posterior entropy is at most I_mu(f^2)/N. Approximation z+epsilon includes zeros with the usual extended values. This bound requires no posterior field-tail estimate. The other, square-root-energy bound in the source lemma does require such an estimate and is not extracted here.

## Role in the source argument

Transferring a functional inequality backward through an observation channel without controlling extreme posterior fields.

## Antecedents and comparison

Entropy tensorization, Jensen and concavity of parallel conductance are established tools. Exhaustive priority of this posterior formulation is not established.

## Checks and limitations

Finite observation channels compare averaged posterior forms against the initial form. Mixtures of product posteriors test the normalized entropy inequality, including biased coordinates and observation events of very small probability.

A test chosen after observing Y is outside this statement. The checks give no SK mixing-time estimate.
