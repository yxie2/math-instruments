# Normalize a subharmonic profile to control its logarithmic potential

[Family 350](../families/openai-math-350.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000521.md) · [Batch B085](../../batches/B085/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let V be subharmonic on D_R, R=1000, with V<=0 and sup over closed D_1 at least -1. Choose x_* in closed D_1 with V(x_*)>=-1. The submean inequality on D_10(x_*) gives integral_(D_8)|V|<=100 pi. Testing the nonnegative Riesz measure eta=(2pi)^(-1)Delta V against a fixed cutoff equal to one on D_5 bounds eta(D_5).

Restrict eta to D_4 as nu and put P(x)=integral log|x-z| dnu(z). The logarithmic kernel has uniformly bounded local L^1 norm as z varies in D_4, so P is locally integrable and Delta(V-P)=0 on D_3. Thus V=h+P there, with h harmonic and uniformly bounded first and second derivatives on D_2 by interior estimates.

The equality initially holds almost everywhere. Taking the limiting disk averages of the subharmonic representatives makes it pointwise, including possible values -infinity at atoms. The normalization supplies the uniform constants; arbitrary additive downward shifts would destroy the L^1 bound.

## Role in the source argument

Obtaining uniform mass and harmonic derivative bounds before applying local corrections to a family of singular profiles.

## Antecedents and comparison

This is a quantitative use of classical Riesz decomposition and harmonic estimates. No new potential representation is claimed.

## Checks and limitations

Explicit radial logarithmic potentials check the normalization, Riesz mass and integrability at an atom; numerical radial integration checks the singular disk-average formula against its closed form.

The example checks do not establish general compactness or prove that the source eigenfunction profiles are subharmonic.
