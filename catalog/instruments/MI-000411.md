# Expose a convex Lorentz cone inside a common-Lagrangian definite-pair constraint

[Family 341](../families/openai-math-341.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000224.md) · [Batch B038](../../batches/B038/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Fix a symplectic form A with A^2>0 and an A-Lagrangian two-plane L. Choose e_1,e_2 vanishing on L and complementary f_1,f_2 with A=e_1 wedge f_1+e_2 wedge f_2. Every C vanishing on L is sum M_ij e_i wedge f_j+c e_1 wedge e_2. Then AC=(tr M/2)A^2 and C^2=(det M)A^2.

Write M=hI+T, with T=[[u,v],[w,-u]], q=(v-w)/2 and r=(v+w)/2. The pair is definite precisely when det T=q^2-r^2-u^2>0. Each sign component q>sqrt(r^2+u^2) or q<-sqrt(r^2+u^2) is convex; h and c are unrestricted.

A two-form pulled back from the quotient by L wedges to zero with both A and C and with itself. Adding such basic forms to either member therefore leaves the whole wedge matrix unchanged. In a torus-fiber setting where translations preserve A and the common Lagrangian plane, translated copies of C lie in one component, so their compact average and the interpolation to that average remain definite. This last statement requires those geometric hypotheses.

## Role in the source argument

Makes a regular-region averaging deformation preserve the pointwise positivity constraint.

## Antecedents and comparison

Elementary wedge algebra identifies a classical Lorentz cone. The source uses this interface in its regular-fiber preparation; no claim about arbitrary definite-pair convexity.

## Checks and limitations

One hundred exact pairs yield 1,100 convex interpolation points and 1,100 basic-addition checks preserving the wedge matrix.

No global torus fibration, singular fiber or closedness-preserving averaging operator is constructed here.

## Possible applications

Convex averaging in a positivity problem that is not convex until a common isotropic subspace is fixed.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
