# Expected medians of stopped-walk endpoints control two smoothing costs

[Family 332](../families/openai-math-332.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000106.md) · [Batch B008](../../batches/B008/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let A be a finite stochastic matrix with stationary probability pi, t>=1 an integer, and x_i real ell1 data. Put p=1/(t+1), q=1-p and $G=p(I-qA)^{-1}$. For each i take three independent indices with law G_i and define

$$y_i=\mathbb E\operatorname{med}(x_{J_1},x_{J_2},x_{J_3}),$$

where the median is coordinatewise. The source derives

$$\sum_i\pi_i\|x_i-y_i\|_1^2+t\sum_{i,j}\pi_i a_{ij}\|y_i-y_j\|_1^2\le\frac{3024}{t}\sum_{s=1}^t\sum_{i,j}\pi_i(A^s)_{ij}\|x_i-x_j\|_1^2.$$

In cut coordinates, h=Gz solves h=pz+qAh, and y=T(Phi(h)). The majority of three independent Bernoulli(h) variables has mean 3h^2-2h^3, so the threshold decomposition proves the median identity exactly.

Construct an alive/dead chain: from alive i, die at i with probability p or move alive to j with probability q a_ij; dead states hold. Attach h_i while alive and z_i after death. This is a cube-valued martingale. With initial binary center e=z_(X0), its terminal quartic Hilbert potential is $\|x_{X_S}-x_{X_0}\|_1^2$, where S is independent geometric on nonnegative integers with mean t. If B is the stationary cut-space approximation cost and E_A the stationary one-step cut-space cost after Phi, the total expected squared martingale jump cost is exactly B+(q/p)E_A=B+t E_A. The death jump is necessary to count B.

The flat-cubic estimate gives B+t E_A<=108 times the geometric endpoint cost. For any stationary chain and metric data, Minkowski gives subadditivity of the square root of the endpoint cost D(s). It implies D(t)<=4W for W=t^-1 sum_(s=1)^t D(s), and decomposing S=t floor(S/t)+(S mod t) gives E D(S)<=28W. Combining yields 3024. Detailed balance is not used; zero pi_i are allowed.

This is a nonlinear modification of data, not a procedure for extending a Lipschitz map to every point of a source space. The source’s extension consequence invokes a separate theorem for dual Banach targets.

## Role in the source argument

Supplies explicit new points realizing the two terms in metric Markov cotype and explains their median interpretation.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Mendel and Naor](https://arxiv.org/html/1301.3963v2) ask the ell1 metric Markov-cotype question, provide a resolvent comparison, and state the dual-target extension theorem. The source adds the stopped-median construction and quartic energy estimate. The earlier [cubic cut smoothing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08749v2) concerns metric cotype, a different invariant.

## Checks and limitations

Read the full stopped-chain, geometric comparison and median proofs. Finite stationary examples compare explicit triple-median enumeration with cut reconstruction, solve the resolvent, verify the exact jump accounting and test the displayed inequality. Examples include nonreversible cycles and zero stationary coordinates.

Stationarity is required for the jump-cost identity and endpoint comparison. New points can leave a subspace containing the data, so the construction does not prove the same result for every subspace of ell1. Numerical examples do not certify the general theorem or establish novelty.

## Possible applications

Nonlinear regularization of ell1-valued data on a stationary finite chain, with simultaneous control of movement from the data and one-step oscillation.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
