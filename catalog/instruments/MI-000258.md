# Derived tensor survival appears as separate bar summands in a square-zero extension

[Family 198](../families/openai-math-198.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000148.md) · [Batch B019](../../batches/B019/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let D be a finite-dimensional algebra over a field k, X a finite-dimensional D-bimodule, and A=D semidirect X the split extension with X^2=0. Inflate a left D-module N through A->D and put F=X tensor_D^L -. Then, with cohomological shifts,

$$D\otimes_A^{\mathbf L}N\simeq\bigoplus_{r\ge0}(F^rN)[r].$$

The ordinary k-bar resolution after tensoring with D has words D tensor A^(tensor n) tensor N in degree -n. Expand each internal A as D plus X. Every nonzero face preserves the number r of X entries: two X entries multiply to zero, and endpoint X actions vanish. This splits the actual complex by r.

Between its r X entries are r+1 blocks of D entries with lengths i_0,...,i_r. The component lies in degree -r-sum i_j. Multiplying it by (-1)^(sum_j(r-j)i_j) identifies its signs with the shifted multiple two-sided bar complex computing F^rN. Iterated free bar resolutions ensure genuinely derived tensors without flatness of X. Only finitely many summands occur in each total degree; no product completion is used.

If D has finite global dimension on both sides and N is finite-dimensional, F^tN=0 for some t implies pd_A(N)<infinity. The decomposition makes D tensor_A^L N bounded; tensoring with D/rad(D) preserves boundedness. A minimal A-projective resolution becomes a zero-differential complex after that tensor, and Nakayama forces it to terminate. Conversely, F^rN nonzero gives cohomology in some degree q<=0, hence a nonzero summand in degree q-r<=-r, so pd_A(N)>=r.

Replacing F by ordinary tensor is invalid in general: higher Tor can survive even when the ordinary iterates conceal it.

## Role in the source argument

Separates finite resolution existence from lower bounds on its length after the source constructs suitable iterates.

## Checks and limitations

Read the full bar and minimal-resolution proofs. Exact finite-field bar differentials check d squared zero, preservation of X-count and homology in a nonflat dual-number example. Directed semisimple-base examples check extinction and survival through path counts.

The nonflat example is used only for the bar formula; its base does not have finite global dimension. A finite computation does not certify the source fixed-algebra counterexample or its simulation theorem.
