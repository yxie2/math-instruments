# Balanced signed dependencies turn mixed-variance equations into a Tarski operator

[Family 245](../families/openai-math-245.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000158.md) · [Batch B021](../../batches/B021/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Consider a strongly connected signed dependency component in which every closed walk has an even number of negative edges. Choose a root and give each vertex the parity of a path from the root. A return path shows this is well defined: two different parities would yield an odd closed walk. Positive edges preserve the sign and negative edges reverse it.

For a complete lattice C(T) at each coordinate, order positive coordinates normally and negative coordinates in reverse. The product remains complete. If an operator is covariant along positive dependencies and contravariant along negative dependencies, it is monotone in this mixed order.

In the source, C(T) consists of candidates between a generic-term set G_T and a strongly-normalizing test set top_T, closed under a specified head-expansion rule. Arbitrary intersections preserve those requirements, with top_T as the empty intersection, so these candidates form a complete lattice. Reduction closure is not assumed. For a product type, the operator tests h n against a codomain candidate for every n in the domain candidate. It is antitone in the domain and monotone in the instantiated codomain; previously processed coordinates are constant. The signed dependencies give exactly the mixed-order monotonicity.

Tarski then supplies a fixed point with no continuity assumption. Explicitly, let a be the supremum of all v with v<=F(v). Monotonicity gives a<=F(a), then F(a)<=F(F(a)), so F(a) is itself among those v and F(a)<=a. Thus F(a)=a. In the source, assigning candidates by actual normalized types makes the required substitution and conversion identities follow from equality of those normal forms.

An odd negative loop is a real obstruction to this method: complementation on the two-element lattice is antitone and has no fixed point. Other source constructions are needed for components not satisfying the parity condition.

## Role in the source argument

Constructs one class of normalization interfaces while accounting for the opposite variances of domains and codomains.

## Antecedents and comparison

The fixed-point step is exactly [Tarski (1955), Theorem 1](https://people.csail.mit.edu/carroll/probSem/Documents/Tarski.pdf). Sign reversal of lattice coordinates is the standard way to handle balanced mixed variance. The source-specific interface obligations are additional to fixed-point existence.

## Checks and limitations

Read the candidate lattice and signed-component proofs and Tarski statement. Exact Boolean-lattice models check mixed-order monotonicity, the supremum-of-postfixed-points construction and absence of odd signed closed walks, with a negative-loop control.

Finite Boolean models do not verify the actual candidate sets, profile invariance or the general normalization theorem. The signed component must satisfy its closed-walk hypothesis; no algorithmic convergence rate follows for arbitrary complete lattices.

## Possible applications

Solving recursive semantic equations with both covariance and contravariance when their dependency signs are consistent.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
