# Extend finite-word policy constraints by compactness

[Family 110](../families/openai-math-110.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000461.md) · [Batch B073](../../batches/B073/README.md)

## Mathematical content

For k labelled servers in an arbitrary metric, index histories by finite request words and preceding label choices. At each history choose a distribution in the finite simplex Delta_k. The product P of these compact simplices is compact, even for an uncountable metric.

For a fixed request word sigma of length m, expected cost is the finite polynomial sum_(j in [k]^m) c(sigma,j) product_t p_(request-prefix,label-history)(j_t). All coefficients are finite distances for this fixed word; no global diameter bound is required. Thus a prescribed expected-cost inequality is a closed subset of P. If every finite collection of these inequalities has a common feasible policy, the finite intersection property supplies one policy satisfying every finite word simultaneously.

The policy can be realized using independent uniforms, or one uniform seed by recursively dividing a half-open interval according to successive label probabilities. Each finite label prefix receives interval length equal to the product of its transition probabilities; zero-probability pieces are empty. This realizes the same expected costs and is measurable in the seed for each fixed oblivious input. No joint measurability in the request coordinates or computational effectiveness follows from the argument.

## Role in the source argument

Removes a finite-input restriction once a compatible finite-family performance guarantee is available.

## Antecedents and comparison

This is a classical compactness argument using finite-coordinate continuity. The source-specific content is its unrestricted-metric behavioral-policy formulation and seed realization; the competitive bound is a separate hypothesis.

## Checks and limitations

Exact rational policy trees compare path products with recursive expected costs and verify seed interval lengths and partitions on every tested input.

Finite tests do not establish product compactness or the missing finite-family feasibility theorem.

## Possible applications

Passing from local feasibility to one horizon-independent online policy without a compact request space.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
