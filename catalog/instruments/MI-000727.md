# Sum geometrically weighted scale errors without assuming a summable perturbation

[Family 218](../families/openai-math-218.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000490.md) · [Batch B078](../../batches/B078/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let s_j=s_0 L^j, L>1, and let a bounded sequence X_j tend to zero. If r and K tend to infinity while s_K/r stays bounded above, then
sum_(j<K) X_j min(1,(s_(j+1)/r)^c)+X_K ->0, for c>0.
The weights have a uniformly bounded sum. Split the sum at a fixed index: each early weight tends to zero, while the late contribution is bounded by the tail supremum of X times that uniform sum. No convergence of sum X_j is required.

For the source's actual local coarse-graining trajectory, finite crossing tests T have step-mismatch probabilities at most C_T X_j min(1,(delta s_(j+1)/a_T)^c). At terminal scale the fixed-size torus has O(M^2) residual potentials with expected total norm O_M(X_K). Delete these to couple to the pure terminal model, glue all step couplings, and take a union bound over the fixed tests. Averaging auxiliary algorithmic randomness conditional on the original environment gives a bound on E TV(law_omega(test vector), pure test law), not merely TV of the averaged law.

The trajectory assumptions and conditional implementations are essential inputs. Uniform constants over placements do not assert a simultaneous coupling of all placements.

## Role in the source argument

Separates a robust analytic summation step from the difficult local probabilistic estimates that feed it.

## Antecedents and comparison

The summable-kernel argument and coupling union bound are classical. The source-specific input is the scale-dependent local mismatch estimate; its proof is outside this extraction.

## Checks and limitations

A slowly vanishing nonsummable sequence is checked against the explicit early/tail bound. Constant errors give a nonvanishing control. Balanced deterministic environment laws show that TV of averaged laws can be zero while expected quenched TV is one half.

The finite tests do not supply the source local estimates, crossing inputs, or SLE identification.

## Possible applications

Recognizing when weighted scale decay is sufficient and when averaging loses the desired mode of convergence.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
