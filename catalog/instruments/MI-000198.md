# Planar face imbalance certifies two disjoint incidence states

[Family 180](../families/openai-math-180.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000120.md) · [Batch B012](../../batches/B012/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let T be a finite simple sphere triangulation with at least four vertices, a proper black-white face coloring, and no separating triangles. Choose one black face t0 as the outer face and its three vertices as roots. Let A be the other black faces and X the nonroot vertices. Euler counting gives |A|=|X|. A state is a bijection r:A to X with r(t) incident to t.

There are two states r,s with r(t) different from s(t) for every t. The incidence network has capacity two at each vertex and face and capacity one on each incidence. Its cut condition is

$$\sum_{t\in A}\min(2,|t\cap S|)\ge 2|S|\quad(S\subseteq X).$$

Integral flow then gives a two-regular bipartite graph whose alternating cycles split into two disjoint perfect matchings. The capacity-one incidence constraint is essential.

Writing U=V(T) minus S, the cut condition is equivalent to e(U)-b(U)<=2|U|-4, where b(U) counts actual black faces contained in U. In each induced component with at least four vertices, every triangular face is an actual original face because separating triangles are excluded. A nontriangular region with boundary length d, counting edge sides with multiplicity, satisfies |b'-w'|<=d-4: signed boundary counting gives 3(b'-w') as a sum of d signs, with parity giving the sharper cases d=4 and d=5. Combining these inequalities with Euler's formula yields e-b<=2v-4 for that component. The component containing the outer roots satisfies the same bound even if it is just the root triangle. Other components need only e-b<=2v. Summing proves the cut condition.

## Antecedents and comparison

The state framework precedes this source; see [Hine and Kalman](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1808.06091v1). The flow principle is classical Ford-Fulkerson, cited in the source; its DOI page was not retrievable in this batch. The selected step is the planar density certificate for two disjoint states.

## Checks and limitations

Checked the flow/density equivalence and boundary parity cases. Enumerations on even bipyramids verify every subset cut and explicitly find two disjoint incidence states.

The result concerns this colored sphere setting and requires the separating-triangle exclusion. A pair of states alone does not prove Hamiltonicity.

## Possible applications

Producing disjoint incidence assignments from planar density bounds and face-color imbalance.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
