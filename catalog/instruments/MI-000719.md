# Upgrade unique supports to a regular C1 norm sphere

[Family 212](../families/openai-math-212.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000485.md) · [Batch B077](../../batches/B077/README.md)

## Mathematical content

For a norm mu on R2, fix u nonzero and a transverse vector e. The left and right derivatives a_-<=a_+ of t->mu(u+te) determine the extreme supporting functionals at u: both take value mu(u) on u, and their values on e are a_- and a_+. If the support is unique, these slopes agree at a. Homogeneity then yields mu(xu+re)=x mu(u)+a r+o(|r|) as xu+re->u with x>0. Since |r| is controlled by the displacement, this is Frechet differentiability.

If supports are unique everywhere off zero, any convergent subsequence of supports at u_j->u lies in the compact dual unit ball and limits to a support at u. Uniqueness forces convergence of the entire sequence, so the derivative is continuous. Homogeneity gives Dmu(u)[u]=1 on the unit sphere. The implicit function theorem supplies a regular C1 curve. Conversely every support at a regular C1 convex boundary point annihilates its tangent and hence determines its unique tangent line.

This is smoothness of the norm sphere. It is distinct from strict convexity: a regular C1 centrally symmetric convex curve can contain a straight segment.

## Role in the source argument

Identifies precisely what a no-corner argument must prove before one can claim a C1 boundary.

## Antecedents and comparison

This is standard convex analysis. The source includes a self-contained planar proof connecting directional slopes, Frechet derivatives and boundary parametrizations; no new convex regularity principle is claimed.

## Checks and limitations

Smooth p-norms test supporting gradients and first-order remainders. A max norm tests nonunique support at a corner. A stadium unit ball supplies a C1 boundary with flat segments, separating smoothness from strict convexity.

The checks do not show that a first-passage time constant has unique supports.

## Possible applications

Keeping differentiability and strict convexity claims distinct in random-metric arguments.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
