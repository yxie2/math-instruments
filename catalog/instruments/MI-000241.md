# Bin-symmetric stopping separates multiplicative coefficients exactly

[Family 023](../families/openai-math-023.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000140.md) · [Batch B017](../../batches/B017/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Suppose a squarefree factor m is processed through descending size bins, replacing each prime norm in a bin by its common lower endpoint. Start the surrogate product at N(r) and stop when it first reaches threshold Z. Fix the final bin j of lower endpoint ell, k>=1 selected factors in that bin and l>=0 unselected factors. All earlier-bin factors are selected and all later-bin factors are unselected. The crossing predicate is

$$N(r)S(m_+)/\ell<Z\le N(r)S(m_+).$$

It depends only on the selected side b=r m_+. Any earlier failed-stage predicate must likewise depend only on factors already selected. The remaining side is a=m_-u.

For a fixed original squarefree m there are binom(k+l,k) ways to select k of its last-bin factors, and either every such selection passes or none does. Give each weight binom(k+l,k)^(-1). Their sum restores the original coefficient exactly. Multiplicative weights split between m_+ and m_-, yielding independent coefficients beta_(j,k)(b) and alpha_(j,l)(a) and the identity

$$\sum_{j,k\ge1,l\ge0}\binom{k+l}{k}^{-1}\sum_{a,b}\alpha_{j,l}(a)\beta_{j,k}(b)K(ab)$$

for the stopped contribution. The product envelope stays in K, rather than being inserted as a coupled coefficient restriction. If K vanishes on nonsquarefree arguments, overlaps introduced by enlarging the independent factor sums vanish term by term. No omitted cross-side coprimality condition is needed.

When there are O(log X) bins and factors, j,k,l contribute only O((log X)^3) outer indices. Other bin occupancies stay inside their respective coefficients. If each bin has ratio at most 1+delta and at most C log X factors occur, then S<=N<=X^(C log(1+delta))S, supplying a controllable surrogate error.

## Role in the source argument

Preserves the separate coefficient classes required by the source's exceptional-moment and bilinear estimates.

## Antecedents and comparison

Classical multiplicative decompositions and finite symmetry averaging are established. The analytic background includes [Dunn-Radziwill](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.07463v3). The selected candidate refinement is the exact last-bin normalization and one-sided predicates; targeted searches did not certify its priority.

## Checks and limitations

Read the full separation proof. Exact finite prime-bin models compare stopped sums with independently collected coefficients, including multiplicative weights, last-bin ties and annihilation of nonsquarefree overlaps. A control using unequal exact sizes shows why an arbitrary within-bin selection needs different weights.

If a stopping test depends on both sides, or if K does not kill added overlaps, the displayed separation is invalid. This finite identity does not prove the required analytic coefficient estimates.

## Possible applications

Turning a multiplicative stopping rule into bilinear sums with independent coefficient data.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
