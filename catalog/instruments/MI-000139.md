# Zero residual moments turn two-score competition into a determinant bound

[Family 096](../families/openai-math-096.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000091.md) · [Batch B004](../../batches/B004/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Continue with the minimal-active-cell optimal partition under the assumption $C>9/(8\pi)$ and m>=5. Choose the smallest normalized centroid radius $t=|z_m|/\sqrt C$, let $e=z_m/|z_m|$, and put

$$a_j=-\frac{\langle z_m,z_j\rangle}{|z_m|^2}>0,\quad\sum_{j<m}a_j=1,\qquad
w_j=z_j/\sqrt C+a_jte.$$

The residual scores $Y_j=\langle w_j,g\rangle$ are independent of $T=\langle e,g\rangle\sim N(0,1)$. For the covariance H of these residuals, let $D_{jk}=\sqrt{H_{jj}H_{kk}-H_{jk}^2}>0$. Then

$$D_{jk}\le\frac3\pi\,t(1+a_j)(1+a_k)\qquad(j\ne k<m).$$

On the winning cell K of z_m, $T\ge0$, $Y_j\le(1+a_j)tT$, and $\mathbb E[1_KY_j]=0$. Normalize $U=Y_j/((1+a_j)t)$ and $V=Y_k/((1+a_k)t)$. Their joint density is bounded by $M=t^2(1+a_j)(1+a_k)/(2\pi D_{jk})$. Adding their zero first moments and taking a positive part bounds $\mathbb E[1_KT]$ by

$$M\,\mathbb E\int_{u\le T,v\le T}(T+u+v)_+\,du\,dv
=\frac{27M}{6}\mathbb E T_+^3.$$

Here the integral is taken for T>=0 and is zero otherwise; its value for T=s>=0 is $(3s)^3/6$. Comparing with $\mathbb E[1_KT]=\sqrt C\,t>3t/(2\sqrt{2\pi})$ yields the determinant estimate.

## Role in the source argument

Links a small winning centroid to the area scale of every pair of residual competitors. Negative off-diagonal covariances then prevent all relevant determinants from being small simultaneously.

## Antecedents and comparison

Residual orthogonal projection, Gaussian independence and density-max bounds are classical. The candidate combination uses exact zero residual moments to integrate over a bounded triangle rather than an unbounded two-score region. Its surrounding conical optimization is inherited from [Khot–Naor](https://arxiv.org/abs/0807.4626).

## Checks and limitations

Recomputed the covariance determinant under scaling and the triangle integral exactly, and checked the Gaussian third positive moment and final constant 3/pi.

The positive determinant uses linear independence of proper subsets of the extremal centroids. The claimed inequality is conditional on the source’s counterexample setup and is used to eliminate it; it is not a constraint on all centered Gaussian covariance matrices.

## Possible applications

Bounding residual covariance through constrained first moments, especially when several zero-moment identities can be added to make a positive-part integration region bounded.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
