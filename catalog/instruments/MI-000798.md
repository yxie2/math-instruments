# Keep the exact target by a boundary-distance tolerance and degree one

[Family 368](../families/openai-math-368.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000526.md) · [Batch B086](../../batches/B086/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let h:Omega->Omega' be an orientation-preserving homeomorphism between bounded domains in R^3, and let g be smooth with det Dg>0. If |g(x)-h(x)|<dist(h(x),R^3 minus Omega')/2 everywhere, then g is a diffeomorphism onto exactly Omega'.

The straight homotopy stays in Omega'. Writing d for distance to its complement, d(g_t(x))<=3d(h(x))/2. If a compact target set has minimum boundary distance m>0, every preimage under the entire homotopy lies in h^(-1)({d>=2m/3}) times [0,1]. Boundedness of the target makes this a compact control set, so the homotopy is proper.

The endpoint g has degree one. It is a proper local diffeomorphism, hence has finitely many preimages of each target point; each contributes positive local degree. Their sum is one, so there is exactly one preimage. The local smooth inverses patch globally. Value closeness alone would not supply local injectivity.

## Role in the source argument

Ensuring local smoothing operations produce a global map onto the original domain rather than merely a nearby image.

## Antecedents and comparison

This is classical degree-theoretic global inversion with an explicit fine tolerance. It protects the target of each individual approximant, independently of convergence of a sequence.

## Checks and limitations

Polynomial perturbations of the identity on a cube check the boundary-distance bound and positive Jacobian. A small-amplitude oscillatory perturbation shows that fine value closeness alone can coexist with a negative derivative.

The tests do not prove the degree theorem or construct the source approximating diffeomorphisms. Boundedness is used in this stated properness argument.
