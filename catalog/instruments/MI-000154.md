# A small column sample approximates a cut using total squared mass

[Family 012](../families/openai-math-012.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000099.md) · [Batch B007](../../batches/B007/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let E be a real symmetric n-by-n matrix, let $Q=n^{-1}\sum_{i,j}E_{ij}^2$, and assume $\max_i\sum_j|E_{ij}|\le d$. Use the source's normalization $\|E\|_\square=n^{-1}\max_{\epsilon,\delta\in\{-1,1\}^n}|\epsilon^TE\delta|$. For every 1<=m<=n there are m columns J and signs sigma on J such that, writing R for its complement and

$$g_i=\operatorname{sgn}\!\left(\sum_{j\in J}E_{ij}\sigma_j\right),\qquad \operatorname{sgn}(0)=1,$$

one has the explicit abstraction of the source's bound

$$\|E\|_\square\le\frac1n\sum_{k\in R}\left|\sum_{i\in R}E_{ik}g_i\right|+2\sqrt{nQ/m}+2md/n.$$

Choose optimizing column signs delta and row sums $r_i=\sum_kE_{ik}\delta_k$. A uniform m-subset gives the unbiased estimator $\widehat r_i=(n/m)\sum_{j\in J}E_{ij}\delta_j$ with variance at most $(n/m)\sum_kE_{ik}^2$. The inequality $|r|-r\operatorname{sgn}(\widehat r)\le2|r-\widehat r|$ and Cauchy-Schwarz yield the first error for some sample. Removing all ordered pairs incident to J costs at most 2md, using both row and column degree bounds; symmetry supplies the latter.

The source takes $n\asymp B^{0.32}$, $m=\lfloor nB^{-0.18}\rfloor$, $Q\le B^{-0.20}$ and $d=O(B^{0.07})$. The two normalized losses are $O(B^{-0.01})$ and $O(B^{-0.11})$. No pointwise bound on entries is used.

## Role in the source argument

Reduces a cut selected after observing the full random matrix to a finite family indexed by sampled columns and signs.

## Antecedents and comparison

Small-sample optimization is established in [Alon et al.](https://www.cs.yale.edu/homes/kannan/Papers/maxcut.pdf) and [Borgs et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0702004). The extracted form keeps the integrated squared-mass and degree costs explicit for the source scaling; it is not a new general sampling principle.

## Checks and limitations

Read the sampling-without-replacement variance argument, sign loss and deletion cost. Exhaustive small matrices and subsets check the explicit bound, including ties, sparse matrices and a large isolated entry.

The good sample is existential and may depend on the optimizing signs. A numerical search for it does not yield an efficient general cut optimizer. The two errors must both vanish in a proposed asymptotic application.

## Possible applications

Approximating adaptive bilinear tests of weighted random kernels when a uniform entry bound is unavailable.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
