# Absorb a small heat-operator perturbation without coefficient derivatives

[Family 351](../families/openai-math-351.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000522.md) · [Batch B086](../../batches/B086/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let H be smooth on P_2=(-2,2) times [-4,0] and satisfy H_t=aH_yy+bH_y+F, with |a-1|<=epsilon_0 and |H|+|b|+|F|<=K. For sufficiently small universal epsilon_0, the spatial derivative is bounded by C(K) on P_1, without bounds on derivatives of a or b.

Choose an L^s heat estimate with s>3. Apply it to a spatial/past-time cutoff V=zeta H; V need not vanish at terminal time. The error (a-1)V_yy is absorbed by choosing C_s epsilon_0<1/2. Interpolation of H_y against H_yy and H on nested cylinders then gives E(R_1)<=E(R_2)/16+C(K)(R_2-R_1)^(-2), where E sums the second spatial and first time derivative norms.

With R_j=7/4-2^(-j)/4 the errors grow as 4^j but the iteration weight is 16^(-j). The remainder vanishes using finiteness of E(7/4) for each individual smooth H, without assuming a uniform bound. Parabolic Sobolev embedding with s>1+2 gives the gradient estimate, including terminal time by continuity.

## Role in the source argument

Obtaining a derivative bound when coefficient amplitudes are controlled but their derivatives are not.

## Antecedents and comparison

This is classical Calderon-Zygmund perturbation, interpolation and nested-domain iteration. The standard heat estimate and embedding are external analytic inputs; their source-specific use keeps constants independent of coefficient derivatives.

## Checks and limitations

Symbolic expansion checks the cutoff identity, and exact sums check the contraction/error-growth iteration. A noncontractive parameter choice supplies a divergent control.

Finite algebra verifies the bookkeeping, not the Lp estimate or Sobolev embedding. Smallness of the principal perturbation is necessary for this proof.
