# Mark one renewal piece to obtain a two-sided reward convolution

[Family 237](../families/openai-math-237.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000500.md) · [Batch B080](../../batches/B080/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Suppose a bridge factors uniquely into positive-height irreducible pieces with multiplicative weights p(gamma). If the discounted bridge mass b(s,0) is finite for every s>0 and diverges as s decreases to zero, its geometric series implies sum p=1. Under iid pieces of this law,
b(s,u)=1/[1-E exp(-sH-uL)].
Because heights are positive, the events that the kth renewal is at a given height h are disjoint in k, and the bridge mass B_h is the probability of a renewal at h.

For a nonnegative reward f on one complete piece put p_f(j)=E[f;H=j]. Summing the reward over all pieces of a bridge gives B_f=B*p_f*B. Mark the rewarded piece and cut immediately before and after it: both outer parts are arbitrary bridges. Thus sum_h exp(-sh) B_f(h)=b(s,0)^2 E[f exp(-sH)], as an identity of nonnegative sums even if infinite.

For f=L this is a total length identity. It never factors E[L exp(-sH)] into separate length and height expectations. Roots remain fixed, and concatenation must use the source's port convention so length and weight are exactly additive and multiplicative.

## Role in the source argument

Transferring additive observables from finite objects to their irreducible components without introducing a false independence assumption.

## Antecedents and comparison

These are classical renewal and marked-reward identities. The source carefully retains the joint piece law and its fixed-root port convention; no new renewal theorem is claimed.

## Checks and limitations

Finite dependent (H,L,D) laws compare direct ordered-list enumeration with renewal and marked-reward convolutions. A correlated example rejects artificial independence of length and height.

The checks do not prove the honeycomb irreducible law has total mass one; that conclusion uses the stated finite-mass and divergence inputs.
