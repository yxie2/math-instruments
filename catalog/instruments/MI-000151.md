# Geometric nesting weights make a two-chain signed observable quantitatively positive

[Family 226](../families/openai-math-226.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000097.md) · [Batch B006](../../batches/B006/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Consider at most two finite chains, ordered from outside inward. Signs $t_g\in\{-1,1\}$ alternate on each chain, with opposite outermost signs if both chains are present. Suppose $U_g,V_g\ge0$ each decrease by a factor of at least two at every inward step. Then

$$-\frac14\sum_{g\ne g'}U_gV_{g'}t_gt_{g'}\ge0.$$

If every positive weight is at least q>0 and some distinct pair has $U_gV_{g'}>0$, the left side is at least $q^2/16$. Within one chain, grouping by the outer index gives alternating tails at least half their first term. Between chains, the alternating sums have opposite signs and magnitude at least half their outer weight.

In the double-dimer construction, at most M eligible arches occur in each cap. Give an arch A the cap-local weight $w(A)=(2M)^{-b(A)}$, where b(A) counts eligible arches in that cap whose endpoints strictly span A. Every eligible inner arch is spanned by an eligible outer arch. The additional factor 1/(2M), summed over at most M choices, gives the required one-half decay of total weights U and V. All positive totals are at least $q_M=(2M)^{-(M-1)}$.

The source’s color calculation makes the same-loop contribution nonnegative and gives the displayed signed expression for distinct loops. Consequently the full observable X obeys $X\ge(q_M^2/16)\mathbf1_{E_M\cap B_I}$, where $E_M$ is the cap cutoff and $B_I$ is the specified crossing anomaly. Combined with the quadratic insertion estimate, a partition into intervals of length h gives

$$\Pr(B_{\delta,h})\le\Pr(E_M^c)+C_M(h+\delta)^2/h.$$

The useful order is delta to zero, then h to zero, then M to infinity.

## Role in the source argument

Prevents cancellation in a signed transfer observable, so its small expectation can detect geometric excursions invisible to puncture data.

## Antecedents and comparison

Alternating-series positivity is elementary; the candidate device is the cap-local choice that enforces it while respecting the transfer factorization. Established topological and nesting-field limits, including [Basok and Izyurov](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.01574v1), do not by themselves supply the curve-control conclusion. Targeted comparison has not established priority for this weighting device.

## Checks and limitations

Read the arch-spanning argument and all color contractions. Exact rational examples test one and two chains, zero weights and the q-squared lower bound. A negative control shows that arbitrary nondecaying weights can make the expression negative.

The alternating sign pattern, at most two chains and quantitative decay are hypotheses. General signed observables do not become positive without the planar structure and cap cutoff. The final probability estimate also needs the source’s marked-cap identity.

## Possible applications

Building positive detection observables from signed planar expansions while keeping each weight dependent only on one local boundary diagram.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
