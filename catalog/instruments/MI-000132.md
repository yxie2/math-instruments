# Finite Hilbert obstructions can be assembled into a compact obstruction in any Banach space

[Family 098](../families/openai-math-098.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000088.md) · [Batch B003](../../batches/B003/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Suppose a fixed Hilbert subset S is lambda-doubling and admits no finite-distortion embedding into any finite-dimensional Euclidean space. Compactness of bounded coordinate products implies that for every target dimension and distortion bound there is a finite witness inside S. Choose $X_j\subset S$ excluding distortion at most j into $\mathbb R^j$.

For any infinite-dimensional real Banach space B, Dvoretzky’s theorem places each finite Hilbert span in B with distortion strictly below 2. Translate and shrink the images into finite clusters $C_j\subset B_B(2^{-j}v,2^{-j}/100)$, where v is a fixed unit vector. Then

$$K_B=\{0\}\cup\bigcup_{j\ge1}C_j$$

is compact, has a universal doubling bound $1+6\lambda^8$, and admits no embedding into any finite-dimensional normed space at any finite distortion.

The individual clusters are at most $\lambda^4$-doubling. For a radius-r ball, clusters at scales at most r/4 fit in one tail ball; at most five larger dyadic scales can meet the ball if more than one such cluster does. Each intersection needs at most $\lambda^8$ half-radius balls. An embedding of K_B with Euclidean target dimension k and distortion D contradicts the witness X_j for any j>=k with j>=2D.

## Role in the source argument

Separates the infinite geometric obstruction from compactness and from the ambient Banach space. The initial noncompact sheet construction supplies finite witnesses; classical finite representability performs the transfer.

## Antecedents and comparison

Finite-witness compactness and Dvoretzky are classical, not newly proved phenomena. The source explicitly uses only the qualitative finite-dimensional Dvoretzky theorem. Related fixed-set obstructions appear in [Lafforgue–Naor](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~naor/homepage%20files/Lp-doubling.pdf); the present conditional assembly applies to the Hilbert obstruction supplied earlier in the manuscript.

## Checks and limitations

Checked the product-compactness quantifiers, recentering cost in the doubling estimate, dyadic scale count and strict distortion margin. Exact rational checks confirm the tail and cluster-separation constants.

This transfer assumes finite witnesses already exist and gives a very large, nonoptimal doubling constant. It uses completeness of the ambient Banach space through the stated setting and finite-dimensional near-Euclidean representability; no single universal subset is asserted to be isometric in all ambient spaces.

## Possible applications

Turning families of finite metric obstructions into one compact example, while separating source-space placement from target-space impossibility.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
