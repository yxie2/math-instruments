# Extract a large influence and bound all moderately large influences from juntas

[Family 106](../families/openai-math-106.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000232.md) · [Batch B040](../../batches/B040/README.md)

## Mathematical content

For an L-Lipschitz H:T^s -> [-1,1] in the sup metric, let E_i average coordinate i and D_i(H)=||(I-E_i)H||_2. Austin's theorem provides a coordinate conditional expectation on at most J(L,tau) coordinates with error below tau, independently of s.

Fix epsilon>0 and J=J(L,sqrt(epsilon)/2). If H has mean zero and ||H||_2^2>epsilon, choose such a junta G on S. Orthogonality gives ||G||_2^2>3 epsilon/4, while the product-space decomposition gives ||G||_2^2<=sum_(i in S) D_i(H)^2. Hence some D_i(H)>=beta=sqrt(epsilon/(4J)).

Apply the junta theorem again with error beta/2. Outside its at most K=J(L,beta/2) coordinates, D_i(H)<=||H-G'||_2<beta/2, since I-E_i is an orthogonal contraction. Thus at most K coordinates have influence at least beta/2. Both beta and K are dimension independent.

## Role in the source argument

Produces bounded influential-coordinate choices before the number of test blocks is fixed.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Austin, Theorem 1.1, version 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1309.3315v4) was checked directly. Its L1 approximation becomes L2 here because bounded functions satisfy ||f-g||_2^2<=2||f-g||_1. The selected influence argument uses standard product projections.

## Checks and limitations

Seventy-five finite product-space models check the orthogonal variance and outside-junta contraction inequalities.

Finite Boolean-product tests do not prove Austin s continuous theorem or the source graph reduction.

## Possible applications

Turning qualitative dimension-free approximation into a bounded coordinate search in a soundness argument.
