# Preserve Iitaka dimension by integral finite pullback of section rings

[Family 033](../families/openai-math-033.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000409.md) · [Batch B067](../../batches/B067/README.md)

## Mathematical content

For a dominant generically finite morphism pi:V'->V of normal projective complex varieties and a rational Cartier divisor D, kappa(V',pi*D)=kappa(V,D). Clear denominators and use Stein factorization to reduce to a finite map. Pullback embeds R(V,D) into R(V',pi*D). Each homogeneous upstairs section satisfies its monic characteristic polynomial over the finite function-field extension. The coefficient of order j is a rational section of jmD, and valuation inequalities above every prime divisor make it regular in the required downstairs divisorial lattice. Normality promotes these codimension-one conditions to global sections.

Thus the graded extension is integral and the section rings have the same transcendence degree. If no positive-degree section exists downstairs, the nonzero norm of an upstairs section rules out one upstairs. Finally, kappa(D)=0 forces every nonzero H^0(V,mD) to have dimension one: two independent sections would have a nonconstant ratio and a positive-dimensional rational image.

## Role in the source argument

Keeps complete plurisection systems under control when introducing generically finite auxiliary models.

## Antecedents and comparison

The underlying characteristic-polynomial and norm construction is classical. [Stacks Project, Norms](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0BCX), especially the finite integral normal-base construction, was read as an algebraic antecedent. The source applies the argument to graded divisorial sections.

## Checks and limitations

Exact cyclic-cover algebras compute multiplication matrices, characteristic polynomials and norms of sections, checking coefficient degree bounds and integral equations.

Cyclic affine models test the algebra; they do not prove all geometric hypotheses of the source normalization or subadditivity.

## Possible applications

Showing that an auxiliary finite cover does not create additional Iitaka dimension.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
