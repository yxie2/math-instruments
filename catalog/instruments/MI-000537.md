# Fix matrix channels before choosing a regular auxiliary coefficient

[Family 262](../families/openai-math-262.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000306.md) · [Batch B055](../../batches/B055/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Suppose locally absolutely continuous matrix blocks G_alpha satisfy |(G_alpha v)'|<=kappa|G_alpha v| almost everywhere for every fixed vector v. If G_alpha(x_0)v=0, Gronwall in both directions gives G_alpha(x)v=0 everywhere. Thus each kernel and row space is constant, including points outside the initial full-measure set. If G_alpha G_beta^T=0 almost everywhere for distinct labels, continuity makes the constant row spaces mutually orthogonal. Their total dimension is at most the physical matrix dimension m, so only finitely many are nonzero.

The source obtains this differential bound from pointwise choices G_alpha'=B_alpha G_alpha with uniformly bounded B_alpha, without first requiring a measurable choice of B_alpha. After its potential relation gives C^2 regularity, restrict G_alpha to its fixed row space and call the resulting full-column-rank matrix E. If B_alpha commutes with G_alpha G_alpha^T, E' lies in range(E). For P=E(E^T E)^(-1)E^T,

P'=(I-P)E'(E^T E)^(-1)E^T+E(E^T E)^(-1)(E')^T(I-P)=0.

Thus the range is fixed as well. In constant bases the active matrix D is square and invertible, and B_0=D'D^(-1) is a regular, uniquely determined coefficient. Under the additional source identities alpha^2 I-B_0^2=(DD^T)^r>0 and D''=alpha^2 D-(r+1)(DD^T)^rD, it satisfies B_0'=r(B_0^2-alpha^2 I). A constant basis diagonalizing B_0 at one point diagonalizes it everywhere by ODE uniqueness. The scalar entries are -alpha tanh(r alpha(x-y_j)), yielding fixed-channel sech-squared profiles. Obtaining the pointwise equality relations is a separate task.

## Role in the source argument

Prevents rotating physical channels in the source proposed matrix equality classification.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Benguria-Loss, A simple proof of a theorem of Laptev and Weidl](https://archive.intlpress.com/site/pub/files/_fulltext/journals/mrl/2000/0007/0002/MRL-2000-0007-0002-a005.pdf), Lemma 3 and its proof on printed pages 199-200, use M prime times M inverse, a matrix Riccati equation and fixed eigenvectors. Those passages were read. The selected source fixes possibly rectangular block spaces first; no new general ODE principle is claimed.

## Checks and limitations

Explicit rectangular matrix curves test the projection derivative and fixed kernels. Symbolic hyperbolic profiles test all active Riccati identities. A rotating-range example shows why range preservation is required.

These checks do not prove that every spectral equality case supplies the required pointwise matrix identities.

## Possible applications

Recovering fixed channels from almost-everywhere matrix relations even when the initial auxiliary coefficient has no chosen regular representative.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
