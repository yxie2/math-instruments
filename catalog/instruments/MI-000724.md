# Glue tree-shaped blocks without losing a translation multiplicity

[Family 216](../families/openai-math-216.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000489.md) · [Batch B077](../../batches/B077/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Suppose a core and successive annuli form a tree of block adjacencies. Fix full relative height data on a separating guard in each annulus. Given these data, the inner and outer configurations are conditionally independent. Let m_i be the guard law in an isolated block, and T_i the ratio of restored to cut partition functions at an interface, with one common translation in the numerator and one translation per block in the denominator.

Then Z_full=Z_core product_i Z_i times integral T_0(u_1) product_(i=1)^(N-1) T_i(u_i,u_(i+1)) product_i dm_i(u_i). Integer block constants modulo one common constant correspond bijectively to edge differences on a tree: fix a root value and recover the others along unique paths. This explains why no multiplicity factor appears and why relative translations can be summed independently at interfaces.

For several nests attached to one connected exterior, integrate the exterior once as a joint kernel in all incident guard data. Its different boundary portions need not be independent. A cycle of block adjacencies would instead impose a zero-sum constraint on the edge differences; the same free product formula would not apply.

## Role in the source argument

Provides an exact partition representation before estimating losses across many annuli.

## Antecedents and comparison

Tree incidence coordinates and conditional-independence factorization are classical. The source applies them with separate translation quotients and an unsplit exterior kernel; no new general tree factorization is claimed.

## Checks and limitations

Finite cyclic height models enumerate global block constants and compare tree-edge coordinates. A triangle exhibits the missing cycle constraint, and a common-exterior example shows why separate marginal kernels cannot replace its joint kernel.

The finite model does not verify the infinite integer-height sums, the chosen guards or the source prescribed-data inequalities.

## Possible applications

Checking topology and gauge normalization before multiplying local partition ratios.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
