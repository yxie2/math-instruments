# Compress a Boolean function and choose a bias-controlled small coordinate

[Family 119](../families/openai-math-119.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000274.md) · [Batch B049](../../batches/B049/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let T_rho be the uniform-cube noise operator, 0<=rho<=1, and H any concave profile on [-1,1]. Sorting the two sections of a Boolean function into their pointwise minimum and maximum preserves its mean and weakly decreases E H(T_rho f). Indeed, noise in the other coordinates leaves the pair center a unchanged and increases its half-spread from |c| to T|f_+-f_-|/2>=|c|. The sum H(a+u)+H(a-u) is even and concave, hence decreases for u>=0. One sorting pass per coordinate yields an increasing function because minimum and maximum preserve monotonicity in already sorted coordinates.

For an increasing Boolean function of mean m with at least four active coordinates, there is an active coordinate with 0<x=E[f(X)X_i]<=b(m), where b(m)=min{3/8,9/16-|m|/2,1-|m|}. Its two section means are m-x and m+x.

Choose the least active coefficient x. It is at most E[fZ] for Z the average of any four active signs. The values 1,1/2,0,-1/2,-1 have probabilities 1,4,6,4,1 divided by 16. At fixed mean, rearranging positive mass onto the highest values of Z maximizes the correlation. With q=(1-|m|)/2, twice the integral over the top fraction q equals 2q for q<=1/16, q+1/16 up to 5/16, and 3/8 thereafter. These are precisely the three pieces of b(m). Allowing fractional mass at a threshold is a relaxation, so the bound also holds when the function depends on additional coordinates.

## Role in the source argument

Restricts the scalar induction domain in the companion Hellinger argument while retaining output bias.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Anantharam-Bogdanov-Chakrabarti-Jayram-Nair, Proposition 2](https://chandra.ie.cuhk.edu.hk/pub/papers/HC/hel-conj.pdf) proves the one-coordinate compression principle for convex entropy profiles; its proof was read. The source states the concave direction and the explicit four-coordinate bias envelope. No exhaustive novelty claim for that envelope.

## Checks and limitations

All Boolean functions on four coordinates are tested for the coordinate-correlation relaxation. Increasing functions with four active coordinates are checked for the chosen coefficient and section means. Numerical noise tests compare each compression step under the Hellinger and quadratic concave profiles.

This verifies the reductions only; the source scalar certificates, dimension induction and continuation proof of Hellinger contraction are not certified.

## Possible applications

Reducing a Boolean noise extremal problem to increasing functions and an explicit two-section domain depending on mean.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
