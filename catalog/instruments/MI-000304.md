# Lift a quasi-isomorphism through nilpotent labels and recover a positive power

[Family 306](../families/openai-math-306.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000171.md) · [Batch B025](../../batches/B025/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let L=Q[t_1,...,t_a]/(t_1^2,...,t_a^2), with augmentation ideal J, and let a finite free L-complex carry a chain map T_L whose reduction modulo J is a rational quasi-isomorphism. Filter its mapping cone by J^r. Since J^(a+1)=0 and the associated graded consists of copies of the reduced cone tensored with J^r/J^(r+1), each graded piece is acyclic. Induction through the finite filtration makes the full cone acyclic.

Thus T_L induces an automorphism F on finite-dimensional rational homology, even if that homology is not free over L. If a cycle u and cocycle v have nonzero all-label coefficient [t_1...t_a]vu, then some positive power also has nonzero coefficient. Indeed, for the characteristic polynomial a_0+...+a_N s^N of F, invertibility gives a_0!=0 and

$$I=-a_0^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^N a_jF^j.$$

Apply the linear pairing obtained from u, v and all-label coefficient extraction. Its nonzero value at I forces a nonzero value at some F^j, 1<=j<=N. A cyclic permutation on N coordinates shows that the first positive recurrence can occur at N.

In the source, distinct even labels encode distributing each insertion to exactly one factor. The relations t_i^2=0 discard repeated labels, with no factorials. The existence and geometric interpretation of the labelled differential, maps and nonzero initial pairing are additional inputs.

## Role in the source argument

Moves a nonzero zero-bridge invariant to a positive number of bridges without assuming freeness of deformed homology.

## Antecedents and comparison

This combines the classical finite-filtration acyclicity argument with Cayley-Hamilton. The source makes its applicability to nonfree homology explicit; no independent novelty claim is made.

## Checks and limitations

Twelve exact nilpotent-ring complexes with nonfree homology verify commuting maps and invertibility on both homology groups. Twelve cyclic examples attain the dimension bound for the first positive recurrence.

The examples do not construct the labelled Floer theory or establish its initial nonzero coefficient. Without invertibility, a nonzero value at the identity need not survive any positive power.

## Possible applications

Retaining a detected coefficient after repeated chain operations over finite nilpotent parameter rings.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
