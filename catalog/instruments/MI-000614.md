# Extend a continuous zero trace by signed truncation

[Family 071](../families/openai-math-071.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000383.md) · [Batch B064](../../batches/B064/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let F be closed in the Riemann sphere and w continuous and real-valued, vanishing on F. If w is locally W^(1,2) off F and has finite Dirichlet energy there, then w is globally W^(1,2) and its weak gradient vanishes almost everywhere on F.

For delta>0, w_delta=sgn(w)(|w|-delta)_+ has compact support away from F by continuity and compactness. Local Sobolev calculus and a finite localization make it globally Sobolev. Its energy is at most the off-F energy of w, and its gradient is zero on F. Since ||w_delta-w||_infinity<=delta, weak compactness in W^(1,2) identifies the limit with w; testing the gradients on F preserves their zero value. No smoothness, measure-zero or capacity-zero assumption on F is needed. The source applies this to the difference between a continuous test and a locally smoothed test having exactly the same boundary trace.

## Role in the source argument

Restores exact boundary data after smoothing in a circle-domain model.

## Antecedents and comparison

This is a classical Sobolev truncation and weak-compactness argument. The complete source proof was read; no new general extension theorem or uniformization theorem is claimed.

## Checks and limitations

Piecewise polynomial zero-trace functions on domains with multiple complementary intervals check uniform truncation error and exact nonincrease of Dirichlet energy.

One-dimensional energy models illustrate the chain rule and compact support. They do not verify circle-domain model construction or arbitrary planar boundary topology.

## Possible applications

Extending finite-energy corrections across a closed set while preserving an exact continuous trace.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
