# Turn a weighted interval comparison into an integrated Jensen gap

[Family 344](../families/openai-math-344.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000256.md) · [Batch B047](../../batches/B047/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let b>0, lambda=pi/b, p_0(t)=lambda^2/sin^2(lambda t), and let positive continuous p have bounded g=log(p/p_0). Let W>=0 be measurable, W(b-z)=W(z) almost everywhere, and W(z)/sin(lambda z) essentially bounded. On 0<x<y<b let nu_xy have density p_0/integral_x^y p_0.

Then the integral over x<y of

W(y-x)[log(integral_x^y p / integral_x^y p_0)-(g(x)+g(y))/2]

equals the integral of W(y-x)[log E_(nu_xy) exp(g)-E_(nu_xy) g], and is therefore nonnegative. All quantities are finite because the averages and logarithmic averages of bounded g are bounded.

The cancellation is exact. Put K(z)=W(z)/sin(lambda z), I_W=integral_0^b W, and F(t)=integral_(0<x<t<y<b) K(y-x)sin(lambda x)sin(lambda y) dx dy. Fubini makes F absolutely continuous. Reflection symmetry gives F'(t)=I_W sin(lambda t)cos(lambda t), hence F(t)=I_W sin^2(lambda t)/(2 lambda). Thus p_0(t)F(t)/lambda=I_W/2: the integrated interval average of g equals (I_W/2)integral g. The integrated endpoint average has the same value by reflection symmetry.

No derivative of W and no integral of p_0 over the entire singular interval is used.

## Role in the source argument

Cancels the linear endpoint terms after the source obtains logarithmic block comparisons along geodesics.

## Antecedents and comparison

The source explicitly identifies this as a logarithmic form of [Kazdan, An Inequality Arising in Geometry](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-61876-5_14), Appendix E, especially E.4-E.8. The publisher title, attribution and preview were checked; the full appendix was not retrieved. Exact correspondence to those numbered equations remains source-attributed. No novelty claim is made for the cancellation.

## Checks and limitations

Read the full Fubini proof. Symbolic checks cover W=sin(z) and W=sin(z)(1+cos(2z)/2) at b=pi; quadrature checks a symmetric discontinuous weight. An asymmetric weight gives unequal linear coefficient densities.

These checks do not establish the global geodesic comparison, its matrix inputs or the metric classification. Removing weight symmetry invalidates this exact cancellation in general.

## Possible applications

Turning endpoint-normalized integral inequalities into convexity remainders while keeping singular model densities integrable on each interior interval.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
