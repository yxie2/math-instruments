# Mix four parity positions by deleting only two mask permutations

[Family 117](../families/openai-math-117.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000275.md) · [Batch B050](../../batches/B050/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Work over F_q of characteristic two, q>=4. Let u_j be an evaluation list such that every quadratic form not identically zero on the list vanishes at no more than a lambda_0 fraction of its indices. On the span of block matrices generated by u_j u_j^T, use the random increment h=(c tensor u_j)(e tensor u_j)^T, with c,e independent uniform vectors and j uniform. Its law is invariant under nonzero scalar multiplication.

For an additive character associated with a linear functional L, write L(h)=c^T M_j e. Averaging first over c gives character expectation q^(-rank M_j). A nontrivial L has a nonzero quadratic entry on the list, so its walk eigenvalue is at most lambda_1=lambda_0+1/q. Centered indicators consequently give the internal-edge estimate <1_D,T1_D>/mu(D)<=mu(D)+lambda_1.

Choose four distinct ordered times t_i, uniform A in the matrix space, and uniform sigma in F_q*. Put alpha_i=1/product_(j!=i)(t_i-t_j) and s_i=(A+t_i h,sigma alpha_i). Quadratic restrictions satisfy sum alpha_i P(A+t_i h)=0, so applying any nonzero F_2-linear functional gives a parity test. Each s_i is uniform.

Conditioning on the first complete position preserves the time and direction laws. The second position changes space by (b-a)h and mask by alpha_b/alpha_a. Scalar invariance separates these changes. At fixed distinct a,b,c, with k=(b-c)/(a-c), the ratio alpha_a/alpha_b=k(b-d)/(a-d) runs uniformly through F_q* minus {k,k^2}. The omitted values are distinct in characteristic two. On centered mask functions the sum of all multiplicative permutations is zero; deleting two gives operator norm at most 2/(q-3). Thus every pair operator has centered norm at most max{lambda_1,2/(q-3)}. For q=4 the bound is valid but need not be useful.

## Role in the source argument

Produces pairwise mixing binary parity queries for the hardness source from its quadratic evaluation pencil.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Alon-Chung, Lemma 2.3](https://www.cs.tau.ac.il/~nogaa/PDFS/Publications/Explicit%20construction%20of%20linear%20sized%20tolerant%20networks.pdf) supplies the centered-indicator spectral calculation; the retrieved author PDF is the 2006 reprint and its proof was read. Finite-field character orthogonality and Lagrange interpolation are classical. The source mask-ratio calculation was read directly.

## Checks and limitations

Exact finite-field enumeration verifies bilinear character averages, four-point quadratic cancellation and every excluded-value set over F_8. Numerical singular values check the finite mask operators.

These checks do not construct the required large evaluation pencil or validate the complete constant-factor hardness reduction.

## Possible applications

Turning low-degree field queries into binary parity tests while retaining pairwise spectral mixing.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
