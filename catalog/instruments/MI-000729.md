# Escape a finite directional slab using strict ellipticity alone

[Family 220](../families/openai-math-220.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000491.md) · [Batch B078](../../batches/B078/README.md)

## Mathematical content

For an iid strictly elliptic nearest-neighbor environment and nonzero direction v, almost surely a finite upper bound on h(X_n)=X_n dot v implies h(X_n)->-infinity. Normalize the largest absolute coordinate of v to one and choose a signed coordinate step a with height increment one.

Fix integers b,k and choose m>b+k. On the event that all heights are at most b but heights at least -k occur infinitely often, split according to the number of columns parallel to a visited at those eligible times. With finitely many columns, the finite slab contains only finitely many eligible sites, so one site is visited infinitely often. In a fixed environment its prescribed m-step path in direction a has probability q_x(omega)>0. Trials spaced at least m steps apart fail with probability at most (1-q_x)^r; no lower bound uniform in x or omega is needed.

With infinitely many eligible columns, choose first eligible visits in fresh columns and nonoverlapping m-step trials. Their departure rows have not previously been exposed, so their conditional annealed success probability is (E omega(0,a))^m>0. Geometric failure bounds again exclude the event. Trial probabilities are conditioned on past information, never on future confinement. A countable union over b,k and starting sites gives the simultaneous conclusion.

## Role in the source argument

Prevents oscillation inside a bounded directional slab under merely strict ellipticity.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Zerner and Merkl, author PDF](https://www.math.uni-tuebingen.de/~zerner/pub/zeroone.pdf), Lemma 4 and its proof on PDF pages 3-5, were read from rendered pages because text extraction was garbled. They already establish this finite-slab escape mechanism for real directions under strict ellipticity. This extraction is an established antecedent, not a new zero-one theorem.

## Checks and limitations

Finite row mixtures test fresh-column success probabilities against exact environment enumeration. Geometric repeated-trial calculations include arbitrarily small positive probabilities and a zero-probability control.

The almost-sure infinite-time conclusion follows from the proof, not a finite simulation. The lemma alone does not decide the probability of a chosen directional sign.

## Possible applications

Separating strict positivity arguments from estimates that actually require uniform ellipticity.
