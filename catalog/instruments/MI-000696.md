# Keep a product uniformly inside the Cayley half-plane

[Family 122](../families/openai-math-122.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000465.md) · [Batch B074](../../batches/B074/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let 0<gamma<=1, and let z be a nonempty product of at most N numbers exp(-a_j+i theta_j), with a_j>0 and a_j>=gamma min(|theta_j|,N theta_j^2). Then Re((1+z)/(1-z))>=c gamma for an absolute c>0.

Write T=sum a_j and psi=sum theta_j. Split absolute angles into U=sum_(|theta|>=1/N)|theta| and V=sum_(|theta|<1/N)|theta|. Cauchy-Schwarz on at most N small angles gives T>=gamma(U+V^2). If either U or V exceeds one, this bounds T below by gamma; otherwise U+V^2>=(U+V)^2/2. Therefore T>=gamma min(1,psi^2)/2, with no choice of a principal angle required.

The Cayley real part is (1-exp(-2T))/|1-exp(-T+i psi)|^2. For T<=1, the numerator is at least (1-exp(-2))T and the denominator is at most T^2+4min(1,psi^2)<=9T/gamma. For T>1, the ratio is at least (1-exp(-2))/4. Thus c=(1-exp(-2))/9 is one valid explicit choice from the proof. In the source recursion, N_j<=4N_(j-1)+1 counts elementary factors; conjugation preserves the damping condition but does not remove a factor from that budget.

## Role in the source argument

Provides a quantitative domain margin for later analytic continuation of products of parameters.

## Antecedents and comparison

The Cayley identity and the two-regime Cauchy-Schwarz estimate are elementary. The source packages them with its recursive multiplicity count; exact priority of that interface is not established.

## Checks and limitations

Numerical tests include cancellation, large angles, tiny damping and repeated factors. A negative control omitting the factor-count multiplier loses the uniform margin.

These checks do not verify the multiscale signal construction or the reconstruction upper bound.

## Possible applications

Keeping multiplicatively combined analytic parameters away from a continuation boundary.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
