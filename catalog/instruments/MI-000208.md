# A shattering corner and dense prefix force an ordered binary anchor into one mode

[Family 190](../families/openai-math-190.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000125.md) · [Batch B013](../../batches/B013/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Start with the 64-by-64 complement of the identity. Replace rows 33 through 64 and columns 60 through 64 by all five-bit words in lexicographic order, obtaining S. Its rows and columns are distinct; each column has at least 31 ones in the first 32 rows and each row at least 31 ones in the first 32 columns. Every row has at least 58 ones and every column at least 48.

In the source matrix, all anchor groups precede all nonanchor positions. Each mode carries a blowup of S, different modes have zero anchor-to-anchor entries, and nonanchor positions have unit-vector or zero signatures against each mode. Variable positions participate in at most four modes; dummy positions are identical within their group. Nonanchor traces on any five nonanchor columns have at most 27 values: variable rows are unions of at most three suffixes, and there are at most five available nonempty suffixes across the selected column classes, giving sum_(j=0)^3 binom(5,j)=26; dummy rows add at most one.

Every ordered copy of S therefore lies entirely in the 64 anchor groups of one mode, selecting one representative per group. Otherwise if at most 32 positions on each axis were anchors, the last 32 rows and last five columns would shatter five nonanchor columns. If more than 32 selected rows are anchors, their density excludes variable columns, leaving at least 63 anchor columns. Common ones force these columns into a single mode, density forces the selected anchor rows there too, and unit-vector signatures exclude the remaining dummy or variable positions. The transpose argument handles the other axis. Distinct rows and columns prohibit taking two representatives from one group.

Append two body rows and columns to form H with upper-right columns e1,e2, lower-left rows e1 transpose,e2 transpose, and body P=((1,0),(1,1)). In every H-copy these signatures force the body positions into the designated row and column roles of that same mode.

## Role in the source argument

Allows the source to count every ordered copy by analyzing only the prescribed small body roles.

## Antecedents and comparison

The ordered binary-matrix problem and earlier polynomial bounds are described in [Alon and Ben-Eliezer, Problem 1.4 and Theorem 1.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1609.04235v3). The fixed anchor and trace-versus-density pinning are the selected candidate mechanism; this is not an exhaustive priority result.

## Checks and limitations

Read the complete pinning argument. Exact construction checks distinctness, prefix density and all 32 corner words; small tree models check nonanchor trace bounds and mode incidences.

The ordering of anchors before all other positions is necessary to locate the shattering corner. A generic dense block without distinct rows and columns or sparse external signatures would not satisfy the proof.

## Possible applications

Encoding designated roles inside an uncolored ordered binary structure while excluding unintended copies.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
