# Amplify a highest-weight multiplicity through leading-exponent sumsets

[Family 210](../families/openai-math-210.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000329.md) · [Batch B057](../../batches/B057/README.md)

## Mathematical content

For a finite set D of C points with affine dimension h, the classical repeated-sumset bound is

|kD|>=f(C,h,k)=binom(k+h,h)+(C-1-h)binom(k+h-1,h), for k>=1.

The source includes a triangulation proof. Choose a generic interior point and assign each point of the convex hull to the full simplex entered by moving slightly toward it. A simplex with e strict barycentric coordinates contributes binom(k-e+h,h) distinct k-fold vertex sums. Exactly one simplex has e=0; counting the C original vertices at k=1 shows that exactly C-h-1 simplices have e=1. Keeping those two contributions gives the bound.

Now suppose a degree-d_0 highest-weight space in a polynomial algebra has dimension at least C. Row reduction gives C polynomials with distinct leading exponent vectors D, all nonnegative and of total degree d_0. Products add leading exponents and highest weights. Choosing one product for each point of kD therefore gives independent highest-weight polynomials at the k-fold weight.

Projection onto h suitable coordinate positions is injective on aff(D), so C<=binom(d_0+h,h). Consequently a computable lower bound z on h comes from the least integer with binom(d_0+z,z)>=C. The source weakens this by capping z at 18 for its arithmetic budget. For j<h, f(C,j+1,k)-f(C,j,k)=binom(k+j-1,j)(k-1)(C-j-1)/(j+1)>=0. Thus an existing lower bound Q(x)>=C in Sym(Sym^6 C^6), with total partition size 6d_0, yields Q(kx)>=f(C,z,k). This amplifies a known multiplicity; it does not establish the initial lower bound.

## Role in the source argument

Transports selected finite multiplicity certificates to many larger weights in the source sixth-power computation.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Matolcsi-Ruzsa, Sumsets and the convex hull](https://arxiv.org/abs/0810.1485v1), Theorem 1.5 on page 2, gives the displayed repeated-set inequality after setting A=B=D and replacing their repetition count by k-1. That statement was read. The leading-value multiplication argument is classical; the source adds an explicit affine-dimension estimate for its multiplicity certificate.

## Checks and limitations

160 finite sumset comparisons verify the dimension and monotonicity bounds. Fifty-four polynomial products have independently calculated distinct leading exponents agreeing with the selected sumset points.

The program does not compute the source full plethysm tables, validate the bounded certificate range, or prove Foulkes comparison.

## Possible applications

Turning a small-dimensional space of algebraic witnesses into quantitatively many witnesses at multiplied degrees and weights.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
