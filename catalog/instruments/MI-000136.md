# Symmetric-slot averaging compares tensor actions with a geometric trace weight

[Family 093](../families/openai-math-093.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000090.md) · [Batch B004](../../batches/B004/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let psi be a finite direct sum of real symmetric tensors, each having at least k slots, with integer $k\ge2$. Its one-slot reduction is the positive matrix rho obtained by flattening one slot against all remaining slots and summing across components; no unit normalization is required. Write $R=\sqrt\rho$. For a self-adjoint matrix H, let $H_{av}$ average its action over the slots of each component. Then

$$\|H_{av}\psi\|^2\le\operatorname{tr}(RHRH)+\frac2k\operatorname{tr}(\rho H^2).$$

For a d-slot component define $a_d=\langle\psi_d,H_{(1)}^2\psi_d\rangle$ and $c_d=\langle\psi_d,H_{(1)}H_{(2)}\psi_d\rangle$. Expansion gives $a_d/d+(1-1/d)c_d$, and $|c_d|\le a_d$ accounts for the 2/k error. The remaining estimate is $|\sum_dc_d|\le\operatorname{tr}(RHRH)$.

The proof expresses the two-slot correlations by a positive map C with $C(I)=C^*(I)=\rho$. Conjugating by rho on its support yields a unital rho-preserving map. Interpolation between its operator-norm and trace-norm endpoint contractions gives the weighted Hilbert–Schmidt contraction producing the geometric trace weight.

## Role in the source argument

Long symmetric tensors make average slot actions close to the geometric matrix energy. In the source this helps convert tensor near-equalities into commutator and square-root-energy control.

## Antecedents and comparison

The source explicitly says its correlation bound also follows from the Wigner–Yanase case of [Lieb’s trace-concavity theorem](https://www.numdam.org/item/RCP25_1973__19__A4_0/). Its alternative proof uses the weighted Schatten interpolation framework of [Beigi](https://arxiv.org/abs/1306.5920v6). The classical correlation inequality is not counted as a new theorem; the averaging formulation exposes its role in a tensor hierarchy.

## Checks and limitations

Checked the expansion by equal and unequal slots, the support restriction and the interpolation endpoints. Finite symmetric-tensor examples test both the correlation bound and the final averaging estimate, including singular reductions and direct sums.

Total symmetry is essential to the common one-slot reduction and cross-slot expansion. The trace is unnormalized. The 2/k error is part of the statement and cannot be dropped merely because the ambient dimension is large.

## Possible applications

Comparing many-body symmetric tensors with one-body matrix energies, and importing quantum trace inequalities into classical analytic near-equality arguments.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
