# A thin tilted cell separates transport-map movement from target movement

[Family 374](../families/openai-math-374.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000110.md) · [Batch B009](../../batches/B009/README.md)

## Mathematical content

On the fixed cube [-1,1]^d with its uniform probability rho, d>=2, choose 0<a<1/2 and b=a/2. The convex potentials $u=\max\{-x_1,x_1,a\}$ and $\widetilde u=\max\{-x_1,x_1,a+bx_2\}$ have three slopes each. Their gradients are the unique quadratic optimal maps to

$$\mu_a=\tfrac{1-a}{2}(\delta_{-e_1}+\delta_{e_1})+a\delta_0,\quad\nu_a=\tfrac{1-a}{2}(\delta_{-e_1}+\delta_{e_1})+a\delta_{be_2}.$$

The central source cells are A={|x_1|<a} and B={|x_1|<a+bx_2}. They have equal mass a, but their symmetric difference has mass b/2. Thus

$$W_2(\mu_a,\nu_a)^2=ab^2,\quad W_1(\mu_a,\nu_a)=ab,\quad\|T_{\mu_a}-T_{\nu_a}\|_{L^2(\rho)}^2=b/2+ab^2.$$

Matching like atoms proves the Wasserstein upper bounds; projection to the second coordinate proves the same lower bounds for every coupling. The first component of the map difference has squared size one exactly on A symmetric-difference B; the second contributes b^2 on B. This proves the map identity by orthogonality.

Consequently W2=a^(3/2)/2 while the map distance is sqrt(a)*sqrt(1+a^2)/2. Every exponent greater than one third fails uniformly, although the source, target container and number of atoms stay fixed. W1 scales as a^2/2, so the same example gives a quarter-power obstruction in W1, not an inconsistency between the two metrics. The small central mass tends to zero; the example does not contradict estimates whose constants depend on a positive lower mass bound.

Optimality itself follows from the supporting inequality x dot y+c(y)<=u(x): the gradient plan attains its integrated upper bound, and the maximizing slope is unique off a finite union of hyperplanes. No transport solver is needed to identify the maps.

## Role in the source argument

Shows exactly how small target displacement can reassign a much larger portion of source mass between distant slopes.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Letrouit’s paper](https://comptes-rendus.academie-sciences.fr/mathematique/item/10.5802/crmath.834.pdf) states the convex-source square-root conjecture and gives instability for other source geometries. This example keeps a uniform convex source and uses a vanishing third atom. The search located this construction in the release and its subsequent expositions; that is not exhaustive priority certification.

## Checks and limitations

Checked every cell mass and distance formula symbolically by slices. Exact rational integrations and finite three-by-three transport linear programs verify the identities for a range of a values, including degenerating masses. The exponent conclusion follows analytically from the formulas, not from a fitted numerical slope.

The construction needs two source coordinates and a vanishing positive atom mass. It is not a failure of continuity: both distances tend to zero. It supplies a sharpness obstruction, while the matching uniform upper theorem requires the separate cell and interpolation arguments.

## Possible applications

Stress-testing target-uniform optimal transport estimates and constructing benchmark examples with explicit map instability.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
