# Convert exact translated relator counts into a uniform quadratic error

[Family 249](../families/openai-math-249.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000192.md) · [Batch B030](../../batches/B030/README.md)

## Mathematical content

In an aligned cellular presentation, an expression z=product_j u_j r_j^{sigma_j} u_j^{-1} has the exact translated chain identity sum_j sigma_j bar(u_j)e_{r_j}=e_{rho_z}. The boundary-basis isomorphism is what upgrades equality of boundary chains to equality of these coefficients.

Evaluate relators as unit quaternions. Suppose every defect has distance at most h from one, each conjugating word has distance at most K(epsilon+h) from one, ordinary relators evaluate exactly to one, and the exact translated signed multiplicities leave one distinguished defect. Removing conjugations costs at most 2nK(epsilon+h)h. Reordering at most n(n-1)/2 pairs costs at most n(n-1)h^2, since |ab-ba|<=2|a-1||b-1|. Equal factors with opposite exponents now cancel exactly. Hence

$$|L(z,v)-L(\rho_z,v)|\le2nK(\epsilon+h)h+n(n-1)h^2.$$

The original short word in epsilon-small generators also satisfies |L(z,v)-1|<=length(z) epsilon by telescoping. Constants depend on finitely many fixed words, not on the size of the finite quotient. Collisions of translated vertices in a quotient add their coefficients and preserve the exact identity.

## Role in the source argument

Controls the distinguished relator defects uniformly over finite quotients in the presentation comparison.

## Antecedents and comparison

The quaternion commutator estimate is elementary. The useful source interface is the exact translated cellular count, without which an uncancelled first-order term can remain.

## Checks and limitations

Two hundred forty noncommuting quaternion models satisfy the stated bound; an uncancelled-factor control has first-order size.

The verification imposes the signed-count identity as an input and does not construct an aligned presentation or its boundary-basis isomorphism.

## Possible applications

Uniform perturbative estimates for products of conjugates and finite-quotient word equations.
