# Prime-exponent concentration gives a summable gcd tensor kernel

[Family 022](../families/openai-math-022.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000139.md) · [Batch B017](../../batches/B017/README.md)

## Mathematical content

For 1/2<u<1, A>=1 and probability vectors alpha,beta on nonnegative integers, define

$$T_q=\sum_{i,j}q^{|i-j|}A^{\mathbf1_{i\ne j}}(\alpha_i\beta_j)^u,\qquad0<q<1/2.$$

Then T_q<=1+O_(u,A)(q^(1/(1-u))). If a probability array m_ij is pointwise dominated by these summands, sufficiently small q forces a common index k with d=(1-alpha_k)+(1-beta_k)=O_(u,A)(q^(1/(1-u))). Its double-off mass is O(d^(2u)); mass with exactly one index k and the other at distance at least a is O(q^a d^u).

The off-diagonal convolution has l^1 norm O_A(q). If T_q>=1, the diagonal and l^(2u) norms first force both probability vectors to concentrate at the same index. Splitting into central, single-off and double-off terms gives T_q<=1-c_u d+O_(u,A)(d^(2u)+q d^u). Since 2u>1, absorption and optimization give the stated scale. Holder's inequality on the geometric tail is independent of support size.

For s>1-u and nonnegative sequences x_L,y_M,

$$\sum_{L,M\ge1}\frac{(x_Ly_M)^u}{(LM/\gcd(L,M)^2)^s}\ll_{u,s}(\sum_Lx_L\sum_My_M)^u.$$

For finite supports, normalize and successively slice by prime exponents. The local constants at large p are 1+O(p^(-s/(1-u))); their product converges. Finitely many small primes have bounded convolution constants. Monotone convergence removes finite support. The strict condition s>1-u is what makes this particular Euler-product proof summable.

## Checks and limitations

Read both proofs. Finite probability vectors check the central/single-off/double-off estimates, and finite prime-coordinate arrays check tensor slicing and dependence within the integer distributions. The numerical checks do not determine universal asymptotic constants.

A factor A on the diagonal would change the near-one normalization and the concentration implication. The borderline s=1-u is outside this proof. No distributional independence between prime coordinates is assumed.
