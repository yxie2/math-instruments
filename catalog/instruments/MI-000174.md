# Continuation records permit signed opposing tests with degree-averaged loss

[Family 330](../families/openai-math-330.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000109.md) · [Batch B009](../../batches/B009/README.md)

## Mathematical content

The selected averaging mechanism has a useful conditional form. Let a finite Delta-regular marked multigraph have reversal-paired incidences, including two incidences per loop. At each vertex x let mu_x be a signed measure on one finite metric state set S, with total variation at most V, and let 0<=b_x<=L satisfy integral b_x dmu_x>=aL. Suppose all but a fraction beta of vertices are good and, at a good vertex, each state belongs to at most one exceptional incidence set E_e.

If for every e:x->y there is a 1-Lipschitz f_e valued in [-L,L], equal to b_x off E_e and to -b_y off E_reverse, then

$$\mathbb E_e\int f_e\,d(\mu_x-\mu_y)\ge 2aL-4LV(\Delta^{-1}+\beta).$$

Indeed, each prescription loses at most 2L times the variation on its exceptional set. Uniform incidence sampling and reversal give the same average loss at both endpoints. At a good x, disjointness per state bounds the average exceptional variation by V/Delta, regardless of |S|; bad x cost at most V.

The source obtains the exceptional sets from finite continuation records. At a good pair (x,j), no sufficiently long matched nonbacktracking walk exists. Record the maximum length through each outgoing incidence and declare only a strict largest record exceptional. If compatible nearby states j,j' match an edge, prepending it to a continuation in another direction gives r_x(e,j)>=1+A_y and r_y(reverse,j')>=1+A_x. If neither direction were exceptional these force A_x>=2+A_x. Thus one prescription must be removed. The source’s label bumps then ensure that close cross pairs remaining in both prescription sets have both values zero; distant pairs have distance at least 2L. McShane extension and clipping supply f_e.

For the operator application, finite-support perturbation gives mass-one signed measures through $\int g\,d\mu_x=(T\delta_x)(g-g(o))+g(o)$. This makes edge pairings insensitive to constants and bounds them by ||T|| times the source-edge length. The continuation and averaging lower bound can contradict that upper bound without assuming coefficient positivity.

## Role in the source argument

Turns local incompatibility of long matches into a lower bound against arbitrary signed finite-rank operators, with error independent of their finite support size.

## Antecedents and comparison

Scalar extension is classical. [Naor’s almost-extension work](https://arxiv.org/html/2009.11373v2) supplies related signed-representation context. The source explicitly compares its continuation records to [Ben-David and coauthors](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.12760); their graph algorithm is not claimed as new here. The adaptation tracks a support state as well as a direction.

## Checks and limitations

Read the zero-record case, strict-max rule, reversal convention and signed variation estimate. Finite continuation models check the incompatible-record inequalities; scalar signed examples check extension, clipping and the pairing loss. Incidence averages check the degree factor and a negative control for counting signed mass instead of variation.

The metric and label conditions producing the two prescriptions are substantive hypotheses. Regularity or an appropriate reversible weighting is needed for the endpoint average. In the source the graph sequence is fixed first, the operator support next, and the walk length and graph index last; reversing that order would invalidate the quantifiers.

## Possible applications

Lower bounds for signed interpolation or approximation operators when each state can be charged to only a few local directions.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
