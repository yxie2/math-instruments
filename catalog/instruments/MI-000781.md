# Retain angle-dependent pressure in a graph Jacobi equation

[Family 336](../families/openai-math-336.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000517.md) · [Batch B084](../../batches/B084/README.md)

## Mathematical content

For the graph u with omega=(1+|du|^2)^(-1/2), nu=(omega grad u,-omega), Z=-partial_z and X=-grad t, suppose H+<X,nu>=p(x,u,omega). The vertical angle obeys L omega=-p_z, where
L=Delta_Gamma+<X_top-p_omega Z_top,grad_Gamma>+|II|^2+Ric(nu,nu)+Hess t(nu,nu)+partial_nu p.
In the final term omega is held fixed while differentiating p in the ambient variables.

For normal speed V, the angle variation is -<Z_top,grad V>. The pressure variation therefore contributes the extra first-order term involving p_omega. Linearizing the residual gives -LV. Vertical translation along Z has normal speed omega; the residual changes by p_z, since the geometry and angle are translation invariant. Its tangential derivative vanishes on the solution, giving the equation.

Omitting -p_omega Z_top changes the operator whenever the prescribed pressure depends on angle.

## Role in the source argument

Keeping the correct drift and zeroth-order terms when designing graph equations with a pressure that reacts to the graph angle.

## Antecedents and comparison

This is the normal-variation/Jacobi method with the dependence of pressure on the angle made explicit. Exact priority of the source formulation is not established.

## Checks and limitations

A manufactured curved graph in a flat product with nonconstant drift and pressure depending on both height and angle checks the full equation. The same example rejects omission of the pressure-angle drift.

The manufactured solution checks a local identity only. It does not establish existence, global gradient bounds or the source width theorem.
