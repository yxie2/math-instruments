# Stabilize a selected branch by exposing one fresh noise per comparison

[Family 089](../families/openai-math-089.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000261.md) · [Batch B048](../../batches/B048/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Suppose a deterministic finite list of named functions is fixed before sampling noises. At a fixed point x, each pair difference can, after conditioning on all other noises, be written c+epsilon alpha with epsilon in {-1,1} and alpha uniform on an interval of length q. Then P(|A(x)-A'(x)|<=t)<=2t/q. For an equally spaced endpoint grid of spacing eta'<=eta the bound becomes (2t+eta)/q.

In an ancestor chain of atoms whose intercepts depend only on earlier noises, use the later atom's own noise. At one node distinct atoms have distinct own noises and intercepts independent of both. This allows a union bound over the predetermined candidate list without conditioning on which atom was ultimately selected.

If at most S named branches are L_*-Lipschitz along paths of length at most rho from x, all have the required pairwise fresh-noise comparison with widths at least q_*, and at most one branch has no noise, the probability of any pair being within 4 L_* rho at x is at most S^2(8 L_* rho+eta)/q_*. Off that event no names cross anywhere on the paths. A continuous min/max selection from the finite list has locally constant selected name, and connected paths sharing x force a single name throughout.

Geometry, portals, inputs and candidate names must be fixed by prior data. A comparison branch built from current noises cannot be called fixed. Repeated occurrences of the same input with the same sign and shift are one name; arbitrary distinct fixed branches have no anti-concentration guarantee.

## Role in the source argument

Makes interpolation formulas persist on a small ball or a connected family of paths in the planar construction.

## Antecedents and comparison

The interval bound and the continuous-selection argument are elementary. The source contribution to this interface is the deterministic bounded candidate list and the precise conditioning requirements; its construction was not certified here.

## Checks and limitations

Exact enumeration of grid noises tests dependent earlier intercepts and the conditional interval bound. A branch reusing the tested noise gives a probability-one tie, exposing invalid conditioning.

Finite-noise checks do not establish the source bounded-depth atom list, planar geometry or universal embedding constant.

## Possible applications

Controlling active branches in randomized min/max formulas with adaptive earlier intercepts but a genuinely fresh final noise.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
