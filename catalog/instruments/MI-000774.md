# Recover the invertible part from a nilpotent kernel and eventual surjectivity

[Family 318](../families/openai-math-318.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000514.md) · [Batch B084](../../batches/B084/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let f:M->X be a map of finite-dimensional vector spaces commuting with an endomorphism S. Suppose ker f is locally S-nilpotent and S^a X is contained in im f for some a. Then f induces an isomorphism between the stable images M_inv=intersection_b S^b M and X_inv=intersection_b S^b X.

The Fitting decomposition splits each space into its invertible and nilpotent S-parts. The kernel has zero intersection with M_inv. Every stable vector of X lies in the eventual image; projecting a preimage to M_inv gives a stable preimage, since f respects the Fitting decomposition. On duals, the direct limit along S is canonically the dual of the stable part.

The source applies this after finite-dimensional cohomology and eventual pole control have been established. Those analytic inputs are additional assumptions, not consequences of this lemma. Finite dimensionality is substantive: differentiation on the polynomials is locally nilpotent and surjective, so its infinite-dimensional stable image is the entire space.

## Role in the source argument

Replacing a comparison that is not initially an isomorphism by an isomorphism after its nilpotent part is removed.

## Antecedents and comparison

This is a classical Fitting-decomposition argument, adapted to a transfer comparison. No new linear algebra theorem is claimed.

## Checks and limitations

Exact conjugated Jordan-block examples check stable-image dimensions, commutation and the induced isomorphism. Negative controls retain an invertible kernel or an unhit invertible target summand.

The checks do not prove cohomological finiteness, continuous-dual exactness or pole estimates. Local nilpotence alone is insufficient in infinite dimension.
