# Build a positive quadratic gauge from repeated moment thresholds

[Family 230](../families/openai-math-230.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000497.md) · [Batch B079](../../batches/B079/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let L_j increase to a nonnegative L with m_n=E L^n in (0,infinity) for every n. Choose ell_n with E L_ell_n^n>3m_n/4 and put T_n=4m_(n+1)/m_n. At A_k=2^k<=T_n use multiplicity N_nk=floor(4^n A_k^n/m_n), and append a cleanup threshold n. Each batch is finite, and all its entries b_ni have a common positive lower bound min(1,m_1/4).

Geometric sums give sum_k N_nk P(L>=16A_k)<=4^n16^(-n)/(1-2^(-n)). For n>=3, truncating L_ell_n at T_n preserves at least m_n/2 of its nth moment; a dyadic level between Y/2 and Y and the floor bound yield sum_k N_nk P(Y>=A_k)>=2^(n-2)-1/[2(1-2^(-n))]>=2^n/8. Cleanup tails are summable because sum_n P(L>=16n)<=E L/16.

For 0<d<2, assign r_ni=exp(-n^2-2a(i-1)(ell_n+n)), a>0, and define h(r)=inf_ni b_ni r_ni^d max(1,(r/r_ni)^2). Only finitely many radii exceed any positive epsilon; for fixed r, smaller radii give terms at least c_0 r^2 r_ni^(d-2), tending to infinity. Hence h(r)>0. The envelope is nondecreasing, continuous, and h(Cr)<=C^2h(r); cleanup entries force h(r)->0 at zero.

If a bounded nonnegative F is bounded by C times every envelope term after finitely many batches, restore the excluded finite terms by increasing C using their positive minima. Interleaving radii do not affect this argument.

## Role in the source argument

Converting moment information into thresholds with many opportunities for success and summably rare large excesses.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Peres and Solomyak, arXiv:math/0406375v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0406375v1), Lemma 1.2 on PDF page 2 and its full proof in Section 5 on page 10, were read. They give the classical quadratic regularization. Positivity here is proved from the particular batch data; it is not inferred for an arbitrary positive input profile. Exact priority of the batch construction is not established.

## Checks and limitations

Finite positive mass laws check moment-based multiplicities, lower success and upper tail bounds. Envelope evaluations in logarithmic coordinates check monotonicity, doubling and finite-exclusion restoration with interleaving batches.

The tests do not construct SLE mass or prove an exact Hausdorff measure statement. The resulting envelope is not asserted to equal an explicit log-log formula.

## Possible applications

Converting moment information into thresholds with many opportunities for success and summably rare large excesses.
