# A compact semigroup anchor permits convex return after adaptive tail choices

[Family 328](../families/openai-math-328.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000107.md) · [Batch B009](../../batches/B009/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let K be a nonempty weakly compact convex subset of a real Banach space with diameter one, and let T:K->K be nonexpansive. Its resolvents satisfy $R_n(a)=a/n+(1-1/n)TR_n(a)$, are nonexpansive, and obey $\|TR_n(a)-R_n(a)\|\le1/n$ and $\|R_n(a)-a\|\le(n-1)\|Ta-a\|$.

Let G be generated by these resolvents and the identity under composition and pointwise finite convex combinations. Put $H_m=\{R_n\circ G:n\ge m,\ G\in\mathcal G\}$ and $A_m=\overline{\operatorname{conv}H_m}^{\rm pointwise\ weak}$. Each A_m is a compact semigroup. The key closure step does not assume weak continuity of a nonlinear map: on any finite tuple, a pointwise weak limit of a convex family can be approximated simultaneously in norm, by separation in a finite Banach product. Approximating h at k(a_i), and k at a_i, then uses nonexpansiveness to approximate h composed with k with error at most twice the tuple error.

The intersection A contains an idempotent P by Ellis's lemma, since right translations h->h composed with k are continuous. For x=P(a), define $D_x=\bigcap_m\overline{\{h(x):h\in H_m\}}^{\rm weak}$. Nested compactness and separation give $x\in\overline{\operatorname{conv}D_x}^{\|\cdot\|}$.

If the weak topology on K is metrizable, this yields an adaptive selection rule. For any tau>0, before step i prescribe an arbitrary finite threshold m_i using the full earlier history. One can select G_i in H_(m_i) until a finite prefix has a convex combination of G_i(x) within tau of x. To see this, choose finitely many targets in D_x whose convex combination is within tau/2 of x, cycle through them with weak error tending to zero, and stop when the selected convex hull meets the tau-ball. Nontermination would put every target in the norm-closed convex hull of the selected points, a contradiction. Coefficients are assigned after selection; no uniform stopping bound is supplied.

## Role in the source argument

Allows later accuracy requirements to depend on earlier maps and detectors while retaining a norm return to one anchor.

## Antecedents and comparison

The source credits Bruck’s [compact convex semigroup theorem](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9939-1975-0383164-3) and the classical [Ellis idempotent argument](https://msp.org/pjm/1958/8-3/pjm-v8-n3-p02-p.pdf). The selected adaptation uses finite simultaneous norm approximation to justify composition and cycles through tail-limit targets. It is not an asserted convergence-rate theorem for Halpern iteration.

## Checks and limitations

Read both approximation arguments and the order of adaptive choices. Finite nonexpansive resolvents and a weak-tail model with orthogonal errors check the displacement and convex-return calculations. These examples do not verify compactness or arbitrary weak closure computationally.

Convexity of the map family is essential to the simultaneous norm approximation. The chosen anchor need not be a fixed point of T; convex combinations of approximate fixed points need not themselves have small defect for nonlinear T. Sequential selection uses weak metrizability.

## Possible applications

Adaptive constructions requiring increasingly accurate approximate solutions while keeping a finite convex combination close to a designated point.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
