# Make the quadratic stationary-phase error uniform in the frequency

[Family 076](../families/openai-math-076.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000445.md) · [Batch B071](../../batches/B071/README.md)

## Mathematical content

With e(t)=exp(2 pi i t), g smooth compactly supported and beta nonzero real, completing the square gives
integral g(y)e(T beta y^2/2-u y)dy = e(sign(beta)/8-u^2/(2T beta))/sqrt(T|beta|) times [g(u/(T beta))+O(T^-1)], uniformly in all real u.

The bracket is exactly integral ghat(xi)e(xi u/(T beta))e(-xi^2/(2T beta))d xi. Insert a positive Gaussian damping factor before exchanging integrals; its explicit Fourier transform is bounded by (T|beta|)^(-1/2), permitting dominated convergence. Removing the last exponential costs at most pi/(T|beta|) times integral xi^2|ghat(xi)|, independent of u.

For a smooth phase with second derivative nowhere zero on a compact interval, a uniform Morse coordinate reduces each interior stationary point to this quadratic formula. Compact support away from endpoints supplies uniform amplitude bounds. Outside the derivative range, integration by parts gives the nonstationary bound. The uniformity covers frequencies whose stationary point reaches the interval endpoints.

## Role in the source argument

Controls a whole Fourier coefficient family without choosing frequency-dependent error constants.

## Antecedents and comparison

This is classical quadratic stationary phase derived directly from Fourier inversion and Gaussian regularization. The source uses explicit uniformity in its wave construction; no new stationary-phase principle is claimed.

## Checks and limitations

Complex Gaussian integrals provide exact closed-form checks of phase and sign for both beta signs and moving frequencies, including far outside the amplitude core. These Schwartz-amplitude checks test the identity and error mechanism, not compact support.

The checks do not construct the packed waves or prove real ultraflatness.

## Possible applications

Separating a uniform Fourier error estimate from later combinatorial wave placement.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
