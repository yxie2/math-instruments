# Detect a nef class by its mass against a nef and big class

[Family 036](../families/openai-math-036.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000415.md) · [Batch B068](../../batches/B068/README.md)

## Mathematical content

On a compact Kahler n-fold, let gamma be nef and C nef and big. If gamma.C^(n-1)=0, then gamma=0 in cohomology. Choose a Kahler form omega and epsilon>0 with C-epsilon omega pseudoeffective. Factoring C^(n-1)-(epsilon omega)^(n-1) into C-epsilon omega times a sum of nef products gives gamma.C^(n-1)>=epsilon^(n-1) gamma.omega^(n-1)>=0. A positive current representing gamma has zero omega mass and is therefore zero. The curve case is immediate.

The source applies this when f:V->A is generically finite onto a torus of algebraic dimension zero and gamma is a rational nef line class. Translation averaging of f_*gamma gives a constant semipositive form. A nonzero rational such form would polarize a positive-dimensional quotient torus, contradicting algebraic dimension zero. Thus f_*gamma=0; projection against a Kahler class on A gives the mixed mass with C=f*eta, which is nef and big, and hence gamma=0.

## Role in the source argument

Turns the absence of algebraic quotients of the torus into vanishing of rational nef line classes upstairs.

## Antecedents and comparison

The positivity, averaging and polarized quotient arguments are classical. The source-specific interface is the transfer through a generically finite map. No novelty of the zero-mass criterion is claimed.

## Checks and limitations

Positive semidefinite Hermitian matrix models check the mixed determinant lower bound and zero detection; a singular test class supplies a failure when bigness is removed.

Constant-form models do not prove the positive-current or torus quotient facts, which are dependencies of the inspected argument.

## Possible applications

Detecting cohomological triviality from a single mixed intersection.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
