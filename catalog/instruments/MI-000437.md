# Use digit-binomial bases to adjust one filtered matrix depth

[Family 319](../families/openai-math-319.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000237.md) · [Batch B041](../../batches/B041/README.md)

## Mathematical content

In the source operation setup, R=k[[x]] has characteristic p, q=p+1, h_g=1+u_g, and a coefficient at depth b is f_b(z)=[x^(qb)] integral nu(g) h_g^z. Assume the balanced moment budget r_m<=kappa m with kappa<=p/(p-1), and averaging by a group Lambda of order q preserves the relevant invariant coefficient.

Choose odd M>=81 with p>16M^2 and 2 floor(M/8)>p^(1/3). For X=p^r<=qb<pX and b>=X/(2M), the measures x^(qb-r_m)D_m, 0<=m<(M+1)X, lie in x^(X+1)Delta because

$$qb-r_m\ge\left(\frac q{2M}-\kappa(M+1)\right)X>X.$$

They leave all lower depths unchanged and alter f_b by binomial(z,m) modulo p. Lucas factorization writes these as products of digit binomials: lower digits span every function of z modulo X, while the rth digit spans all polynomials of degree at most M. Hence that degree-M polynomial can be chosen independently at each lower label. Averaging under Lambda preserves the coefficient because q is invertible and x^(qb) is invariant.

Each stage has finitely many depths and labels; stage-X corrections vanish modulo x^(X+1). They converge in the complete operation algebra, preserve reduction modulo x, and stabilize every previously processed matrix block.

## Role in the source argument

Allows construction of a lower-triangular operator with prescribed successive matrix constraints and one fixed reduction.

## Antecedents and comparison

Lagrange/Amice moment cancellation combined with classical Lucas digit factorization. The source operation-algebra realization is a separate input, not established by the binomial identity.

## Checks and limitations

Six exact finite-field Lucas basis/rank models check independent lower-label control. One explicit large prime/M choice checks the numerical valuation budget.

Small-prime basis tests are algebraic models only and do not satisfy all source large-prime hypotheses. No chromatic operator or counterexample is realized.

## Possible applications

Successive local coefficient corrections that converge without changing earlier layers.
