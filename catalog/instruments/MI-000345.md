# Use a tree energy budget to bound the frequency of unfavorable ancestors

[Family 246](../families/openai-math-246.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000191.md) · [Batch B030](../../batches/B030/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let P_i be the masses of a probability flow at level n of a rooted tree. Let m_i count bad ancestors among levels 0 through n-1. Suppose a>1, D>0, b>0, tau>0, and positive sizes Delta_i satisfy

$$\sum_{|i|=n}\Delta_i^2\le E_0,\qquad \Delta_i^2\ge\Delta_0^2P_i a^{D((b+\tau)m_i-\tau n)}.$$

Jensen inequality for the exponential yields

$$\frac1n\sum_{|i|=n}P_i m_i\le\frac{\tau}{b+\tau}+\frac{\log_a(E_0/\Delta_0^2)}{D(b+\tau)n}.$$

Conservation of probability identifies the sum on the left with the average bad mass of the first n levels. Thus the bad mass cannot stay above any theta>tau/(b+tau) at all sufficiently large levels. For the source choice b=10 tau, infinitely many levels have good mass at least one half. This does not say that every late level is good; rare unfavorable levels are compatible with an average bound.

## Role in the source argument

Finds infinitely many levels on which the geometric good-node comparison can be applied to substantial probability mass.

## Antecedents and comparison

This is a standard exponential-moment/Jensen estimate. The extracted contribution is the precise tree conservation bookkeeping and the distinction between average frequency and pointwise levels.

## Checks and limitations

Three hundred sixty finite binary-tree levels verify the ancestor-count identity and the Jensen bound.

The transition probabilities, geometric energy bounds and infinite tree are hypotheses, not outputs of these tests.

## Possible applications

Stopping-time arguments and multiscale constructions with a bounded energy budget.
