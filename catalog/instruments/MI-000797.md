# Convert weighted moments into derivative bounds on a comparable-radius ball

[Family 365](../families/openai-math-365.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000525.md) · [Batch B086](../../batches/B086/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Assume r>0, r^(1/p_*) has a uniform Lipschitz bound, and smooth D,R have uniform C^m bounds together with ||chi D/r||_2+||chi R/r^2||_2<=C, where chi=1 near the compact set under study. Put d_*=10+n/2, eta=1/(2d_*), choose p_*>2d_* and m>=ceil(10+3d_*).

At a point with small r(y), choose s=r(y)^eta. Since eta>1/p_*, the radius r is comparable to r(y) on B(y,s). Thus the unweighted L2 norms on that ball are bounded by C r(y) and C r(y)^2. Taylor expansion and finite-dimensional polynomial norm equivalence give
|partial^alpha U(y)|<=C s^(-|alpha|-n/2)||U||_(L2(B(y,s)))+C_m s^(m-|alpha|).
For |alpha|<=10, substitution yields derivatives of D bounded by C r(y)^(1/2), and derivatives of R by C r(y)^(3/2). The Taylor remainder has at least the latter power. No derivative of r itself is taken.

## Role in the source argument

Extracting pointwise derivative gains from weighted integral errors while a geometric length scale tends to zero.

## Antecedents and comparison

This is classical local interpolation applied on a radius chosen through a Lipschitz fractional power. The exponents encode the source order-ten comparison, not an optimal interpolation theorem.

## Checks and limitations

Exact exponent inequalities and finite polynomial Gram matrices verify the scaling and derivative-evaluation bound; manufactured fractional-radius profiles check comparability at the chosen scale.

Weighted moments and uniform C^m bounds are essential inputs. The lemma does not establish the source transfer identity or the final inverse uniqueness conclusion.

## Possible applications

Extracting pointwise derivative gains from weighted integral errors while a geometric length scale tends to zero.
