# Repair a sampled cover using the quadratic number of pair failures

[Family 175](../families/openai-math-175.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000351.md) · [Batch B060](../../batches/B060/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let H consist of r-subsets of X, 1<=r<=h, h>=64, and |H|rho^r<=2^(h+1), where 0<rho<1. Sets containing at least 2^(12h) members of H have a containment cover G with sum_(I in G) rho^|I|<=3 times 2^(-h).

Set m=2^(12h), T=2^(4h) and pi=2^(-8h). Include every I of size at most r whose weighted degree d(I)=#{e in H:I subset e}rho^(r-|I|) exceeds T. Their total cost is at most 2^r|H|rho^r/T. Call edges avoiding these generators regular. Grouping regular edges by intersection with B gives sum_e rho^|e minus B| <= T sum_(i=0)^r binom(|B|,i).

Sample every ordered pair of regular edges independently with probability pi, including diagonal pairs, and add its union. Expected cost is at most pi |H|rho^r 2^r T. For every ordered m-tuple of distinct regular edges with no sampled pair among them, add its full union. This repairs every remaining target set for every outcome. Its m squared ordered pair indices are distinct, so survival has probability (1-pi)^(m squared). Iterating the extension estimate bounds the sum of tuple-union costs by [T(1+rm)^r]^m. Thus expected repair cost is at most that quantity times 2^(-pi m squared), at most 2^(-m). The inequality 4h+13rh+1<=18h^2<=2^(4h) supplies the final exponent. Total expected cost is at most 2^(2-2h)+2^(-m)<=3 times 2^(-h). Coincident unions reduce cost but do not merge the independent sampling indices.

## Role in the source argument

Produces a small integral cover for a level set with many witnesses, for use in the discrete-convexity argument.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Fischer and Person, Further remarks on fractional vs. expectation thresholds](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.21782v1), Section 1.1 Definition 3 and Section 2 Lemma 6, already construct covers from unions of sampled edges. Pages 2-3 were read. Their construction is an antecedent, not a proof of this exact regularize-pair-repair bound; earliest priority for the latter was not established.

## Checks and limitations

Small analogues enumerate all ordered-pair sampling outcomes and verify deterministic coverage, exact expected-cost bounds and the extension inequality. Integer exponent estimates are checked for h=64 through 512.

The enormous h>=64 cover is not enumerated; its bound rests on the inspected proof. Tests do not certify the later discrete-convexity theorem.

## Possible applications

Constructing cheap covers where an uncovered large tuple must avoid quadratically many independent local choices.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
