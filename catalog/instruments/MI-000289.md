# Charge overlapping descendant seeds through disjoint generation intervals

[Family 081](../families/openai-math-081.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000163.md) · [Batch B023](../../batches/B023/README.md)

## Mathematical content

In a nested measurable partition under a root R, let F be a finite family of cells. Choose a descendant S(Q) of each Q in F with depth at most N below Q and mu(Q)<=M mu(S(Q)). Let O be a family with sum_(T in O)mu(T)<=K mu(R). Assume that whenever S(Q) strictly precedes Q' in generation and Q' is contained in S(Q), some T in O contains Q' and has generation between that of S(Q), inclusive, and Q', exclusive. Then

$$\sum_{Q\in\mathcal F}\mu(Q)\le M(N+1)(1+K)\mu(R).$$

Choose a charge T for each eligible ordered pair before fixing a point; only finitely many selected charges are needed. At a point away from partition boundaries, list all parents whose seeds contain it, ordered by generation. Start a group at the first parent Q, and include all listed parents through generation k(S(Q)). This group has at most N+1 members. If a next parent Q' remains, nesting makes it eligible for a charge containing the point.

The generation intervals from which successive charges are drawn are disjoint. The number of groups is therefore at most one plus the number of selected charge cells containing the point. Hence the seed multiplicity is bounded by (N+1)(1_R+sum_T 1_T). Integrate and use the parent-to-seed mass bound. No disjointness of the chosen seeds, nor a bound on gaps between selected generations, is required.

## Role in the source argument

Turns the source implication from a later flatness failure to an intervening oscillatory ancestor into a packing bound.

## Antecedents and comparison

This is a finite stopping-time charging argument of the type used in Carleson packing. The source makes the overlap and generation-gap accounting explicit. A targeted comparison did not establish historical novelty for this exact formulation.

## Checks and limitations

Read the complete proof. On 150 nonuniformly weighted binary trees, all eligible-pair charges are constructed and the pointwise inequality is checked at 9,600 leaves, followed by the integrated bound.

The geometric existence of seeds, the mass ratio M and the Carleson estimate for O are inputs. Without the intervening-charge hypothesis, a long nested chain of self-seeds violates the proposed bound.

## Possible applications

Converting a chainwise obstruction into an aggregate mass estimate without forcing a disjoint selection.
