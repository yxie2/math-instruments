# A strict kernel-density deficit gives an explicit finite-host threshold

[Family 161](../families/openai-math-161.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000152.md) · [Batch B020](../../batches/B020/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let H be a finite simple graph with v vertices and m>=1 edges. Let U be a symmetric [0,1]-valued kernel on a finite probability space, with mu=E U>0 and Delta=mu^m-t(H,U)>0. Then some finite simple graph G on n vertices violates t(H,G)>=p(G)^m whenever

$$n>\frac{\binom v2+m\mu^m}{\Delta},\qquad p(G)=2|E(G)|/n^2.$$

Sample n independent labels from the kernel space and, conditional on these labels, independent Bernoulli edges for unordered distinct vertex pairs. Set all loops to zero. Then E p(G)=(1-1/n)mu. For injective maps of V(H), the expected edge product is exactly t(H,U). Noninjective maps occupy proportion at most binom(v,2)/n, so |E t(H,G)-t(H,U)| is bounded by that quantity.

Jensen and (1-1/n)^m>=1-m/n yield E p(G)^m>=mu^m-m mu^m/n. The displayed threshold makes this strictly larger than E t(H,G), so a realization has the required strict inequality. Such a realization has an edge automatically. For the source H, binom(35,2)=595 and m=66.

The collision estimate is essential: when two source edges collapse to the same unordered host pair, their indicators coincide, rather than representing independent Bernoulli trials.

## Role in the source argument

Finishes the transfer from a weighted negative-correlation witness to an ordinary finite simple graph.

## Antecedents and comparison

This is an explicit finite form of the kernel-sampling method in [Lovasz-Szegedy, Limits of dense graph sequences](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0408173). The novelty of a supplied violating kernel is a separate question; none is established by the transfer itself.

## Checks and limitations

Read the proof. Exact rational enumeration of labels and edge outcomes for small kernels checks expected densities, collision bounds and Jensen, including maps that reuse an edge.

The verification uses small patterns, including a triangle as a strict-deficit control. It does not construct or verify the source bipartite H deficit. The size estimate is a sufficient threshold and is not optimized.

## Possible applications

Converting any strict finite-kernel inequality into a quantitatively bounded finite witness.
