# Lift inverses and elementwise finite orders through a matrix-nil ideal

[Family 247](../families/openai-math-247.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000344.md) · [Batch B059](../../batches/B059/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Suppose R=F2 direct-sum I as a vector space, with the F2 copy containing the identity, I a two-sided ideal, and M_d(I) nil for every d: each individual matrix over I is nilpotent. A square matrix over R is invertible exactly when its reduction over F2 is invertible.

For the nontrivial direction, lift the inverse of the reduction as a scalar matrix B. Then AB=I+U with U in M_n(I). If U^m=0, the finite series I+U+...+U^(m-1) is the inverse of I+U in characteristic two. This yields an inverse of A without a common nilpotence index.

For A invertible, let d be the finite order of its reduction in GL_n(F2). Then A^d=I+V for a nilpotent V. If V^m=0 and 2^e>=m, repeated squaring gives A^(d*2^e)=I+V^(2^e)=I. Thus each invertible matrix has finite order. The number d can have odd factors, so this argument does not make GL_n(R) a 2-group. Nor does it impose one exponent on all elements.

The source needs a further torsion theorem for the kernel of the Steinberg-to-elementary map before it can lift periodicity to its presented group. This matrix lemma supplies neither that theorem nor the existence of the required infinite algebra.

## Role in the source argument

Extracts matrix-group periodicity from a matrix-nil algebra while keeping the separate central-kernel problem visible.

## Antecedents and comparison

Finite geometric inversion and (I+V)^(2^e)=I+V^(2^e) are classical. The matrix-nil hypothesis is the substantive algebra input, and the selected reduction introduces no claimed new general ring principle.

## Checks and limitations

80 matrix models over the noncommutative ring F2*I3 plus strictly upper triangular 3 by 3 matrices verify the invertibility equivalence. All 27 invertible samples satisfy the explicit geometric inverse and finite-order lift. An order-three matrix over F2 is a control against a false 2-group inference.

The test ring has a uniform nilpotence bound; the analytic proof only needs elementwise nilpotence. No infinite finitely presented algebra or Steinberg-kernel computation is tested.

## Possible applications

Transferring invertibility and finite-order information from a finite quotient through matrix-nil perturbations.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
