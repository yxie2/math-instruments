# Bound a local difficulty deficit by the number of varying branches

[Family 056](../families/openai-math-056.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000432.md) · [Batch B070](../../batches/B070/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Write w(u)=ceil(N max(u,0)), w_v=w(2-a(v;Y,Theta)), and d_h=sum_(j>=1) w(1-j(1-b_h)) for effective boundary coefficients 0<=b_h<1. At an irreducible surface V of a terminal fourfold model, let r_(V,h) count normalization branches of S_h over the generic point of V. Suppose N b_h is integral for the fixed labels, and let r_var be the number of branches of the remaining labels. Then the local correction satisfies

sum_(center(v)=V) w_v - sum_h r_(V,h) d_h >= -max(r_var-1,0).

The local ambient surface is regular. If m_h is the multiplicity of S_h there, the first blowup has log discrepancy 2-sum m_h b_h>1. Normalization length gives r_(V,h)<=m_h, with residue degrees included in the multiplicity sum. The first valuation pays the first default terms using ceil(sum x_i)>=sum ceil(x_i)-(r-1); integral fixed terms cause no rounding loss.

A higher default requires b_h>1/2. The inequality sum m_h b_h<1 permits at most one such label and forces its multiplicity to be one. Taking its local equation x as a parameter, the distinct monomial valuations v_j(x)=j,v_j(t)=1 satisfy a(v_j)<=1+j(1-b_h). They pay all higher defaults. The estimate does not assume simple normal crossings at V.

## Role in the source argument

Isolates the exact local rounding debt that a separate global branch estimate must control.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Alexeev, Hacon and Kawamata, Termination of (many) 4-dimensional log flips](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0605137), section 2, defines weighted difficulty with echo subtraction and normalization branches; PDF pages 4-5 were read. The selected source uses a ceiling weight and an explicit varying-branch deficit. Priority for this exact bound is not established.

## Checks and limitations

Exact rational coefficient and multiplicity searches check first-valuation payment, the unique large-coefficient case, all higher defaults and sharp examples of the ceiling loss.

The arithmetic checks do not prove that the full difficulty is globally bounded or that every flip lowers it.

## Possible applications

Using integer rounding without assuming denominators of all varying coefficients are uniformly bounded.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
