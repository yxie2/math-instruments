# Retain integral torsion in a Morse lower bound after stabilization

[Family 347](../families/openai-math-347.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000519.md) · [Batch B085](../../batches/B085/README.md)

## Mathematical content

For a finite free integral chain complex, let rho_i be the rank of H_i and tau_i the minimum number of generators of its torsion subgroup. Then rank C_i>=rho_i+tau_i+tau_(i-1), hence total chain rank>=sum rho_i+2 sum tau_i. Indeed rank C_i=rho_i+rank B_i+rank B_(i-1), and Smith normal form gives rank B_i>=tau_i.

For a Morse function F on W times R^k equal outside a compact set to Q=-|u|^2+|v|^2, choose endpoint levels +/-A outside the core range and a truncation ball of radius T with T^2>A. The side boundary has Q-critical values only +/-T^2, so it introduces no cells in the level band. The relative Morse complex has one generator per critical point. Shrinking v to zero retracts the endpoint pair to W times a disk-annulus pair in the d negative directions. Its homology is H_(i-d)(W), including d=0 and both definite signatures.

The chain bound is unchanged by this degree shift and gives the stated stable-Morse lower bound under the exact-quadratic convention.

## Role in the source argument

Preserving information that can be lost by using one field of coefficients throughout a stabilized counting problem.

## Antecedents and comparison

These are classical Smith normal form and relative Morse-theory arguments. The source contribution here is explicit control of the truncation boundary and convention at infinity; no new general Morse inequality is claimed.

## Checks and limitations

Exact Smith normal forms of two-term complexes check primary torsion and degree bounds. Mixed-prime examples show that the generator count is a maximum over primes, not their sum.

The checks do not establish geometric handle cancellation or any counterexample. A weaker condition at infinity needs an additional reduction argument.
