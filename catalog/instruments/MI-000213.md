# Separated integer frequencies reproduce a finite collection of phase averages

[Family 144](../families/openai-math-144.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000127.md) · [Batch B014](../../batches/B014/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let P_j(z,w)=sum_nu c_(j,nu)(z) w^nu be finitely many Laurent polynomials in n unit-modulus variables, with finitely many coefficients in L1 of the circle. For any eta>0 there are positive integers L1,...,Ln such that every integral of P_j(z,e(L1 z),...,e(Ln z)) differs by less than eta from its integral over independent Haar phases and z. Here e(x)=exp(2 pi i x).

Let D bound all absolute exponents. By Riemann-Lebesgue decay choose R so that the sum of the relevant nonconstant coefficient Fourier tails beyond R is below eta. Choose successively

$$L_j>R+D\sum_{i<j}L_i.$$

If nu is nonzero and j is its last nonzero coordinate, |sum_i nu_i L_i|>R. Each nonconstant monomial therefore contributes only a small Fourier coefficient; the constant term agrees exactly. Any additional finite lower bounds depending on earlier choices may be imposed at the same time.

This is approximation for finitely many fixed tests, not independence of the substituted phases as random variables. For coefficients with finite Fourier support in [-R,R], the same choice gives exact agreement. A test chosen afterward, w1 e(-L1 z), has diagonal integral one and product integral zero.

## Role in the source argument

Replaces auxiliary independent phases by one circle coordinate while retaining a finite list of spectral and moment tests.

## Antecedents and comparison

This is a direct application of the classical Riemann-Lebesgue lemma and a last-nonzero-coordinate argument. No separate novelty claim is made for the lemma.

## Checks and limitations

Read the frequency selection and error summation. Exact integer-frequency tests check vanishing of every nonconstant integral and the adaptive-test counterexample.

The frequencies depend on the tests and tolerance. Arbitrary measurable tests or tests chosen after the frequencies are fixed are not controlled.

## Possible applications

Deterministic realization of finitely many phase moments and diagonal approximation of product integrals.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
