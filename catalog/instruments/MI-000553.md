# Construct a horizontal-strip chain from dominance

[Family 205](../families/openai-math-205.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000322.md) · [Batch B056](../../batches/B056/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let theta be a partition and let tau dominate theta. Put t equal to the last part of theta. Remove the bottom box from the t tallest columns of tau, choosing the rightmost columns among equal heights. This tie rule keeps the remaining column heights nonincreasing, so they define a partition nu. The removed boxes form a horizontal strip of size t.

For the first k rows, the number of removed boxes is max(0,t-tau_(k+1)). If tau_(k+1)>=t, the original dominance inequality suffices for nu. If tau_(k+1)<t, use dominance through row k+1 and theta_(k+1)>=t to obtain the required prefix inequality after subtracting t. Also tau has at most length(theta) rows, and its last row has size at most t, so that row disappears. Thus nu dominates theta with its last part removed. Iteration constructs a horizontal-strip chain with successive sizes theta_1,...,theta_l.

Conversely, after k horizontal strips a chain has at most k rows, so its final shape must dominate the strip-size partition. If the final shape is theta itself, equality of every prefix forces the unique chain of successive first-row blocks. By the classical Pieri rule, these chains compute the multiplicities in the Young permutation module M^theta: support is exactly the dominance upper set and the diagonal multiplicity is one.

Choosing the leftmost of tied columns can fail: removing a bottom box from the first column of column heights (2,2,2) leaves (1,2,2), which is not a partition in that order.

## Role in the source argument

Supplies a classical triangularity and support tool used before the source proposed cyclic-vector construction.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Law lectures, notes by Tomczak](https://math.berkeley.edu/~ltomczak/notes/Mich2022/RepSn_Notes.pdf), Corollaries 3.14-3.15 on page 42 and Lemma 3.17 with Corollary 3.18 on page 44, give diagonal multiplicity one, dominance triangularity and the horizontal-strip rule. Those statements and their short proofs were read. The source packages the support argument as an explicit deletion algorithm; the resulting Young-rule statement is classical.

## Checks and limitations

All 30970 dominance pairs through total size 15 are converted into strip chains. Every intermediate partition, prefix-loss identity and dominance inequality is checked, including the tie-rule failure control.

The finite checks do not verify the later proposed polytabloid vector or any staircase tensor-square multiplicity in arbitrary degree.

## Possible applications

Producing a concrete tableau or strip-chain witness instead of relying only on an abstract dominance support statement.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
