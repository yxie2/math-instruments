# Separate formal coefficients by deep Frobenius roots

[Family 311](../families/openai-math-311.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000219.md) · [Batch B037](../../batches/B037/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let C be a complete algebraically closed valued field of characteristic p with coefficient field k=algebraic-closure(F_p), whose nonzero elements have norm one. Fix q=p^h, h>=1, a tuple s in the open unit polydisc, and arbitrary coefficients b_l in C. Suppose for every m,

$$\limsup_{N\to\infty}\left|\sum_{|l|\le m}b_l^{1/q^N}s^l\right|\le\|s\|^{m+1}.$$

Then every k-linear functional lambda:C->k satisfies sum_l lambda(b_l)s^l=0. No continuity or boundedness of lambda is required.

For fixed m choose a k-basis beta_j of the finite coefficient span. A Frobenius subsequence fixes its finitely many coordinate coefficients. Write the truncated expression as sum_j beta_j^{1/q^N}v_j. Divide v_j by one of maximum norm V, and pass to a further subsequence fixing their residues rho_j. Linear independence makes sum beta_j rho_j nonzero, whose deep-root norm tends to one; the residue error has norm bounded strictly below one. Thus the expression has norm tending to V. It follows that V<=||s||^{m+1}, and applying lambda to the finite coefficient expansion gives the same bound. The resulting k-coefficient series converges and vanishes as m tends to infinity.

If the hypothesis holds on a whole set, one fixed lambda detecting a nonzero coefficient yields a common nonzero k-valued formal equation, despite point-dependent subsequences.

## Role in the source argument

Descends a formal equation from a large valued coefficient field to its residue coefficient field.

## Checks and limitations

One hundred twenty finite Puiseux-monomial models at four depths check the norm-separation limit step.

Finite models do not implement arbitrary discontinuous functionals or certify formal orbit-density consequences.

## Possible applications

Extracting residue-field equations from asymptotic Frobenius constraints without interchanging a discontinuous functional and an infinite sum.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
