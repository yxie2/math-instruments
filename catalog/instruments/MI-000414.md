# Compare positive matrix decrements with inverse increments without commutativity

[Family 357](../families/openai-math-357.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000226.md) · [Batch B038](../../batches/B038/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let Q>0 and 0<=Delta<=epsilon Q, with epsilon<1. Put H=Q^{-1}, Q'=Q-Delta and E=Q^{-1/2}Delta Q^{-1/2}. The exact identity

$$(Q')^{-1}-Q^{-1}=Q^{-1/2}[(I-E)^{-1}-I]Q^{-1/2}$$

and scalar functional calculus give

$$H\Delta H\ \le\ (Q')^{-1}-H\ \le\ (1-\epsilon)^{-1}H\Delta H.$$

For a decreasing positive sequence Q_i with this relative decrement bound, summing compares sum_i H_i Delta_i H_i with H_k-H_0. No matrices from different steps need commute. The source further uses slow-growth and heat-decrement estimates to compare other mass matrices to this telescoping budget; those geometric inputs are separate from the extracted algebraic inequality.

## Role in the source argument

Provides a stable matrix budget when normalization changes across scales.

## Antecedents and comparison

Classical resolvent identity and positive functional calculus. The reusable point is that conjugation handles noncommutativity directly.

## Checks and limitations

Six hundred noncommuting positive-matrix steps and thirty telescoped sums satisfy both Loewner bounds.

No source heat-kernel estimate or mass-matrix comparison is independently certified by these tests.

Adaptive matrix normalization, cumulative inverse sensitivity and scale-dependent quadratic-energy estimates.
