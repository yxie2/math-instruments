# A cancellation report saves degree while retaining more than the revealed-coordinate variance

[Family 192](../families/openai-math-192.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000118.md) · [Batch B011](../../batches/B011/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Take independent centered variance-one inputs (u,z_1,...,z_m), m>=3, intervals I_j and a finite-cut-point selector iota(u). Let B mean all leaves lie in their intervals, P mean all leaves except the selected one pass, and A=P minus B. Report only tag A on A; report all leaf values on B; on P-complement report u, its selected index and the unselected leaf values.

Although A depends on all m+1 inputs, its indicator satisfies

$$1_A=\sum_i1_{\{\iota(u)=i\}}\prod_{j\ne i}1_{I_j}(z_j)-\prod_j1_{I_j}(z_j).$$

Every summand uses at most m coordinates. On any finite product alphabet every output indicator has the same coordinate-decomposition property: a P-complement report already contains a failing unselected leaf, so it imposes no hidden restriction on the omitted coordinate.

Write L=u+sum z_j. Predict it by a fixed b on A and by the sum of reported values otherwise. The omitted coordinate is independent of each ordinary report and has variance one, so the predictor error is 1-Delta_b, where Delta_b=E[1_A(1-(L-b)^2)]. Thus Var(E[L|report])>=m+Delta_b.

The source gives a strict Gaussian gain. For large finite odd m set R=sqrt(8 log m), h=m^(-3/2), interval centers a_i=1-R+2R(i-1)/(m-1), and I_i=[a_i-h,a_i+h]. Select the nearest center to 1+u for |u|<=R, and the middle interval outside. If beta_i,mu_i,s_i^2 are each interval's Gaussian mass, conditional mean and variance, put p*=product beta_i, S=sum s_i^2 and b=sum mu_i-1. Exact subtraction of the B contribution gives

$$\Delta_b=p_*(1+S-J),\quad J=\int\frac{\phi(t)}{\beta_{\iota(t)}}\left[(1+t-\mu_{\iota(t)})^2+S-s_{\iota(t)}^2\right]dt.$$

Here S<=m^(-2), the central J is O(R(R^2+1)exp(2R)m^(-1/2)) and the tail is O((R+1)m^(-5/2)); both tend to zero. One sufficiently large finite m gives Delta_b>3p*/4>0. The gain may be extremely small; the construction is not an efficient numerical counterexample.

## Role in the source argument

Supplies an observation retaining more than m units of variance at a cell-indicator degree cost of only m input observations.

## Antecedents and comparison

Conditional coordinate sums already appear in [Blais-Tan-Wan](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1506.01055v1). Their parity decision-tree setting differs from these cancellation-defined cells. The probability projection identity is standard; the selected candidate is the specific report and positive Gaussian deficit.

## Checks and limitations

Checked the cancellation and conditional-error proof and the Gaussian central/tail estimates. Exact finite product laws check output cells, predictor errors and retained variance. Numerical normalized Gaussian integrals check the subtraction identity at finite parameter choices without claiming a certified positive-gain example at the eventual huge scale.

The ordinary omitted variable must stay independent of the report. A low-degree indicator can depend on all coordinates, so this is not a depth-m decision tree. The asymptotic positivity argument, not a small finite quadrature, supplies the Gaussian gain.

## Possible applications

Designing information-bearing partitions with low-degree cells and separating algebraic cost from the number of variables a report depends on.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
