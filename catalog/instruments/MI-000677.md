# Round near-sign Fourier coefficients with a total-defect budget

[Family 076](../families/openai-math-076.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000446.md) · [Batch B071](../../batches/B071/README.md)

## Mathematical content

For X in [-1,1]^N put mu=(1/2)sum(1-|X_k|). There exist real signs epsilon with sup_t |sum(epsilon_k-X_k)e(kt)| <= C[1+sqrt(mu log(80N/mu))]. At mu=0 the error is exactly zero.

Write X_k=sigma_k(1-2p_k), 0<=p_k<=1/2, sum p_k=mu. Form real and imaginary Fourier rows on a 20N-point grid. Round p down to a fine dyadic grid with total error at most one. At scale 2^-j, apply a real-matrix discrepancy bound to the s_j coordinates with odd numerator. Reverse all discrepancy signs if necessary to make their sum nonpositive. This preserves the discrepancy norm, keeps entries in [0,1], and prevents total mass growth. Thus s_j<=min(N,2^j mu).

The real-matrix bound C0 sqrt(s log(2R/s)) follows by iterating Lovett-Meka partial coloring and summing the geometrically decreasing errors. Summing the dyadic stage errors gives 1+C0 sqrt(mu log(80N/mu))/(sqrt(2)-1). The final p entries are 0 or 1 and define signs. Cauchy's derivative estimate for the error polynomial gives S<=G+(pi e/20)S, hence a finite-grid bound controls the whole circle. This last step is applied to the error polynomial, not to the original relaxed polynomial.

## Role in the source argument

Turns a small total coefficient defect into a uniform rounding error, suitable for preserving both upper and lower modulus estimates.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Lovett and Meka, arXiv:1203.5747v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1203.5747v2), Theorem 4 on page 3, supplies the real-vector partial-coloring statement; the statement and surrounding reduction were read. The source derives defect-sensitive rounding from that established input. Exact priority of the defect formulation is not established.

## Checks and limitations

Finite exact dyadic states use exhaustive discrepancy sign selection, checking mass monotonicity, support budgets, final signs, zero defect and the Fourier error identity. Dense-circle evaluations check the stated grid-to-circle factor on the resulting errors.

Small exhaustive sign searches do not implement the Lovett-Meka algorithm or prove an effective construction of the source ultraflat polynomials.

## Possible applications

Rounding relaxed trigonometric designs when only a small amount of coefficient mass remains undecided.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
