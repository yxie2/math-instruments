# Make subchain coordinate lists local before using a game soundness bound

[Family 106](../families/openai-math-106.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000232.md) · [Batch B040](../../batches/B040/README.md)

## Mathematical content

For a subchain I of at most s layers, the source phase pullbacks h_(I,t) have Lipschitz constant at most sL independently of alphabet sizes and projection fibers. There are at most (m+1)^s allowed coefficient choices. Apply the junta theorem to each with error gamma and take the union of chosen scalar coordinates. The resulting block lists have total size at most d=max(1,J(sL,gamma)(m+1)^s) and approximate every coefficient choice simultaneously.

Choose the first valid coordinate subset in a fixed ordering, so identical subchain data give identical lists. This is an existence choice used in the proof, not a runtime claim.

For two separated subchains, leave one separating game occurrence unconditioned. Their lists depend only on the respective own question and fixed background. Pad both to d positions. Each pair of positions defines a deterministic game strategy, of success probability at most sigma. An intersection of projected terminal lists implies success for one such pair, so its probability is at most d^2 sigma. Average over the fixed background. Dependence on the opposite question would invalidate this argument.

## Role in the source argument

Converts analytic approximations into locally decodable bounded lists before coefficient sampling and alignment.

## Antecedents and comparison

Direct combination of Austin junta approximation, deterministic tie-breaking and a standard list-to-strategy union bound. No independent novelty claim.

## Checks and limitations

Two hundred exact finite projection-game/list models compare the collision event with d^2 times the brute-force optimal game value.

The source tuple construction must supply the stated locality and projection implications. The full graph hardness proof was not checked.

## Possible applications

Soundness proofs where approximating coordinate sets must remain local to each verifier side.
