# Encode admissible permutations by independent inversion digits

[Family 169](../families/openai-math-169.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000248.md) · [Batch B044](../../batches/B044/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let G on [n] be given by a nondecreasing endpoint function h with h(i)>=i and edge i<j iff j<=h(i). Put l_a=# {b<a: {b,a} is an edge}. Call a permutation admissible if every adjacent ascent is an edge.

Delete its largest letter a. If b,a,c were consecutive, adjacency of b,a gives h(b)>=a>c, so the newly consecutive pair b,c is allowed whenever it is an ascent. Conversely insert a at the beginning or immediately after any earlier neighbor. These are exactly the allowed slots. Reading those neighbors from left to right yields newly created inversion counts l_a,l_a-1,...,0, each once.

Successive insertion gives a bijection with digits 0<=j_a<=l_a and inv_G(w)=sum j_a. In particular the inversion generating polynomial is product_a(1+q+...+q^l_a). Reversal swaps the nonedge-ascent and nonedge-descent conventions and complements the edge inversion count to |E(G)|.

Provides explicit nonnegative inversion coordinates for the permutation indexing used later in the source.

## Checks and limitations

All 64 natural unit interval graphs through five vertices, with 1,069 admissible words, satisfy the exact generating-polynomial identity.

The insertion formula alone supplies no elementary-basis shape map or positivity proof.

## Possible applications

Sampling and enumerating constrained permutations with an additive statistic.
