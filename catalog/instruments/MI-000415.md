# Control inverse-matrix derivatives without multiplying the exponential base

[Family 357](../families/openai-math-357.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000226.md) · [Batch B038](../../batches/B038/README.md)

## Mathematical content

For sigma>=1 define L_0=1 and L_m=(m+1)((m+1)!)^sigma for m>=1. The normalized interior convolution delta_m=sum_{l=1}^{m-1}L_l L_{m-l}/L_m tends to zero, while the full convolution is bounded by C_sigma L_m.

Indeed the l=1,m-1 terms are O(m^{-sigma}), the next pair O(m^{-2sigma}), and the remaining factorial ratio is at most 24/((m+1)m(m-1)). Their polynomial prefactor and number give O(m^{2-3sigma}), also O(m^{-sigma}).

Suppose a smooth matrix function J is invertible at a point, ||J^{-1}||<=sqrt(A), and ||D^m J||/m!<=B^m L_m with A,B>=1. Differentiating J J^{-1}=I and dividing by m! cancels the binomial coefficients. After factoring B^m, inverse-coefficient majorants obey

$$a_m\le A L_m+\sqrt A\sum_{l=1}^{m-1}L_l a_{m-l}.$$

Choose M so sqrt(A) delta_m<=1/2 for m>=M, bound the finitely many lower orders and absorb the tail by induction. Thus ||D^m J^{-1}||/m!<=C_{A,sigma} B^m L_m for all m, with the same B. Transposition preserves the estimate. This avoids introducing a new factor C^m at each iteration.

## Role in the source argument

Keeps all derivative orders under one budget during the source nonlinear metric-renormalization iteration.

## Checks and limitations

Exact normalized convolution ratios are evaluated through order 200 for sigma=1,2. Eighteen noncommuting matrix-series models check inverse coefficients and base scaling through order twelve.

Finite orders do not prove the asymptotic estimate by themselves or verify the source geometric iteration.

## Possible applications

Iterative inverse-Jacobian corrections where repeated losses in an exponential derivative constant would prevent convergence.
