# Choose one affine correction that hides a coordinate on every input

[Family 134](../families/openai-math-134.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000470.md) · [Batch B074](../../batches/B074/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let a finite monoid M act linearly on a finite F2-vector space U. There exist l>=1 and beta:M^l->U such that for every v and every tuple d, some coordinate j makes a->v rho(d1...d_(j-1) a d_(j+1)...dl)+beta(d1,...,a,...,dl) constant on M. The same beta serves all v,d.

Put m=|M| and q=|U|; trivial cases use beta=0. Choose beta independently uniformly at all tuples. Conditional on beta(d), constancy along a given coordinate line prescribes its other m-1 values, with probability q^(1-m). Noncentral points of different lines are disjoint, so the probability of no successful line is p_l=(1-q^(1-m))^l. A failure event reads only the radius-one Hamming neighborhood of d. Events at centers farther than two are jointly independent, giving dependency degree at most D_l=q(1+lm+l^2m^2).

Choose l large enough that p_l<=1/(4D_l). The finite local lemma, or its proved bound u(1-u)^D with u=1/(2D_l), gives positive probability of avoiding every failure event. Thus beta can be fixed before v and d. Exhaustive finite search after choosing l is an effective existence procedure, but no useful runtime bound follows. The variable coordinate j is allowed to depend on the state and tuple; beta is not.

## Role in the source argument

Adds a deterministic correction so that one interval product can be omitted by suitable local tests.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Erdos and Lovasz, Problems and results on 3-chromatic hypergraphs and some related questions](https://www.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1975-34.pdf), section 2, printed pages 616-617, proves the dependency-graph bound 1/(4d); those pages were visually read. The local lemma is classical. The source applies it to affine coordinate-line failures; exact priority of that application is not established.

## Checks and limitations

Exact small-neighborhood enumeration checks the failure probability. A finite two-element monoid model constructs a correction and checks every tuple and state; the displayed local-lemma inequality and dependency count are checked separately.

One finite model does not certify arbitrary monoids or the subsequent generalized star-height construction.

## Possible applications

Making a fixed preprocessing correction work for every query while allowing the omitted coordinate to adapt.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
