# Extract convergence from sublinear deterministic-block oscillation

[Family 154](../families/openai-math-154.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000480.md) · [Batch B076](../../batches/B076/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let Z_k be uniformly bounded measurable complex functions on a probability space. Suppose every deterministic k_1<...<k_(w+1) satisfies sum_i integral max_(k_i<=l<=k_(i+1)) |Z_l-Z_(k_i)| <= C sqrt(w), with a common C. Then Z_k converges almost everywhere.

If the tail diameter omega is positive on a set E={omega>2 delta} of measure d>0, then for every deterministic K the supremum over l>=K of |Z_l-Z_K| exceeds delta on E. Monotone convergence chooses a finite deterministic endpoint L>K with the corresponding integral at least delta d/2. Repeat to form successive blocks. Their total lower bound w delta d/2 eventually contradicts C sqrt(w). The tail diameter therefore vanishes almost everywhere, and completeness gives a limit.

The same proof works with any uniform upper bound o(w). The endpoints depend on the laws of the functions, not on the sampled point. A bound only for one prescribed partition would not support this selection.

## Role in the source argument

Turns a finite oscillation estimate into a pointwise convergence statement before mixing is used.

## Antecedents and comparison

The source explicitly calls this a standard finite-block argument. [Jones, Kaufman, Rosenblatt and Wierdl, Oscillation in ergodic theory](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0143385798108349), publisher abstract and metadata checked, establishes oscillation methods leading to pointwise convergence. Its full proof was not read here; no claim of a new criterion is made.

## Checks and limitations

Finite weighted spaces implement deterministic endpoint selection for divergent periodic sequences and check the linear witness lower bound. Convergent finite-variation sequences give a control bounded independently of the block count.

Finite examples cannot certify an infinite-sequence convergence theorem or the required analytic estimate for ergodic averages.

## Possible applications

Separating existence of a pointwise limit from identification of its value.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
