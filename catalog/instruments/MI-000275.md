# Large-base binomial digits test binary containment with unique division witnesses

[Family 242](../families/openai-math-242.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000156.md) · [Batch B021](../../batches/B021/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Assume a complete single-fold polynomial graph for b^n on bases b>=2 is available. To test that every 1-bit of x is a 1-bit of y, first require 0<=x<=y and use three power calls to define B=2^(y+1), H=B^x and N=(B+1)^y. Impose

$$N=sBH+dH+r,\qquad d<B,\quad r<H,\quad d=2t+1.$$

Two Euclidean divisions uniquely determine s,d,r. Every binomial coefficient of (B+1)^y is at most 2^y<B, so no carries occur in base B; the digit d is exactly binom(y,x). Over F2, (1+Z)^y is the product of (1+Z^(2^i)) over the 1-bit positions of y. Distinct choices have distinct exponents, so binom(y,x) is odd precisely when x's 1-bits are contained in y's.

For a true instance, the three power outputs and all their auxiliaries are unique by the assumed power graph. The divisions, oddness witness t=(d-1)/2 and inequality slacks are unique as well. False instances have no witness. The boundary x=y=0 gives B=2,H=N=1,s=r=t=0,d=1.

Evaluating powers numerically establishes the arithmetic masking identity. Replacing those calls by polynomial systems with unique complete witnesses is a separate and stronger input.

## Role in the source argument

Restricts binary positions in the source computation code without introducing multiple auxiliary witnesses.

## Antecedents and comparison

The source credits Julia Robinson for large-base extraction, Jones-Matiyasevich for binary masking, and Lucas for parity. The same established single-fold bookkeeping is discussed by [Cantone-Cuzziol-Omodeo](https://lematematiche.dmi.unict.it/index.php/lematematiche/article/view/2703). No novelty is claimed for the masking identity.

## Checks and limitations

Read the entire proof. Exact large-integer calculations check all 5,151 pairs 0<=x<=y<=100, including the zero boundary, the extracted binomial coefficient and binary containment.

The source power-graph proposition is not audited here. These computations therefore verify digit extraction and uniqueness of division data, not the claimed unconditional single-fold Diophantine representation.

## Possible applications

Encoding finite binary support constraints while retaining an exact count of complete witnesses.
