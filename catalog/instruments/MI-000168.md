# A flat cubic converts quartic potential into martingale jump energy

[Family 332](../families/openai-math-332.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000106.md) · [Batch B008](../../batches/B008/README.md)

## Mathematical content

On a finite weighted cube Q=[0,1]^B, use the weighted ell1 and Hilbert norms above and apply $\phi(r)=3r^2-2r^3$ coordinatewise, writing Phi. For any binary vertex e, let $F_e(u)=\|u-e\|_H^4$, a=u-e and delta=u'-u. The source proves

$$\|\Phi(u')-\Phi(u)\|_{1,w}^2\le108\{F_e(u')-F_e(u)-4\|a\|_H^2\langle a,\delta\rangle_H\}.$$

For either bit d, $|\phi'(r)|\le6|r-d|$. Integration and Cauchy-Schwarz give the increment bound $6\|a\|_H\|\delta\|_H+3\|\delta\|_H^2$. The remainder R has the exact form

$$R=2\|a\|_H^2\|\delta\|_H^2+(2\langle a,\delta\rangle_H+\|\delta\|_H^2)^2.$$

Consequently $\|a\|_H^2\|\delta\|_H^2\le R/2$ and $\|\delta\|_H^4\le4R$. Squaring the increment estimate yields the constant 108.

For a Q-valued martingale M_m converging almost surely, and any initial-sigma-field-measurable binary e, conditional expectation kills the linear term. Summing and using bounded convergence for the potential and monotone convergence for the jumps gives

$$\mathbb E\sum_{m\ge0}\|\Phi(M_{m+1})-\Phi(M_m)\|_{1,w}^2\le108\{\mathbb EF_e(M_\infty)-\mathbb EF_e(M_0)\}.$$

The random center may depend on the starting state. Flatness at both binary endpoints is essential: replacing Phi by the identity would compare a quadratic small increment at e with a quartic remainder and fail uniformly.

## Role in the source argument

Controls the cumulative squared ell1 cost of a nonlinear smoothed martingale by a terminal potential that equals a squared original-data distance.

## Antecedents and comparison

The cubic is already used for nonlinear cut smoothing by [Cheng, Wang and Xiang](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.08749v2); it is not new here. The selected source mechanism is the quartic remainder and its martingale telescoping. General metric Markov-cotype martingale methods appear in [Mendel and Naor](https://arxiv.org/abs/1301.3963v2).

## Checks and limitations

Read the polynomial expansion and conditional cancellation. Rational cube examples check the inequality and exact remainder; finite revealing martingales check telescoping. A scalar identity-map control shows the necessity of endpoint flatness.

The center must be a binary vertex measurable initially, and the martingale must stay in the finite weighted cube. The bound does not assert that the cubic is a linear averaging operator or that its constant is optimal.

## Possible applications

Constructing dimension-independent nonlinear martingale-energy estimates when a map flattens near the discrete terminal states.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
