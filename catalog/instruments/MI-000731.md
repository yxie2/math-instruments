# Differentiate Gaussian Gibbs observables with the correct replica diagonals

[Family 222](../families/openai-math-222.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000492.md) · [Batch B078](../../batches/B078/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let P be independent of centered Gaussian fields with covariances C_0,C_1, H_t=sqrt(1-t)H_0+sqrt(t)H_1, and Delta=C_1-C_0. For Z_t=P(exp H_t),
d E log Z_t/dt = (1/2) E <Delta_11-Delta_12>_t.
For a bounded, field-independent r-replica test F, the derivative of E<F>_t is one half the expectation of F times
sum_(a,b<=r) Delta_ab - 2r sum_(a<=r) Delta_(a,r+1) + r(r+1) Delta_(r+1,r+2) - r Delta_(r+1,r+1).
Consequently its absolute value is at most (2r^2+r)||F||_infinity ||Delta||_infinity.

For a field Y appearing with coefficient s, integration by parts similarly gives E<Y_1 F>=s E<F(sum_(a<=r) C_(1,a)-r C_(1,r+1))>. Bounded variance K supplies E Z^p and E Z^(-p) <= exp(p^2 K/2), p>=1, by Jensen. These bounds justify truncating countable labels and taking endpoint limits under the stated assumptions. They do not allow the reference measure to depend on the same fresh field.

## Role in the source argument

Provides a reusable covariance calculus with a uniform derivative bound and control of inverse normalizers.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Guerra and Toninelli, arXiv:cond-mat/0204280](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0204280), Section 3 on PDF pages 4-5, was read through the square-root Gaussian interpolation and integration-by-parts identities. This is a classical antecedent; no new Gaussian interpolation principle is claimed.

## Checks and limitations

Finite-state numerically differentiated Hessians are contracted with covariance increments and compared against explicit replica sums for one and two replicas. Gaussian quadrature checks the log-partition derivative and positive and inverse moment bounds.

The finite tests do not construct the source limiting Gibbs measures or justify any perceptron-specific covariance identification.

## Possible applications

Detecting missing diagonal terms and unsupported dependence assumptions in Gaussian perturbation proofs.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
