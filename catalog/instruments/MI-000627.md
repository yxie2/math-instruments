# Reduce tame roots of annular units to a coordinate root

[Family 019](../families/openai-math-019.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000396.md) · [Batch B065](../../batches/B065/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Over a complete algebraically closed p-adic field with value group Q, an analytic unit on an open annulus has the form u=c S^d(1+h), with integer d and |h|<1 pointwise. At a value-group radius, the reduction of the normalized unit and its inverse in the Laurent polynomial ring forces a unique dominant monomial. A change of dominant exponent would create a tie at a value-group radius, so one term dominates throughout. On every compact subannulus the remaining ratio has norm strictly below one.

For a prime ell different from p, the binomial series (1+h)^(1/ell) converges uniformly on compact subannuli and these local series agree. Since c has a root in the ground field, adjoining an ell-th root of S then supplies an ell-th root of u, also for negative d. On a disk a unit has constant absolute value. The tame assumption is essential to this series argument: for ell=p and h=p, the n-th binomial term has p-adic valuation -v_p(n!), so it does not tend to zero.

## Role in the source argument

Separates tame Kummer behavior into a winding exponent and a small analytic unit.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Baker, Payne and Rabinoff](https://arxiv.org/abs/1404.0279), Proposition 2.2(1), records the closed-annulus factorization and its Newton-polygon antecedent. Pages 4-5 were read. The open-annulus argument uses compact subannuli and compatible series. This is established background, not a new root-extraction theorem.

## Checks and limitations

Exact binomial coefficients check p-adic integrality and convergence valuation bounds for tame degrees; the wild degree supplies a divergent-series control. Laurent valuation models check one dominant term across compact subannuli.

Finite coefficients and valuation models do not verify the global section-conjecture proof. Divergence of this series does not assert nonexistence of roots in the algebraic closure.

## Possible applications

Understanding tame covers locally on annuli and separating coordinate monodromy from small-unit factors.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
