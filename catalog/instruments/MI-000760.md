# Identify a first-order graph domain by a bounded cutoff commutator

[Family 270](../families/openai-math-270.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000507.md) · [Batch B082](../../batches/B082/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Write D=D_0+V(x), with constant self-adjoint first-order symbol satisfying sigma_D(xi)^2=|xi|^2 I/2 and smooth self-adjoint matrix potential V. For a real scalar cutoff chi, [D,chi]=-i sigma_D(grad chi), so its pointwise operator norm is |grad chi|/sqrt(2).

If psi and D psi lie in L^2 distributionally, localizing makes V psi square integrable and the constant-symbol Fourier estimate yields psi in H^1 locally. Choose radial chi_R equal to one on the ball of radius R, supported in radius 2R and with gradient at most C/R. Then D(chi_R psi)=chi_R D psi+[D,chi_R]psi tends to D psi in L^2. Compactly supported H^1 vectors can be mollified in graph norm because V is bounded on the fixed support.

Thus the minimal graph closure equals the distributional maximal domain. Since the latter is the adjoint domain of the symmetric core, D is essentially self-adjoint. Averaging approximants over a commuting compact group gives the same result on its invariant subspace. In the source massless gauge-invariant case, the separate square identity makes all unit-spinor charge graph norms equal; their completions then have a common zero-form kernel.

## Role in the source argument

Making the closed operator used in a limiting argument explicit instead of silently assuming a common core.

## Antecedents and comparison

The source cites Lundholm, Zero-energy states in supersymmetric matrix models (2010), Theorem 3.1, for the undeformed full-space result. That thesis was not reread. The extracted cutoff proof is a classical constant-symbol argument, not a new general domain theorem.

## Checks and limitations

Pauli-symbol matrices check the exact commutator norm, including arbitrary gradient directions. An explicit Gaussian vector and compact radial cutoff check graph-norm error decay for an unbounded smooth potential.

Finite checks do not establish local elliptic regularity or the source supersymmetry square identity. No BFSS bound state is constructed.
