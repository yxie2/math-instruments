# A field-norm coefficient isolated in base digits excludes small determinant residues

[Family 191](../families/openai-math-191.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000126.md) · [Batch B013](../../batches/B013/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Fix an odd prime r and odd extension degree d (the source takes d=41). For three groups X^(j) of 3d field coordinates, form three columns Z(X^(j)) in F_(r^d)^3. Apply every coefficient Frobenius conjugation to their determinant and multiply. The resulting polynomial N has coefficients in F_r, degree d in each group, and is alternating. With M=binom(4d-1,d) degree-d monomials, it is a sum of T=binom(M,3) determinants det(f_ia(X^(j))), where each f_ia is a scalar multiple of one monomial. Grouping the six permutations proves this without dividing by 3!, so r=3 is allowed.

For label xi in F_(r^d), choose X(xi) representing (1,xi,xi^2). Then N on three distinct labels is the nonzero norm of their Vandermonde product. Set k=T^2+1 and place the three rows' a-th designated digits at i_a=a, j_a=Ta, ell_a=T^2-(T+1)a. Their sum is k-1 exactly when all three labels a of the determinant summands agree.

Choose L divisible by r, a prime B>100k^2 L^3, and h=B^k. For each column choose positive digits in {1,...,L}, with residue f_ia(X(xi)) at the designated positions and zero modulo r elsewhere. Each residue has L/r digit choices. For the determinant polynomial F(Y)=sum gamma_u Y^u one has |gamma_u|<=6k^2L^3, and gamma_(k-1) modulo r is precisely N. Thus for three distinct column labels gamma_(k-1) is a nonzero integer.

The truncated integer S=sum_(u<k) gamma_u B^u represents the determinant modulo h. It satisfies |S|<(h/2), while the lower digits have absolute value at most (3/50)(B^(k-1)-1). Consequently |S|>(47B^(k-1)+3)/50, so the determinant has no representative in [-floor(B^(k-1)/2),floor(B^(k-1)/2)]. Multiplication by any determinant-one residue matrix preserves this obstruction. Small lifted determinants therefore require a label collision.

## Role in the source argument

Makes a small determinant event rare before the independent cap and lattice-counting stages.

## Antecedents and comparison

Finite-field norms and the alternating monomial basis are classical; the source cites [Lidl and Niederreiter](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9781139172769), whose publisher page was not retrievable here. The selected candidate is the isolated-position code plus a quantitative carry gap, not the field norm itself.

## Checks and limitations

Read the monomial grouping, position uniqueness and carry estimates. Exact small-degree models check determinant coefficient placement and residue gaps; these use reduced parameters and do not instantiate the enormous d=41 construction.

Odd degree and odd characteristic ensure alternation as used here. Distinct field labels, bounded digits and the base-size condition are all needed. The obstruction alone does not produce the final geometric point set.

## Possible applications

Transferring a nonvanishing algebraic invariant into a quantitative gap for small integer representatives of a modular determinant.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
