# Use harmonic-exact orthogonality to force a Hodge norm growth gap

[Family 034](../families/openai-math-034.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000411.md) · [Batch B067](../../batches/B067/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let M be compact Kahler of complex dimension 2n, D Kahler, H smooth closed semipositive of rank at most n, and z in H^(n,n)(M) with [H]^n z=0. For R>=1, the source proves

||z||_(D+RH) <= R^((n-delta_n)/2)||z||_(D+H), delta_n=1/(binom(2n,n)+2).

At fixed R set omega=D+RH, Q=RH, and let gamma be the harmonic representative. In an omega-unitary frame let b_i in [0,1] be Q's eigenvalues. The derivative of the squared norm of the fixed representative with respect to log R has coefficient Tr Q-b_I-b_J on gamma_(I,bar J). Let e be n|gamma|² minus that derivative density. If rank Q=n, write K for its kernel, a=gamma_(K,bar K), d_0=n-Tr Q, and p=product_(b_i>0)b_i. Then e>=d_0|a|²+sum_(I,J)!=(K,K)|gamma_(I,bar J)|²; also (1-p)²<=d_0 and 1-p²<=2d_0.

For top forms A=omega^n/n! wedge gamma and B=Q^n/n! wedge gamma, these inequalities give |A-B|²<=N_n e and |B|²>=|gamma|²-2e, N_n=binom(2n,n). They also hold at smaller rank. A is harmonic while B is exact by [H]^n z=0, hence orthogonal. Integration gives (N_n+2) integral e>=||gamma||². Keeping the minimizing harmonic representative fixed gives an upper derivative for the minimum norm; nearby metric comparison makes that minimum locally Lipschitz. Integrating its almost-everywhere differential inequality yields the stated exponent. Without the annihilation condition, the uniform gap is not asserted.

## Role in the source argument

Obtains a quantitative improvement over the largest possible middle-degree metric growth.

## Antecedents and comparison

The ingredients are standard Hodge-Riemann/Lefschetz theory, the minimum-norm property and a tensor variation calculation. The complete source proof of the explicit constant was read. No primary antecedent with this exact gap was established, so no novelty conclusion is drawn.

## Checks and limitations

Random exterior-tensor models check both pointwise inequalities. Two-cell models with exact harmonic/exact orthogonality check the integrated deficit, and flat-torus constant-form models check metric growth under the annihilation condition.

The finite models test the norm algebra, not a uniform rank bound for Hodge structures or the global index theorem.

## Possible applications

Turning a cohomological vanishing relation into a uniform quantitative loss in metric-growth exponent.
