# Account-specific expectation discounts let one deterministic choice control many statistics

[Family 178](../families/openai-math-178.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000134.md) · [Batch B015](../../batches/B015/README.md)

## Mathematical content

At a state x take a finite nonempty probability distribution of candidate next states. For statistics Z_i(x)>=1, discounts D_i>0 and fixed weights a_i>=0, set r_i=max(1,E[Z_i(x')|x]/Z_i(x)) and D_i'=D_i/r_i. Some candidate satisfies

$$\sum_i a_iD_i'Z_i(x')\le\sum_i a_iD_iZ_i(x).$$

Choose a minimizer: its value is no larger than the conditional average. If along the chosen history E[Z_i'|x]<=(1+c_(i,t))Z_i with c>=0 and D_(i,0)=1, then D_(i,t)^(-1)<=product_(j<t)(1+c_(i,j))<=exp(sum_(j<t)c_(i,j)). Apply this to 1+Y for nonnegative Y that can vanish. A label may instead die permanently with account zero. With fixed label weight 1/F, discounted total <=K and reciprocal discount <=B, each live account is <=FKB. A normalized pth-power statistic consequently costs only (FKB)^(1/p).

For a scalar X with threshold T, fixed b>0 and ordinary increments satisfying |Delta|<=b, E Delta<=mu and E Delta^2<=nu, mu,nu>=0, the account Z=1+((X-T)_+/b)^p, even p>=2, obeys E Z'<=[1+C_p(mu/b+nu/b^2)]Z. This follows from a positive-part Taylor bound with a quadratic remainder. Other transitions may adjust the threshold only if the account does not increase, or set it permanently to zero. Two signs give two-sided control.

## Role in the source argument

Selects one pairing that preserves heterogeneous trace, row and entry bounds while charging each statistic its own predictable growth.

## Antecedents and comparison

The method of conditional expectations is classical; [Raghavan](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0022000088900037) and [Wigderson-Xiao, Section 3](https://theoryofcomputing.org/articles/v004a003/v004a003.pdf) give antecedents. The latter explicitly composes estimators by addition. The discounts and dead-label convention are the extracted bookkeeping.

## Checks and limitations

Read the weighted-average proof and Taylor argument. Exact rational transition systems check monotonicity and discount products, including dead labels. Finite scalar-increment distributions check an explicit valid Taylor constant for several even orders.

Efficient construction also requires efficiently computing candidate statistics and expectations; the averaging lemma alone supplies no polynomial running-time guarantee. Initial fixed weights cannot be renormalized over survivors without changing the argument.

## Possible applications

Derandomizing multiple local error controls whose expectation growth rates differ.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
