# Make every row tight by one decreasing multiplicative sweep

[Family 113](../families/openai-math-113.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000267.md) · [Batch B049](../../batches/B049/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let n>=2 and G be symmetric off the diagonal, with 0<alpha<=G_ij<=R^2. Initialize v_i=R, then visit each coordinate exactly once in any fixed order, replacing v_i by max_(j!=i) G_ij/v_j.

Throughout the sweep, v_i v_j>=G_ij and alpha/R<=v_i<=R. Indeed, feasibility makes each update nonincreasing, and all other coordinates remain at most R. The updated coordinate has a tight incident edge. That equality survives: if the other endpoint is visited later, its update cannot decrease it without violating the existing equality. Thus every row of G_ij/(v_i v_j) has maximum exactly one after a single sweep.

The source takes R=2 and G_ij<=3. This formulation exposes the same proof for any feasible common initialization. The output can depend on the visitation order and is not asserted to minimize a global scaling objective. Zero lower bounds remove the stated positive lower bound and may make division ill-defined.

## Antecedents and comparison

A direct feasibility-preserving coordinate argument. No independent novelty claim is made for the sweep; the source supplies its use with estimated matching ratios.

## Checks and limitations

Exact rational tests use positive symmetric arrays and multiple visitation orders, checking every intermediate product, monotonicity, lower bounds and persistent row witnesses. An infeasible initialization defeats monotonicity.

This does not validate the ratio estimator or the source polynomial-time approximation scheme.

## Possible applications

Quickly producing a maximal feasible reciprocal scaling when rowwise tightness is needed but a global optimum is unnecessary.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
