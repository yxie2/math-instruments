# Preserve a global priority suffix while pruning only locally relevant list entries

[Family 124](../families/openai-math-124.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000214.md) · [Batch B035](../../batches/B035/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let K_1,...,K_l be global upsets in a finite poset with a fixed linear-extension rank. A qualifying job has key (largest i with x in K_i, rank(x)), ordered lexicographically. For cutoff (d,r), replace the list by K_d intersect {rank>=r},K_{d+1},...,K_l. Its qualifier set is exactly the old jobs with key at least (d,r), and it preserves their old order. Rank suffixes are upsets.

Now prune entries whose intersection with a child set W is empty. If x belongs to W and qualifies, every old entry containing x survives, so its largest retained old index equals its old largest index. A global qualifier y may lose larger indices, but cannot gain them. Hence if the new key of x is less than that of y, the old key comparison had the same direction.

This is a one-sided implication with x in W. It does not say that every global pair keeps its order after local pruning.

## Role in the source argument

Maintains the relevant inherited priority comparisons while shortening the source list representation.

## Antecedents and comparison

This is elementary ordered filtering. Its main reusable caution is the distinction between a local job whose memberships all survive and a global job that may lose memberships.

## Checks and limitations

Two hundred random finite-poset models verify suffix selection, retained ordering and the one-sided pruning implication. An explicit outside-child pair reverses order under pruning.

No source global scheduling hierarchy or boundary invariant is constructed by these tests.

## Possible applications

Locally pruning a list-based priority representation while retaining the comparisons actually required by a recursive argument.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
