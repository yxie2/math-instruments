# Control translated cut lengths by coarea and vanishing l2 pairings

[Family 315](../families/openai-math-315.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000236.md) · [Batch B041](../../batches/B041/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let F take vertex values in [0,1], with dF in l2 of edges. Let nonnegative metric edge lengths lambda be in l2. Suppose the near-edge relation has bounded incidence in both variables and group translations move every finite edge set eventually off every other finite set. For the edges S(F,t) straddling a level t,

$$\int_0^1\sum_{e\in S(F,t)}\sum_{e'\sim e}\lambda(ge')\,dt
=\sum_{e'}\left(\sum_{e\sim e'}|dF(e)|\right)\lambda(ge').$$

Tonelli proves the equality. Bounded incidence makes the parenthesized sequence l2. The pairing is finite by Cauchy-Schwarz and tends to zero as g escapes: approximate both factors by finite supports, separate their translates, and bound the errors in l2.

For bounded-size cells, a dual edge path gives a diameter bound by a constant times the same nearby-length sum. If almost every level cut has finitely many dual components, choose a subsequence whose length integrals are summable. Almost every corresponding length sum then tends to zero. In a compactification where translates of each fixed vertex converge to one point, finitely many component anchors and the diameter bound make the whole cut converge uniformly to that point. These last geometric hypotheses must be established separately.

Converts finite energy into decay of translated cuts used by the source proposed convergence action.

## Antecedents and comparison

Discrete coarea, Cauchy-Schwarz and the classical finite-support proof of vanishing regular-representation matrix coefficients. No novel general decay theorem is claimed.

## Checks and limitations

One hundred exact piecewise-constant coarea integrals and a finite-support translation model check the algebraic interface.

The compactification, finite-component cut property and global convergence action are not constructed by the tests.

## Possible applications

Turning square-summable gradients and lengths into almost-everywhere small translated separators.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
