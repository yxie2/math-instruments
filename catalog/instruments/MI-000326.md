# Rule out deterministic branches by testing selected subsets

[Family 373](../families/openai-math-373.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000182.md) · [Batch B027](../../batches/B027/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let nu be a probability measure on an open set B and let H_1,...,H_4 be homeomorphisms onto open images, all five sets B,H_1(B),...,H_4(B) pairwise disjoint. Set

$$\mu=\tfrac13\nu+\tfrac16\sum_{j=1}^4(H_j)_\#\nu.$$

There is no Borel map T preserving mu and satisfying T(x) in {H_1(x),...,H_4(x)} for nu-almost every x in B. Indeed S_j={x in B:T(x)=H_j(x)} is Borel and H_j(S_j) is Borel. Preservation and S_j subset T^{-1}(H_j(S_j)) imply

$$\tfrac13\nu(S_j)=\mu(S_j)\le\mu(H_j(S_j))=\tfrac16\nu(S_j).$$

Thus every S_j has zero measure, contradicting their almost-everywhere cover of B. Additional preimages can only strengthen the contradiction. Nonatomicity is unnecessary for this obstruction.

The same proof works whenever the source coefficient exceeds every branch coefficient and all copies remain disjoint. Matching the total mass of a target component does not suffice: the selected image subset has its own stricter capacity. To apply this to an optimal-transport problem, one must separately prove that every optimizer is forced to use the specified branches.

## Role in the source argument

Turns a previously established optimal-support description into an obstruction to deterministic transport.

## Antecedents and comparison

This is an elementary pushforward-capacity argument. It is not a proof of a Coulomb counterexample without the source dual support certificate, which was not audited in this batch.

## Checks and limitations

All 5,460 branch assignments on weighted finite source sets of sizes one through six violate a selected-subset capacity. A control matches a whole component total while still violating its selected-subset capacity.

Finite copies illustrate the mass argument. They neither construct the smooth density nor force the Coulomb-optimal support.

## Possible applications

Detecting unavoidable randomization or mass splitting in prescribed-branch transport problems.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
