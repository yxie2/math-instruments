# Extract causal rational flow tables and round them with a diameter budget

[Family 110](../families/openai-math-110.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000462.md) · [Batch B073](../../batches/B073/README.md)

## Mathematical content

On a finite rational metric of diameter D, with k distinct initial servers and horizon H, use variables p_(w,c) for the configuration distribution after request prefix w and flows f_(wr,c,j) for a legal strongly lazy response to the next request r. Outflows equal p_(w,c); inflows give p_(wr,c'). Shared prefix variables enforce causality across every continuation. For each word, the sum of its prefix flow costs is constrained by a times its exact offline optimum.

Assume the source existence input makes this finite rational LP feasible with a<=C0 log^2(k+1). Minimizing a>=0 gives an attained rational optimum: eliminate other variables by Fourier-Motzkin, minimize the resulting closed rational polyhedron on the line, and back-substitute. No numerical value of C0 is needed to perform this finite computation. Recover response probabilities f/p when p>0, and use a fixed legal response when p=0.

Reserve the smallest legal label; round every other probability down to a multiple of 2^-b and assign the residual to the reserved label. Total variation is at most k2^-b. Choose b=ceil(log2(kH^2)). Couple original and rounded policies until their first disagreement. By time h the disagreement probability is at most hk2^-b; each full trajectory costs at most hD, so expected cost increases by at most kh^2 2^-b D<=D. Formerly unreachable configurations are covered by their legal default rows. The table uses b fair bits per request, but this argument gives no efficient table size or construction time.

## Role in the source argument

Turns an existence-based competitive guarantee into a finite exact object with bounded random-bit consumption.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Mömke, A Competitive Ratio Approximation Scheme for the k-Server Problem in Fixed Finite Metrics](https://arxiv.org/abs/1303.2963), section 4, pages 6-7, already gives rational finite-horizon strategy polyhedra, prefix consistency and conditional-policy recovery; those pages were read. The source uses configuration flows and explicit dyadic coupling bounds. Rational LP elimination and coupling are established tools.

## Checks and limitations

Small exact policy flows test conservation, reconstruction, legal zero-mass defaults and all short-word costs. Dyadic rounding is checked against exact cost enumeration and the additive-diameter budget; LP optimality is not inferred from these tests.

The checks do not establish the squared-logarithmic existence bound or polynomial per-request implementation.

## Possible applications

Separating finite implementability and randomness precision from the further problem of efficient online computation.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
