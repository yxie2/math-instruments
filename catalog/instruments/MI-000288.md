# Fractional cell variance preserves second moments on moving measures

[Family 081](../families/openai-math-081.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000163.md) · [Batch B023](../../batches/B023/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Suppose Radon measures mu_j have common global upper n-growth, lower n-growth at support points on admissible radii, support diameters tending to infinity, and weak convergence to nu=q times n-dimensional volume on a fixed plane L, with q>0. For finitely many real functions w_j^a, assume locally uniform L2 bounds and bounds on

$$\mathcal E_{\mu_j,U}(w)=\iint_{U\times U}\frac{|w(x)-w(y)|^2}{|x-y|^{n+1}}\,d\mu_j(x)d\mu_j(y).$$

On one subsequence, there are local L2 functions v^a on L such that mu_j, w_j^a mu_j and |w_j^a|^2 mu_j converge on each of the source's bounded continuity regions to nu, v^a nu and |v^a|^2 nu, respectively, against all continuous tests. In particular the localized squared norms converge.

The lower growth bound forces bounded pieces of the moving supports into a shrinking tube around L. Partition a bounded projection box into cells of side h. At each fixed partition level, every cell has mass at least (q/2)h^n for large j, and its support diameter is at most (sqrt(n)+2)h. If m_C is the cell average, the exact variance identity gives

$$\int_C|w-m_C|^2d\mu_j=\frac1{2\mu_j(C)}\iint_{C^2}|w(x)-w(y)|^2d\mu_j(x)d\mu_j(y)\le C_nq^{-1}h\,\mathcal E_{\mu_j,C}(w).$$

Summing disjoint cell squares introduces no number-of-cells factor. A diagonal subsequence makes the finitely many cell averages converge at every fixed level and region. The O(h) squared approximation error makes their limiting step functions Cauchy in L2. First pass j to infinity at fixed h, then let h decrease to zero; this retains the norms as well as signed moments. Continuous ambient approximants and polarization then give the weighted squared-moment limit. Local limits agree on overlaps using test functions vanishing at the overlap boundary.

An arbitrary representative of v need never be evaluated on the moving supports. The result is formulated as convergence of three measures, until a later argument identifies a particular ambient representative.

## Role in the source argument

Retains normalized normal-displacement energy when the supporting measure converges to a flat plane.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Kuwae-Shioya, Definition 2.4 and Lemma 2.1](https://archive.intlpress.com/site/pub/files/_fulltext/journals/cag/2003/0011/0004/CAG-2003-0011-0004-a001.pdf) gives an established framework for strong convergence and norm preservation in varying Hilbert spaces. The source supplies an explicit flat-plane finite-partition proof with a fractional-energy estimate; equivalence to a particular general compactness theorem was not audited.

## Checks and limitations

Read the full compactness proof, its energy definition and localization regions. Twelve atomic grid models check the exact weighted variance identity and its geometric energy bound. An oscillating zero-mean model retains nonzero squared mass, showing why first moments alone do not suffice.

Atomic grid measures do not satisfy the continuum AD assumptions. They test the finite-cell algebra only, not support convergence, compactness, the source energy estimate or rectifiability.

## Possible applications

Passing nonlinear quadratic quantities to a limit when both functions and their underlying measures vary.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
