# Use finite neutral generators to control non-cancellation in all degrees

[Family 066](../families/openai-math-066.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000442.md) · [Batch B071](../../batches/B071/README.md)

## Mathematical content

In the source pre-pinning setup write A_j=in_w(Y_j+) and B_j=in_w(Y_j-). Assume pair products A_j B_j lie in a field K*, old weights lie on a rational line with bounded denominators, and new weights are independent modulo that line. Once the finite old neutral ratios A_j^(d_j) A_0^(-t_j) enter K*, a power of every weight-kernel monomial is a product of those generators and pair products. Every weight-zero monomial is therefore algebraic over K*, simultaneously in all degrees; no later index depending on polynomial degree is needed for this algebraic statement.

For a finite polynomial whose least-w group has nonzero initial sum, multiply by opposite factors for one term in that group. The new least initial has weight zero and is algebraic over K*. A valuation trivial on K* is trivial on every nonzero algebraic element: a polynomial equation with nonzero constant term would otherwise have a unique term of least value.

The remaining transfer is conditional on the source bridge: it supplies value at most zero for this complemented sum. If the original terms are tied and pair values opposite, each complemented term has value zero; the valuation inequality supplies value at least zero. Equality prevents cancellation. The bridge can then transfer exactness onward. The bridge's unrestricted-cost conclusion is an input here, not proved by the finite-generator observation.

## Role in the source argument

Separates a uniform algebraic reason for all-degree control from the geometric transfer needed to use it.

## Antecedents and comparison

Rational weight kernels, algebraic-extension triviality and the unique-minimum valuation argument are classical. The pinning/bridge interface is source-specific and its global validity and priority are not certified.

## Checks and limitations

Integer weight kernels check bounded-denominator neutral powers; Laurent polynomial examples over Q(sqrt(2)) check nonzero least initials and explicit cancellation controls. Polynomial equations check why a nonzero valuation of an algebraic element would force a unique minimum.

The tests do not prove the bridge, the eventual entry of neutral generators into K*, or simultaneous validity of all source cone tests.

## Possible applications

Avoiding an invalid exchange between an eventual index and unbounded polynomial degree.
