# Recover a Lefschetz filtration from a common lowering operator

[Family 043](../families/openai-math-043.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000227.md) · [Batch B039](../../batches/B039/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let H be finite dimensional over a characteristic-zero field. Let F and G be finite exhaustive separated increasing integer filtrations, and let e,f shift each filtration by at most +2,-2 respectively. Suppose e on each associated graded satisfies hard Lefschetz with the same center r, and

$$[[f,e],e]=-2e.$$

Then F=G. Indeed, on a primitive string v,e v,...,e^ell v, the canonical lowering operator sends e^j v to j(ell-j+1)e^(j-1)v. The displayed identity forces the induced f to be this operator: ad(e)^2 is injective from weight -2 to weight +2 in End(H). Thus h=[e,f] acts on the kth graded piece as k-r.

For either filtration A, (h-(k-r))A_k is contained in A_(k-1). Multiplying the distinct linear factors over the finite grading range gives a square-free annihilating polynomial for h. Consequently h is semisimple and

$$A_k=\bigoplus_{j\le k}\ker(h-(j-r)I).$$

This reconstructs both filtrations from the same operator. A further grading preserved by the data permits the argument degree by degree.

## Role in the source argument

Reduces equality of the geometric filtrations to construction and compatibility of one common lowering operator.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Hausel-Mellit-Minets-Schiffmann](https://arxiv.org/html/2209.05429v2), Propositions 8.2-8.3 and Lemma 8.4, already relate lowering operators, semisimple gradings and equality of filtrations. Those selected passages were compared; no exhaustive priority assessment was made.

## Checks and limitations

Nine exact sums of Lefschetz strings check the commutator, injectivity and annihilating polynomial. A conjugated-flag control shows that a shared raiser alone can leave different filtrations.

No geometric operator is constructed by this linear-algebra check. Finite examples do not replace the representation-theoretic proof.

## Possible applications

Comparing weight, perverse or other filtrations once a common raising/lowering calculus is available.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
