# Turn ideal approximation into exact membership after a spectral cutoff

[Family 303](../families/openai-math-303.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000235.md) · [Batch B041](../../batches/B041/README.md)

## Mathematical content

If J is the closure of a directed increasing union of closed ideals J_lambda and z is positive in J, then every cut (z-epsilon)_+, epsilon>0, belongs to some J_lambda. Choose b in that ideal with ||z-b||<epsilon. The positive quotient image of z has norm below epsilon, so its cutoff is zero. In particular, the ideals generated by (z-delta)_+ densely exhaust the ideal generated by z as delta decreases to zero.

For positive u,v, membership u in Id(v) is equivalent to every irreducible representation annihilating v also annihilating u: apply separation of points to C/Id(v). Finally, two nonnegative continuous functions of one positive element, vanishing at zero and having the same open positivity set on its spectrum, generate the same hereditary algebra and are Cuntz equivalent, because their support projections agree.

## Role in the source argument

Retains exact ideal membership and quotient signals after taking spectral cuts.

## Antecedents and comparison

Standard quotient functional calculus, primitive representation separation and support-projection rules. No first-priority claim.

## Checks and limitations

One hundred finite commutative quotient models check cutoff membership and equality of positivity supports.

The finite models do not test an infinite directed limit. The epsilon cutoff is essential to the finite-stage conclusion.

## Possible applications

Replacing approximate ideal information by exact localization at a positive spectral scale.
