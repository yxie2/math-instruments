# Bound ordered mark lists under a dependent pin-weighted Poisson law

[Family 271](../families/openai-math-271.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000508.md) · [Batch B082](../../batches/B082/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Weight a finite-volume Poisson transposition picture by 2^(n_D), where n_D counts cycles avoiding a prescribed pin set D. Inserting a transposition either joins two cycles or splits one. Joining cannot increase n_D; on splitting a pinned cycle at least one descendant remains pinned. Thus one insertion increases n_D by at most one and the weight by at most two.

The multivariate Poisson insertion identity then gives, for any nonnegative F of j marks,
E_D sum_(ordered distinct marks) F <= 2^j integral F d lambda^j.
The identity follows directly by canceling the k!/(k-j)! choices of distinguished marks against the Poisson coefficient 1/k!. Nonnegativity permits infinite integrals. Restrictions on time order or positions are included in F.

Continuous boundary pins can be approximated by increasing finite dense time sets: at fixed volume there are finitely many marks and cycles, each boundary visit contains a positive-length interval, and eventually every such cycle is detected. The weights are uniformly bounded by 2^(number of slots), so the normalized densities converge in L^1. Independent fresh marks combine with the bound by a product factorial measure; arbitrary conditioning on an exposure is not covered.

## Role in the source argument

Counting many possible paths despite dependence introduced by cycle weights and pins.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Last and Penrose, Lectures on the Poisson Process](https://stoch.math.kit.edu/img/Last/lastpenrose2017.pdf), Theorem 4.4 and its complete proof on printed pages 30-31 (PDF pages 42-43), were read. The source estimate is the classical Mecke formula plus its explicit cycle-weight insertion bound.

## Checks and limitations

Exhaustive finite permutations, pin subsets and transpositions check the cycle-count increment. A dependent weighted two-coordinate Poisson law checks factorial identities and the 2^j bound, including repeated coordinate types but distinct marks.

This is factorial-moment domination, not a claim that the pinned process is independent Poisson or that the same estimate survives every conditioning.
