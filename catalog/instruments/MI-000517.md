# Choose an independent Frobenius-constrained nullvector

[Family 208](../families/openai-math-208.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000289.md) · [Batch B052](../../batches/B052/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let k be perfect of characteristic p and let alpha_1,...,alpha_r be F_p-linearly independent. There exist F_p-linearly independent lambda_i with sum_i alpha_i lambda_i^(p^j)=0 for 0<=j<=r-2. For r=1 choose any nonzero lambda.

For r>=2 set beta_i=alpha_i^(p^(-(r-2))) and let B have rows (beta_i^(p^s))_i for s=0,...,r-2. A nonzero row relation would be an additive polynomial of degree at most p^(r-2) vanishing on the p^r-element prime-field span of the beta_i, impossible. Thus B has rank r-1.

Its row space meets F_p^r only at zero. If c_i=f(beta_i) is prime-field valued for an additive polynomial f of degree at most p^(r-2), then f^p-f vanishes on all p^r points and has degree at most p^(r-1), so it is zero. A nonzero positive-degree f cannot satisfy f^p=f as a polynomial, and f has no constant term. Hence f=0.

Choose a nonzero lambda in the one-dimensional kernel. Any prime-field relation among its coordinates lies in the annihilator of that kernel, namely the row space of B, and therefore vanishes. Finally the rows, in reverse order, are (alpha_i^(p^(-j)))_i; raising the nullvector equations to p^j yields the desired constraints. The source uses these as the equations forcing a chosen rank-support point to remain in the support of a restricted Steinberg module.

## Role in the source argument

Selects an injective elementary-abelian-group embedding compatible with the source chosen nonprojective support point.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Coulembier-Flake, A conjecture on the tensor ideal for an elementary p-group generated by the restriction of a Steinberg module](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.02722v1), Lemma 3.2.2 and Proposition 4.2.5/Corollary 4.2.7, give the Moore determinant criterion and the same support equations; their statements and the support proof were read. The reverse nullvector selection was checked in the source. No earliest-priority claim for that selection is made.

## Checks and limitations

Exact arithmetic in finite extension fields of characteristics two, three and five constructs nullvectors, checks every Frobenius equation and checks prime-field independence of both coordinate lists. Dependent input coordinates supply a failure control.

Solving the finite-field equations does not establish the rank-variety theorem, restricted tilting or the source categorical classification.

## Possible applications

Solving several Frobenius-linear constraints while preserving injectivity of an elementary-abelian-group embedding.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
