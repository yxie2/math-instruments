# Concentrate reserved sampling roles before adaptive selection

[Family 136](../families/openai-math-136.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000247.md) · [Batch B044](../../batches/B044/README.md)

## Mathematical content

For a stationary finite Markov chain whose transition operator has mean-zero L2 norm at most lambda<1, bounded centered functions f_j at different times obey |Cov(f_s(X_s),f_t(X_t))|<=C lambda^|s-t| when their L2 norms are bounded by sqrt(C). Thus the average of q samples has variance at most C(1+lambda)/((1-lambda)q).

Independent auxiliary choices can be integrated out of off-diagonal terms; their conditional variances enter the diagonal bound. Chebyshev and a finite union bound give any prescribed fixed confidence for a fixed finite list of candidate roles using a sufficiently large fixed q.

In the source, uniform averaging over the output start coordinate makes walk positions stationary. Analyze every reserved true candidate before a minimum or uncertain acceptance selects one. Do not condition the concentration estimate on the realized answer path. A fixed number of fixed-degree walk labels and polynomial-support auxiliary choices yields polynomial support per output coordinate; the confidence is still an average over coordinates.

## Antecedents and comparison

Classical spectral-gap variance estimates. The extraction records the unconditioned averaging contract, not a new concentration theorem.

## Checks and limitations

Thirty-two time-varying-function models enumerate all 729 paths of a six-sample, three-state chain and verify the variance bound.

No per-start or success-conditioned guarantee follows from these tests. The complete adaptive reduction is outside scope.

## Possible applications

Analyzing a finite list of candidates before an algorithm makes a data-dependent choice.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
