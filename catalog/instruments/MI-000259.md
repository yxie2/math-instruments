# Two vanishing couplings remove a simple resolvent pole and permit a fixed complement

[Family 201](../families/openai-math-201.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000149.md) · [Batch B019](../../batches/B019/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let M(xi) be a formal matrix series over a field E, with zero a semisimple eigenvalue of M0=M(0). Write Pi for the projection onto ker(M0) along im(M0), and suppose G=Pi M'(0) Pi restricted to the kernel is invertible. Then M(xi)^(-1) exists over E((xi)) with at most a simple pole.

Let compatible series L(xi),B(xi) satisfy L(0)Pi=0 and Pi B(0)=0. If J is zero on ker(M0) and M0^(-1) on im(M0), then

$$\lim_{\xi\to0}L(\xi)M(\xi)^{-1}B(\xi)=L(0)JB(0).$$

Here the limit is the constant term of the regular resulting series. In the kernel/image decomposition, M has blocks xi G+O(xi^2), O(xi), O(xi), and invertible M0|im+O(xi). Block inversion gives a xi^(-1) kernel block, bounded off-diagonal blocks and the limiting inverse on the image. Both kernel couplings are O(xi), so every unwanted contribution vanishes.

One can use a fixed complementary idempotent instead of Pi. Suppose e^2=e, f=1-e, fM0f is invertible on fV and e:ker(M0)->eV is an isomorphism. Then L0 J B0=L0 f(fM0f)^(-1)f B0. For b=B0u in im(M0), solutions of M0x=b differ by ker(M0); exactly one has ex=0, and L0 has the same value on all of them.

With only one vanishing coupling, a finite extra constant can survive: M(xi)=xi, L(xi)=xi, B(xi)=1 gives product one although the proposed regular-part expression is zero.

## Role in the source argument

Removes a resonant intermediate state when transferring a leading free-word coefficient to an auxiliary partner.

## Antecedents and comparison

The proof is standard block inversion plus a choice of solution modulo a kernel. The source-specific value is the precise two-coupling condition and the fixed-complement replacement; historical novelty is not claimed.

## Checks and limitations

Read both conclusions. Finite block matrices, including nonorthogonal spectral decompositions and oblique complements, check the Laurent leading term and convergence. One-sided coupling and degenerate-derivative controls show why the hypotheses matter.

A nonsemisimple zero or a singular compressed derivative can create higher poles. The statement is formal algebraic regularity, not a uniform numerical conditioning estimate near zero.

## Possible applications

Regularizing transfer functions whose input and output both suppress a resonant mode.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
