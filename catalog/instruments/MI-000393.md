# Stack common tiling equations using a full-difference cyclic partition

[Family 155](../families/openai-math-155.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000215.md) · [Batch B036](../../batches/B036/README.md)

## Mathematical content

For any fixed number s of colors, choose a sufficiently large fresh prime q and a partition Z/qZ=E_1 disjoint-union ... disjoint-union E_s with E_i-E_i=Z/qZ for every i. A random coloring works: for each nonzero difference, (q-1)/2 disjoint pairs give failure probability at most (1-s^{-2})^{floor(q/2)}, and a union bound over colors and differences tends to zero.

For finite nonempty F_i in an abelian group H, set F=union_i(F_i times E_i). A common unique-representation complement A for all F_i gives A times {0} as a complement of F. Conversely, in any tiling B+F, a fixed horizontal fiber can receive at most one translated E_i of each color, since two such translates intersect. The exact-cover equation sum_i n_i|E_i|=q, with n_i in {0,1}, forces every n_i=1. Projection is injective on B and its image is a common complement of all F_i.

Finite-index periodicity passes to the projection; a periodic common complement lifts. Choosing q coprime to an existing finite cyclic factor preserves cyclicity of its product with Z/qZ. No constant-height assertion about arbitrary B is needed.

## Role in the source argument

Converts a finite conjunction of tiling constraints to one tile while preserving the relevant periodicity alternative.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Greenfeld and Tao, Section 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2211.15847v3) gives stacking using a stronger rigid-partition condition. The selected source uses same-color differences and a fiber-count argument with a fresh prime.

## Checks and limitations

Forty-eight exact-cover models over small finite groups decode all 385 stacked complements; every decoded projection is a common complement and every tested common complement lifts.

Finite groups do not test infinite-group aperiodicity or the dimension-three encoding.

## Possible applications

Combining exact-cover constraints while retaining a witness decoder and control of auxiliary finite factors.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
