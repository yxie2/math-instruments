# Count retained slots through one global binomial coefficient

[Family 364](../families/openai-math-364.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000524.md) · [Batch B086](../../batches/B086/README.md)

## Mathematical content

If cut j retains k_j of q_j available slots, write Q=sum q_j and H'=sum k_j. Then product_j binom(q_j,k_j)<=binom(Q,H')<=(eQ/H')^H' for H'>0; for H'=0 the cost is one. The first inequality follows by selecting the indicated term in the coefficient of z^H' in product(1+z)^q_j=(1+z)^Q.

When Q<=p+C H and H'<=C H with H<=delta p, the logarithmic retained-slot cost is at most C delta p(1+log(1/delta)), plus the same order for bounded choices at retained slots. The source counts a label once at its first input and once per subsequent retention; chronology already determines its layer. Bounded choices at first-input slots belong to a baseline C^p and must not be presented as a small retention cost. For H<=Lp, the coarser cost is C_L^p.

## Role in the source argument

Preventing an artificial exponential factor in the number of time layers when summing persistent histories.

## Antecedents and comparison

This is the standard binomial/entropy estimate with explicit accounting for retained rather than newly created slots. No new combinatorial inequality is claimed.

## Checks and limitations

Exhaustive small allocations verify both inequalities and the zero-slot case; scaled examples check the entropy rate against a separate baseline cost.

The combinatorics do not prove the kinetic history budget Q<=p+C H or the analytic summability of the full expansion.

## Possible applications

Preventing an artificial exponential factor in the number of time layers when summing persistent histories.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
