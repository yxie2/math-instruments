# Read approximate tree geometry from determinant-one matrix growth

[Family 146](../families/openai-math-146.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000283.md) · [Batch B051](../../batches/B051/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let B_j be real 2 by 2 matrices with determinant one, D_j=||B_j||>=1, and a_j a most expanded unit input direction. Define R_jp=||B_j B_p^(-1)||/(D_j D_p) and Q_jp=max{|a_j wedge a_p|,D_j^(-2),D_p^(-2)}. Then Q_jp<=R_jp<=3Q_jp. The sine term follows by testing the input perpendicular to a_p; the other lower bounds use ||B||=||B^(-1)||. Decomposing an input along a_p and its perpendicular gives the sum of the three terms as an upper bound.

The sine triangle inequality implies Q_jp<=2 max{Q_jl,Q_lp}, hence R_jp<=6 max{R_jl,R_lp}. For a transfer cocycle with g(j,p)=log_M||B_j B_p^(-1)||, taking negative logarithms gives the Gromov-product inequality for B_i(j,p)=g(i,j)+g(i,p)-g(j,p), with additive error log_M 6. Dividing distances by a scale tending to infinity removes this error.

The same comparison truncates cancellation. Suppose g is the growth semimetric, 0<=g(u,v)<=|u-v|, and L=i-n,R=i+n,L'=i-s,R'=i+s with 1<=s<=n. Put D=n-min{g(i,L),g(i,R)} and ell=g(i,L)+g(i,R)-g(L,R). The shorter wings have growth at least s-D. Applying the four-point bound twice along L',L,R,R' gives their cancellation loss at least min{ell,2s-2D}-2 log_M 6. Thus cancellation over a long interval remains detectable at a shorter matched scale, up to the wing deficits and a uniform additive error when M>=2.

## Role in the source argument

Supplies tree-like limiting distance arrays and a controlled way to shorten long cancellations in the standard-map source.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Bestvina, Real trees in topology, geometry, and group theory](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9712210), Definition 2.10 and Lemma 2.13, give the Gromov-product condition and real-tree embedding context; those passages were read. The selected matrix estimate is proved directly in the source. This audit does not establish its historical priority.

## Checks and limitations

Numerical determinant-one matrix models check the singular-direction comparison and four-point bound, including nearly aligned directions. Transfer chains check the truncation bound. A three-dimensional determinant-one control shows that the same single-direction formula need not hold there.

Finite matrix checks do not prove positive Lyapunov exponents, positive entropy or the source probability estimates.

## Possible applications

Reducing cancellations in two-dimensional cocycles to approximate tree geometry and selecting comparable observation scales.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
