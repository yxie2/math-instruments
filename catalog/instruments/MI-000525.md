# Recover the missing determinant direction after an oblique projection

[Family 360](../families/openai-math-360.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000294.md) · [Batch B053](../../batches/B053/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let V>0, |e|=1, q=p.e>0, p_i=p+d e with d>0, and m,beta,gamma>0. Suppose V(e,e)<=beta q^2 and a positive-definite V_i dominates m P^T V P+gamma p_i p_i^T, where P=I-e p^T/q projects onto p-perp along e. Then

det(V_i)/det(V) >= m^(n-1) gamma(p_i.e)^2/S >= m^(n-1)(gamma/beta)[(p_i.e)/q]^2 >= m^(n-1) gamma/beta.

Here S is the Schur complement of V in the basis [Q,e], with Q an orthonormal basis of p-perp. Indeed the transformed V has blocks [[A,z],[z^T,h]], where S=h-z^T A^(-1)z>0 and S<=h=V(e,e). The projected matrix loses exactly its last direction; the rank-one addition contributes determinant m^(n-1) det(A) gamma(p_i.e)^2. The nonorthogonal change-of-basis determinant cancels in the ratio.

In the source, L>=0, Le=0, H<=L and H>=V-beta pp^T imply L>=P^T V P and V(e,e)<=beta q^2; dropping an additional positive center term only weakens these inequalities. Its outer bound gives the assumed domination, and gamma/beta is proportional to a chosen large parameter K. The matrix result is conditional on obtaining that configuration from the geometric jets.

## Role in the source argument

Produces the source determinant gain proportional to K that is compared with the independent Jacobian bounds in its transport-regularity contradiction.

## Antecedents and comparison

The proof uses classical Schur-complement factorization and monotonicity of determinant for positive-definite matrices. The full selected source calculation and its hypotheses were read; no novelty claim for this linear algebra identity is made.

## Checks and limitations

Exact rational positive-definite matrices verify the determinant ratio, its lower bound, and nonorthogonal change-of-basis cancellation. A missing rank-one direction leaves determinant zero.

The checks do not validate the limiting jet configuration, exponential-Jacobian comparison or uniform bi-Holder theorem.

## Possible applications

Comparing determinants when transverse positivity is known and a separate rank-one term controls the remaining direction.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
