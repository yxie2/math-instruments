# Nonlinear Jacobian compatibility forces a linear symmetric matrix field to commute

[Family 101](../families/openai-math-101.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000089.md) · [Batch B003](../../batches/B003/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let $X_1,\ldots,X_m$ be real symmetric m-by-m matrices, $\sum_rX_r^2=I$, and $A(x)=\sum_rx_rX_r$. Put $t(a)=-\log\Phi(-a)$ and $q=t'$, where $\Phi$ is the standard Gaussian distribution function. If $F\in W^{1,2}_{loc}(\mathbb R^m;\mathbb R^m)$ has weak Jacobian $DF=q(A)$ almost everywhere, then an orthogonal change of input and output makes F a product of signed exponential quantile maps, plus a translation:

$$U^TF(Uy)=(\sigma_i t(\sigma_i y_i))_{i=1}^m+w,\qquad \sigma_i\in\{-1,1\}.$$

The local algebra is especially portable. Jacobian compatibility requires $(\partial_rq(A))e_s=(\partial_sq(A))e_r$. Since $q'(0)\ne0$ and $q''(0)\ne0$, its constant term gives $X_re_s=X_se_r$. The linear term then cancels its common leftmost-A factors and gives $[X_r,X_s]x=0$. Thus all coefficients commute. After simultaneous orthogonal diagonalization and rotating the input as well, coefficient symmetry leaves only the ith diagonal entry of the ith coefficient. The sum-of-squares normalization makes it a sign.

The commutation part of this proof uses only analyticity near zero and nonzero first and second derivatives; this is a consequence of the displayed coefficient argument, not a claim that every such scalar function has the exponential-law conclusion.

## Role in the source argument

Classifies the linear matrix fields obtained when higher-chaos slack vanishes in the entropy argument. Applied to Gaussian input, the specified q gives independent mean-one exponentials up to signs and orthogonal transformation, with covariance I.

## Antecedents and comparison

Commutation of weak mixed derivatives, analytic functional calculus and simultaneous diagonalization are standard. The source isolates their short combination for this transport field. [Fradelizi–Marín Sola](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07406) provides the surrounding entropy and isotropic-constant context; it is not evidence that this local lemma is new. Targeted searches did not establish priority for the exact formulation.

## Checks and limitations

Checked the cancellation and the input/output rotation explicitly. Exact finite tensor checks compare the linear compatibility residual with the coefficient commutator, including noncommuting coefficient arrays that satisfy the constant-term symmetry.

Without q''(0) != 0, the argument need not force commutation; for an affine q, Hessian fields from cubic potentials provide noncommuting examples. The sum-of-squares identity is needed for the signed rank-one classification, not for the earlier commutation deduction. This lemma alone does not establish the global entropy inequality.

## Possible applications

Equality classification in matrix-valued transport arguments and tests of whether a prescribed nonlinear matrix field can actually be the Jacobian of a map.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
