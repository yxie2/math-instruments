# Turn independent uniform root samples into a mean-square Ward estimate

[Family 219](../families/openai-math-219.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000299.md) · [Batch B054](../../batches/B054/README.md)

## Mathematical content

For a Hermitian n-by-n matrix H, z=E+i eta with eta>0, and G=(H-zI)^(-1), the exact Ward identity is G*G=eta^(-1)Im G. Conditional on H, if v and v' are independent uniform vertices,

E_(v,v') |G_(v,v')|^2 = Im(Tr G/n)/(n eta).

Thus any independent bound Im(Tr G/n)<=C gives C/(n eta). In a regular graph, a uniform root or oriented edge together with uniformly chosen admissible continuation orders gives a uniform marginal at each fixed exploration position. Using positions from two independently sampled explorations supplies the required conditional independence; their images may coincide. Positions within the same exploration need not be independent.

This distinction is substantive. For H=0 and E=0, sampling v'=v gives 1/eta^2, whereas two independent roots give 1/(n eta^2). Uniform marginals alone do not justify the mean-square improvement.

## Role in the source argument

Bounds sampled off-diagonal resolvent expressions in the source exploration argument without excluding collisions between independently sampled roots.

## Antecedents and comparison

The Ward identity is classical resolvent algebra, obtained by subtracting G and G*. The complete source sampling proof was inspected; no novelty is claimed for the identity or for averaging it over independent uniform indices.

## Checks and limitations

Numerical Hermitian resolvents verify the matrix and sampled identities. Exact enumeration of nonbacktracking walks on regular graphs checks uniform fixed-position marginals. A same-root control demonstrates the failure of the independence assumption.

The tests do not supply the required spectral-density bound, a local law or a universality theorem.

## Possible applications

Converting a normalized trace estimate into a conditional mean-square entry estimate for independently sampled local explorations.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
