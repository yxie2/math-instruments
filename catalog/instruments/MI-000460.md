# Smooth an indefinite second-order operator with small projected negative mass

[Family 172](../families/openai-math-172.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000249.md) · [Batch B044](../../batches/B044/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let C be real symmetric, U a d-by-k real matrix, and U C U^T=I_d. For every eta>0 there is a nonnegative compactly supported smooth b of positive integral with integral (D_C b)_- ||Ux||^2 < eta integral b, where D_C=(1/2)sum C_ij partial_i partial_j.

Write H=im U^T, V=ker U. C is positive definite on H. Choose S=epsilon P_H+L P_V with epsilon small and L large enough that S+C>0 by a Schur complement. Then tr(U S U^T) is arbitrarily small, and S+tC stays positive for 0<=t<=1 even if C is indefinite.

For the centered Gaussian density phi_Q, differentiation gives partial_t phi_(S+tC)=D_C phi_(S+tC). Hence b_*=integral_0^1 phi_(S+tC)dt has mass 1 and D_C b_*=phi_(S+C)-phi_S. Its weighted negative mass is at most tr(U S U^T). Smooth cutoff preserves the strict inequality for large radius, because derivatives have uniform Gaussian decay and r -> r_- is 1-Lipschitz.

## Role in the source argument

Produces a signed second derivative whose negative part has arbitrarily small energy in the observed directions.

## Antecedents and comparison

Classical covariance differentiation and Schur complement, arranged to control only the U-projected moment.

## Checks and limitations

Eighty indefinite two-dimensional covariance paths verify positivity and the Gaussian derivative identity numerically.

Finite checks do not verify the cutoff limit, arbitrary-dimensional construction or the source tensor hypothesis.

## Possible applications

Building smooth signed stencils with small negative energy in selected directions.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
