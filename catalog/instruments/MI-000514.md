# Cancel p-orbits to compare coefficient restriction with Brauer projection

[Family 202](../families/openai-math-202.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000288.md) · [Batch B052](../../batches/B052/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let k have characteristic p, X be finite, and Q a p-subgroup. Coefficient restriction Br_Q:(kX)^Q->k C_X(Q) is a unital algebra homomorphism. For the coefficient at g in C_X(Q) of a product, Q conjugates the pairs (a,b) with ab=g. Coefficient products are constant on each orbit; every nonfixed orbit has size divisible by p. Fixed pairs are precisely those with both factors in C_X(Q), proving multiplicativity.

Suppose Q<=H<=N_X(Q) and C_X(Q)<=H. For central v in kX, let s_H(v) restrict coefficients to H and let pi_Q:kH->k[H/Q]. Every coset hQ is Q-conjugation invariant. The same orbit cancellation proves pi_Q(s_H(v))=pi_Q(Br_Q(v)). Both restricted elements are central in kH. Since a normal p-subgroup acts trivially on every simple kH-module, their central-character values agree on every block.

For a split coefficient field, composing a block central character of H with s_H therefore gives the unique residue character of a block of X: equality with Br_Q makes this composition multiplicative even though arbitrary coefficient restriction need not be an algebra map. The normalizer and centralizer hypotheses are part of the statement.

## Role in the source argument

Makes the source block assignment compatible with quotient characters used in its weight transform.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Webb, A Course in Finite Group Representation Theory](https://www-users.cse.umn.edu/~webb/RepBook/RepBookLatex.pdf), Lemma 12.5.3 and Corollary 12.5.4, identify the classical Brauer quotient with fixed-point support and coefficient truncation; the complete passages were read in the author prepublication version, printed pages 245-246. The normalizer quotient identity is checked directly in the source.

## Checks and limitations

Exact small group algebras test multiplicativity on all invariant orbit-basis pairs and compare quotient coefficients for central class sums. A wrong-characteristic example fails multiplicativity.

These checks do not validate the source chain inversion, curve specialization or Alperin weight theorem.

## Possible applications

Replacing a nonmultiplicative coefficient restriction by a multiplicative projection after passing to a normal p-subgroup quotient.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
