# Use matching decompositions to control every fixed-propagation representation

[Family 307](../families/openai-math-307.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000512.md) · [Batch B083](../../batches/B083/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let T have propagation at most R on ell^2(Z) tensor H_0, with compact matrix entries, and put N_R=sup_z |B(z,R)|. Its support is a bipartite graph of maximum degree N_R. Greedy edge coloring partitions it into at most 2N_R-1 matchings. The corresponding T_a each have at most one block in every row and column, so ||T_a||_op<=||T||_op and T_a^*T_a is diagonal.

The algebra of bounded diagonal compact entries is itself a C*-algebra. Every star representation of the algebraic Roe algebra is contractive on it. Therefore ||pi(T_a)||^2<=||T_a^*T_a||_op, and summation gives
||T||_op<=||T||_max<=(2N_R-1)||T||_op.
Applying this to differences transfers operator-norm convergence with ONE common propagation bound to maximal-norm convergence.

On a fixed finite vertex support, the operators already form the compact-operator C*-algebra, so their maximal and concrete norms agree exactly. Their closure is the compact ideal. None of these facts identifies the maximal and reduced norms on the entire completed algebra.

## Checks and limitations

Finite block matrices check greedy matching counts, decomposition, diagonal squares and component norm bounds.

Finite matrices do not compute the universal norm of an infinite Roe algebra. Allowing the propagation bound to grow invalidates the stated convergence inference.
