# Differentiate a minimum area through compactness of the entire minimizer set

[Family 260](../families/openai-math-260.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000502.md) · [Batch B081](../../batches/B081/README.md)

## Mathematical content

If g_j>=c_j g with c_j tending to one and g_j tends to g uniformly on compact sets, lower comparison for every cut and an upper test by a fixed nearly minimizing cut give convergence of minimum areas. For convergence of minimizers themselves, the source additionally assumes uniform filling extensions and confinement in a common compact set. BV compactness, lower semicontinuity and convergence of masses then give strict convergence of the vector normal measures.

To see why continuous tangent-plane integrals converge, approximate the limiting unit normal by a continuous vector field V with |V|<=1. Weak convergence of the vector measures and their total variations bounds the integral of |nu_j-V|^2 by twice the mass minus its pairing with V. This becomes small, and continuous functions of the unoriented tangent plane pass to the limit.

If g_s=g+s h+o(|s|) uniformly on that common compact set, surface area has the uniform expansion P_(g_s)(M)=P_g(M)+s L_h(M)+o(|s|)P_g(M), with L_h(M)=(1/2) integral_M tr_(TM) h dA. Testing a fixed g-minimizer gives the upper right derivative; taking actual g_s-minimizers, extracting a compact limit and using strict convergence gives the lower one. The right derivative of the minimum area is therefore min L_h over ALL g-minimizers. No smooth choice of minimizing surface is required.

## Role in the source argument

Handling changes of minimizing geometry without incorrectly assuming differentiability of the optimizer.

[Rockafellar and Wets, Variational Analysis](https://sites.math.washington.edu/~rtr/papers/rtr169-VarAnalysis-RockWets.pdf), Theorem 10.31, printed page 448 (PDF page 456), was read for the classical active-gradient envelope statement; its proof was not audited in full. The source supplies the perimeter compactness and uniform metric expansion needed for this geometric application.

## Checks and limitations

Finite competing surface planes check the Gram-determinant derivative and the right minimum over active derivatives, including ties and differing left derivatives.

The finite checks cannot establish BV regularity, end confinement or the admissibility of the source minimizing surfaces. Compactness is an assumption with substantive geometric content.
