# Expand coupled time histories as a zero-mass signed perturbation

[Family 224](../families/openai-math-224.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000494.md) · [Batch B079](../../batches/B079/README.md)

## Mathematical content

For labeled times t_1,...,t_n and T>=max|t_i|, define w_t as the integral over s in [-T,T] of the point mass at (1_(s<=t_i))_i, minus T times each constant history. This is independent of T, has total mass zero and variation norm at most 4T. If all 1/2+q t_i lie in [0,1], the history from one shared uniform mark has exactly law P_0+q w_t, where P_0 gives mass one half to each constant history. Moreover ||w_t-w_t'||_TV<=2 sum_i |t_i-t_i'|, using total variation norm of a signed measure.

Multiplying over sites and independent color replicas gives an exact finite subset expansion. Group selected site-replica coordinates by distinct sites, assign a nonempty set I_i of replicas at each selected site, and use degree sum_i |I_i|. A shared site can be selected in both replicas and contributes q^2, not just q. For Poisson sites, the factorial moment formula replaces the existing-site sum by inserted nuclei, with ordered-tuple factor intensity^k/k!. The signed operation changes colors; the insertion is a representation of a sum, not a claim of geometric pivotality.

The geometry cutoff multiplies the observable; it is not conditioning on the cutoff event. Exponential moments of the finite site count justify the fixed-mesh expansion. Uniform mesh limits require separate coefficient bounds.

## Role in the source argument

Organizing signed perturbations without losing common-time or shared-site dependencies.

## Antecedents and comparison

Product expansion and Poisson factorial moment identities are classical. No novelty of those identities is claimed. The source organizes time labels and shared-geometry replicas explicitly.

## Checks and limitations

Exact rational time histories, including ties and negative times, are compared to interval lengths of the uniform mark. A two-site, two-replica test checks the full signed expansion and a control omitting double selections.

Finite algebra does not prove mesh-uniform summability or percolation scaling limits.

## Possible applications

Organizing signed perturbations without losing common-time or shared-site dependencies.
