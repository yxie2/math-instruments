# Capture a hypergraph support by hitting one low-degree vertex

[Family 186](../families/openai-math-186.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000354.md) · [Batch B060](../../batches/B060/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let n>=r>=3, 1<=m<=n, k>0 and L=(k/r)^(r/(r-1)). Assume rL(m-1)/(n-1)<=1/2. For any set S of distinct r-edges with 1<=|S|<=L, a uniform m-subset B of vertices satisfies

Pr(1<=#{e in S:e meets B}<=k)>=m/(2n).

Write s=|S|, V=union S and v=|V|. Since s<=binom(v,r)<=v^r, the average degree is rs/v<=r s^((r-1)/r)<=k. Choose a nonisolated vertex u of degree at most k before sampling B. Conditional on u belonging to B, a union bound gives probability at least 1-(v-1)(m-1)/(n-1)>=1/2 that no other vertex of V belongs to B. On this event the surviving support edges are exactly all those incident with u. The chosen u may depend on S, since the probability inequality is used separately for each nonnegative Fourier summand.

Thus a bound on restricted Fourier degrees through k can detect original support sizes through (k/r)^(r/(r-1)). The source combines this with a separate analytic averaged-restriction bound. That analytic input is not part of this extracted lemma.

## Role in the source argument

Bridges hypergraph vertex degrees and numbers of Fourier support edges.

## Antecedents and comparison

The source explicitly extends the graph companion single-vertex capture argument. The proof uses only average degree and a fixed-size sampling union bound. No claim of an independently new capture principle is made.

## Checks and limitations

Forty supports of 3- and 4-uniform hypergraphs are tested by enumerating every two-vertex block in admissible ambient sizes. The selected vertex degree and capture probability both pass.

The check does not establish the separate low-degree influence estimate, its asymptotic regime or the headline hypergraph bound.

## Possible applications

Transferring estimates for conditionally retained coordinates to larger original combinatorial supports.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
