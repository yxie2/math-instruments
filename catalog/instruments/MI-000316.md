# Normalize the ramified trace before taking an orthogonal quotient

[Family 048](../families/openai-math-048.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000177.md) · [Batch B026](../../batches/B026/README.md)

## Mathematical content

For a local ramified map x=w^e in characteristic zero, normalize the trace pairing by the relative differential:

$$\langle b,c\rangle=\operatorname{Tr}\left(\frac{a(w)bc}{e w^{e-1}}\right),$$

where a is a unit. With a=1, its matrix on 1,w,...,w^(e-1) is the antidiagonal identity: the trace of w^(i+j-e+1)/e is one exactly when i+j=e-1 and zero otherwise. Multiplication by a preserves perfection. This removes the apparent ramification singularity while retaining the correct trace normalization.

In the source rank-four bundle E, self-duality and the two counts h^0(E(-1))=3 and h^0(E(-2))=2 force splitting degrees (3,0,0,-3). The positive line P=O(3) is isotropic and orthogonal to the zero-degree summands. Its orthogonal quotient P-perp/P is a trivial rank-two bundle with a constant nondegenerate form. Global sections modulo H^0(P) identify with every fiber of that quotient.

At an unramified marked point, suppose a local frame tau satisfies tau^2=s(z) dz f^2 while the reference differential is E(z) dz f^2. The trace coefficient of that one-dimensional fiber factor is s(q)/E(q). Coefficient evaluation therefore has squared dual norm E(q)/s(q). If it annihilates P, its metric dual lies in P-perp, and passing to the orthogonal quotient preserves that norm. The reciprocal is essential; it is a dual-form calculation.

## Role in the source argument

Transfers prescribed quadratic data to normalized residue-evaluation functionals used in the first gluing obstruction.

This is a concrete local computation of finite-morphism duality, followed by classical isotropic reduction and the splitting theorem on P1. The source curve and section counts supply the geometric hypotheses; no new duality theorem is claimed.

## Checks and limitations

The computations do not establish existence of the source curve or its section counts, marked-point properties or full principal-parts identification.
