# Descend a semiample numerical representative through Picard isomorphism

[Family 036](../families/openai-math-036.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000416.md) · [Batch B068](../../batches/B068/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let p:W->X be birational with W smooth projective and X normal projective with rational singularities over C. Suppose D is rational Cartier and p*D is numerically equivalent to a semiample rational divisor A_W. After clearing denominators and killing the torsion of the numerically trivial quotient modulo Pic^0, one has O_W(r A_W)=p*O_X(r D) tensor P_W for some P_W in Pic^0(W). Rational singularities give p*:Pic^0(X)->Pic^0(W) an isomorphism, so P_W=p*P_X.

Set A_X=O_X(rD) tensor P_X. Then p*A_X=O_W(r A_W). Since p_*O_W=O_X, projection formula identifies the section spaces of all powers. If a section system generated the pullback but had a base point downstairs, every point above it would be a base point, a contradiction. Hence A_X is semiample and its rational divisor divided by r is numerically equivalent to D. The conclusion concerns a representative of the numerical class; it does not remove the Pic^0 twist.

## Role in the source argument

Transfers numerical semiampleness from a smooth birational model to a rational-singularity base.

## Antecedents and comparison

This combines classical Picard descent for rational singularities, the torsion difference between numerical and algebraic triviality, and connected-fiber descent of sections. No new general descent theorem is claimed.

## Checks and limitations

Exact finite torsion quotient models check denominator/torsion clearing, and pullback evaluation matrices check the base-point implication on fibers.

The models do not establish the geometric Pic^0 isomorphism or rational singularities.

## Possible applications

Keeping numerical equivalence distinct from linear equivalence in birational semiampleness arguments.
