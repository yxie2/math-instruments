# Replace a globular pasting shape by a finite spine of controlled height

[Family 312](../families/openai-math-312.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000220.md) · [Batch B037](../../batches/B037/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Write a finite nonempty globular-sum shape recursively as a vertex or [S_1,...,S_p], a string whose slots are suspensions of smaller shapes. Define T_0(S)=[v(S_1),...,v(S_p)] using the first vertex in each slot, and T_m(S)=[T_{m-1}(S_1),...,T_{m-1}(S_p)] for m>=1; vertices remain fixed.

Then T_m(S) has height at most m+1 and contains every cell through dimension m. The inclusion into S is built from finitely many source inclusions d_j -> d_{j+1} with j>m: building a child from its first vertex and suspending raises every attachment index by one.

Together with low-cell invariance under high attachments, a parallel pair of m-cells in the free model F(S) lifts uniquely to F(T_m(S)). Its arity now has height at most m+1, so the coherator's admissible-filler axiom joins the pair. This proves free-shape contractibility without using weak equivalence of elementary expansions. In R_q F(S), low-dimensional pairs lift to F(S), while higher pairs have their unique filler by definition.

## Role in the source argument

Provides a bounded-height arity on which the exact filler axiom applies.

## Antecedents and comparison

The source cites Lanari 2020 for free-shape contractibility. The finite-spine argument is an explicit implementation of classical globular pasting and suspension.

## Checks and limitations

One hundred recursive shapes at five cutoff levels preserve the low-dimensional cell counts, satisfy the height bound and reconstruct all cell counts from attachments strictly above the cutoff.

Counting checks do not certify all face maps or arbitrary coherator semantics.

## Possible applications

Replacing a large pasting diagram by the part needed to solve a specified-dimensional lifting problem.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
