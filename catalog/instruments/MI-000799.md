# Pass derivatives through a finite-piece map when the parameter rank collapses

[Family 368](../families/openai-math-368.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000526.md) · [Batch B086](../../batches/B086/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let q_j,q_0 be Lipschitz maps on a compact Lipschitz parameter piece D, with images in a common compact subset, q_j->q_0 uniformly, Dq_j->Dq_0 almost everywhere and uniformly bounded derivatives. Let v be Lipschitz near the images and agree on finitely many closed covering pieces C_nu with C1 extensions v_nu. Then D(v composed with q_j)->D(v composed with q_0) in every finite L^r(D), with no rank assumption on Dq_0.

On almost every point of q_j^(-1)(C_nu), locality of weak derivatives gives the product Dv_nu(q_j)Dq_j. Remove the countable union of exceptional sets over j and nu. Every branch containing q_0 has the same tangential product there. Any branch recurring along q_j contains q_0 by closedness, and its continuous derivative therefore gives the correct limiting product. Finiteness of the branch family yields convergence of the entire sequence; the derivative bound gives dominated convergence.

The proof works on the parameter domain. Pulling back an ambient almost-everywhere chain rule would be invalid when the limiting map takes positive parameter measure into a lower-dimensional singular set.

## Role in the source argument

Justifying derivative convergence through line or plane compressions without an unjustified pullback of ambient null sets.

## Antecedents and comparison

This is a finite-piece chain-rule and locality argument, adapted to collapsed strata. The finite closed-piece hypothesis is stronger than ambient Lipschitz regularity alone.

## Checks and limitations

Piecewise absolute-value examples with maps collapsing onto the crease check strong tangential convergence. An oscillatory compression has uniform value convergence but nonconvergent derivatives, demonstrating the need for the derivative hypothesis.

The finite branch structure and derivative convergence cannot be dropped by this proof. No global Sobolev diffeomorphic approximation theorem is certified.
