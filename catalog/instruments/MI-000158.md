# A monotone rank record controls bad primes chosen adaptively

[Family 020](../families/openai-math-020.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000101.md) · [Batch B007](../../batches/B007/README.md)

## Mathematical content

The source's deletion argument has a finite probabilistic core. A subset A of a pool of N items determines an integer record R(A) in [0,R_max], nonincreasing when items are added, and adaptive data D(A). Assume that for nested sets differing by one item, equal records imply equal data. Each fixed data value marks at most b pool items. For positive integers ell,T with ell+T-1<=N, choose t uniformly from ell,...,ell+T-1, then a uniform t-subset A. The expected fraction of its items marked by D(A) is at most

$$R_{\max}/T+\frac b{N-\ell-T+2}.$$

Delete a uniform item q from A. The remainder is a uniform (t-1)-subset; conditional on it, q is uniform in its complement. If F(t)=E R(A) at size t, the probability that deletion changes the record is at most F(t-1)-F(t). Averaging consecutive sizes telescopes to R_max/T. Without conditioning on record stability, the probability q is marked by the deleted data is at most b/(N-t+1). If the record is stable, the data are identical, so a mark for the full data must come from one of these two events.

In the source, each path retains the entire space of bounded-degree polynomials vanishing on a residue class. Adding a prime shrinks the point set and enlarges its vanishing kernel. Until the first changed codimension, nested equal-dimensional kernels coincide, so deterministic subsequent patches coincide. Mixed-radix encoding of their codimensions gives a bounded monotone integer R. A single selected vanishing polynomial would not provide this stability.

The data are nonzero patch minors of size X^O(1). A pool prime is at least X^zeta, so their product has only O(1/zeta) such prime divisors. As the pool grows and the consecutive block sizes remain fixed, the bound becomes B0/T+o(1), uniformly in the point and the other fixed blocks. Paths are defined through all stages, even when an intended dimension drop fails.

## Role in the source argument

Prevents circularity when the variety and its bad reduction primes depend on the same auxiliary primes used to count points.

## Antecedents and comparison

Global determinant methods are developed by [Salberger](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0508326v3) and [Xiao](https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.05587v2). The selected mechanism is the source’s full-kernel stability plus randomized deletion; the general telescoping argument is elementary. Targeted searches did not establish a priority claim.

## Checks and limitations

Read the rank monotonicity, equality-of-kernels implication and conditioning argument. Exact subset enumeration checks the finite deletion bound. A negative control with constant records but changing data shows why stability is necessary.

Polynomial height and nonzero minors are essential for a uniform bounded number of bad large primes. Randomizing over consecutive subset sizes is part of the estimate. The lemma does not itself prove smooth patches, dimension decrease or the final point count.

## Possible applications

Adaptive algebraic counting arguments in which auxiliary primes change the equations whose exceptional reductions must be controlled.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
