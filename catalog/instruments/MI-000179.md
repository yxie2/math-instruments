# Sample/value gluing preserves an expected worst-order guarantee

[Family 111](../families/openai-math-111.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000111.md) · [Batch B010](../../batches/B010/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Fix a finite labeled matroid and a finite seed law R, independent of all weights. A hidden-vector rule chooses a mask B0(R) before seeing values, initially observes the fixed vector w on this mask, rejects its masked elements and processes the remaining labeled arrivals irrevocably and feasibly. Assume, for every nonnegative w,

$$\mathbb E_R\min_\pi \operatorname{reward}(w,R,\pi)\ge c\operatorname{OPT}(w).$$

Let the sample/value pairs (S_e,V_e) have mutually independent coordinates, with S_e and V_e identically distributed for each e; distributions may differ across labels. Define the glued vector W by W_e=S_e on B0(R) and W_e=V_e elsewhere. Conditional on any seed, W has the same product law as V. Consequently W is independent of R, although its definition uses R.

Simulate the hidden rule using only the masked sample coordinates and the arriving unmasked values. Even an arrival order depending on all of S,V,R has reward at least min_pi reward(W,R,pi), pointwise. Integrating gives expected reward at least c E OPT(V). Nonnegative rewards and integrable OPT(V) justify the expectation and integrability; there is no need to assume integrability of loop values separately.

This argument needs E_R min_pi, not min_pi E_R: the latter allows the adversary less information and cannot be substituted. The rule must ignore unused sample coordinates so its decisions really are the stated simulation.

## Role in the source argument

Transfers a deterministic-weight, random-mask guarantee to unknown product distributions without hiding any random seed bits from the adversary.

## Antecedents and comparison

The simulation follows the established [Azar-Kleinberg-Weinberg reduction, Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1307.3736v1). The extracted interface makes the minimum-before-expectation requirement explicit for the fully informed adversary. It is not claimed as a new sample-to-secretary principle.

## Checks and limitations

Finite nonidentical Bernoulli laws check the conditional glued distribution for every mask. A two-seed, two-order reward table checks the strict distinction between expected minima and minimum expectations.

The substantial missing input is a hidden-vector rule satisfying the displayed bound. The simulation alone gives no positive constant for general matroids and no polynomial running-time guarantee.

## Possible applications

Auditing adversary information in sample-based online selection and transferring robust fixed-input analyses to product distributions.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
