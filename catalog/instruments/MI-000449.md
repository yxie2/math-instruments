# Recover atomic positivity from every polynomial-square moment

[Family 280](../families/openai-math-280.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000243.md) · [Batch B043](../../batches/B043/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let Lambda be a countable subset of R bounded below and let complex numbers c_lambda satisfy sum exp(epsilon lambda)|c_lambda|<infinity for some epsilon>0. If sum |p(lambda)|^2 c_lambda is real and nonnegative for every complex polynomial p, then every c_lambda is real and nonnegative.

For the finite complex atomic measure mu with these masses, polynomials are dense in L2(|mu|). To see this, a vector orthogonal to all polynomials gives a finite measure g|mu| with a smaller positive exponential moment. Its Laplace transform is analytic on a strip containing the imaginary axis and all derivatives at zero vanish. Analytic continuation and Fourier uniqueness force this measure to vanish. Approximate the indicator of any atom in L2(|mu|); the squares converge in L1(|mu|), so its mass is a limit of nonnegative real polynomial-square moments.

The source uses this to pass from fixed-radius positivity with polynomial energy insertions to positivity on separate energy grades. The exponential moment is obtained from convergence at a slightly larger radius.

## Role in the source argument

Separates energy grades without presupposing positivity of each summand.

## Checks and limitations

Forty-four exact finite atom-isolation tests include negative and nonreal masses as controls.

Finite Vandermonde interpolation does not test the analytic polynomial-density argument. The source fusion positivity input is not audited here.

## Possible applications

Extracting coefficientwise positivity from generating functions when the coefficients are initially complex.
