# Use commuting forms and right multiplication to extract one monomial ideal

[Family 200](../families/openai-math-200.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000318.md) · [Batch B056](../../batches/B056/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let f_1,...,f_n be a homogeneous regular sequence in C[x_1,...,x_n], of degrees a_i. Suppose k extends C, Delta is a finite central division k-algebra, and a finite commuting collection of degree-one forms in Delta[x], including the central variables x_i, generates a commutative algebra A. Assume A contains t_i satisfying homogeneous identities

t_i^(a_i)=c_i f_i+sum_(l<i) B_il f_l+sum_(d>i) C_id t_d,

with c_i nonzero in k and B_il,C_id in A. Then the monomials t^alpha form a left Delta-basis of Delta[x]. Indeed A is finite over k[x]. The identities force every common geometric zero of the t_i to lie over x=0. The central variables show that Delta[x] is Cohen-Macaulay as an A-module at the graded maximal ideal; the t_i are therefore a regular system of parameters. The quotient by their right multiples has Hilbert series dim_k Delta, concentrated in degree zero. Induction gives spanning by t-monomials, and the homogeneous dimension count gives independence.

For a homogeneous ideal I containing the f_i, take all least exponents of elements of the two-sided ideal Delta I, comparing alpha_n,...,alpha_1 lexicographically. Right multiplication by t_j translates the exponents without moving a left coefficient. Thus the set is upward closed. Division-algebra elimination counts its positions in each degree. In the displayed identity, every C_id t_d has a positive coordinate with index greater than i, so a_i e_i is the least exponent of the resulting element of Delta I. The upward set defines one monomial ideal containing all x_i^(a_i), with the same Hilbert function as I.

The forms commute with each other; their left coefficients need not commute with the forms. Constructing the triangular identities is a separate, unverified input.

## Role in the source argument

Turns the source auxiliary division-coefficient construction into the scalar monomial ideal required by EGH.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Stacks Project, Proposition 10.103.4, Tag 00N6](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00N6) gives the regularity of a parameter sequence on a Cohen-Macaulay module; its statement and proof were read. That classical theorem underlies the source basis criterion. The extraction also uses ordinary elimination over a division ring, with the side of multiplication specified explicitly.

## Checks and limitations

Exact calculations in the rational Hamilton quaternion algebra test commuting noncentral forms, all homogeneous basis dimensions through degree 6, and 56 right exponent shifts. A left coefficient that fails to commute with a form supplies a side-of-multiplication control.

The rational quaternion model tests the algebraic mechanism outside the manuscript complex-function-field setup. It does not establish existence of triangular identities for an arbitrary complex regular sequence.

## Possible applications

Obtaining a consistent exponent ideal from noncommutative coefficient data while avoiding an unjustified interchange of coefficients and variables.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
