# Recover a class and its strong maps from small pairs before uniformizing

[Family 240](../families/openai-math-240.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000340.md) · [Batch B059](../../batches/B059/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Fix an infinite cardinal lambda and a finitary language of size at most lambda. An AEC with LS number at most lambda is determined by its models of size at most lambda together with its strong inclusions between those models, taken up to isomorphism as pairs. Representing small structures on ordinals and pairs by a distinguished subset gives a set of possible codes.

For an actual model M, its small strong submodels form a nonempty directed family covering M. The downward LS axiom gives an upper member for any two, and coherence makes their inclusions strong. Conversely, a directed union of allowed small models with allowed strong inclusions is a model. A strong inclusion M<=N is recovered by a directed presentation of N containing a nonempty directed subfamily with union M. Both membership and the strong relation are therefore definable in set theory from the code.

The required directed-union property follows from the chain axioms by cardinal induction: close smaller index sets under a chosen binary upper-bound operation, form their increasing sequence and apply the induction and chain union clauses. Coherent diagrams of embeddings have a direct limit obtained from tagged elements (i,x), identifying two when they agree in some common later model. Finitarity makes interpretations well-defined, and injectivity is preserved.

If one assumes the qualitative statement that every AEC categorical unboundedly often is categorical on a tail, each valid small code has a definable bound: either beyond all its categoricity cardinals, or at the start of a categorical tail. Replacement bounds these bounds for the set of codes at fixed lambda. A cardinal above all of them is a uniform threshold, with the same threshold in premise and conclusion. This proves an equivalence of formulations; it does not prove the assumed qualitative statement.

## Role in the source argument

Separates set-theoretic uniformity over all AECs from the substantive transfer theorem for one AEC.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Vasey, Part I](https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.07024), Remark 1.3 on page 3, explicitly states the same qualitative/uniform equivalence by a Hanf-number argument; that remark was read. The source spells out the small-pair coding and directed presentations. The equivalence itself is not new.

## Checks and limitations

20 finite coherent diagrams with 764 tagged representatives check equality in a common later model, map coherence, the quotient identification and descent of compatible embeddings.

The finite diagram check does not verify transfinite induction, AEC recovery or Replacement. Those are analytic/set-theoretic arguments inspected in the selected proof, not computationally certified results.

## Possible applications

Turning a class-dependent eventual threshold into a uniform threshold when the relevant structures and maps have set-sized small codes.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
