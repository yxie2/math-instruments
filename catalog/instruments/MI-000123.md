# A tested-set conditioning budget gives an explicit threshold exponent

[Family 277](../families/openai-math-277.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000085.md) · [Batch B002](../../batches/B002/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let $G$ be a finite two-player game with entangled value $v<1$ and answer-alphabet product $d\ge1$. For a joint finite-dimensional strategy on $k$ repetitions, write $I_i$ for its binary wins, $W=\sum_iI_i$, $E_C=\{I_i=1\text{ for }i\in C\}$ and $p_C=\Pr(E_C)$. Fix $0<\delta<1-v$ and $0<T\le\delta/4$.

Assume, uniformly over every repetition length, strategy and proper tested set with $p_C>0$, that

$$\tau(C):=\frac{\log(1/p_C)+|C|\log d}{k-|C|}\le T
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\frac1{k-|C|}\sum_{i\notin C}\Pr(I_i=1\mid E_C)\le v+\delta/4.$$

Then, with natural logarithms,

$$\Pr[W\ge\lceil(v+\delta)k\rceil]\le\exp\!\left(-\frac{\delta T}{16(1+\log d)}k\right)$$

for every $k\ge1$, and for the supremum over finite-dimensional strategies. No attainment of the entangled value is needed.

The sampling step draws indices with replacement, so it makes no independence assumption on the indicators. A large upper tail supplies a short fixed tested set of sufficiently large probability whose conditional remaining win rate is too high. Independent tensor copies remove the lower bound on k without changing the exponent. A conditioning error $B\tau^{1/4}$ permits $T=(\delta/(4B))^4$ and hence a fifth-power threshold exponent.

## Role in the source argument

Separates a probabilistic threshold conversion from the analytic conditioning estimate, allowing improved conditioning bounds to be reused without repeating the sampling argument.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Rao, Section 8](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~anuprao/pubs/parrep.pdf) already converts tested-coordinate control into classical concentration; [Yuen](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.04340v1) uses sampling with replacement; [Bavarian--Vidick--Yuen](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.07466) proves threshold amplification for anchored games. [Song, TR26-199](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/199/) supplies the conditioning input used here. The card isolates the explicit budget-to-rate formula and all-length step; it does not attribute tested-coordinate sampling itself as new.

## Checks and limitations

Checked the fixed-set selection argument, the constants in c/r and c(1+R/r), the proper-subset condition at large k, and N ceil(qk) >= ceil(qNk) in the tensor-copy step.

The conditioning hypothesis must hold uniformly for all lengths and all admitted strategies, not just one fixed strategy. An all-wins theorem is not by itself the required threshold estimate. The supplied Comparator uses delta^13; it is not evidence that the stronger delta^5 statement was kernel-checked in this batch.

## Possible applications

Potential use: convert a different, independently proved conditioning modulus into a threshold concentration rate, while retaining its parameter dependence.
