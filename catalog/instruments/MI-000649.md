# Cancel canonical tails to isolate the zero Hilbert frequency

[Family 037](../families/openai-math-037.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000418.md) · [Batch B068](../../batches/B068/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let T be a positively graded normal Cohen-Macaulay domain of dimension n with T_0=C, and write its degrees as lambda times integers. Suppose every stabilizer order m away from the vertex satisfies lambda m<1. If H_T(s)=sum dim(T_y) exp(-sy) has polar part sum_j a_j j!/s^(j+1), set P_T(y)=sum_j a_j y^j. Form the signed measure mu_T=sum_y dim(T_y) delta_y+(-1)^(n-1) sum_y dim((omega_T)_y) delta_-y. For every Schwartz F with smooth Fourier transform compactly supported in (-1,1), integral F dmu_T=integral F(y)P_T(y)dy.

Canonical duality H_omega(z)=(-1)^n H_T(z^-1) turns the two tails into the difference of Laurent expansions at zero and infinity. Partial fractions give polynomially weighted root-of-unity lattice combs; any Laurent polynomial part cancels. A root of unity of order d can be a Hilbert pole only if it fixes a nonvertex point, so d divides some stabilizer order m. Its nonzero frequencies have absolute value at least 1/(lambda d)>1. Poisson summation therefore removes every nonzero frequency in the allowed band. The remaining polynomial density is exactly the polar part at s=0.

## Role in the source argument

Replaces a signed graded-dimension sum by an exact polynomial integral for suitably band-limited test functions.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Stanley, Hilbert functions of graded algebras](https://math.mit.edu/~rstan/pubs/pubfiles/30.pdf), Theorem 6.1(ii), printed page 77, states the multigraded canonical-module identity; that statement and its proof reduction were read. Rational Hilbert series and Poisson summation are classical. Priority for the complete stabilizer/bandwidth formulation was not established.

## Checks and limitations

Exact polynomial and weighted-polynomial rings check canonical negative coefficients, root-of-unity terms, polar densities and the frequency exclusion inequality.

The checks cover the algebraic and frequency steps, not arbitrary graded domains, infinite distribution convergence or the fourfold volume gap.

## Possible applications

Constructing exact low-frequency trace identities from graded algebra and stabilizer bounds.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
