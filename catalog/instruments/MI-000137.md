# Scale-integrated covariance maps are Lipschitz in Hilbert–Schmidt distance

[Family 093](../families/openai-math-093.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000090.md) · [Batch B004](../../batches/B004/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Fix $L\ge1$, define $f_L(q)=q/(1+q/L)$ for q>=0, and set $h_T(v)=\sqrt{1+f_L(Tv^2)}$. For arbitrary positive semidefinite matrices A and B of the same size, including noncommuting and singular ones,

$$\int_0^\infty\|h_T(A)-h_T(B)\|_F^2\frac{dT}{T^2}
\le K_L\|A-B\|_F^2,$$

where

$$K_L=\int_0^\infty\frac{dq}{(1+q/L)^3(1+(1+1/L)q)},\qquad0<K_L\le\log(1+L).$$

For v>0, differentiating and substituting q=Tv^2 gives exactly $\int_0^\infty|h_T'(v)|^2dT/T^2=K_L$, independent of v. The fundamental theorem of calculus, Cauchy–Schwarz and Tonelli give the scalar Lipschitz bound, also at zero. Using separate eigenbases for A and B turns it into the matrix estimate by summing scalar bounds weighted by squared eigenvector overlaps. Simultaneous diagonalization is unnecessary.

## Role in the source argument

Controls Gaussian covariance observations across every scale, allowing replacement of a square-root matrix field by its limiting model without losing contributions from small spectral blocks. Coupling with the same Gaussian noise makes the mean squared observation difference exactly the matrix Frobenius difference.

## Antecedents and comparison

The eigenbasis argument is the standard Frobenius functional-calculus mechanism, also visible in the source of [MI-000133](MI-000133.md). The scale-integrated derivative identity for this bounded covariance map is the particular extracted estimate. Targeted comparison has not established its historical novelty.

## Checks and limitations

Differentiated h_T, checked the scale substitution and read the separate-eigenbasis proof. Numerical quadrature tests singular and noncommuting positive matrices against K_L and the logarithmic upper bound.

The result is in Hilbert–Schmidt norm, not an arbitrary operator norm. L is held fixed when applying a convergence argument. Numerical quadrature is not a proof of the universal inequality; that follows from the displayed scalar calculation and eigenbasis reduction.

## Possible applications

Comparing matrix-valued observations uniformly over scales and preserving trace mass when the spectrum becomes diffuse.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
