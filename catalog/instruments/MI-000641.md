# Descend a globally generated power through the finite Stein norm

[Family 033](../families/openai-math-033.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000410.md) · [Batch B067](../../batches/B067/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let r:P->S be proper surjective between connected compact complex manifolds, and A a holomorphic line bundle on S. In the Stein factorization P->T->S, let the finite map q have generic degree d. If r*(A^n) is globally generated, then A^(nd) is globally generated.

Connected-fiber pushforward identifies upstairs sections with sections of q*(A^n), which remain globally generated. On the covering locus of q, define the norm of a function as its product over d sheets. Properness bounds this product near the omitted analytic subset; normality of S extends it holomorphically. If the function is nonzero on an entire fiber, apply the same argument to its inverse to see that the extended norm is nonzero there. Norm(q*c)=c^d, so norms of locally framed sections glue to sections of A^(nd).

For each base point, choose a section nonzero at every point of the finite fiber: each vanishing condition is a proper hyperplane, and finitely many such hyperplanes cannot exhaust the complex section space. Its norm is nonzero at the base point. The exponent nd is independent of the point. This handles branching and possible nonflatness without assuming that q_*O_T is locally free. Rational bundles are treated after clearing denominators.

## Role in the source argument

Returns semiampleness from auxiliary proper parameter spaces to the original compact base.

## Checks and limitations

Ramified cyclic-cover norms check multiplicativity, the power on pullbacks and nonvanishing across a branch point. Exact finite hyperplane tests select sections simultaneously nonzero on a fiber.

Finite flat cyclic examples do not test the analytic extension theorem for nonflat maps; that step rests on the inspected properness and normality proof.

## Possible applications

Descending a uniform basepoint-free exponent from a proper cover with positive-dimensional fibers.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
