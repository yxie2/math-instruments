# Dual cotype gives bounded sign-indexed lifts with prescribed moments

[Family 326](../families/openai-math-326.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000104.md) · [Batch B008](../../batches/B008/README.md)

## Mathematical content

For an integer m>=1, define c_m(Y) by

$$\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^m\|y_i\|^2\Bigr)^{1/2}\le c_m(Y)\Bigl(\mathbb E_\epsilon\|\sum_i\epsilon_i y_i\|^2\Bigr)^{1/2}.$$

If c_m(G*)<=D_g and c_m(F)<=D_f, with D_g,D_f>=1, set $a=(2LD_g\sqrt m)^{-1}$, $b=(2LD_f\sqrt m)^{-1}$, where the source uses the sufficient Kahane constant L=24. For any x_i in the unit ball of G there are P_epsilon in that ball with $\mathbb E\epsilon_iP_\epsilon=ax_i$. For any y_j in the unit ball of F* there are Z_delta in that ball with $\mathbb E\delta_jZ_\delta=by_j$.

The first moment map $U:L_\infty(\{-1,1\}^m;G)\to\ell_2^m(G)$ is onto, with right inverse $(x_i)\mapsto\sum\epsilon_i x_i$. Its adjoint is the Rademacher sum in L1(G*). Cotype and Kahane give $\|U^*z^*\|\ge(LD_g)^{-1}\|z^*\|_2$. Quotient duality bounds the infimum lift norm by LD_g times the tuple norm. The factor two leaves slack, so no attainment of the infimum is assumed.

For the dual-valued lift, the map $V:(w_j)\mapsto\sum\delta_jw_j$ from ell2^m(F) to L1(F) is bounded below by (L D_f)^-1. The functional $Vw\mapsto b\sum y_j(w_j)$ has norm at most one half. Hahn-Banach extends it, and finite probability-space duality represents the extension by Z in L_infinity(F*). No general vector-valued L1 duality on a nonatomic space is needed.

The same estimates hold in finite L2 sums because the fixed-length cotype inequality can be squared and averaged. If Y has cotype q, then $c_m(Y)\le C_q(Y)m^{1/2-1/q}$.

## Role in the source argument

Turns vector tuples into uniformly bounded sign-indexed inputs while preserving the first moments needed by the source’s Walsh coefficient argument.

## Antecedents and comparison

The ingredients are classical moment comparison and duality, covered in [Pisier’s course notes](https://webusers.imj-prg.fr/~gilles.pisier/ihp-pisier.pdf). The source gives explicit sufficient constants and separates the primal quotient lift from the dual Hahn-Banach lift. No new Kahane inequality is claimed.

## Checks and limitations

Read the stopping-time moment comparison and both lift constructions. Exhaustive small sign cubes check moments and norm bounds for explicit Hilbert-space lifts; these examples do not solve the Banach-space optimization problem in general.

The primal lift requires cotype information about G*, not G. The assertion is existential; a linear selection of lifts or an efficient algorithm is not supplied. The fixed-length bound must be checked before using the constants.

## Possible applications

Replacing finite vector tuples by bounded random-sign data with exact first moments in Fourier, tensor or operator estimates.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
