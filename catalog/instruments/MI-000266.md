# Orthogonal fixed-density interpolation preserves nonconstancy at an exact equality

[Family 161](../families/openai-math-161.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000152.md) · [Batch B020](../../batches/B020/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Suppose H contains a cycle, has m edges, and a finite simple graph G supplies t(H,G)<p^m at p=p(G) in (0,1). Let W0 be its adjacency kernel. Independently take balanced signs a,b and W1(a,b)=p+delta ab, where 0<delta<=min(p,1-p).

The sign expansion gives t(H,W1)=sum_A p^(m-|A|) delta^|A|, summed over edge subsets A whose degrees are all even. The empty subset contributes p^m, and a cycle contributes a positive extra term. Hence t(H,W1)>p^m.

On the product of the two underlying spaces set Ws((x,a),(y,b))=(1-s)W0(x,y)+s W1(a,b). Every Ws has mean p and values in [0,1]. The centered summands depend on independent coordinates, so

$$\|W_s-p\|_2^2=(1-s)^2p(1-p)+s^2\delta^2>0.$$

The polynomial t(H,Ws) crosses p^m at an interior point s*, while this norm identity rules out a constant kernel there. In a finite probability space with positive atom masses, two Cauchy-Schwarz inequalities give t(C4,W)>=p^4, with equality forcing constant row means and constant row inner products. The diagonal pairs then force every row to equal p. Thus the nonconstant W at s* has t(C4,W)>p^4.

Sampling W gives expectations within O(1/n) of the K2, H and C4 densities and variances O(1/n), since disjoint vertex images use independent random variables. Chebyshev and a union bound select finite graphs whose edge and H densities approach p and p^m, but whose C4 densities approach a strictly larger value than p^4. This is a conditional failure of the forcing conclusion at one density.

## Role in the source argument

Shows how the same strict density deficit would yield a nonquasirandom equality example, without cancellation to a constant kernel.

## Antecedents and comparison

The ingredients are standard sign expansion, interpolation and [finite-kernel sampling](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0408173). The source assembles them as a consequence of its claimed deficit. No independent priority claim is made for this consequence.

## Checks and limitations

Read the complete proof. Exact even-subgraph expansions are checked for small cyclic and acyclic patterns. A triangle model supplies a numerical crossing, verifies the orthogonal norm identity and exhibits a positive C4 excess.

The small triangle model does not establish the source bipartite counterexample. A cycle is required for the strict upper endpoint. The finite graphs attain equality only asymptotically, and the argument does not work at every prescribed p.

## Possible applications

Constructing exact moment equalities that retain a measurable obstruction to uniformity.
