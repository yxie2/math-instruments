# Prescribe the mean of a loop by a smoothly varying positive density

[Family 333](../families/openai-math-333.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000187.md) · [Batch B029](../../batches/B029/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let p_b(t) be a smooth finite-dimensional family of circle-valued periodic paths in R2 and let c_b lie in the interior of the convex hull of each path, uniformly over a compact parameter set. There are smooth strictly positive densities rho_b with average rho_b=1 and average rho_b p_b=c_b. They can be patched to prescribed positive solutions on an overlapping parameter neighborhood.

At one parameter, select finitely many path points whose convex hull contains c in its interior. Replace a positive barycentric combination by narrow smooth bumps and add a small positive constant density. The mass and two moment constraints have rank three. A fixed linear right inverse adjusts their coefficients under small parameter changes; sufficiently small changes preserve positivity. A finite cover and partition of unity patch the local densities because all three constraints are linear.

The change of periodic coordinate theta=int_0^t rho_b(s) ds is an orientation-preserving circle diffeomorphism. Reparametrizing the path by its inverse turns the prescribed weighted mean into the ordinary theta-mean. Strict positivity is needed for this smooth invertible change of parameter.

## Role in the source argument

Makes the leading velocity loop have the desired mean while preserving previously arranged collar data.

## Antecedents and comparison

This is classical convex averaging and partition-of-unity patching with an explicit finite-rank constraint system. It is not a new general moment theorem.

## Checks and limitations

Ninety-six positive full-circle density and convex-patching models preserve mass and both first moments; the maximum checked mean error is below 1.6e-14.

The full-circle Poisson-kernel models do not verify every varying path or the source global geometry.

## Possible applications

Smooth parameter-dependent reparametrization under prescribed first moments and relative boundary data.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
