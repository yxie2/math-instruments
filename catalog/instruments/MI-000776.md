# Correct two tree test vectors with the same gate values

[Family 331](../families/openai-math-331.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000515.md) · [Batch B084](../../batches/B084/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let rho be the supremum of the square root of the sum of squared segment sums over disjoint segments in the source forest, and X the dual completion. If H is finite ancestral, x=P_H x and y=Q_H y, then (||x+y||+||x-y||)/2 >= sqrt(||x||^2+||y||^2/12).

For primal norming vectors f on H and k outside H, both of rho-norm at most one, let m_g be the maximal absolute initial-segment sum in the cone starting at gate g. Then sum m_g^2<=1. At each parent choose the shortest suffix with absolute f-sum at least 2s m_g, if it exists. Give c_g=m_g the opposite sign of that suffix; otherwise choose either fixed sign. Set v_+=c+k and v_-=c-k.

Cone-initial sums of v_+ and v_- share the sign of c and have magnitude at most 2m_g. In a disjoint-segment test, small head-tail cross terms cost at most 8s^2 sum m_g^2. A large positive cross term is absorbed by splitting off the selected suffix, which has the opposite sign. Pure tail squares cost at most 4s^2. Thus rho(f+s v_+)^2 and rho(f+s v_-)^2 are at most 1+12s^2.

Pair these vectors with x+y and x-y. The common correction c cancels on averaging; optimizing s gives the stated bound. Finite support density passes it to the completed dual space.

## Role in the source argument

Obtaining a two-sided dual-norm estimate even when a one-sided tail perturbation can cancel the head.

## Antecedents and comparison

The segment norm and head-tail strategy are classical ingredients. Exact priority of this shared-correction formulation and constant is not established.

## Checks and limitations

Finite binary trees enumerate all disjoint-segment families by subset dynamic programming and check the corrected primal test-vector bounds for several ancestral sets, random inputs and scales.

These checks test the primal correction, not an optimization of the infinite-dimensional dual norm. Reflexivity and the separate renorming obstruction are not numerically certified.

## Possible applications

Obtaining a two-sided dual-norm estimate even when a one-sided tail perturbation can cancel the head.
