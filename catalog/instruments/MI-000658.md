# Orthogonalize parallel summands without changing the metric completion

[Family 052](../families/openai-math-052.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000427.md) · [Batch B069](../../batches/B069/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Suppose a connected Kahler manifold (X0,g) has parallel holomorphic complementary projectors Q1,Q2. Define h(v,w)=g(Q1v,Q1w)+g(Q2v,Q2w). Then h is positive, makes the splitting orthogonal, and is parallel for the same torsion-free connection. That connection is therefore its Levi-Civita connection and preserves the complex structure, so h is Kahler. The representing positive self-adjoint endomorphism is parallel, hence its eigenvalues are constant and ag<=h<=bg for global a,b>0.

In local product coordinates, complementary vector fields V,W commute. The equal vectors nabla_V W=nabla_W V lie in the two disjoint summands, so both vanish. Metric compatibility then makes each block depend only on its own coordinates. Intrinsic distances are uniformly bi-Lipschitz, so their completions are canonically bi-Lipschitz homeomorphic. Given a continuous contraction f:X->Z identifying the completion, d(x,y)=rho(f(x),f(y)) is a continuous pseudodistance with zero classes exactly the fibers. If g is real analytic and the projectors holomorphic, h is real analytic as well.

## Role in the source argument

Makes supplied parallel factors orthogonal while preserving the collapsed space needed for continuation arguments.

## Antecedents and comparison

This is standard parallel-tensor orthogonalization, local Riemannian product geometry and completion invariance under uniform metric comparison. The source applies it to a canonical contraction.

## Checks and limitations

Exact oblique projector models verify positivity and orthogonality; numerical eigenvalue bounds compare distances and a finite quotient model checks the pseudometric zero classes.

The models do not establish the source parallelism theorem or identify a canonical metric completion.

## Possible applications

Changing the metric to match a supplied splitting while retaining its global completion.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
