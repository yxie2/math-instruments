# Certify a Hermitian determinant optimum by finitely many contact directions

[Family 338](../families/openai-math-338.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000223.md) · [Batch B038](../../batches/B038/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let v(x,xi) be lower semicontinuous, homogeneous of degree two in a complex fiber, and bounded between locally continuous positive quadratic forms b_0 and b_1. Maximize det D over positive Hermitian matrices with xi*D xi<=v(x,xi) for all xi. The feasible semidefinite closure is compact and contains b_0, so a maximizer is positive definite. Strict concavity of log det makes it unique.

The optimal logarithmic value is lower semicontinuous: for a feasible D at x_0, (1-epsilon)D has a uniform positive margin on the compact unit sphere, and lower semicontinuity preserves that margin nearby. This argument does not assert continuity of D itself.

Normalize the optimum to I. Its contact set E={unit xi:v(x,xi)=1} is compact. If a Hermitian H is strictly negative on E, then I+epsilon H is feasible for small positive epsilon, so tr H<=0. Separating I from the closed cone generated by xi xi* for xi in E would give a contradictory H. Therefore

$$I=\sum_i\alpha_i\xi_i\xi_i^*,\qquad \alpha_i>0,\quad \sum_i\alpha_i=n.$$

Conversely this resolution bounds tr D<=n for every feasible D and certifies det D<=1. Repeating each contact divides its weight without changing the certificate.

## Role in the source argument

Converts a fiberwise extremum into finitely many weighted contact tests.

## Checks and limitations

Sixteen independent finite contact-constrained log-determinant optimizations recover I with maximum matrix error below 1.2e-7; the contact resolutions are checked.

The tests use finite constraints and do not construct the source disc envelope or certify its PDE comparison.

## Possible applications

Replacing an infinite family of quadratic constraints by an optimality certificate supported on finitely many directions.
