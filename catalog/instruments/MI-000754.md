# Control a Lorentz change of basis with a bounded unit timelike vector

[Family 264](../families/openai-math-264.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000504.md) · [Batch B081](../../batches/B081/README.md)

## Mathematical content

For a Lorentz Gram matrix G of signature (-,+,+,+) and a vector U with U^T G U=-1, define K(G,U)=G+2(GU)(GU)^T. Decomposing a vector as aU+Z with Z Lorentz-orthogonal to U gives K(aU+Z,aU+Z)=a^2+G(Z,Z)>0. The matrix determinant lemma gives det K=-det G.

Bounds on G,G^(-1),U give upper bounds on K and a positive determinant lower bound, hence a lower bound on its smallest eigenvalue. If G=M^T g_c M and U_c=M U_F, then K(G,U_F)=M^T K(g_c,U_c)M. Uniform bounds on both metrics, their inverses and both vector representations therefore bound M and M^(-1).

Bounded Lorentz Gram matrices alone are insufficient: a Lorentz boost preserves the Minkowski matrix while its Euclidean operator norm grows exponentially with rapidity. The bounded timelike vector excludes precisely that escape.

## Role in the source argument

Preventing unbounded boosts when comparing a geometric frame to a coordinate frame.

## Antecedents and comparison

This is the standard positive metric associated with a chosen timelike direction, used here to make the frame-change hypothesis quantitative. No new Lorentz algebra identity is claimed.

## Checks and limitations

Random congruences check positive definiteness, determinant and frame transformation. Large boosts with fixed Gram matrices show why the extra vector hypothesis is necessary.

The finite algebra does not provide the source spacetime estimates, holonomy limits or curvature blow-up.
