# Separated displacement scales keep unbounded sheet multiplicity uniformly doubling

[Family 098](../families/openai-math-098.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000088.md) · [Batch B003](../../batches/B003/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Take $r_j=1000^{-j}$ and pairs of positive integers $(N_j,W_j)$ in which every pair recurs infinitely often. Above a base point in $\mathbb R^2$, level $j$ permits either zero or $r_je_{j,i}$ in mutually orthogonal directions. Label $i$ is allowed on the union of its periodically colored horizontal strips of width $r_j$ and vertical strips of width $W_jr_j$. Only finitely many levels are nonzero at each point.

Despite unbounded $N_j,W_j$, the resulting Hilbert subset is 76800-doubling. In a ball of radius $t$, levels with $r_j>t$ are fixed. At most one level lies in $(t/64,t]$, where the base projection encounters fewer than 300 labels. The remaining displacement tail has squared diameter at most

$$2\sum_{r_j\le t/64}r_j^2\le\frac{2(t/64)^2}{1-10^{-6}}<(t/32)^2.$$

Partition the base square of side $2t$ into $16^2$ cells of side $t/8$, and group points by cell and intermediate label. Every group has diameter below $\sqrt{33}\,t/32<t/2$. Selecting a center from each nonempty group gives at most $300\cdot16^2$ intrinsic half-radius balls.

## Role in the source argument

Makes room for arbitrarily many recurring branch labels while keeping metric covering complexity uniformly bounded at every radius.

## Antecedents and comparison

Multiscale doubling constructions have substantial antecedents, including [Lafforgue–Naor’s Lp obstruction](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~naor/homepage%20files/Lp-doubling.pdf). The particular union of horizontal and vertical permissions is important here because it supplies common sheets at later crossings. The covering argument uses elementary geometric-series and cell counts.

## Checks and limitations

Checked coarse-coordinate rigidity, the single-intermediate-level claim, strip counts and the exact rational tail and diameter bounds. All centers lie in the constructed subset.

This covering estimate alone is no obstruction to finite-dimensional embedding. Recurrence and the shared horizontal/vertical sheet domains are needed by the later derivative argument. The displayed space is not the compact Banach-space example.

## Possible applications

Constructing metric spaces with large combinatorial complexity but bounded local covering counts, provided fine-scale tails and the number of active intermediate labels can both be controlled.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
