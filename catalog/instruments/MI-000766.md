# Slice a simplex naturally while limiting the number of nonvertex outputs

[Family 302](../families/openai-math-302.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000510.md) · [Batch B083](../../batches/B083/README.md)

## Mathematical content

For x=(x_0,...,x_j) in the probability simplex put s_a=sum_(b<a)x_b. Divide [0,1] into ell equal slices I_r. Define rho_r(x)_a as ell times the length of [s_a,s_(a+1)] intersect I_r. Each rho_r is a continuous simplex-valued map.

A monotone map between vertex sets merges consecutive mass intervals, allowing empty fibers. Intersection length with a fixed slice is additive under such merging, so pushing forward x commutes with every rho_r, including for degeneracies. Thus the construction is natural in the simplex category.

A nonvertex rho_r requires an internal break s_a strictly inside I_r. Distinct nonvertex slices require distinct breaks; there are only j. Breaks at slice endpoints and zero coordinates introduce no extra nonvertex values. Consequently, among any n>j selected factors at least one receives a vertex. The source uses this counting fact to organize compatible nullhomotopy parameters, but that further stable construction is not part of the extracted guarantee.

## Role in the source argument

Distributing homotopy parameters among many factors with a deterministic bound on how many remain away from vertices.

## Antecedents and comparison

This is an explicit cumulative-mass interval construction. A targeted search located the source formulation but did not establish its exact priority; absence of an independently located formula is not evidence of novelty.

## Checks and limitations

Exact rational simplex grids check normalization, the at-most-j bound and naturality under all small monotone maps. An arbitrary vertex permutation supplies a control showing why monotonicity matters.

The finite checks do not prove the compatible Adams lift or quantitative nilpotence theorem. Naturality under arbitrary vertex permutations is not asserted.
