# Add an explicit correction when moving a quadratic-envelope center

[Family 377](../families/openai-math-377.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000186.md) · [Batch B028](../../batches/B028/README.md)

## Mathematical content

For continuous f(x,t) with subquadratic spatial growth uniformly on compact time intervals, define P_l f(x,t)=sup_y[f(y,t+l)-|y-x|^2/(2l)] and M_l f(x,t)=inf_y[f(y,t-l)+|y-x|^2/(2l)]. The quadratic cost confines optimizers, gives continuity including l=0, and yields P_l P_h=P_{l+h}, with the analogous infimum identity.

Suppose f is a viscosity subsolution of F[f]=D2f[(grad_x f,1),(grad_x f,1)]=0, using F>=0 for upper tests. For an affine center path x(l)=x_0+d l,

$$l\longmapsto P_l f(x_0+dl,t)+|d|^2l\log l$$

is convex, with l log l=0 at zero. If psi touches the envelope from above at l_0 and y_0 is an optimizer, Psi(y,l)=psi(l)+|y-x(l)|^2/(2l) touches f(y,t+l) from above. Put q=(y_0-x(l_0))/l_0. The Hessian of the cost in direction (q,1) equals |d|^2/l_0, so psi''+|d|^2/l_0>=0. Since (l log l)''=1/l, the correction supplies convexity.

At a fixed center the correction vanishes. Time reversal and sign change give concavity of the lower envelope for supersolutions. Uniform subquadratic control also keeps optimizers in one compact set under local uniform convergence, permitting convergence of the envelopes.

## Role in the source argument

Controls changes in envelope slopes and the cost of shifting centers in the source propagation argument.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Evans-Savin, Sections 2.4 and 3.1](https://www.math.columbia.edu/~savin/evans-savin.pdf) contain the same limiting PDE and quadratic slice comparisons. The source explicitly tracks the moving-center Hessian through the logarithmic correction; this targeted comparison does not establish first priority.

## Checks and limitations

The cost semigroup and moving-center directional Hessian are symbolic identities. One hundred exact piecewise-affine supremum-envelope models satisfy composition.

The script checks the algebra of the viscosity test, not the universal viscosity argument, contact propagation or the source Liouville theorem.

## Possible applications

Quantitative envelope comparisons along moving paths when fixed-center convexity alone does not control travel.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
