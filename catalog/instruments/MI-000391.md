# Bound surviving upward-closed injections by a shared boundary slot

[Family 124](../families/openai-math-124.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000214.md) · [Batch B035](../../batches/B035/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Suppose a recursive interval has at most two inherited initial list entries, and new entries are upward-closed injections. Assume the injections are pairwise disjoint on the current parent W. Every injection surviving pruning on a child W' contains a job x in W', and every such high job has a strict successor z in a fixed boundary slot b' contained in W.

Upward closure puts z in the same injection. Disjointness on W forces distinct surviving injections to use distinct members of b'. Therefore at most |b'| injections survive. In the source three-machine construction |b'|=3, giving at most five pruned entries in total.

The injections may overlap outside W; disjointness is needed only where the boundary witnesses lie. The bound is for the pruned list or its carry. Appending the next injection may temporarily create a sixth entry before pruning. Establishing the global upset and successor invariants is a separate input.

## Role in the source argument

Limits the amount of inherited list information in one same-direction run of the scheduling construction.

## Antecedents and comparison

The counting step is an elementary injection into a finite boundary set. It is a conditional compression certificate, not a new scheduling algorithm by itself.

## Checks and limitations

All 2,426 tested boundary-witness families verify that four pairwise disjoint nonempty witness sets cannot fit in a three-element boundary.

The source global boundary and change-of-direction arguments were not audited, so the full five-entry invariant across the entire hierarchy is not independently certified.

## Possible applications

Bounding the number of surviving disjoint monotone predicates using a small shared boundary.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
