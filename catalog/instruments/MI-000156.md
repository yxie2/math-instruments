# A weighted affine family has small norm on mean-zero functions

[Family 013](../families/openai-math-013.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000100.md) · [Batch B007](../../batches/B007/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let q be an odd prime, use probability L2 on F_q, and let G(0)=0 with ||G||_2<=1. Define

$$T_GF(t)=q^{-1}\sum_{r\ne0}\overline{G(r)}F(r-r^2t).$$

On the mean-zero subspace,

$$\|T_G\|_{2\to2}^2\le\sqrt3\,q^{-1/2},\qquad \|T_GG\|_2^2\le|\mathbb EG|^4+\sqrt3\,q^{-1/2}.$$

Each $U_rF(t)=F(r-r^2t)$ is unitary. In $T_G^*T_G$, $U_r^*U_s$ acts by $t\mapsto(s/r)^2t+s-s^2/r$. Every nonidentity affine map has at most two ordered representations: its slope determines s/r up to two possibilities, and its translation then determines r. The identity coefficient is at most 1/q. Thus its affine coefficients c(a,b) satisfy $\sum_{a,b}|c(a,b)|^2\le3/q^2$. In the nonzero additive Fourier basis the squared Hilbert-Schmidt norm is at most $q\sum|c|^2$, proving the estimate. Constants and mean-zero functions are preserved separately, and $T_G1=\overline{\mathbb EG}$.

The source applies this to bottom-pair Mellin coefficients. If g(0)=0, ||g||_2<=1 and $\epsilon=\max_{\chi,a}|\mathbb E_xg(x)\chi(x)e_q(ax)|$, set

$$P_\chi(d)=\frac q{q-1}\mathbb E_x(g\overline\chi)(x)\overline{(g\overline\chi)(x-d)}.$$

Then $\sum_\chi|\widehat{P_\chi\chi\eta}(a)|^2\le\frac q{q-1}(\epsilon^4+\sqrt3q^{-1/2})$ for every eta,a. Multiplicative characters, including the principal character, vanish at zero. The autocorrelation value P_chi(0) is retained before the multiplicative twist sets it to zero. Inversion and the parametrization $1/x=r-tr^2$ reduce this assertion to the operator bound.

## Role in the source argument

Controls mixed Fourier-Mellin terms in the source’s quartet analysis without a pointwise bound on g.

## Antecedents and comparison

The source explicitly places the estimate beside [Gowers](https://arxiv.org/abs/0710.3877v1) and [Gill](https://doi.org/10.1017/fms.2016.8). The representation-count calculation is a concrete weighted instance of established mixing principles; no claim of a new general quasirandomness theorem is made.

## Checks and limitations

Checked the affine composition, the exceptional identity fiber and Fourier normalization. Finite-field matrix computations test the restricted operator norm, the affine fiber multiplicities and the Mellin inequality, including functions concentrated at one nonzero point.

This lemma alone does not prove the tree comparison or the arithmetic prime-sumset conclusion. The q^-1/2 bound is for the squared operator norm. Dropping the zero convention for the principal character changes the Mellin formula.

## Possible applications

Weighted finite-field incidence and correlation estimates where the function is controlled in L2 but can have large pointwise values.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
