# Regularize a logarithmic Green singularity through a positive-semidefinite defect

[Family 369](../families/openai-math-369.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000180.md) · [Batch B027](../../batches/B027/README.md)

## Mathematical content

For disk points put h(x,y)=|x-y|^2/((1-|x|^2)(1-|y|^2)), so G=(4 pi)^{-1}log(1+1/h). Set L(h)=1/log(1+1/h), L(0)=0, and f(h)=h+1/2-L(h).

The kernel h+1/2 is congruent to (1/2)(1+|x|^2)(1+|y|^2)-2 x.y, hence has at most one positive eigenvalue on any finite set. Meanwhile f(h(x,y)) is positive semidefinite. To see the latter, express L as the average of h^{1-alpha}(h+1)^alpha, 0<alpha<1. A Stieltjes representation with r=1/(1+v), where v has density sin(pi alpha) v^{alpha-1}/(pi(1+v)), gives

$$f(h)=\int_0^1\mathbb E_\alpha\frac{r(1-r)}{h+r}\,d\alpha.$$

Here E(1-r)=alpha. The kernel k=1/(1+h) is positive semidefinite, and 1/(h+r)=sum_{j>=0}(1-r)^j k^{j+1} is a nonnegative Schur-power series. Thus 0<=f(h)<=1/2 and its finite kernel matrices are positive semidefinite.

For 0<epsilon<=1 define

$$\frac1{C_\epsilon}=4\pi[L+\epsilon f]=4\pi[h+1/2-(1-\epsilon)f].$$

Subtracting a positive-semidefinite kernel preserves at most one positive direction. C_epsilon is continuous, strictly positive, bounded by 1/(2 pi epsilon), and increases to G as epsilon decreases to zero, including its infinite diagonal.

## Role in the source argument

Permits bounded-kernel quadrature before taking a singular Green-kernel limit.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Agler-McCarthy, Corollary 1.12](https://www.math.wustl.edu/~mccarthy/public_papers/completenevpick.pdf) gives the established reciprocal inertia criterion for irreducible positive-definite kernels. [Qi-Zhang-Li, Theorem 4.1 and Corollary 4.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1303.3122) give earlier logarithmic-mean integral representations. Those references do not by themselves certify this PDE application.

## Checks and limitations

Six scalar logarithmic-mean integrals and 220 finite disk regularizations pass, including defect positivity, reciprocal inertia, monotonicity and diagonal normalization.

Floating-point matrices do not establish all configurations, the integral representation or continuum limits. Positive entries and a reciprocal inertia property must not be confused with an independently assumed positive-definite kernel.

## Possible applications

Approximation of singular kernels while preserving a finite-matrix signature constraint.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
