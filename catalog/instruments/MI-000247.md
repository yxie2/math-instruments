# Cyclic column factorization upgrades derivation bounds without descending through a kernel

[Family 288](../families/openai-math-288.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000143.md) · [Batch B018](../../batches/B018/README.md)

## Mathematical content

If P is a unital von Neumann algebra on K with a cyclic vector, every unit xi=(xi_i) in K^h factors as xi_i=v_i zeta for one unit zeta in K and a column contraction v in M_(h,1)(P).

For N=P', a cyclic vector for P separates N. Restrict N to the closure of its orbit on that vector; this is a faithful normal standard-form representation. The normal positive functional omega(x)=sum_i <x xi_i,xi_i> therefore has a unit vector representative zeta. The map x zeta -> (x xi_i)_i is an isometry, and extending it by zero gives an N-intertwining contraction. Its coordinate operators belong to N'=P.

Apply this to P=sigma(A)'' for a bounded rectangular derivation Delta whose domain representation sigma is cyclic. Suppose ||Delta||_col<=c||Delta||. For ||X||<=1 in M_h(A), matrix Kaplansky density and quotient norms give columns w_alpha in A with ||w_alpha||<=2 and sigma(w_alpha)zeta->xi. The identity

$$\Delta_h(X)\sigma(w_\alpha)=\Delta_{h,1}(Xw_\alpha)-\lambda_h(X)\Delta_{h,1}(w_\alpha)$$

bounds its action on zeta by 4c||Delta||. Taking the strong limit at fixed h, then the suprema, gives ||Delta||_cb<=4c||Delta||. The source obtains c=4 sqrt(2) from the classical homomorphism row estimate applied to a normalized triangular homomorphism and its adjoint counterpart. The preceding metric card then gives ||V||<=(1+4c)^2||Delta||.

Crucially, Delta need not vanish on ker(sigma): the displayed derivation identity is used on chosen lifts in A, not on a presumed quotient derivation.

Supplies a cyclic-domain implementer that later arguments can use on right-supported subspaces.

## Antecedents and comparison

The column factorization uses standard-form theory. The uniform row input is [Dickson, Lemma 2.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1404.3070), which credits Christensen. Cyclic similarity belongs to the established similarity literature; this card emphasizes the rectangular lifting details rather than asserting a new cyclic theorem.

## Checks and limitations

Read the standard-form and lifting proofs. Finite cyclic modules check common-vector factorization, including singular density matrices. Direct-sum algebra examples check the amplified identity when Delta is nonzero on ker(sigma).

Cyclicity is essential to this factorization argument. A scalar algebra on a two-dimensional space cannot factor two independent output vectors through one vector using scalar operators. No separability is needed for the abstract proof.

## Possible applications

Passing from one-column estimates to all matrix levels while avoiding an unjustified kernel quotient.
