# Separate the affine maximal equation with a square-root graph ansatz

[Family 353](../families/openai-math-353.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000377.md) · [Batch B063](../../batches/B063/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let F,P>0 on an open subset of R^m, with H=D²F positive definite, and put k=m+2, gamma=(m+3)/(m+2). If det H=F P^(-gamma) and tr(H^(-1)D²P)+kP/F=0, then u(x,t)=sqrt(F(x)²+t²) has positive Hessian and solves the classical affine maximal equation in n=m+1 variables.

The quadratic-form identity is D²u=(F/u)H+(F²/u³)(dt-(t/F)dF)². The determinant-one basis X_i=e_i+(t/F)F_i e_t diagonalizes it, giving det D²u=F^(m+2)u^(-(m+3))det H. With zeta=t/F, its determinant weight w=(det D²u)^(-(m+2)/(m+3)) equals P h(zeta), where h=(1+zeta²)^(k/2). Direct Hessian evaluation gives

(F/u)tr((D²u)^(-1)D²w)=h tr(H^(-1)D²P)+(P/F)((1+zeta²)h''-m zeta h').

The last bracket is kh. Thus the second equation makes the affine operator vanish. If F,P are radial and y=F'>0,y'>0, the equations become y^(m-1)y'=r^(m-1)F P^(-gamma), (y^(m-1)P')'=-k r^(m-1)P^(1-gamma), F'=y. Smooth initialization at r=0 and global positivity are separate tasks.

## Role in the source argument

Reduces construction of affine-maximal graphs to a radial second-order system.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Wang, Affine maximal hypersurfaces](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0304398), equations (1.3)-(1.4) and (6.1), records the classical equation and singular graph sqrt(|x prime|^9+x_10²). PDF pages 1-2 and 9 were read. This directly motivates the ansatz; it does not establish the source smooth continuation or priority for the general two-function reduction.

## Checks and limitations

Symbolic differentiation checks the Hessian determinant and separated trace identity in several dimensions, and the scalar identity for h. Finite positive-Hessian samples check definiteness.

The checks do not solve the reduced system globally, construct the smooth dimension-ten example or certify its asymptotics.

## Possible applications

Converting a fourth-order geometric equation into coupled lower-order equations with an explicit convexity test.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
