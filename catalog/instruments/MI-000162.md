# Support propagation creates an invariant convex set for a condensing map

[Family 322](../families/openai-math-322.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000103.md) · [Batch B008](../../batches/B008/README.md)

## Mathematical content

The source's geometric step admits this abstraction. Let E be a nonempty closed subset of the unit sphere of a real Banach space, and let g:E->E be continuous. Suppose every v and g(v) have a common norm-one support. Suppose also that every unit vector sharing a support with g(v) belongs to E. Then E contains a nonempty closed bounded convex set C with g(C) contained in C.

Start with v0 in E. Given a finite convex hull contained in E, choose a strictly positive combination a of all its vertices and add g(a). A common support of a and g(a) takes value one on every old vertex, since their coefficients are positive. It therefore norms the enlarged hull; every point in that hull shares the support with g(a) and lies in E. Choose the combinations by repeatedly approximating all finite rational combinations, with a vanishing positive perturbation on all currently available vertices. The chosen a's are dense in the final closed convex hull C. Since their images are among the vertices, continuity gives g(C) contained in C.

Assume in addition that, for a map b:C->K into a totally bounded metric space,

$$\|g(v)-g(v')\|\le\gamma\|v-v'\|+L d_K(b(v),b(v')),\qquad0\le\gamma<1.$$

Refine each finite diameter cover of H by a finite small-diameter partition of the b-values. Letting the parameter mesh tend to zero gives $\chi(g(H))\le\gamma\chi(H)$ for the Kuratowski measure of noncompactness. Darbo's theorem supplies a fixed point of g in C. This does not assert that g is a norm contraction.

In the source, M>0 is an attained radial-distance defect, t is in (0,1), and the return map pairs opposite sphere contacts about ty and t f(y). Its positive radii obey s=p+M, r=u+M and all lie in [1-t,1+t]. The estimate is

$$\|g(v)-g(v')\|\le\frac{up}{rs}\|v-v'\|+\frac{2|u-u'|}{r}+\frac{2u|p-p'|}{rs}.$$

Thus the two scalar parameters p,u suffice, and $\gamma=(1-M/(1+t))^2<1$. A fixed direction aligns the two source and target chords. A separate chord argument is still needed to contradict M>0.

## Role in the source argument

Obtains aligned extremal chords without imposing compactness, finite dimension or strict convexity on the ambient spheres.

## Antecedents and comparison

The fixed-point step is [Darbo’s theorem](https://www.numdam.org/item/RSMUP_1955__24__84_0/). The earlier attainment step uses standard [Banach ultrapowers](https://www.numdam.org/item/SEDP_1971-1972____A9_0/). The extracted contribution is the source’s support-propagation construction and its return-map estimate, not either classical theorem.

## Checks and limitations

Checked why strict positivity forces a common support on all earlier vertices, why the selected combinations are dense, and why partitioning both scalar parameters removes the additive error in the noncompactness bound. Finite face and parameter examples check these algebraic steps; finite sets cannot test a nonzero measure of noncompactness.

The closed extremal set, propagation rule and global parameter estimate must all be proved in an application. Merely having common supports for v and g(v) does not give the stated propagation rule. The fixed point alone is not the final sphere-isometry theorem.

## Possible applications

Fixed-point arguments on extremal subsets of nonsmooth unit spheres when norm contraction fails but the error factors through finitely many bounded parameters.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
