# Pass finite convex limits together with their supporting vectors

[Family 228](../families/openai-math-228.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000496.md) · [Batch B079](../../batches/B079/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Finite convex functions converging pointwise to a finite function on an open convex domain converge locally uniformly. Values at the vertices and center of a surrounding cube bound the local oscillation. Extending chords inside that cube bounds all nearby secant slopes uniformly. A finite net then upgrades pointwise convergence to uniform convergence on compact subsets.

Consequently, if x_n->x in the domain and v_n belongs to the subdifferential of F_n at x_n, the v_n are bounded and every limit belongs to the subdifferential of F at x. This follows by passing the supporting inequalities to the limit. It is an outer-closedness assertion, not convergence of every selected gradient to a unique vector.

For a planar finite convex F, the subdifferential equals the convex hull of reachable gradients. Differentiability points have full measure; local boundedness gives compactness of their limiting gradients. Points approaching x along a direction show that reachable gradients and the subdifferential have the same support function. The directional blowups [F(x+ae)-F(x)]/a converge locally uniformly in e to that support function.

## Role in the source argument

Preventing loss of slope information when passing convex free energies to a limit.

The reachable-gradient theorem in that reference was not independently read. No new convex analysis is claimed.

## Checks and limitations

Polyhedral convex functions and smooth log-sum-exp approximations check uniform value bounds, supporting inequalities, nonunique limiting supports, and directional blowups. A nonconvex narrow-spike sequence controls the missing convexity hypothesis.

Finite examples do not prove a thermodynamic pressure limit or persistence of a phase transition.

## Possible applications

Preventing loss of slope information when passing convex free energies to a limit.
