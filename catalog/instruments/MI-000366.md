# Flatten a quadratic null hypersurface with an explicit conformal wave chart

[Family 079](../families/openai-math-079.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000202.md) · [Batch B032](../../batches/B032/README.md)

## Mathematical content

In coordinates x=(t,b1,b2,y), let q(x)=ty-b1^2-b2^2 and Box=4 partial_t partial_y-partial_b1^2-partial_b2^2. After centering, a linear q-isometry removes a beta*y term from F=b2+beta*y+kq. The remaining chart, with v=(0,0,k,0), is

$$\Phi_v(x)=\frac{x+v q(x)}{D(x)},\qquad D(x)=1+2\langle v,x\rangle+q(v)q(x).$$

Its inverse is Phi_{-v}; q(Phi_v(x))=q(x)/D(x), its differential rescales the metric by D^{-2}, and its b2 coordinate is (b2+kq)/D. Thus it flattens F=0 on a region where D is nonzero.

For w=D^{-1}, direct differentiation gives Box w=0 and Box(w Phi_v,j)=0 for each coordinate. The second-order chain coefficients are D^{-2} times the inverse metric, proving for every smooth test function U

$$\Box(w\,U\circ\Phi_v)=w^3(\Box U)\circ\Phi_v.$$

The induced transformation of a hyperquadric c+d t+a1 b1+a2 b2+beta y+hq is linear on coefficients and preserves a1^2+a2^2-4 beta d+4hc. Buffered domains bounded away from D=0 are needed for quantitative derivative estimates. Frequency projection and packet transport are additional arguments.

## Role in the source argument

Provides the algebraic coordinate change used to compare thin round-cone configurations with cylinder configurations.

## Antecedents and comparison

Conformal spacetime geometry is classical; [Bateman’s paper](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Transformation_of_the_Electrodynamical_Equations) provides historical context for these transformations. The displayed wave identity is verified directly here rather than inferred from that historical citation.

## Checks and limitations

Symbolic checks verify the four inverse coordinates, 16 metric entries, five wave zero/first-order identities, 16 second-order chain coefficients and discriminant preservation.

The checks do not verify positive-band localization, packet association, error bounds or the local-smoothing theorem.

## Possible applications

Exact coordinate changes for wave equations and null hypersurfaces, with explicit denominator control.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
