# Split invariant measurable subspaces by killing a contracting graph

[Family 339](../families/openai-math-339.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000255.md) · [Batch B047](../../batches/B047/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let f preserve a probability measure and let an invertible measurable finite-dimensional cocycle preserve E=direct-sum E_i. Assume distinct exponents lambda_1<...<lambda_r and, for every epsilon>0, upper and lower bounds C_epsilon(v)^(+/-1) exp((lambda_i+/-epsilon)n) on each block, with tempered C_epsilon. Every measurable invariant subspace S splits almost everywhere as direct-sum (S intersect E_i). No tempered frame for S is needed.

Write E=E_< direct-sum E_r, K=S intersect E_< and I=projection_(E_r) S. In the measurable direct-sum norm, S/K is the graph of L:I -> E_</K. Invariance and the quotient norm give

||L_(f^n v)|| <= C_epsilon(v)^2 exp((lambda_(r-1)-lambda_r+2epsilon)n) ||L_v||.

Choose epsilon below half the exponent gap. The graph norm tends to zero. For every a>0, stationarity gives mu{||L||>a}=integral 1_{||L_(f^n v)||>a} dmu, which tends to zero by dominated convergence. Therefore L=0 almost everywhere, and induction splits S.

The underlying reusable principle is that a finite measurable nonnegative quantity with a stationary distribution cannot converge to zero along almost every forward orbit unless it is zero almost everywhere. The probability hypothesis supplies the finite measure needed here.

## Role in the source argument

Separates invariant polynomial-relation subspaces into Lyapunov blocks before the source normal-form construction.

## Antecedents and comparison

The proof combines classical exponent separation with stationarity. [Kalinin-Sadovskaya, Normal forms for non-uniform contractions](https://doi.org/10.3934/jmd.2017014) is related primary context for measurable normal forms; its published abstract and bibliographic record were checked, not an exact lemma-by-lemma equivalence.

## Checks and limitations

The complete selected argument was read. Finite periodic graph equations check the zero-graph conclusion for strictly contracting monodromy; equal-exponent and infinite-measure controls show why the gap and recurrence assumptions matter.

Finite cycles do not verify a general measurable cocycle or establish the geometric source hypotheses. On the integer shift with counting measure, the nonzero graph L_n=2^(-n) tends to zero forward, illustrating why the stationarity argument needs finite measure.

## Possible applications

Separating measurable invariant constraints into spectral or Lyapunov sectors without first controlling a frame for the constraint space.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
