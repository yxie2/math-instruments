# A small lower deficit prevents a fixed loss when changing probabilities

[Family 083](../families/openai-math-083.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000164.md) · [Batch B023](../../batches/B023/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Suppose probability measures satisfy nu=(1-eta)omega+eta rho, with 0<=eta<1. If a function B has integral at most one under nu and B>=1-delta everywhere, then

$$\int B\,d\omega\le1+\frac{\eta\delta}{1-\eta}.$$

Subtract the known lower contribution eta(1-delta) of the remainder probability before dividing by 1-eta. Merely using B>=0 gives a loss of order eta; retaining delta makes the loss vanish with the actual deviation. The bound is attained in a two-point model with omega and rho supported at different points.

The source constructs nu by convolving uniform probability on a slightly enlarged interval with a nonnegative band-limited Schwartz kernel. Its density dominates (1-eta) times uniform probability on the original interval, with eta=O(G^(-1/16)). If the predicted factor never exceeds one, transfer is immediate. Otherwise a logarithmic oscillation estimate gives its global lower deficit delta=O(p K_* upsilon), where upsilon is the relative mass drop. A denominator term that could be large cancels when comparing logarithms at two parameter values.

Blending a positive predictor beta with the constant one, B=(1-theta)beta+theta, preserves an upper average of one, reduces its lower deficit, and satisfies log B-log beta>=-theta log beta. The source uses that inequality to absorb a separate baseline error before transferring measures. The resulting loss is O(eta p K_* upsilon), rather than a fixed amount at each level. Summing or integrating products of such factors still needs the relevant pathwise and conditional-average hypotheses.

## Role in the source argument

Keeps a summable relative-drop error through the change from the probability used by the Fourier estimate to spatial uniform averages.

## Antecedents and comparison

The displayed mixture bound is elementary measure algebra, and blending uses concavity of logarithm. The source contribution being extracted is the explicit lower-deviation input and its use in the multilevel estimate; no novelty is claimed for the mixture identity.

## Checks and limitations

Read the probability construction, lower-deviation and blending arguments. Five hundred finite probability mixtures check transfer and logarithmic blending, and three two-point examples attain the transfer bound.

Finite mixtures do not verify the band-limited majorant, global logarithmic oscillation estimate or the later path-product theorem. An upper average bound alone does not retain the small factor upsilon.

## Possible applications

Changing reference measures in iterative estimates while keeping the error tied to the quantity actually consumed at each step.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
