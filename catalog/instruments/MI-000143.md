# Picard inversion on the full product path space gives a Brownian sampler

[Family 231](../families/openai-math-231.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000093.md) · [Batch B005](../../batches/B005/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let I be countable. Suppose $b:[0,\infty)\times\mathbb R^I\to[0,1]^I$ is jointly Borel and satisfies $\|b(t,y)-b(t,y')\|_\infty\le L\|y-y'\|_\infty$ whenever the difference is bounded. Every input $w\in\prod_i C([0,\infty),\mathbb R)$ then has a unique solution

$$Z_i(w)(t)=w_i(t)+\int_0^t b_i(s,Z(w)(s))\,ds.$$

Starting with $Z^0=w$, the Picard increments obey

$$\sup_i|Z_i^{k+1}(t)-Z_i^k(t)|\le \frac{L^kt^{k+1}}{(k+1)!}.$$

These summable majorants control differences uniformly over coordinates and compact time intervals. They do not bound w itself or require a common coordinate modulus of continuity. Parameterized integration and rational-time evaluations give a Borel causal solution map; it inherits every equivariance of b.

For a binary law nu, form finite posterior tilts as in MI-000142 along finite exhaustions of I. If these are uniformly L-Lipschitz, their limsup drift satisfies the hypotheses. In the planted model $Y_i(t)=tX_i+B_i(t)$, $X\sim\nu$, the innovations $W_i=Y_i-\int_0^t b_i(s,Y(s))ds$ have joint product Wiener law. Deterministic uniqueness gives $Y=Z(W)$. Hence

$$\Psi_i(w)=\mathbf1\{\limsup_{n\to\infty}Z_i(w)(n)/n>1/2\}$$

is a Borel sampler with full configuration law nu. Gaussian tails and a countable Borel-Cantelli argument identify all integer-time slopes simultaneously.

## Role in the source argument

Converts a dimension-free posterior response estimate into a measurable sampling rule from independent noise, preserving the symmetries of the target law.

## Antecedents and comparison

Picard iteration and Brownian innovations are classical. [Nam, Sly and Zhang](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.09484v2) already construct Ising factors through an infinite-dimensional Brownian observation equation. The particular reusable feature here is an everywhere-defined inverse on the unrestricted product path space, under a uniform finite-tilt response criterion. This is not an assertion that Brownian posterior sampling originated in the release.

## Checks and limitations

Checked the factorial induction, uniqueness relative to the same unbounded input, and law transfer through the reference observation model. Finite quadrature examples exercise the Picard majorants, with discretization error reported separately.

Uniform finite-tilt response is a substantive assumption. This is an exact existence criterion, not a finite-time approximation guarantee. Integer-time slope recovery is an infinite-time operation.

## Possible applications

Sampling invariant binary fields and testing whether a finite-dimensional sensitivity estimate suffices for an infinite-volume IID factor.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
