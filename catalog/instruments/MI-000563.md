# Turn deletion lines into curves and complete an almost-pencil cover

[Family 162](../families/openai-math-162.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000332.md) · [Batch B058](../../batches/B058/README.md)

## Mathematical content

For a prime s congruent to 2 modulo 3, f(x,y)=(x^3,y) permutes the affine plane. The image of a line y=dx+z with d nonzero is F_(d,z)={(x^3,dx+z)}. Every affine line meets this curve in at most three points: vertical and horizontal lines give at most one, and other slopes give a nonzero cubic equation. Uniformly selecting z deletes any specified point with probability 1/s. Independent choices for the s-1 slopes therefore give survival probability (1-1/s)^(s-1), unchanged by f.

Here is the deterministic cover step, separated from the probabilistic existence claim. Suppose Z meets every affine line in at least delta*s points, and a cover by a lines and b individual points has a+b/2<=s. Suppose a separate near-pencil result supplies at least (1-rho)s distinct cover lines through one projective point p. There are at most rho*s other lines and at most 2rho*s individual point resources. Any missing affine line through p would have at most 1+3rho*s of its points covered. If delta*s>1+3rho*s, no such line can be missing.

A finite point p would require all s+1 lines, exceeding the budget. Thus p is at infinity, all s lines of its parallel class occur, and a=s,b=0. The source uses delta=1/8 and rho=1/100 for sufficiently large s. Its assertion that a random Z satisfies the near-pencil hypothesis depends on a separate deletion theorem, which is not established by this extracted step.

## Role in the source argument

Combines survival under changed coordinates with rigidity of low-cost mixed covers.

## Antecedents and comparison

The map uses the classical criterion gcd(3,s-1)=1 and the polynomial root bound. The missing-line argument is an elementary conditional deduction. No earliest-priority claim is made for the source combination, and its asymptotic existence theorem remains a dependency.

## Checks and limitations

402 cubic images and 98,352 shape-line pairs over fields of orders 5,11,17 verify the intersection bound. All 625 deletion outcomes at order 5 verify equal survival. All 6,794 mixed covers within budget of the full order-3 plane yield exactly its four parallel-class equality covers.

The full-plane check is a model of cover rigidity, not a construction of the sparse source gadget. Neither the almost-pencil theorem nor the eventual Ryser counterexample is certified.

## Possible applications

Separating the coordinate system convenient for deleting sets from the one convenient for proving cover rigidity.
