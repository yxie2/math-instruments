# Choose nested recurrence sets before sampling a sequence of transitions

[Family 255](../families/openai-math-255.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000195.md) · [Batch B031](../../batches/B031/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let J be second countable and unimodular, Q compact of positive finite Haar measure, and S subset Q of positive measure. For compactly supported probability measures sigma_j assume mu(Q supp sigma_j)<=D_j mu(Q). There are nested positive-measure sets S_0 subset ... subset S_h=S such that, writing a_j=mu(S_j)/mu(Q),

$$a_{j-1}\ge a_j^2/(2D_j),\qquad \Pr\{xu_j\in S_j\}\ge a_j/(2D_j)\quad(x\in S_{j-1}),$$

where u_j has law sigma_j*check(sigma_j). Indeed the operator T_j associated with this law obeys int_A T_j1_A = int(int 1_A(zs^{-1})d sigma_j(s))^2 d mu(z)>=mu(A)^2/(D_j mu(Q)). Work backwards, retaining points where the transition probability is at least half the average lower bound.

Independent increments then keep every intermediate product x u_1...u_j in S_j with probability at least product_j a_j/(2D_j). For fixed h, subexponential initial density and subexponential D_j imply subexponential losses. Letting h grow would require a separate estimate because the repeated squaring compounds losses.

## Role in the source argument

Provides many product parameters while keeping all intermediate states inside a selected measurable set.

## Antecedents and comparison

This is a positive-convolution Cauchy-Schwarz argument followed by backward thresholding. No new general recurrence theorem is claimed.

## Checks and limitations

Sixty-eight exact three-stage cyclic-group models verify the convolution identity, retained masses and complete success probabilities.

The finite tests do not verify the later polynomial-image and geometric applications.

## Possible applications

Multistage sampling with state constraints and explicit mass budgets.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
