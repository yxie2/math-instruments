# Use a heat factorization to identify a dense preimage domain

[Family 280](../families/openai-math-280.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000243.md) · [Batch B043](../../batches/B043/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let E be a dense subspace of H and let bounded operators A,B satisfy AB=S, where A is injective, B has dense range, and S maps E bijectively onto E. Then E is contained in ran A and A^{-1}(E)=B(E), which is dense in H. Here A^{-1}(E) means a preimage set, not a globally bounded inverse.

Indeed AB(E)=E gives one inclusion. If A xi belongs to E, then A xi=AB S^{-1}A xi, and injectivity gives xi=B S^{-1}A xi. Density follows from continuity and dense range of B. In the source, S=s_0^{L_0}, 0<s_0<1, and E is the algebraic finite-energy subspace; S^{-1} preserves E even when it is unbounded on H.

## Role in the source argument

Provides a common dense domain for annulus-regularized fields and sesquilinear forms.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Henriques-Tener, Lemma 7.9](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.20735v3#S7) states this annulus result. Its full proof was checked and matches the source argument. This is an explicit antecedent, not a new lemma.

## Checks and limitations

Ten finite noncommuting factorizations check AB=S and the preimage reconstruction identity.

Finite models cannot establish infinite-dimensional density or construct annulus representations.

## Possible applications

Transporting dense algebraic domains through smoothing factorizations.
