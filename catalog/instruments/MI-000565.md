# Charge paired-subspace intersections to polynomial root multiplicity

[Family 165](../families/openai-math-165.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000334.md) · [Batch B058](../../batches/B058/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let W be an N-dimensional real vector space and let U_i,V_i, 1<=i<=m, satisfy U_i intersect V_i=0. The source proves

sum_(i,j) dim(U_i intersect V_j)<=floor(m^2/4)N.

Choose distinct nodes t_i. For Z contained in W, let P_h(Z) be the Z-valued polynomials of degree at most h satisfying P(t_i) in U_i, and write k_h(Z)=dim P_h(Z)-dim P_(h-1)(Z). Evaluation at a non-node t has kernel (x-t)P_(h-1)(Z), so its image dimension is k_h(Z). Lagrange interpolation gives sum_(h=0)^(m-1) k_h(Z)=sum_i dim(U_i intersect Z).

The nested evaluation images for Z=W have increments ell_h=k_h(W)-k_(h-1)(W)>=0. Choose columns introduced at degree h to extend their bases. A nonzero maximal minor at stage h has degree at most sum_(e<=h) e*ell_e. Projection modulo V_j bounds its rank at t_j by k_h(W)-k_h(V_j): the bound holds off the nodes by containment of evaluation images, extends by polynomial minors, and projection is injective on U_j. The determinant therefore vanishes to order at least k_h(V_j) at t_j. Thus sum_j k_h(V_j)<=sum_(e<=h)e*ell_e.

Summing in h yields sum_(i,j)dim(U_i intersect V_j)<=sum_e e(m-e)ell_e<=floor(m^2/4)N. Equality occurs by splitting W=P direct-sum Q and assigning (P,Q) to floor(m/2) indices and (Q,P) to the others.

For real d by d matrices with X_i-Y_i invertible, take U_i,V_i to be the graphs of X_i,Y_i in R^(2d). Their intersections are the kernels of X_i-Y_j. Removing the m full-rank diagonal terms gives sum_(i!=j)rank(X_i-Y_j)>=2 floor(m/2)floor((m-1)/2)d, also sharply.

## Role in the source argument

Converts local diagonal disjointness into a global lower budget for ranks of off-diagonal matrix differences.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Guruswami-Kopparty, Explicit Subspace Designs](https://www.math.utoronto.ca/swastik/subspace-designs.pdf), Claim 16 on PDF page 10, converts intersection dimension into determinant root multiplicity and then sums a degree budget; that claim and proof were read. It concerns a structured polynomial construction and does not state the displayed inequality for arbitrary paired subspaces. Targeted searches leave earliest priority of the exact source inequality unresolved.

## Checks and limitations

15 exact rational subspace families test 57 polynomial-filtration stages, including evaluation dimensions, node-rank loss and multiplicity budgets. Five families attain equality. Eighteen graph-of-matrix examples attain the rank-sum bound. A one-pair control shows why diagonal disjointness is necessary.

These are finite checks of the selected proof, not a proof assistant certificate or a validation of the full crossing-number application.

## Possible applications

Obtaining global rank or intersection constraints from a family of locally transverse pairs.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
