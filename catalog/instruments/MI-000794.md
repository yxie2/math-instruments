# Center isolated labels without deleting full-array geometry

[Family 364](../families/openai-math-364.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000524.md) · [Batch B086](../../batches/B086/README.md)

## Mathematical content

For a contact partition pi of labels R, let S be its singleton labels and B the union of nonsingleton blocks. Define E_pi=chi_pi sum_(A subset S)(-1)^(|S minus A|) f_(S minus A) F_(B union A). Keep chi_pi on the FULL array even when labels are deleted from F. Then chi_pi F_R=chi_pi sum_(A subset S) f_(S minus A) E_(pi_A), by the cancellation sum_(D subset A subset S)(-1)^(|A minus D|)=0 unless D=S.

Equivalently, if iota_D^R asserts that every deleted label in D is isolated from all other displayed labels, the assembled transform is E_R=sum_D(-1)^|D| iota_D^R f_D F_(R minus D), with the same unsigned inverse. The identity iota_D^R iota_A^(R minus D)=iota_(D union A)^R explains the inversion. Since exactly one contact pattern occurs at each array, there is no multiplicative cost equal to the number of partitions. For normalized one-body factors, triangle inequality and integration give both L1 operator bounds at most 2^U on arrays of size at most U.

## Role in the source argument

Removing independent factors without breaking the interaction components that must remain in the dynamics.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Pulvirenti and Simonella, arXiv:1405.4676](https://arxiv.org/abs/1405.4676), equations (1.12)-(1.14) on PDF page 7, were read for the established subset correlation-error transform and inverse. The source adds contact-pattern restrictions and retention of nonsingleton components.

## Checks and limitations

Exact enumeration of every contact graph on up to five labels checks the collapsed transform, inverse and indicator composition. A two-label contact rejects deleting the geometry while centering its vertices independently.

These finite identities hold for arbitrary arrays and prove no smallness or propagation of chaos. Dropping the full-array restriction changes the transform.
