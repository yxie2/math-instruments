# Marked matroid pivots admit a timeline-independent pattern bound

[Family 111](../families/openai-math-111.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000111.md) · [Batch B010](../../batches/B010/README.md)

## Mathematical content

In a finite matroid, let V be n nonloops. Fix an ordered list of old labeled occurrences, marked zero or one, whose labels span the ground set. Fix at most s movable labeled occurrences. Labels may repeat. Insert the movable occurrences anywhere, in any order, and choose their marks arbitrarily. For f in V record the mark of the first occurrence after which f becomes spanned. The number of resulting vectors is at most

$$4^s\sum_{\ell=0}^s\binom n\ell.$$

Delete old occurrences already spanned by their old predecessors: they stay redundant under insertions. The remaining old list is a basis b1,...,br. If A denotes all movable labels, the drops rk(A|B_{j-1})-rk(A|B_j) are zero or one and sum to at most s. An old basis element with drop zero must survive greedy processing in every interleaving. Thus at most s old elements can be omitted; choosing omissions and the retained movable occurrences gives at most 4^s retained occurrence bases.

For each fixed retained basis, f has a unique minimal nonempty spanning subset. Its last occurrence is the pivot. Encode the fixed old positions by widely spaced powers of two, with signs representing marks. Movable positions receive signed powers in the gaps, using at most s real variables. Each spanning subset has a signed sum whose sign is the last member's sign, since distinct powers are superincreasing. Therefore the recorded vector is a strict sign pattern of n affine forms in at most s variables. The classical hyperplane-region bound supplies the binomial sum. Neither the old timeline length nor an additional permutation factor appears.

## Role in the source argument

Bounds the possible adaptive residual sets before the focal random mask is revealed, using the number of inserted generators rather than the number of path updates.

## Antecedents and comparison

Greedy bases, fundamental-circuit supports and strict hyperplane-region counts are classical. The source combines them to count marked first-spanning events with repeated occurrence labels. Targeted searches did not identify an earlier statement of this exact package; that is not a priority certificate.

## Checks and limitations

Read the redundancy, retained-basis and superincreasing-score steps. Exhaustive small binary linear matroids check pivot vectors over all insertion positions, movable orders and marks, including repeated labels and old redundant occurrences.

V excludes loops, whose first-spanning event is not an inserted occurrence. Old labels must span the ground set. The movable occurrences are fixed before counting; allowing their labels to vary needs an extra count.

## Possible applications

Uniform concentration over adaptively modified matroid filtrations and complexity bounds for first-spanning decisions.
