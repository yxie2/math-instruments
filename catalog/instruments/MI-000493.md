# Choose a maximum-capacity forest before normalizing free variables

[Family 115](../families/openai-math-115.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000272.md) · [Batch B049](../../batches/B049/README.md)

## Mathematical content

In a feasible bipartite margin problem with at most d edges, split capacities into small b_s<W and large b_e>=W, with W>=1. Choose a maximum-total-capacity spanning forest of the large-edge graph, with one root per component. Its non-tree edges are free. The exchange property gives b_e<=b_t for every tree edge t on the fundamental path of a free edge e.

Fix each small-edge incidence separately in [0,b_s]; the row and column values of one small edge are allowed to differ. For free values z_e in [0,b_e+1], solve all nonroot margins by leaf elimination. The tree values are unique affine functions of the free values, integral for integral input data. Root discrepancies are independent of free variables.

Compare with a feasible table X*. Sum signed margin equations over the component below a tree edge, using row sign +1 and column sign -1. Internal large edges cancel. Crossing free edges contribute their signed changes, and every small incidence inside the component contributes its own signed change. Internal small incidences cannot be cancelled unless their two values agree. This cut identity, the path capacity comparison, and at most d edges yield |z_t-X*_t|<=4d b_t and root discrepancy at most 2dW.

Normalize u_e=z_e/(b_e+1). Each derivative of z_t/b_t in a free coordinate is zero or has magnitude (b_e+1)/b_t<=2. Consequently the convex penalty A[sum_roots |discrepancy|+sum_tree dist(z_t,[0,b_t])/b_t] has Lipschitz constant at most 2 A d^2 in the infinity norm of u. The maximum-capacity choice is what makes the normalized derivative independent of capacity ratios.

## Role in the source argument

Provides dimension-controlled geometry for the cell-bounded source while retaining mismatched small-edge profiles.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Cryan-Dyer-Randall, Section 2, Definition 3 and Theorem 1](https://homepages.inf.ed.ac.uk/mcryan/cdr.pdf) explicitly use maximum-capacity spanning trees and the fundamental-path capacity comparison. Those arguments were read. The source adapts the coordinates to separated small incidences and a convex penalty.

## Checks and limitations

Exact bipartite models compare matrix elimination with signed cut sums, test root independence and normalized coefficients, and include mismatched small incidences internal to a cut. An arbitrary low-capacity spanning tree loses capacity-independent control.

The tests do not establish the source partition-function or counting complexity estimates. Tightening the original cell ranges is a separate preprocessing step.

## Possible applications

Parametrizing constrained flows with coefficients controlled after capacity normalization, including relaxed endpoint profiles.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
