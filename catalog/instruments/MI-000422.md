# Detect an exact Jacobian-algebra length by one finite rank condition

[Family 064](../families/openai-math-064.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000230.md) · [Batch B039](../../batches/B039/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Fix mu>0 and polynomial jets g in three variables of degree at most N>=mu+1 with zero constant and linear terms. Let A_mu=C[x,y,z]/m^(mu+1). Multiplication by the three partial derivatives gives a matrix A_mu^3 -> A_mu whose entries depend polynomially on the jet coefficients. The locus where its cokernel has dimension mu is locally closed and is exactly the locus where the analytic Jacobian algebra C{x,y,z}/J(g) has length mu.

To see the nontrivial direction, let A be that local Jacobian algebra with maximal ideal n. If dim A/n^(mu+1)=mu, among its mu+1 successive layers at least one vanishes. For that j, n^j=n^(j+1); Nakayama applied to the finitely generated ideal n^j gives n^j=0. Hence A itself has the tested finite length. Conversely, an algebra of length mu has n^mu=0 and therefore passes the truncated test. The argument excludes infinite-length Jacobian algebras.

## Role in the source argument

Makes a fixed-Milnor-number condition a finite algebraic rank stratum in a jet parameter space.

## Antecedents and comparison

Standard finite-length Nakayama and determinantal rank stratification. No new general local-algebra principle is claimed.

## Checks and limitations

Five exact Jacobian models have lengths one through five. Five infinite-length controls fail; four controls test the danger of using a smaller truncation.

The rank test alone does not establish topological constancy on the stratum or the source simultaneous-resolution claims.

## Possible applications

Turning isolated-singularity length conditions into computable constructible jet loci.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
