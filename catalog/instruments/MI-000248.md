# Reduced density operators remove arbitrary unitaries from a tensor spectral bound

[Family 292](../families/openai-math-292.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000144.md) · [Batch B018](../../batches/B018/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let Gamma have a finite nonempty set S and a constant kappa>0 such that every unitary representation without invariant vectors satisfies sum_s ||sigma(s)eta-eta||^2>=kappa||eta||^2. Assume sigma on K has no nonzero finite-dimensional subrepresentation. For arbitrary unitaries U_s on any E, with no group relations required,

$$\sum_{s\in S}(U_s\otimes\sigma(s)-I)^*(U_s\otimes\sigma(s)-I)\ \ge\ \frac{\kappa^2}{4|S|}I.$$

For a unit xi in E tensor K, let T be its reduced density operator on K. With delta_s=||(U_s tensor sigma(s))xi-xi|| and T_s=sigma(s)T sigma(s)*, partial trace gives ||T_s-T||_1<=2 delta_s. Powers-Stormer gives ||sqrt(T_s)-sqrt(T)||_2^2<=||T_s-T||_1.

Conjugation by sigma on Hilbert-Schmidt operators has no invariant vector: a nonzero commuting compact operator would yield a finite-dimensional invariant eigenspace of its absolute value. Apply the gap to sqrt(T), whose Hilbert-Schmidt norm is one. Then kappa<=2 sum_s delta_s<=2 sqrt(|S|)(sum_s delta_s^2)^(1/2), proving the bound.

The square-root inequality follows by setting F=X-Y, J=sign(F) for positive Hilbert-Schmidt X,Y: ||X^2-Y^2||_1>=Tr(J(X^2-Y^2))=Tr(|F|(X+Y))>=Tr(F^2). Pair X+Y-F=2Y and X+Y+F=2X with F_+ and F_- to obtain the last step. Trace-class approximation justifies the calculation in arbitrary ambient Hilbert spaces.

## Role in the source argument

Provides a uniform lower bound for arbitrary coordinate tuples before applying nuclear tensor-norm comparisons.

The density-operator mechanism is established; retaining arbitrary unrelated U_s is the explicit interface extracted here.

## Checks and limitations

Read the argument. Positive matrices test Powers-Stormer and random purifications test partial-trace bounds. A finite Pauli conjugation model checks the analogous estimate after subtracting the invariant Hilbert-Schmidt component.

No nonzero finite-dimensional model satisfies the no-finite-dimensional-subrepresentation hypothesis. The finite checks therefore do not test the full weak-mixing conclusion. Removing the invariant component is essential in finite analogues.

## Possible applications

Transferring a representation gap through an uncontrolled tensor factor without imposing relations on that factor.
