# Convert a spectral lower bound to a logarithmic drift weight

[Family 336](../families/openai-math-336.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000517.md) · [Batch B084](../../batches/B084/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Assume -4 Delta+Scal>=1 as a quadratic form on a complete connected manifold without boundary. On a compact manifold choose its positive ground eigenfunction. Otherwise exhaust by connected smooth relatively compact domains containing a fixed point o. Normalize each positive Dirichlet ground eigenfunction v_j by v_j(o)=1. Its eigenvalue is at least one and is bounded above by a single fixed compactly supported test function.

Harnack chains and elliptic estimates give a subsequence converging on compact sets to a smooth positive v with -4 Delta v+Scal v=lambda v, lambda>=1. With w=-2 log v,
Scal+2 Delta w-|dw|^2=Scal-4 Delta v/v=lambda>=1.
This argument requires no global upper or lower bound for w and no uniform bounded-geometry hypothesis.

## Role in the source argument

Replacing a global spectral assumption by a pointwise weighted-curvature inequality suitable for local geometric arguments.

## Antecedents and comparison

This is the classical positive-solution/exhaustion principle followed by the logarithmic ground-state transform. The source bibliography attributes the positive-solution background to Allegretto and Moss-Piepenbrink. Their original proofs were not read in this batch; the complete source proof was read.

## Checks and limitations

Symbolic differentiation checks the logarithmic identity. Constant-potential interval models check normalized positive ground states and eigenvalues along exhaustion; these are Schrodinger analogues, not scalar-curvature examples.

Finite and symbolic checks do not prove the compactness/existence theorem. The drift weight need not be bounded and does not by itself prove a width bound.
