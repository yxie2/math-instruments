# Independent instances obey a direct product under adaptive individual query caps

[Family 284](../families/openai-math-284.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000138.md) · [Batch B016](../../batches/B016/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let mu be a distribution on the promised inputs of a partial Boolean function P. Suppose every deterministic decision tree using at most L queries has mu-success at most beta. For r independent instances, any randomized algorithm that interleaves queries arbitrarily, uses at most L queries on each instance on every branch, and has only independent additional side information, succeeds on all instances with probability at most beta^r.

Fix its coins and independent side information. A complete query transcript restricts each input separately: the adaptive choice of the next queried coordinate is determined by the recorded answers. The conditional input law therefore remains a product of the individual posterior laws. For each instance take V_h to be the best conditional success with its remaining query allowance. Revealing one legal bit cannot increase its expected V_h, by the one-instance Bellman equation; the other factors stay fixed. The product of the V_h is a supermartingale, initially at most beta^r. At termination, the probability of all the chosen answers is bounded by the product of the posterior best-guess probabilities, hence by this product value.

An aggregate budget rL is insufficient. For two independent uniform two-bit AND instances, an individual cap L=1 gives beta=3/4 and product bound 9/16. With a total budget two, query the first bit of the first instance; if it is zero query one bit of the second, otherwise query the second bit of the first. The resulting all-correct probability is 3/4. Correlated instances likewise invalidate the independent-product conclusion.

## Role in the source argument

Controls simultaneous recovery of many independent address values despite an adversarial adaptive query schedule.

## Antecedents and comparison

The conditional factorization and product-value proof are already explicit in [Drucker, Lemmas 3.1-3.2 and the following product process](https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/files/19272888/Drucker_2012_Improved_direct_product_theorems_for_randomized_query_complexity.pdf). This card records an established reusable tool, not a novel direct-product theorem.

## Checks and limitations

Read the proof and the antecedent. Exact dynamic programming optimizes all adaptive interleavings on small independent instances and checks the product optimum; separate controls allow only a total budget or correlate the inputs.

Input-dependent side information can destroy posterior factorization. A bound on expected queries or total queries does not supply the per-instance branch cap.

## Possible applications

Proving lower bounds for adaptive algorithms that must recover many independently hidden local values.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
