# Detect a projective cup power using complementary boundary fillings

[Family 349](../families/openai-math-349.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000258.md) · [Batch B047](../../batches/B047/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let C(S^(m+1)) be finite-perimeter sets modulo null sets with volume symmetric-difference distance, and let their mod-two boundaries lie in the flat component of zero. The boundary map is a double cover with deck involution E -> E^c, provided the standard small-filling theorem is used. Its degree-one class lambda records whether a lifted loop ends at the initial filling or its complement.

Set p=m+3 and Q=closed B^(m+2) times [-1,1]. Suppose q -> E_q is continuous in volume, E_(-q)=E_q^c on the boundary of Q, E_(0,-1) is empty and E_(0,1) is the whole sphere. The cycles partial[E_q] descend to Q/(q~-q on boundary), homeomorphic to RP^p. The vertical segment closes to a loop whose filling lift goes from empty to whole sphere, so the pulled-back lambda is the nonzero generator alpha in H^1(RP^p;F_2). Since H*(RP^p;F_2)=F_2[alpha]/(alpha^(p+1)), its pth power is nonzero.

The fillings give local branches of the pulled-back cover. They do not give a single filling on the quotient, which would trivialize the very cover being detected. Continuity in volume implies flat continuity of boundaries; it does not by itself imply no concentration of boundary mass.

## Role in the source argument

Verifies the topological detection property of a candidate sweepout independently of its later mass estimates.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Hatcher, Algebraic Topology](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher/AT/ATch3.pdf), Theorem 3.19, p. 220, gives the projective cohomology ring; the statement and beginning of its proof were read. The source uses ordinary double-cover monodromy to identify its pullback generator.

## Checks and limitations

Read the selected covering and monodromy proofs and checked the dimension p=m+3 and boundary quotient. This topological criterion has an analytic review record; the finite program does not certify it.

The small-filling theorem is an external input. No claim that a geometric family satisfying all min-max regularity and mass conditions has been independently constructed.

A topological test for antipodally glued parameter families of boundaries.
