# Small-field gains and an adaptive path cutoff make a critical Osgood estimate integrable

[Family 236](../families/openai-math-236.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000095.md) · [Batch B005](../../batches/B005/README.md)

## Mathematical content

At the critical tree parameter $b=d-1\ge2$, $\theta=b^{-1/2}$, let $V(t)=\mathbb E(\Delta h_o(t))^2$ for the compatible copies. On a finite horizon assume the source’s established comparison inputs: V is absolutely continuous, $V(0)=0$, $0\le V\le H^2$, and its weighted path estimates hold. Write $K>\max(1,H^2)$, $I=1+\log(K/V)$ when V>0, and $\omega=\min(\sqrt t,1)$.

For $a(z)=(1-\tanh^2z)/(1-\theta^2\tanh^2z)$, put $S=\mathbb E[\log a(Y)-\log a(Y')]^2$ using corresponding cavity fields. The source supplies $\mathbb E|Y-Y'|^2\le CV$, a deterministic difference bound, and the marginal tail $\Pr(|Y|>t^{1/8})\le \exp[-c\log^2(1/t)]$. Because $|(\log a)'(z)|\le2|\tanh z|$, splitting small and large fields gives

$$S\le C_TV\left(t^{1/4}+\frac{I}{\log^2(1/t)}\right),\qquad S\le CV.$$

The exceptional contribution is bounded by $\min(CV,C_Te^{-c\log^2(1/t)})$; a split according to whether $V\le e^{-c\log^2(1/t)/2}$ proves the logarithmic gain without independence of Y and Y'.

The weighted path inputs are $Q_r\le CrS$, $L_r\le V$, and, for $r\ge C_0/\omega$, exponentially damped versions with errors $C_Tr^2e^{-\alpha b^{\gamma r}}$ and $C_Te^{-\alpha b^{\gamma r}}$. Choosing

$$R=\left\lceil C_1(\omega^{-1}+\log I)\right\rceil$$

absorbs the double-exponential tails beyond R into V-squared times a summable sequence. Consequently

$$\sum Q_r\le C_T[S(\omega^{-2}+\log^2 I)+V^2],$$
$$\sum\sqrt{Q_r}\le C_T[\sqrt S(\omega^{-3/2}+(\log I)^{3/2})+V],$$
$$\sum L_r\le C_T[V(\omega^{-1}+\log I)+V^2].$$

Inserting these in the source’s variance bound $V'\le C(V+\sum Q_r+\sum L_r+\sqrt V\sum\sqrt{Q_r})$ yields $V'\le k_T(t)V[1+\log(K/V)]$, where for sufficiently small $t\le t_*\le e^{-1}$ one can take

$$k_T(t)=C_T\left(1+t^{-3/4}+\frac1{t\log^2(1/t)}\right),$$

and k is bounded later. This is integrable at zero. The Osgood integral diverges at V=0, forcing $V\equiv0$. The small-field gain specifically replaces the nonintegrable term V/t that the coarse estimate S<=CV would leave.

## Role in the source argument

Isolates the analytic mechanism used at the threshold, where the tree’s branching growth cancels the squared correlation decay and a direct Gronwall bound is insufficient.

## Antecedents and comparison

Osgood uniqueness and exponential path summation are classical. The earlier [Nam-Sly-Zhang construction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.09484v2) supplies the Brownian Ising-factor setting below a constant multiple of the reconstruction scale. The candidate refinement here is the particular small-field gain, discrepancy-dependent cutoff and integrable critical coefficient. Targeted comparison does not establish priority for all components.

## Checks and limitations

Read the moment-to-tail argument, small-log-loss split, three path sums and final absolutely continuous comparison. Scalar tests check the bad-event bound and cutoff absorption in logarithmic coordinates; exact integral formulas verify the displayed coefficient is integrable.

The preceding critical information and weighted path estimates are substantial hypotheses, not consequences of this scalar calculation. These selected checks do not certify the full sharp threshold theorem or arbitrary-solution pathwise uniqueness.

## Possible applications

Other critical stochastic comparisons in which a coarse 1/t divergence can be improved using a vanishing derivative at typical small fields and rapidly decaying exceptional path events.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
