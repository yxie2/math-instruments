# Chamber multiplicities control skeleton weights and the loss in expansion

[Family 177](../families/openai-math-177.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000133.md) · [Batch B015](../../batches/B015/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let T be a finite pure n-complex, X its s-skeleton, and 0<a<=c(tau)<=b for the number of chambers containing each s-face tau. Let mu_i and nu_i be the intrinsic normalized weights induced by uniform top-face sampling in T and X, respectively. Put Lambda=binom(n+1,s+1)|T(n)|/|T(s)|. For 0<=i<=s,

$$\frac{\Lambda}{b}\mu_i(\sigma)\le\nu_i(\sigma)\le\frac{\Lambda}{a}\mu_i(\sigma).$$

At degree s, nu_s(tau)=Lambda mu_s(tau)/c(tau). At lower degrees, both weights are obtained by summing over containing s-faces and dividing by binom(s+1,i+1); chamber-face double counting gives the identity for mu. Summing the coordinate bounds gives the same inequalities for weighted support norms and distances to any common cochain subspace.

If in degrees i,i+1 a common coboundary map satisfies ||delta f||_(mu,i+1)>=eta dist_(mu,i)(f,A), then

$$\|\delta f\|_{\nu,i+1}\ge\eta\frac ab\operatorname{dist}_{\nu,i}(f,A).$$

The common Lambda cancels across degrees. In particular, constant chamber multiplicity gives identical weights. In the source, panel multiplicity is constant, while the lower skeleton has multiplicities between one and M; this transfers a cosystolic inequality with a factor 1/M. Replacing A=Z^i by B^i requires a separate proof of vanishing cohomology.

## Role in the source argument

Moves an available expansion inequality to the normalization used by the final construction without losing an uncontrolled size factor.

## Antecedents and comparison

This is elementary incidence double counting followed by comparison of weighted norms. The source-specific utility is preserving one normalization factor across degrees. No historical novelty claim is made.

## Checks and limitations

Read the double count and distance comparison. Exact rational weights on small pure complexes check every face inequality; exhaustive F2 cochains check distances and expansion ratios. A cycle with nonzero H1 shows that a cocycle-distance bound cannot be renamed a coboundary-distance bound.

Purity ensures all weights are positive. The same cochain spaces and map must be used in the compared degrees. Cohomology vanishing is an independent input.

## Possible applications

Transferring weighted expansion estimates between a complex and its skeleta or other uniformly comparable face distributions.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
