# Build a chromatic filtration from one compatible family of basis maps

[Family 318](../families/openai-math-318.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000514.md) · [Batch B084](../../batches/B084/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Suppose an S-module D has specified maps z_I:Sigma^(-d(I))S->D, where d(I)=sum_{i in I}(2i-1), z_empty is the unit, and the SAME maps indexed by I subset [n-t] give a K(t)-equivalence for every 1<=t<n. Assume also the rational graded dimension count for subsets of [n], and the stated nonzero rational unit condition when n=1.

For X=L_(n-1)D, remove the first block L_(n-1)S plus Sigma^(-1)L_(n-1)S. Its cofiber is one chromatic level lower. Subsequently remove the block indexed by max I=j, using the original z_I and localization adjunction. Compatibility makes the previously removed blocks the prescribed direct summands at every subsequent height. Rational independence of the specified maps, together with the dimension count, identifies the last rational block and leaves zero cofiber.

Take G_j=fib(X->Q_j), where Q_j is the successive cofiber, and refine each block by pullbacks. This yields 2^n sphere-localization subquotients, with the unit piece first. These are FILTRATION quotients; attaching maps need not vanish. Equal values of d(I) do not identify different basis vectors.

## Role in the source argument

Organizing local equivalences into a global filtration while retaining potentially nontrivial attachments.

## Antecedents and comparison

Successive cofiber and pullback constructions are classical. The substantive source input is the compatible family of localized bases, which is kept as an explicit hypothesis.

## Checks and limitations

A graded vector-space model checks blocks and degree collisions using one common basis. An integral two-term complex with differential p shows that the corresponding filtered object need not be the direct sum of its quotients.

Independent basis choices at different heights or a rational dimension count alone do not supply the compatibility. The source chromatic splitting theorem is not certified.
