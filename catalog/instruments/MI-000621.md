# Preserve a polynomial envelope exponent through anisotropic sampling

[Family 080](../families/openai-math-080.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000390.md) · [Batch B064](../../batches/B064/README.md)

## Mathematical content

For nested caps with scales K' >= K >=1, use native packet coordinates X_(K,v)(x,t)=((x-2vt)/K,t/K²), unit cells T, centers z_T and w_T(z)=(1+dist(X_(K,v)(z),T))^(-P). For a fixed child T' and its parent grid, the source proves, for sufficiently large P>k+2,

sum_T w_T(z)w_T'(z_T)<=C_P w_T'(z),

with the same exponent P on both sides and a constant independent of K'/K. Put r=K/K' and a=1+d_T'(z). The parent-to-child coordinate map has spatial coefficients O(r), time coefficient r², determinant r^(k+2) and bounded normalized shear. Parent centers at child distance at least a/2 contribute O(a^(-P)) times a bounded parent lattice sum. For the remaining centers, if a is large, parent distance is at least ca/r; their contribution is at most C(a/r)^(-P) times a child-scale lattice sum O(r^(-(k+2))). This is O(a^(-P)r^(P-k-2)). Bounded a is handled by the first lattice bound.

Restricting to d_T(z)>L gives the bound C_P[L^(-(P-k-1))+r^(P-k-2)]w_T'(z). When child distance is bounded, the second term is unnecessary. The exponent loss is avoided because P exceeds the anisotropic volume exponent k+2, not just the spatial dimension k.

## Role in the source argument

Keeps one envelope power available through a hierarchy of packet resamplings.

## Antecedents and comparison

This is a classical near/far lattice-convolution split with anisotropic volume bookkeeping. The source provides the same-exponent and tail formulation. No packet, restriction or endpoint theorem follows from this local estimate alone.

## Checks and limitations

Finite parent lattice windows in one spatial dimension test the scale map, shear determinant, near/far split and normalized envelope sums at several scale ratios. Window truncation is reported explicitly.

Positive finite sums are partial sums, not certificates of a universal infinite-lattice upper bound. The general bound rests on the inspected lattice-tail proof.

## Possible applications

Preventing repeated multiscale convolution from exhausting the polynomial decay of packet weights.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
