# Use invariant differentials and p-bases to prove formal smoothness

[Family 311](../families/openai-math-311.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000219.md) · [Batch B037](../../batches/B037/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let a reduced closed formal subgroup H of a smooth formal group over a perfect field k of characteristic p have coordinate ring B=k[[T_1,...,T_r]]/I and embedding dimension e. Then B is isomorphic to k[[X_1,...,X_e]].

Every power series has a finite Frobenius expansion sum_{0<=a_i<p} T^a f_a(T)^p. Consequently ordinary derivations are determined by the T_i, so the ordinary differential module has the usual finite quotient presentation. The group-difference automorphism (x,y)->(x,y-_H x) identifies the diagonal conormal module with B tensor_k (m_B/m_B^2), proving Omega^1_{B/k}=B^e.

At a minimal prime, reducedness identifies the localization with a field K. For its complete local component D of dimension d, a finite parameter map k[[x_1,...,x_d]] -> D gives a finite extension K/L. Frobenius identifies the degrees [K:L] and [K^p:L^p], even for inseparable extensions; hence [K:K^p]=p^d and dim_K Omega^1_{K/k}=d. Localization also gives dimension e, so every component has dimension e. Dimension equals embedding dimension, and completeness yields the asserted power-series presentation.

Reducedness cannot be omitted: alpha_p has coordinate ring k[[x]]/(x^p), one-dimensional invariant differentials and dimension zero.

## Role in the source argument

Turns the reduced subgroup defined by vanishing equations into a smooth formal object before applying formal-group classification.

These examples do not construct the source formal closure or its annihilating homomorphism.
