# Weighted telescoping finds a common witness without minimum branch weights

[Family 328](../families/openai-math-328.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000107.md) · [Batch B009](../../batches/B009/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Consider a finite ordered rooted tree whose positive child weights sum to one. Let W_v be the product of weights along the root-to-v path, with root weight one. Switch leaves one at a time in their order, and let Delta_s be the resulting consecutive differences of the root outputs. Suppose $\|\Delta_s\|\le W_s$. Set d_s=Delta_s/W_s, so each d_s belongs to the unit ball.

For every non-root node v let f_v have dual norm at most one, and suppose its whole descendant block satisfies

$$f_v\!\left(\sum_{s\succeq v}\Delta_s\right)\ge W_v-\eta_v,\qquad\eta_v\ge0.$$

Because the leaves below v form a consecutive block, their actual root differences telescope even when all internal maps are nonlinear. The deficits are nonnegative, and

$$\sum_sW_s\sum_{v\preceq s,\ v\ne r}(1-f_v(d_s))\le\sum_{v\ne r}\eta_v=E.$$

For any 0<delta<1, leaves having some ancestor with f_v(d_s)<=1-delta have total weight at most E/delta. If E<delta, at least one leaf has a single vector d_s detected above 1-delta by every ancestor functional. No division of an error bound by the smallest W_s is needed.

In the source, nonexpansive tree sensitivity gives the leaf bound and a separate prediction argument gives eta_v=5e_v with sum e_v<=1/128. Taking delta=1/2 leaves bad weight at most 5/64. For one consistent infinite finitely branching tree, finite witnesses at every depth give an infinite feasible branch. If the ambient unit ball is weakly compact, nested weakly closed constraints along that branch have a common vector. Pointwise convergence of its functionals to zero would then be impossible. All of these additional hypotheses must be established before that final contradiction.

## Role in the source argument

Converts many block estimates into one common vector along a path while allowing arbitrarily small branch probabilities.

## Antecedents and comparison

Telescoping, weighted Markov bounds, finite branching and weak compactness are established tools. The source’s specific work lies in producing block detection for nonlinear outputs. The preceding [semigroup background](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9939-1975-0383164-3) does not by itself provide these tree estimates.

## Checks and limitations

Exact rational tree weights, including very small ones, check telescoping and the weighted bad-leaf bound. A nonlinear scalar tree checks that root-output differences telescope without treating its internal maps as affine.

The same fixed tree must work at every depth before passing to an infinite branch. Norm bounds on both vectors and functionals ensure nonnegative deficits. Weak compactness of the original domain is not a substitute for weak compactness of the normalized-difference ball.

## Possible applications

Extracting simultaneous witnesses from hierarchical nonlinear constructions with summable absolute error budgets.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
