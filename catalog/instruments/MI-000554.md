# Compress invariant cosets with three-cycle differences

[Family 206](../families/openai-math-206.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000323.md) · [Batch B057](../../batches/B057/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let X act on a finite set I as a product of independent natural alternating groups A_n, n>=7, each possibly repeated diagonally on several copies of its alphabet, with additional fixed letters. Let s bound the number of X-orbits on I, and let D be any finite group of order at most a. The source bounds the number of X-invariant subgroups of D^I and the invariant left or right cosets of each, independently of the alphabet sizes.

Combine c repeated copies of one alphabet into coordinates in E=D^c. If x belongs to an invariant left coset xJ, then x^(-1)x^sigma belongs to J for every three-cycle sigma. Such a difference has support on at most three coordinates and can put x_i^(-1)x_j at a selected position. Moving that position to two fresh coordinates and multiplying by the inverse translate produces a two-coordinate element with values t,t^(-1). Two-transitivity moves this pair to any ordered pair of positions.

Keep position 2 as reference. Right-multiply by pairs placing x_i^(-1)x_2 at position i and its inverse at position 1. Then every position except possibly the first becomes the reference value, with all accumulated correction at position 1. The order of multiplication is explicit and requires no commutativity of E.

Let C be the tuples constant beyond the first two positions on each alphabet. One can take |C|<=a^(3s). Every invariant coset meets C. Every pair multiplier is an X-translate of an element of J intersect C, so J is generated by X-translates of J intersect C. This gives at most 2^(a^(3s)) possibilities for J and at most a^(3s) invariant cosets for each J. The representatives are fixed by a control that fixes those reserved positions.

## Role in the source argument

Makes the source subgroup counting depend on the number of controlled orbits rather than the potentially huge alphabet sizes.

## Antecedents and comparison

The proof uses classical three-cycle generation and two-transitivity, and its finite support calculation was read in full. A targeted search for invariant subgroups of direct powers under alternating coordinate actions did not establish earliest priority for this precise estimate. The catalogue records it as a source-specific quantitative assembly, not a new general group-theoretic principle.

## Checks and limitations

120 tuple models over the nonabelian group S3, with one or two repeated copies and alphabet sizes 7,8,11, test 800 explicit pair corrections built from actual three-cycle differences and translates.

The tests check the coordinate mechanism, not an enumeration of all invariant subgroups or the source global computable lattice bound.

## Possible applications

Bounding invariant algebraic data uniformly when coordinate sets grow but the local coefficient group and orbit complexity stay bounded.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
