# Compare residual character summands by integral group-ring evaluation

[Family 030](../families/openai-math-030.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000242.md) · [Batch B042](../../batches/B042/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Suppose T is free of rank r over Z_l[C_n] and carries a continuous group action commuting with the group ring. For a character theta:C_n -> O* into a finite extension valuation ring, set T_theta=T tensor_(Z_l[C_n],theta) O. It is a free rank-r stable lattice. Over characteristic zero it realizes the theta character summand by semisimplicity.

If theta and theta prime have the same residue character, reducing these tensor products gives the identical module T tensor_(Z_l[C_n],bar theta) k, with its group action. This holds even if l divides n. A rational character projector contains 1/n and cannot replace the integral freeness hypothesis.

For n=3p and chi=psi eta with orders p and 3, p-power roots of unity reduce to 1 at p, and 3-power roots reduce to 1 at 3. Consequently the corresponding reductions link eta to chi at p, chi to psi at 3, and psi to the trivial character at p, provided the free integral module is available. Passing through rational isomorphisms preserves semisimplified reductions, even when a natural integral pullback has nonunit index.

## Role in the source argument

Supplies the source congruence chain without using nonintegral character projectors at the switching primes.

## Antecedents and comparison

Standard base-change and residual-character algebra. The geometric freeness needed by the source is a separate theorem, not implied by rational character decomposition.

## Checks and limitations

120 dual-number residue-algebra models compare evaluations at characters 1 and 1+epsilon when the characteristic divides the cyclic group order.

The tests do not verify the source cohomological group-ring freeness or any automorphy-lifting hypotheses.

## Possible applications

Constructing congruent stable lattices when the residual characteristic divides a symmetry-group order.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
