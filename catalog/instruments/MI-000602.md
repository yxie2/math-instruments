# Obtain two GNS multiplication isometries from scalar conditional expectations

[Family 290](../families/openai-math-290.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000371.md) · [Batch B062](../../batches/B062/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let N and B be subalgebras in a von Neumann algebra with faithful normal state phi and phi-preserving conditional expectations. Assume E_N(b)=phi(b)1 for b in B and E_B(x)=phi(x)1 for x in N. On the algebraic tensor product of their GNS spaces define R(x Omega tensor a Omega)=xa Omega and L(x Omega tensor a Omega)=ax Omega. Then both maps extend isometrically. For example,

phi(a* x* y b)=phi(x*y)phi(a*b)

by applying the B-bimodular expectation E_B; the reverse order uses E_N. Thus the multiplication vectors have exactly the tensor-product inner products, without assuming that N and B commute. Consequently U=L*R is a contraction. The source obtains the scalar expectation hypotheses using a separate bicentralizer input; that input is not supplied by this elementary calculation.

## Role in the source argument

Transfers ordered operator products to a tensor Hilbert space while retaining their mixed coefficients.

## Antecedents and comparison

The argument is the standard GNS calculation for orthogonal subalgebras. The source specialization is explicitly conditional on scalar expectations; no bicentralizer claim is certified.

## Checks and limitations

Diagonal and Fourier-diagonal maximal abelian subalgebras of matrix algebras verify both product Gram matrices, scalar expectations and noncommutation.

Matrix examples test only the conditional product lemma, not type III1 bicentralizers or the later spectral identification of U.

## Possible applications

Representing two orders of multiplication by isometries even when the underlying subalgebras do not commute.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
