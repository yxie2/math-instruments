# Extend bounded finite-propagation operators as multipliers by a positive square

[Family 307](../families/openai-math-307.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000512.md) · [Batch B083](../../batches/B083/README.md)

## Mathematical content

A bounded finite-propagation D need not have compact entries, but DT and TD do for every algebraic Roe operator T, and their propagation remains finite. Reduce D to matching components. For a matching contraction, D^*D is diagonal and W=(1-D^*D)^(1/2) is a bounded diagonal operator. The identity
T^*T-(DT)^*(DT)=(WT)^*(WT)
has every term in the algebraic Roe algebra. Applying arbitrary star representations yields ||DT||_max<=||T||_max. Apply the same argument to D^* and take adjoints for right multiplication.

The compatible left and right actions extend to a multiplier. Summing matching bounds gives ||D||_M<=(2N_R-1)||D||_op. The nondegenerate concrete representation, which contains the compact ideal, extends to multipliers and sends this one to D; hence the assignment is faithful and ||D||_op<=||D||_M. A single matching has equality of norms. Products and adjoints agree on the dense algebra and therefore after extension.

## Role in the source argument

Transporting bounded shifts and group-action operators into a maximal algebra without falsely requiring their entries to be compact.

## Antecedents and comparison

This is standard multiplier theory, with the positive-square argument making contractivity independent of the concrete representation. Spakula-Willett Lemma 1.10 explicitly notes that its partial isometries are multipliers in the compact-entry case; that primary proof sketch was read.

## Checks and limitations

Finite block matching contractions verify the square identity, left and right contraction and multiplier product/adjoint identities. Noncompact-entry issues and infinite completion are outside these tests.

Faithfulness of this assignment on finite-propagation operators does not imply faithfulness of the concrete representation on the entire maximal Roe algebra. No assembly-map counterexample is certified.
