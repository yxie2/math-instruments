# Average a Fourier restriction without discarding exterior degree

[Family 186](../families/openai-math-186.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000355.md) · [Batch B060](../../batches/B060/README.md)

## Mathematical content

For a Boolean function on a finite Bernoulli-p product space, 0<p<1, split coordinates into free F and assigned H. Let f_y(x)=f(x,y), with y sampled from the product law on H. For each A contained in F, orthonormal product Fourier expansion gives

hat(f_y)(A)=sum_(D subset H) hat f(A union D) chi_D(y),

so E_y hat(f_y)(A)^2=sum_(D subset H) hat f(A union D)^2. Therefore the expected restricted Fourier mass on 1<=|A|<=k equals the original mass on supports S satisfying 1<=|S intersect F|<=k. There is no bound on |S intersect H|.

For each free coordinate e, averaging the conditional pivotal event gives E_y I_e(f_y)=I_e(f), hence E_y I(f_y)=sum_(e in F) I_e(f). These are exact identities and require neither graph symmetry nor monotonicity. The source later chooses F as every edge incident with a vertex block, allowing permutations supported on that block to survive arbitrary conditioning outside it.

## Role in the source argument

Preserves exactly the Fourier mass and influence needed before applying a bound to each conditional function.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Kelman, Kindler, Lifshitz, Minzer and Safra](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.10579v2), Lemma 2.7 and its Parseval proof on page 8, give the standard uniform-product restriction identity; that proof was read. The selected source writes out the biased-product version and the influence identity. No novelty is claimed for orthogonality under restriction.

## Checks and limitations

Fifty-four restrictions at biases .2, .5 and .8 verify every coefficient-square identity and averaged influence. Cases include parity, monotone thresholds and arbitrary truth tables, so symmetry and monotonicity are not silently assumed.

Numerical finite-product identities do not verify the subsequent low-degree analytic estimate or sharp threshold-width theorem.

## Possible applications

Averaging conditional Fourier estimates without imposing an unintended bound on the assigned-coordinate degree.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
