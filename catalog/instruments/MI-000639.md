# Force a full torsion graph from two polarization multipliers

[Family 016](../families/openai-math-016.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000408.md) · [Batch B067](../../batches/B067/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let a principally polarized abelian surface A in characteristic zero be geometrically isogenous to a product of a non-CM elliptic curve and a CM elliptic curve. The two nonisogenous simple factors have unique elliptic images E,F in A, hence descend to the field of A. Their addition isogeny phi:E times F->A pulls the polarization back to a diagonal one with positive multipliers n_E,n_F. Its kernel has size n_E n_F and is contained in E[n_E] times F[n_F].

Because E,F are embedded, both kernel projections are injective. Therefore n_E n_F<=n_E² and n_E n_F<=n_F², forcing n_E=n_F=n. Both projections are then bijections onto full n-torsion, so the kernel is the graph of a field-defined isomorphism F[n]->E[n]. The isogeny degree is n² and its pullback polarization is n times the product. Every product-to-A isogeny factors through this addition map; its degree is at least n², with equality only when the two source factor maps have degree one.

## Role in the source argument

Identifies the arithmetic gluing level with the minimal product-isogeny degree.

## Antecedents and comparison

This is classical polarized product gluing combined with uniqueness of nonisogenous simple factors. The inspected source proof uses only degree comparison and injective projections at the decisive step. No new classification of principally polarized surfaces or unlikely intersections is asserted.

## Checks and limitations

Finite groups (Z/nZ)² and invertible torsion maps check graph sizes and both projections; integer multiplier searches verify that the two size inequalities force equality.

Arbitrary finite torsion isomorphisms need not yield a principally polarized quotient; the tests check only the kernel argument after its geometric hypotheses.

## Possible applications

Recovering a common gluing level and minimal degree from polarization data.
