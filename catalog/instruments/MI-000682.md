# Extract a Fourier energy lower bound from centered density alone

[Family 090](../families/openai-math-090.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000451.md) · [Batch B072](../../batches/B072/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let C be locally finite in R2 with N_R=#(C intersect B_R) and N_R/(pi R^2)->1. For M_R(xi)=sum_(x in C intersect B_R) exp(2 pi i x.xi), every fixed epsilon>0 satisfies liminf (1/N_R) integral_(|xi|<=epsilon)|M_R|^2>=1.

Choose a smooth cutoff phi equal to one on B1 and supported in B_(1+d), with 0<=phi<=1. Fourier inversion pairs M_R with R^2 phihat(Rxi) to give N_R. The omitted tail is at most N_R delta_R, where delta_R is the Schwartz Fourier tail and tends to zero. Cauchy-Schwarz and Plancherel give integral_low |M_R|^2 >= N_R^2(1-delta_R)^2/(R^2||phi||_2^2). Density and ||phi||_2^2<=pi(1+d)^2 yield (1+d)^-2 after division by N_R; send d down to zero.

For real even Schwartz f with fhat>=0, the exact finite pair-sum identity is sum_(x,y) f(x-y)=integral fhat|M_R|^2. Continuity at zero and the preceding low-frequency mass imply liminf (1/N_R)sum_(x!=y) f(x-y)>=fhat(0)-f(0). Any pointwise majorant of f off zero inherits this bound. No separation or periodicity of C is used.

## Role in the source argument

Extends the Fourier comparison stage to arbitrary locally finite configurations of the specified centered density, once a suitable minorant is supplied.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Cohn and Kumar, Universally optimal distribution of points on spheres](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0607446), Lemma 9.2 and Proposition 9.3, give the low-frequency and LP mechanism for periodic configurations; the statements and proofs on PDF pages 38-40 were read. The source replaces that periodic input by a cutoff argument using only centered density. Exhaustive priority of this density formulation was not established.

## Checks and limitations

Finite Gaussian pair sums are compared to independently integrated Fourier intensities for both separated and tightly clustered points. The normalized cutoff lower-bound arithmetic is checked for several density and tail budgets.

Finite examples do not prove an asymptotic density statement or construct sharp triangular-lattice minorants.

## Possible applications

Using a near-zero Fourier mass estimate when periodic approximation or uniform separation is unavailable.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
