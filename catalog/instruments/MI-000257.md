# Twisted central-idempotent selection reads a prescribed extinction time

[Family 198](../families/openai-math-198.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000148.md) · [Batch B019](../../batches/B019/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let R be a unital algebra, e a central idempotent, and alpha an automorphism. Define H(Y)=alpha^*(eY), meaning that r acts as alpha(r) on the selected subspace. Set e_j=alpha^j(e). Then

$$H^{\circ j}(Y)\cong(\alpha^j)^*(e_0e_1\cdots e_{j-1}Y).$$

At stage j, selection by e is the original action of e_j, and the next scalar restriction advances the twist to alpha^(j+1). Centrality makes every product image an R-submodule before twisting. If e_0,...,e_(m-2) act as identity on a nonzero Y_m and e_(m-1) acts as zero, the first m-1 iterates survive and the mth is zero.

The source obtains e_j=(1+z_j)/2 from shifted central involutions. If a finite quotient has independent central images z_0,...,z_(m-1), choose their character chi with signs +1 through m-2 and -1 at m-1. The character idempotent p_chi=|Z|^(-1) sum_(z in Z) chi(z)^(-1)z is nonzero, and C[F]p_chi supplies Y_m. This finite-dimensional module need not remain a module of the same quotient after twisting: alpha is applied to R, and preserving the quotient kernel is unnecessary.

Unbounded extinction for one fixed finitely presented R additionally requires finite quotients with arbitrarily long independent sign windows. That existence is an input to this extracted mechanism, not a consequence of the elementary iteration identity.

## Role in the source argument

Creates module families with controlled iteration lifetimes before the source transfers them to finite-dimensional homological data.

## Antecedents and comparison

Restriction of scalars, commuting central idempotents and finite-group character projectors are classical. The special fixed group and its quotient construction supply the source-specific input and are outside this check.

## Checks and limitations

Read the induction and character-module argument. Exact finite sign algebras check all extinction positions and character idempotents. A quotient-kernel control shows why the twist must be taken over the original algebra.

Choosing a different finite sign algebra for each m does not prove existence of one fixed finitely presented algebra with unbounded extinction. The source requires its separate group construction for that step.

## Possible applications

Encoding a finite stopping time in an iterated module functor while retaining a fixed ambient action.
