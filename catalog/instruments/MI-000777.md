# Telescope a midpoint-lens deficit into diamond distortion

[Family 331](../families/openai-math-331.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000515.md) · [Batch B084](../../batches/B084/README.md)

## Mathematical content

If an infinite epsilon-separated sequence y_j satisfies ||x+y_j||,||x-y_j||<=R, the paired estimate gives R^2-||x||^2>=epsilon^2/48. Choose a finite ancestral H approximating x. Compactness of P_H on the bounded sequence gives i!=j with a small projected difference. Set z=(y_i-y_j)/2: its head is small, its tail has norm at least epsilon/2 minus the error, and ||x+z||,||x-z||<=R. Apply the paired estimate and let the error vanish.

For a noncontracting D-Lipschitz embedding of the depth-k countably branching diamond graph, let L_m be the supremum of normalized lengths of the generation-m edges. For a parent edge, normalize its midpoint displacements by the half-edge distance. Distinct middle vertices give an infinite 2-separated sequence. The lens estimate yields L_m^2<=L_(m+1)^2-1/12. Since L_0>=1 and L_k<=D, telescoping gives D^2>=1+k/12. Suprema need not be attained.

## Role in the source argument

Turning an explicit tail convexity estimate into a quantitative obstruction that accumulates at each graph generation.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Baudier et al., arXiv:1612.01984v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.01984v1), Theorem 4.1 and its proof on PDF page 40, were read: power-type asymptotic midpoint convexity already implies a k^(1/p) distortion lower bound. The source provides an explicit additive square loss for its chosen norm, not a new general obstruction principle.

## Checks and limitations

Finite Hilbert lens examples and finite branching diamond distance calculations check the normalization and telescoping arithmetic.

Finite graph checks do not replace the infinite-sequence compactness argument. The coefficient 1/12 must not be confused with the stronger constant claimed in a different manuscript.
