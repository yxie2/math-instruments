# Use discounted bad-piece mass and truncated rewards to control a joint renewal law

[Family 237](../families/openai-math-237.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000500.md) · [Batch B080](../../batches/B080/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Assume the source finite bridge estimates B_h comparable to h^(-1/4), length mass M_h<=C h^(13/12), diameter-restricted mass B_h{D>x}<=C x^(-1/4), and a positive fraction of B_h carried by lengths >=c h^(4/3). Renewal factorization gives 1-E exp(-sH) comparable to s^(3/4), hence P(H>x)<=C x^(-3/4).

Under discounted finite bridge lists let d=1-E exp(-sH), q=E[exp(-sH);D>x]. The probability a list contains a bad piece is exactly q/(d+q). A bad piece forces the whole bridge to be bad. Taking s=A/x, the assumed finite bridge diameter bound makes that probability at most C A^(-1/4); choose A so it is at most one half. Then q<=d, and splitting at H=x gives the diameter tail C x^(-3/4). It does not follow from the height tail alone.

Marked rewards give (sum_(h<=x)B_h)^2 E[L;H<=x]<=sum_(h<=3x)M_h, hence E[L;H<=x]<=C x^(7/12). Splitting P(L>n) at H=n^(3/4) gives C n^(-9/16). The positive mass of long bridges supplies the matching Laplace-deficit lower bound; a first-moment upper estimate alone cannot do so.

More generally, independent nonnegative increments with P(Y>t)<=t^(-a+o(1)), 0<a<1, have sums at most k^(1/a+o(1)) almost surely. If 1-E exp(-uY)=u^(a+o(1)) too, the lower exponent matches. Dyadic truncation and exponential Markov bounds are summable; Borel-Cantelli and monotonicity fill the intervening k.

## Antecedents and comparison

The mechanisms are standard renewal discounting, tail balancing and dyadic sum estimates. The exponent inputs are source-specific and remain unverified dependencies; the card does not claim a new unconditional honeycomb exponent.

## Checks and limitations

Finite joint increment laws verify the exact bad-piece ratio and the marked truncated-reward inequality. Symbolic exponent balancing checks 7/12 and 9/16; a large-diameter, small-height law controls the invalid height-only inference.

No numerical experiment certifies the almost-sure sum exponent or the required finite honeycomb mass estimates.
