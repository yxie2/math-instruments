# Obtain positive off-diagonal decay without presuming a spectral atom

[Family 215](../families/openai-math-215.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000488.md) · [Batch B077](../../batches/B077/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Assume a nonzero positive Kallen-Lehmann measure rho with polynomial growth, supported on masses mu>=m_*>0, and Euclidean invariance. For t>0 the separated kernel is
C(t,b)=integral rho(dmu^2) integral_(R) dp/(2pi) exp(ipb-t sqrt(p^2+mu^2))/(2 sqrt(p^2+mu^2)).
Exponential damping makes this and its derivatives locally absolutely convergent for t>0. Rotate a separation of length r>0 to (r,0); every integrand is then positive, so C is strictly positive away from coincidence. For r>=1, factor out exp(-m_* r) and bound exp(-r(E-m_*)) by exp(-(E-m_*)). The remaining integral is finite by polynomial growth, giving C(r)<=C0 exp(-m_* r).

In the source, positivity of m_* is deduced from an already supplied Hilbert-space mass gap and a nonzero one-field norm: spectral weight below the gap would produce forbidden low energies at small spatial momentum. That gap is an input here. Neither positivity nor exponential decay requires rho to have an atom at its lower edge. They therefore do not establish an isolated particle pole.

## Role in the source argument

Separates the basic massive two-point bound from the stronger pole-isolation task.

## Antecedents and comparison

The Kallen-Lehmann representation and exponential damping of positive spectral measures are classical. The original representation papers were not independently audited in this batch. The card makes the no-atom distinction explicit and claims no new spectral-representation theorem.

## Checks and limitations

Numerical quadrature compares momentum integrals with modified Bessel kernels and verifies the decay envelope for atomic and continuous compactly supported mass measures, including an atomless lower edge.

The quadrature does not certify the source continuum axioms, gap, spectral measure or particle pole.

## Possible applications

Avoiding the mistaken inference from an exponential correlation bound to a one-particle spectral atom.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
