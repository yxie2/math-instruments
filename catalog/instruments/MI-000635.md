# Reverse regular nilpotent boundary monodromy inside a compact group

[Family 014](../families/openai-math-014.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000404.md) · [Batch B066](../../batches/B066/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let N be regular nilpotent in pgl_n over a finite extension of Q_ell, and let A lie in a compact subgroup H_0 with Ad(A)N=qN, where q>1 is an integer. After a finite coefficient extension, choose an eigenvector v_0 generating a Jordan chain v_i=N^i v_0. The eigenvalues of a lift of A are a_0 q^i and are distinct, so this eigenbasis exists. The projective diagonal cocharacter c(z)v_i=z^i v_i commutes with A and satisfies Ad(c(z))N=zN.

In a closed faithful linear representation, a lattice invariant under the compact H_0 gives a compact open subgroup J containing H_0. Choose an integer e>1 congruent to -1 modulo a sufficiently high power of ell. Then e is prime to ell and -1/e is close enough to 1 that P=c(-1/e) belongs to J. Consequently P commutes with A and P exp(-e aN) P^(-1)=exp(aN) for every a. This aligns opposite boundary orientations while preserving the same arithmetic action. Compactness is used for the invariant lattice and the neighborhood condition, not for diagonalizing A.

## Role in the source argument

Makes a boundary-monodromy identification compatible with a fixed Frobenius action and finite compact quotients.

## Antecedents and comparison

The ingredients are standard regular nilpotent linear algebra, scaling cocharacters and invariant lattices for compact p-adic groups. The source assembles them for its boundary gluing. No external earliest-priority claim or eigensheaf existence theorem is asserted.

## Checks and limitations

Exact conjugated Jordan-chain matrices check the scaling, commutation and exponential identities. Rational valuations check that e=-1 modulo ell^k makes the conjugator approach the identity.

Matrix examples do not construct nodal torsors, specialize eigencomplexes or prove nonvanishing nearby cycles.

## Possible applications

Matching nilpotent monodromies with opposite orientations without changing a commuting arithmetic symmetry.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
