# The determinant image of a stabilizer controls singular special-linear orbits

[Family 191](../families/openai-math-191.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000126.md) · [Batch B013](../../batches/B013/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let R=Z/(B^k) for prime B, h=B^k, and let C be a 3-by-3 matrix with at least one unit entry. Smith reduction gives C=P diag(1,D,E) Q with D=B^b, E=B^e and 0<=b<=e<=k. The value h represents a zero diagonal residue but remains a positive integer in counting formulas. The inverse-image row lattice Lambda_C in Z^3 has index DE and contains E Z^3. Also b>=j exactly when all two-by-two minors vanish modulo B^j.

For the left special-linear orbit O_C one has

$$|O_C|\ge\frac{21}{64}\frac{h^8}{D^3E^2}.$$

Let H be the GL3 stabilizer. In diagonal coordinates its first column is fixed, its second has three adjustments annihilated by D and its third three annihilated by E. Hence |H|<=D^3E^3. Its determinant image contains all units congruent to one modulo h/E: at least (1-1/B)E of them, including the singular case E=h. Therefore

$$\frac{|\operatorname{SL}_3(R)C|}{|\operatorname{GL}_3(R)C|}
=\frac{|\det H|}{|R^\times|}\ge E/h.$$

Use |GL3(R)|=h^9(1-1/B)(1-1/B^2)(1-1/B^3)>=(21/64)h^9 to finish. Uniform G in SL3 makes GC uniform on its orbit, with unchanged row span and determinant. The determinant-image factor is necessary to avoid losing another power of E in the estimate.

## Role in the source argument

Bounds the chance of a prescribed residue matrix after random determinant-preserving coordinates, including matrices of rank less than three.

## Antecedents and comparison

Smith normal form and orbit-stabilizer counting are established. The source credits [Smith, 1861](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstl.1861.0016); its publisher page was not retrievable in this batch. The selected item is the explicit singular SL-orbit budget and determinant-image factor.

## Checks and limitations

Read the diagonal reduction and all singular stabilizer cases. Exact enumeration over small prime-power rings checks group orders, orbit sizes, determinant-image ratios and row-span cardinalities.

A unit entry is required for the first Smith factor to be one. The constant is a uniform lower bound, not an exact orbit formula. General composite moduli require additional decomposition.

## Possible applications

Uniform sampling and lattice counts under determinant-preserving transformations over finite local rings.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
