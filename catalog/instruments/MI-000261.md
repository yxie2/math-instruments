# Nested filtration profiles determine the generic intersection exactly

[Family 204](../families/openai-math-204.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000150.md) · [Batch B019](../../batches/B019/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let H=F1 contain F2 contain ... contain F_(s+1)=0 be a filtration of a finite-dimensional complex vector space and P its full invertible stabilizer. For A,C contained in H set a_b=dim(A intersect F_b), c_b=dim(C intersect F_b), f_b=dim F_b. On a nonempty Zariski-open subset of P,

$$\dim(A\cap gC)=\max\left(0,\max_b(a_b+c_b-f_b)\right).$$

Every term is a lower bound by the dimension inequality inside F_b. To attain them, use coordinates adapted to the flag. A basis with profile a has a_b-a_(b+1) generic vectors supported in F_b, and likewise for c. Their allowed supports are nested. Hall's criterion therefore has only the suffix constraints: at level b, a_b+c_b vectors must fit into f_b coordinates. A matching from the deepest level outward leaves exactly the largest positive deficit unmatched.

A matching exhibits a nonzero minor of that size, so generic rank attains the bound. Each separate list remains independent, making the combined rank deficiency exactly the intersection dimension. The stabilizer is transitive on subspaces with a prescribed profile, allowing A to be fixed and only C to move. Nonvanishing of the relevant minor is an open condition.

## Role in the source argument

Controls intersections of incoming annihilators and outgoing kernels using all nilpotent filtration levels.

## Antecedents and comparison

The proof explicitly invokes [Hall, Theorem 1](https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms/s1-10.37.26). Generic rank through matching is established; the card preserves the complete filtration profile needed in the source application.

## Checks and limitations

Read the proof. Exhaustive small profiles compare the formula with a maximum matching. Random integer flag-preserving matrices check attained intersection dimensions after specialization to a finite field.

The ordinary ambient dimension inequality alone misses deeper filtration obstructions. A single finite-field sample can lie outside the open set, so tests seek an attained bound rather than asserting every sample is generic.
