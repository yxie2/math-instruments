# Use a split third boundary to obtain a finite plus construction for all coefficients

[Family 321](../families/openai-math-321.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000198.md) · [Batch B031](../../batches/B031/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let B be finite, connected and at most two-dimensional, P=pi_1(B), and K the perfect normal closure of r elements. Attach r two-cells along them, obtaining B' with fundamental group G=P/K. The preimage bar B in the universal cover of B' is connected with fundamental group K, hence H_1(bar B;Z)=0.

For R=ZG, the relative chain complex of this cover pair is R^r in degree two. The long exact sequence makes H_2 of the simply connected cover surject onto R^r. Choose lifts of its standard basis and represent them by spheres using Hurewicz. Attach r three-cells along their projections to obtain X.

Writing C_2(tilde X)=C_2(bar B) direct-sum R^r, projection q onto the new summand satisfies q partial_3=id. Thus partial_3 is injective and every map phi:C_3->M extends as phi q, proving H^3(X;M)=0 for every left R-module M. The relative complex is R^r --id--> R^r in degrees three and two. Degreewise splitting survives tensoring by every right R-module N, so H_*(B;N)=H_*(X;N). No flatness of N is needed.

## Role in the source argument

Produces the source finite D(2) complex while preserving the local-system homology used by its separate obstruction.

## Antecedents and comparison

This is the finite Quillen plus construction; [Mannan 2009, Section 2](https://msp.org/agt/2009/9-3/agt-v9-n3-p06-s.pdf) gives the same finite two/three-cell mechanism and coefficient control. It is not a new plus-construction theorem.

## Checks and limitations

Sixty split-boundary models reduced over non-field coefficient rings verify the left inverse and extension identities.

These are chain-algebra tests; attaching-sphere realization and the source obstruction to a two-dimensional model are not computed.

## Possible applications

Finite topology constructions preserving twisted homology with uniform control over coefficient modules.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
