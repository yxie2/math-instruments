# Obtain weighted-generator regularity from the original heat resolvent

[Family 356](../families/openai-math-356.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000380.md) · [Batch B063](../../batches/B063/README.md)

## Mathematical content

On a full-support RCD(K,N) space with finite N>1, let V be bounded and globally Lipschitz and m_V=exp(V)m. Equivalent bounded densities preserve weak gradients and W^(1,2). Testing the weak generator with exp(plus-or-minus V) times a Sobolev test gives D(Delta_V)=D(Delta), Delta_V u=Delta u+Gamma(V,u), with equivalent graph norms. The energy identity controls the gradient term. This does not assert preservation of the narrower test class.

For sufficiently large a, the original resolvent R_a=(a-Delta)^(-1) has L² and L-infinity norm at most a^(-1), and gradient bounds C a^(-1/2) in both norms. The infinity gradient estimate follows by integrating the original heat-gradient estimate. On E={z in W^(1,2):z and |grad z| bounded}, with the sum of the four L² and L-infinity norms, z -> R_a(h+Gamma(V,z)) is a contraction for large a. Its fixed point solves (a-Delta_V)z=h. Weighted energy gives uniqueness. If xi lies in D(Delta_V) with xi and Delta_V xi bounded, set h=(a-Delta_V)xi; uniqueness identifies xi with this fixed point, and Sobolev-to-Lipschitz yields a globally Lipschitz representative.

The argument uses original heat estimates before assigning any curvature bound to the weighted space, avoiding dependence on a weighted regularity statement that the later proof needs to establish.

## Role in the source argument

Supplies regular weighted multipliers for a subsequent Bochner approximation argument.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Brena and Gigli, Fine representation of Hessian of convex functions and Ricci tensor on RCD spaces](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07536), Remark 2.4, explicitly identifies a weighted heat regularity obstacle in reversing weak Hessian bounds to convexity. Pages 13-15 were read. The source uses bounded Lipschitz weights and a resolvent contraction to obtain the particular generator regularity above; this comparison does not certify its full later measure-change theorem.

## Checks and limitations

Fourier-Galerkin circle models compare the drift-resolvent fixed point with a direct linear solve and check the norm contraction for sufficiently large a.

Finite Fourier models do not prove heat-gradient bounds, infinite-dimensional domain equality or the every-geodesic theorem.

## Possible applications

Proving regularity after a bounded measure change using estimates available before the change.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
