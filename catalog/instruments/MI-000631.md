# Recover a common power correction from two subfield cosets

[Family 009](../families/openai-math-009.md) · [Source manuscript](../papers/P-000400.md) · [Batch B066](../../batches/B066/README.md)

## Mathematical content

Let F have characteristic p and finite p-degree, C=F^p, and X,Y be p-independent. Suppose U in C(X)^*, V in C(Y)^*, with their classes modulo C^* outside the F_p spans of [X],[Y]. If X C(Y/X)^* intersects U C(V/U)^*, there is a unique 1<=n<p such that U^n lies in C+CX^n and V^n in C+CY^n, both coefficients nonzero.

For a witness Z, use commuting Euler derivations D_X,D_Y from a p-basis. Set B_X=U/D_XU, A_X=B_X-1, and similarly for Y. The equations (D_X+D_Y)Z=Z=(B_XD_X+B_YD_Y)Z imply q_X+q_Y=1 and A_Xq_X+A_Yq_Y=0, where q_i=D_iZ/Z. Both q_i are nonzero and killed by D_X+D_Y. Hence D_XA_X/A_X=D_YA_Y/A_Y belongs to C(X) intersect C(Y)=C. The p-monomial eigenbasis forces A_X=c_X X^m and A_Y=c_Y Y^m with the same nonzero m modulo p. Put n=p-m. Then D_X kills U^n/(X^n+c_X X^p), so the quotient is in C^*. The analogous identity holds for Y. If another r worked, logarithmic differentiation would make A_X a scalar multiple of X^(p-r); distinct p-monomials force r=n.

## Role in the source argument

Synchronizes a projective power ambiguity when reconstructing additive lines.

## Antecedents and comparison

[Cadoret and Pirutka, Reconstructing function fields from Milnor K-theory](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.04944), section 3.4, Lemma 22 and its differential argument, already recover lines up to powers from related intersections. Pages 11-13 were read. The source specializes the mechanism to degree-p subfields and Euler eigenvectors; no independent novelty certification is made.

## Checks and limitations

Exact rational-function calculations in characteristics 2, 3, 5 and 7 check witness equations, common Euler eigenvalues and uniqueness of the correcting exponent on explicit nonmonomial families.

Examples verify the differential mechanism, not the global recovery of all projective lines or fields.

## Possible applications

Recognizing affine linear combinations from multiplicative and subfield incidence information.

These are proposed uses; no subsequent application is recorded for this instrument.
